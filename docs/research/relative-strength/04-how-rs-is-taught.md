## 1. IBD / Investor's Business Daily / MarketSmith (O'Neil ecosystem)

**One-sentence definitions (IBD's own words, syndicated on Yahoo Finance):**
- RS line: "The Relative Strength line gauges a stock's performance vs. the S&P 500 index." … "A rising line means the stock is outperforming the broader market." — IBD, *Relative Strength Rating Pinpoints A Stock's Power* (2012), https://finance.yahoo.com/news/relative-strength-rating-pinpoints-stocks-214500318.html
- RS Rating: "The RS Rating gauges a stock's performance vs. all other stocks in IBD's database over the past 12 months." … "An RS rating of 90 or better means a stock is already outperforming 90% or more of the market."
- RS line on the chart: "the squiggly blue line found under the stock's price bars." … "The steeper the upslope of the RS line the stronger the outperformance." — IBD, *Use The Relative Strength Line To Identify Emerging Leadership* (2015), https://finance.yahoo.com/news/relative-strength-line-identify-emerging-210700981.html

**Analogies IBD uses:**
- Baseball playoffs: a team only needs to beat the other teams; a stock that falls 15% while the market falls 25% is showing strength.
- Pitching: "RS Ratings are similar to fastballs thrown by the best pitchers."
- O'Neil's gardening line (MarketSmith HK): "Relative price performance is a good way to tell which of the five or six stocks you own is your true leader" and "weed the flower patch by selling your worst-performing stocks first." https://www.marketsmith.hk/v2/blog/identifying-market-leaders-understanding-rs-line-and-rs-rating?lang=en

**Rules of thumb:**
- 80+: "you should prioritize those with a Relative Strength Rating of at least 80."
- 87 average: "IBD research shows, from 1950 through 2008, the average RS Rating of the best-performing stocks just prior to their winning runs was 87."
- RS line before new high: "Even more bullish is when the RS line jumps to new highs before the stock breaks out." … "A base breakout from a stock with a declining RS line is weaker and more likely to fail."
- Blue dot: "a bullish combination of new highs for the line while the stock is near a buy point." https://www.inkl.com/news/stock-of-the-day-hits-this-buy-point-after-analyst-notes-superior-economics
- Not a buy signal: "a high RS Rating doesn't guarantee a winning run; it alone is not a buy signal." … "The trick is to find a stock that has managed to build or maintain a high RS Rating while basing."

**Best plain-English framing found (MarketSmith India):** "It answers a narrower question: is this security behaving better or worse than the comparison we chose?" … "Change the benchmark, and the question changes." … "The feature is not a clock." … "Relative strength belongs in that system. It is not the system." https://marketsmithin.substack.com/p/the-stock-rose-did-it-lead

## (b) Recommended three-level explanation

**10 words:** Is this stock beating the market, or just riding it?

**30 words:** Relative strength compares a stock's price to the market (usually the S&P 500). When the RS line rises, the stock is winning that race; when it falls, the market is winning.

**100 words:** Relative strength (RS) asks one question: is this stock doing better or worse than the market? We divide the stock's price by the S&P 500 every day and draw the result as a line. A rising line means the stock is outperforming; a falling line means it is lagging, even if its price is going up. Think of a beach ball held under water: a strong stock keeps pushing up while the market presses down, and springs higher when the pressure lifts. RS tells you *which* stocks to study, not *when* to buy. It is not RSI, which measures a stock's own momentum.

## (c) Glossary (plain English)

1. **RS line** — The stock's price divided by the market's, drawn as a line. Up = beating the market; down = lagging it. Only its direction and its own prior highs matter.
2. **RS Rating** — A 1–99 score ranking the stock's price gain over roughly the past year against every other stock. 90 means it beat 90% of them.
3. **RS new high** — The RS line is higher than at any point in the lookback. The stock is outperforming by the most it has recently.
4. **RS new high before price** — The RS line hits a new high while the price is still below its own high. Leaders often do this before breaking out.
5. **Benchmark** — What you compare against (S&P 500, Nasdaq, a sector). Change it and the answer changes.
6. **Mansfield RS** — Weinstein's version: the RS ratio measured against its own 52-week average so it swings around zero. Above zero and rising = outperforming.
7. **RRG quadrant** — Leading (strong and gaining), Weakening (strong but fading), Lagging (weak and fading), Improving (weak but gaining); they tend to rotate clockwise.
8. **Momentum** — How fast a price (or an RS line) is moving.
9. **Relative weakness** — The stock is losing ground to the market, even if its price is rising.
10. **Leader vs laggard** — A leader beats the market and its group; a laggard trails them.
11. **Defensive RS** — RS that rises only because the stock fell less than a falling market. It can fade when the market recovers.
12. **Lookback** — The window used for a rating or a "new high" (52 weeks, 3 months). Shorter reacts faster but flips more often.
13. **Percentile rank** — "Beat X% of the group."
14. **Divergence** — Price and the RS line disagree: price makes a new high but RS does not (warning), or price falls while RS rises (hidden strength).
15. **Rotation** — Money moving from one group of stocks to another; RS lines turn up in the new group and down in the old one.

## (d) 5-card onboarding tour

1. **"Beating the market, or just riding it?"** — RS compares your stock to the S&P 500. *A stock can go up and still lose the race.*
2. **"Read the slope, not the level."** — The RS line rises when the stock outperforms and falls when it lags. *Ignore whether the line sits above or below the price; it is not a moving average.*
3. **"Watch for RS new highs."** — When the line tops its old high before the price does, big buyers may be stepping in early. *IBD's winners averaged an RS Rating of 87 before their runs.*
4. **"Strong on a weak day."** — Stocks that close green, or barely dip, while the market sells off are showing real demand. *Like a beach ball held under water, they tend to pop first when selling stops.*
5. **"RS says where to look, not when to buy."** — Combine it with a base, volume and your own stop. *High RS after a huge run can mean the stock is extended, not safe.*

## (e) 8 most common misconceptions, with one-line corrections

1. **"RS is RSI."** — RS compares a stock to a benchmark and has no bounds; RSI measures the stock's own momentum on a 0–100 scale.
2. **"A rising RS line means the price is going up."** — It means the stock is doing *better than the market*; both can be falling.
3. **"A falling RS line means the stock is going down."** — The stock may be rising while the market rises faster.
4. **"RS rose during the sell-off, so it's a leader."** — It may just have fallen less; defensive names often give that back when growth returns.
5. **"RS 99 means buy now."** — RS is a filter, not a timing signal; a 99 after a parabolic move is often extended.
6. **"Any benchmark will do."** — A small-cap versus the S&P 500, or a stock versus the wrong sector, misleads; say what you compare to.
7. **"Good RS excuses a bad chart."** — "a pretty base with a falling RS line is distribution wearing a costume."
8. **"High RS means low risk."** — It means outperforming, not protected; in a bear market the leader simply falls less.
