## What the table actually says

⭐ **THE HIGH TIGHT FLAG IS NOT MERELY FIRST, IT IS DOUBLE THE SECOND.** 22% at one
month against 9-11% for everything behind it. That is the widest gap in the table and
it is the reason the pattern has the reputation it has.

🔴 **AND IT CONTRADICTS ITS OWN DETAIL PAGE, WHICH RANKS IT 30 OF 39.** Both numbers
are Bulkowski's, on the same site. They are not in conflict — *rank* folds in failure
rate, throwback rate and how often the target is met, while this table is raw move —
but a catalog that quoted one without the other would be telling half a story in
whichever direction it chose. ⛔ Record both, always, and say which question each
answers. This is the same discipline as `_no_average`, applied to a statistic instead
of a threshold.

⚠️ **THE UP SIDE IS ROUGHLY TWICE THE DOWN SIDE AT EVERY RANK.** Best long is 22%,
best short is −10%. Nothing here is symmetric, and a product that offers bearish
setups with the same prominence as bullish ones is implying a parity the measurements
do not support.

⚠️ **`Rounded top` APPEARS IN BOTH TABLES** — 7/10/14% up and −6/−4/−4% down. One
pattern, two breakout directions, both profitable in their own direction. ⛔ It is
therefore not a "bearish pattern", and filing it as one — which is what a taxonomy
built from names rather than measurements would do — would mislabel it.

⭐ **THE THREE-MONTH COLUMN IS WHERE SWING SETUPS SEPARATE.** Rectangle bottom, Eve &
Adam double bottom and rounded top all *improve* from month one to month three (8→14,
7→14, 7→14), while flags **decay** (8→7) and the high tight flag plateaus (22→21).
That is the difference between a base that keeps working and a thrust that spends
itself, and it is a genuinely useful thing to tell a member choosing a holding period.

⛔ **NONE OF THESE NUMBERS IS A FILTER.** They describe what followed the pattern in a
back-test; no stock can satisfy "average rise 22%" today. They belong beside a
criterion as the reason to care about it, never inside one.
