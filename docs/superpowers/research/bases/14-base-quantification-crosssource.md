# Base Quantification — Cross-Source Comparison

**Organising principle:** every other file in this corpus covers one authority. This one covers one
MEASUREMENT at a time, so that where two houses disagree the disagreement is visible on a single row
pair instead of buried in two separate documents. Single-candlestick patterns are out of scope.

**Scope note on evidence class.** Throughout, a number is marked as either ASSERTED (the house states
it as a rule, with no sample behind it) or MEASURED (the source states a sample size and period). These
are not interchangeable. IBD's "80% of fourth-stage bases fail" and Bulkowski's "62% throwback rate on
913 perfect trades" are different kinds of claim and are never mixed in the same conclusion here.

**Spread.** Base-depth limits run from 10% (IBD's lower flat-base figure) to 75% (the deepest number
any source attributes to O'Neil), with the bulk of the published mass between 12% and 35%. Within the
cup-with-handle alone the published ceiling is variously 30%, 33%, or 35%, and the floor variously
12% or 20%. The only *market-conditional* formulation on record is O'Neil's "1-1/2 to two times the
market average", which makes the limit a ratio to the index drawdown rather than a constant — every
other source states a constant.

**Irreconcilable.** IBD's own flat-base article names 35% as the depth "you might see with a cup-shaped
structure **or double bottom**", while the IBD chart-patterns handout caps the double bottom at
"Max 15%". These cannot both be right for the same named pattern: a 25%-deep double bottom is a
textbook example under one and a disqualified structure under the other. Separately, IBD's cup depth
is published three ways — 12%–33%, 20%–30%, and 35% — and a 32%-deep cup passes the first, fails the
second, and passes the third. Neither conflict is averaged here; both numbers stand as published.

**Computability.** Fully computable from daily OHLCV. Fix the base window `[t0, t1]`. Take
`baseHigh = max(High[t0..t1])` and `baseLow = min(Low[t0..t1])` — IBD's "intraday peak to intraday
trough" wording forces H/L rather than closes, which changes the answer on any gappy series.
`depth = (baseHigh − baseLow) / baseHigh`. The primitives are a rolling max of `High`, a rolling min
of `Low`, and the index of the max (the base high must precede the base low for a corrective base;
without that ordering constraint the formula also fires on a rising channel). **Not computable from
the symbol's own daily OHLCV:** the bear-market allowance and O'Neil's "1-1/2 to two times the market
average" both require a *second* series (the index) and a definition of the concurrent market
drawdown window — an implementation must supply both, and the window is not published by anyone.

**Spread.** Published minimums run from 3 days (Darvas, to establish one box boundary) through 2 weeks
(Qullamaggie) and 5 weeks (IBD flat base) to 9 weeks (IBD ascending base); published maximums exist for
only three structures — 65 weeks (cup, both IBD and Bulkowski), 3 weeks (Bulkowski flags), and 5 weeks
(IBD high tight flag). Everything else is open-ended at the top.

**Irreconcilable.** IBD requires a cup-with-handle to run "at least seven weeks" and states flatly that
"One, two or three weeks do not get the job done"; Qullamaggie's breakout setup accepts a consolidation
"usually 2 weeks to 2 months". A two-week consolidation after a 40% run is a valid, tradeable
Qullamaggie base and is explicitly disqualified by IBD. Separately, Bulkowski's flag is "less than 3
weeks long" while IBD's high tight flag is "three to five weeks" — a four-week consolidation after a
double is a flag to IBD and is not a flag to Bulkowski. These are not reconciled; both stand.

**Computability.** Computable from daily OHLCV, with one trap. Base length in *weeks* is not
`bar_count / 5`: IBD counts calendar weeks from the week containing the base high to the week
containing the breakout, so the primitive is a weekly resample (`W-FRI`) of the daily bars and a count
of resampled rows, not a division. The base start `t0` must itself be derived — for a corrective base
it is the bar of the prior swing high (`argmax(High)` over a lookback), which means base length and
base depth share the same window-detection primitive and must be computed together or they will
disagree. **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone:** nothing here; but note that Darvas's 3-day rule
is a *sequential state machine* over daily bars, not a windowed aggregate, and cannot be expressed as
a rolling min/max.

the lowest close" must not exceed 1% to 2% — the span of the cluster. A three-week run closing at 100.0,
101.4, 102.8 has pairwise differences of 1.4% each (a valid three-weeks-tight under the first rule) and
a high-to-low span of 2.8% (a rejected pattern under the second). No reading satisfies both, and an
implementation must name which sentence it implements. Separately, Deepvue ships RMV < 15 on its
indicator page and RMV < 10 in its Tight Range screener preset; both are the same vendor's own number
for the same idea, and they are not averaged here.

**Computability.** The IBD numbers are fully computable from daily OHLCV once the ambiguity above is
resolved. Resample to weekly (`W-FRI`) closes; the pairwise test is
`max(|C_w − C_{w−1}| / C_{w−1}) <= 0.015` over the three-week window; the span test is
`(max(C_w) − min(C_w)) / min(C_w) <= 0.02`. Closing-range tightness per bar is `(C − L) / (H − L)`;
range compression is `mean(H − L over last N bars) / ATR(50)`. **Two things are not computable as
published.** `RMV` is Deepvue's proprietary measure — the docs give the 0-to-100 scale and the 15-bar
default lookback but not the formula, so neither the 10 nor the 15 threshold can be reproduced on other
data; an implementation must substitute a standard measure (an ATR ratio, or the standard deviation of
returns over the window) and say plainly that the substitution is its own. And for base tightness in
general — as opposed to the three-weeks-tight pattern — there is simply no number to implement, from any
house, so the criterion must either carry an owned threshold or be dropped.

there is more agreement than on the magnitude: 50 days is named by both the Kacher/Morales rule and
LuxAlgo.

**Irreconcilable.** No head-to-head numeric conflict, because only one house-lineage publishes a
magnitude. The conflict that does exist is a category error waiting to happen: Bulkowski's 70–77% are
measured frequencies of a downward volume *trend* across his sample, and they will be misread as
"volume must be 70% of average" by anyone skimming. They are recorded here explicitly so that an
implementation does not silently convert a frequency into a threshold.

**Computability.** Fully computable from daily OHLCV. `avgVol50 = SMA(Volume, 50)`; a dry-up bar is
`Volume < 0.5 * avgVol50`. Two choices are *not* published and must be owned: (a) whether the 50-day
average is taken as of the dry-up bar or as of the base start — these differ materially inside a base
where volume has already been falling for weeks, and a trailing average will chase the dry-up down and
under-report it; (b) how many consecutive dry-up bars are required — every source describes "the VDU
candle" in the singular, which would make a single quiet day sufficient. The pocket-pivot rule is
computable as `Volume[t] > max(Volume[s] for s in [t-10, t-1] where Close[s] < Close[s-1])` with
`Close[t] > Close[t-1]`; note it needs the *sign* of each prior bar, so it is a masked rolling max, not
a plain one.

**Spread.** Required breakout volume runs from +40% above average (IBD, Minervini via secondaries) to
2× average (Weinstein via secondaries and EdgeStacker's formalization) to 3× as the stated ideal
(Weinstein via secondary) — that is, from 1.4× to 3.0×. The averaging window varies at least as much as
the magnitude: 5 days (Bulkowski's measurement methodology), 50 days (IBD, EdgeStacker, and the
Kacher/Morales VDU rule), and one *weekly* bar's average (Weinstein via Next Big Trade). Bulkowski
alone publishes no threshold and instead publishes the measured monotone relationship.

**Irreconcilable.** IBD requires "at least 40% above its 50-day average"; Weinstein (as reported)
requires at least 2× average volume. A breakout on 1.5× the 50-day average is a confirmed IBD breakout
and an unconfirmed Weinstein breakout — the two rules disagree on a very large fraction of real
breakouts, since the band between 1.4× and 2.0× is where most of them land. The window conflict is
sharper still and cannot be papered over: comparing a breakout day to the prior *5* days (Bulkowski)
versus the prior *50* days (IBD) inverts the answer inside a base, because a base with a genuine
volume dry-up has a depressed 5-day average, so a breakout that is unremarkable against 50 days can
read as a 3× expansion against 5 days. Neither window is averaged with the other here; both are
recorded, and any implementation must pick one and say which.

**Computability.** Fully computable from daily OHLCV. `avgVol = SMA(Volume, N)` with `N ∈ {5, 50}`
per the house being implemented; IBD's test is `Volume[breakout] >= 1.40 * SMA(Volume, 50)[breakout]`;
Weinstein's, as formalised by EdgeStacker, is `Volume[breakout] >= 2 * SMA(Volume, 50)[breakout]`.
Two traps. First, Bulkowski's window explicitly *excludes* the breakout bar ("leading to, but not
including, the day of breakout"); IBD's wording does not say, and a 50-day SMA that includes the
breakout bar is biased upward by that bar, making the test very slightly self-defeating — an
implementation must choose and state it. Second, the Weinstein weekly formulation is a different
computation entirely (resample to weekly, compare the week's total volume to a mean of prior weekly
totals) and will not agree with any daily version on breakout weeks that start slow.

successive contractions is stated as approximately ½ by LuxAlgo (as a formula) and by Minervini via a

0.67 then 0.50 (18/12/6 and 15/10/5), and 0.40–0.60 (25 → 10-or-15 → 3-to-8). The minimum tightness of

is squarely "typical" under TrendSpider's "2-6 pullbacks" and is outside the typical band under
LuxAlgo's "two to four contractions typical" and TradingSim's "2–4". More sharply, LuxAlgo's stated
formula `Depth k+1 ≈ Depth k / 2` and Deepvue's own worked example `15% → 10% → 5%` disagree at the
first step, since 10 is two-thirds of 15, not half — the vendor's formula rejects the vendor's own
illustration. Neither is adjusted here to fit the other.

**Computability.** Computable from daily OHLCV, but it requires a swing-detection primitive that is
itself unpublished, which is where implementations will silently diverge. The chain is: (1) detect
swing highs and lows within the base window — typically a fractal/pivot detector with a lookback `k`
(a bar is a swing high if `High[t] == max(High[t-k..t+k])`); **no house publishes `k`**; (2) pair each

`depth_i = (SwingHigh_i − SwingLow_i) / SwingHigh_i`; (3) require `depth_{i+1} < depth_i` for all `i`,
and optionally `depth_{i+1} / depth_i <= 0.5` for the halving formulation; (4) require
`count(contractions) >= N` for a chosen `N`. Every step after (1) is arithmetic on daily bars; step (1)
is a free parameter that changes the contraction count on the same chart. **Not computable from daily
OHLCV alone:** nothing, but be aware that the answer is a function of `k` and that reporting a
contraction count without reporting `k` is meaningless.

after the fact (Qullamaggie: the opening-range high on the breakout day). Offsets run from zero
(Bulkowski, LuxAlgo) to a flat $0.10 (every IBD pattern) to unspecified "some points" (Darvas). Only
IBD publishes a maximum acceptable distance above the pivot: 5%, stated in two separate articles and
explicitly sized against the 7%–8% loss-cutting rule.

**Irreconcilable.** IBD triggers on price *trading* 10 cents above the pivot; Bulkowski confirms only
when price *closes* above the highest peak. On a breakout that spikes through the pivot and closes back
inside the base — a common failure shape — IBD has a signal and Bulkowski has none, on the identical
bar. This is not a difference of degree that can be split; it is a difference in which field of the bar
is read (`High` versus `Close`). Separately, IBD's two flat-base articles anchor the pivot differently
("the highest price in the pattern" versus "the price high made before the decline"), which produces
two different buy points on any flat base whose right side runs above its left. And IBD's two
three-weeks-tight articles disagree on the add-on size for the identical signal — "one-eighth or a
quarter" of a full position versus "say, 5%" — a factor of 2.5 to 5 apart. None of these is averaged.

**Computability.** Mostly computable from daily OHLCV. `pivot = max(High[t0..t1]) + 0.10` for the IBD
flat base; the cup-with-handle version needs the handle sub-window to be detected first, which is a
separate unpublished problem (see §10). Bulkowski's version is `Close[t] > max(High[t0..t1])`, using
`Close` on the left and `High` on the right — the asymmetry is deliberate and must be preserved. The
buy zone is `pivot <= Price <= pivot * 1.05`. The three-weeks-tight trigger is
`max(High[w1..w3]) + 0.10` — note that this pattern is *defined* on weekly closes (§3) and *triggered*
on "the highest intraday price in the tight pattern", so a correct implementation reads `Close` to
qualify the pattern and `High` to place the pivot, over the same three bars.
**Two things are not computable as published.** First,
IBD's "+10 cents" is a nominal dollar amount: on a $12 stock it is 0.83% and on a $900 stock it is
0.011%, so it does not survive translation to a percentage, and it is undefined for non-USD instruments
and for anything with a split history that has not been adjusted consistently — an implementation must
decide whether to keep 10 nominal cents or substitute a percentage, and must say which. Second,
Qullamaggie's opening-range high requires **intraday bars** (1-, 5-, or 60-minute) and cannot be
derived from a daily OHLCV series at all; a daily-only implementation must either refuse the entry
criterion or substitute a proxy (e.g. prior-day high) and disclose the substitution.

**Spread.** There is no spread on the headline measurement, because **no house publishes a maximum
distance above a moving average at entry** — the answer is unanimously absent, and this was checked
directly against IBD, Minervini's own site, Qullamaggie's own site, Stockbee, ChartMill, Deepvue and
TrendSpider rather than inferred from silence. What *is* published is a set of one-sided conditions:
above the 50/150/200-day (Minervini), above the 30-week (Weinstein), above the 10-week for a handle
(IBD), and "surfing" the 10- and 20-day (Qullamaggie). The only two-sided distance rules anywhere in
this file are measured from something other than an MA: Minervini's 25% (or 30%) above the 52-week low
and within 25% of the 52-week high, and IBD's 5% above the pivot.

**Irreconcilable.** Minervini's own trend template is published with two different floors for the same
criterion — "at least 25% above its 52-week low" and "at least 30% above the 52-week low" — traced to
two different books. A stock 27% off its 52-week low passes the template under one edition and fails it
under the other. Both are recorded; no midpoint is proposed. Note also that Weinstein's 30-week MA and
Minervini's 150-day MA are described as equivalents by secondaries ("30-week moving average (roughly
the 150-day MA)") but are not: a 30-week MA computed on weekly closes and a 150-day MA computed on
daily closes give different values on the same day, and an implementation that treats them as the same
line is making an undeclared substitution.

**Computability.** Fully computable from daily OHLCV. `SMA(Close, 50)`, `SMA(Close, 150)`,
`SMA(Close, 200)`; slope as `SMA200[t] > SMA200[t-30]` (InvestorStack's operationalisation of "trending
up for at least 1 month" — note that this tests only the endpoints and would pass a line that dipped
and recovered, which is a different claim from "trending up"); 52-week extremes as
`max(High[-252:])` and `min(Low[-252:])`, or on closes — **no source states whether the 52-week
high/low is taken on intraday extremes or on closes**, and the two differ by several percent on volatile
names. Distance above an MA is `(Close − SMA_n) / SMA_n`. **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone:**
the RS rating threshold of 70 — that is IBD's proprietary percentile ranking of a stock against the
whole market, which requires the full cross-section of symbols and IBD's own formula, not the
symbol's own bars. A screener can compute *a* relative-strength percentile from a universe it holds,
but it is not IBD's number and should not be labelled as such.

**Spread.** Required prior advance runs from 20% (IBD, between stages) through 30% (IBD general base;
Qullamaggie's floor) and 50% (Minervini's preference) and 85% (Deepvue's Power Play default) to
90%–120% for the high tight flag. Time windows are attached by only four sources: 1–3 months
(Qullamaggie), 4–8 weeks (IBD's flagpole), ≤2 months (Bulkowski's flagpole), and 6 months (Deepvue's
default). IBD's headline 30% and Minervini's 50% carry **no window at all**, which makes them
under-specified as screening criteria.

**Irreconcilable.** The high tight flag is the sharpest conflict in this file. Bulkowski requires
"at least 90% (shoot for a double) in 2 months or less"; IBD requires "100% to 120% in only four to
eight weeks". A stock up 95% in eight weeks is a textbook Bulkowski high tight flag and is disqualified
by IBD's floor. A stock up 140% in six weeks passes Bulkowski's floor and *exceeds* IBD's 120% ceiling —
IBD's formulation is a band, Bulkowski's is a floor, so they disagree at both ends. Neither is adjusted.
Separately, Qullamaggie's 30% floor and Minervini's 50% preference cannot both be the gate for the same
"prior advance" concept: a stock up 35% in two months is a full Qullamaggie setup and, by Minervini's
sentence, not yet where "the strongest VCPs" form.

**Computability.** Computable from daily OHLCV once a window is chosen — and for IBD's 30% and
Minervini's 50% the window must be *invented*, because neither publishes one. The primitive is
`advance = (baseHigh − priorLow) / priorLow` where `baseHigh = High` at the start of the base and
`priorLow = min(Low)` over a lookback ending at the prior swing low. Qullamaggie's version is directly
computable as `max over w in {21, 42, 63} of (Close[t] / Close[t-w] − 1) >= 0.30`, which matches his
"past 1-3 months" over three timeframes. His *ranking* formulation ("the 1 or 2% of stocks that are up
the most") is **not computable from one symbol's bars** — it needs the full universe cross-section on
the same date. Bulkowski's and IBD's flagpole rules are computable as
`Close[t] / min(Low[t-40..t]) − 1 >= 0.90` (Bulkowski, ~2 months) versus a banded version for IBD;
note that the flagpole's *start* is itself an unpublished detection problem — neither house says
whether the pole begins at a swing low, at a breakout, or at the lowest low in the window.

general market starting a new major uptrend). Handle duration runs from a 1-week minimum with **no
maximum** (Bulkowski, and IBD's own article likewise states only a 1–2 week minimum) against a
secondary-attributed one-month ceiling, with Bulkowski's measured median at 22 days. Handle position is
the one criterion with genuine cross-source agreement — the upper half of the cup — stated by IBD in two
places, Bulkowski, and the O'Neil-attributed secondary. Drift direction is required by IBD and
quantified by nobody.

**Irreconcilable.** IBD contradicts itself on handle depth in its own two publications: the Investor's
Corner article says handles "should be no more than 12% deep from top to bottom in bull markets", while
the Customer Center says the drop "should be contained within 10 to 15 percent of its peak". A 14%
handle is disqualified by the first and permitted by the second, and the two also disagree on the
*anchor* — "top to bottom" of the handle versus "of its peak". Separately, Bulkowski states "1 week
minimum with **no maximum**" while the O'Neil-attributed secondary states the handle "should complete
within a month"; a six-week handle on an otherwise perfect cup is a valid Bulkowski pattern and a void
pattern under that rule. That one is doubly interesting because Bulkowski's own measurement — median 22
days, with shorter handles performing better — points toward a ceiling that his stated rule refuses to
impose. Both stand as published.

**Computability.** Computable from daily OHLCV **only after the handle sub-window is detected**, and no
house publishes how to detect it. The chain: locate the cup's right rim as the local high following the
cup low; the handle is the drift from that rim to the next swing low and back. Then
`handleDepth = (rimHigh − handleLow) / rimHigh <= 0.12` (IBD's bull-market rule); `handleDuration =
bars from rim to breakout >= 5`; "upper half" is `handleLow > (cupHigh + cupLow) / 2` measured, per IBD,
"from the cup's peak to the bottom"; the midpoint variant is
`(handleHigh + handleLow)/2 > (cupHigh + cupLow)/2`; "above the 10-week moving average" is
`handleLow > SMA(weekly Close, 10)` — computable, but note it is the one base-structure rule in this
file that depends on an indicator rather than on the pattern's own geometry, so a handle can be
disqualified by MA drift alone without its shape changing; "within 15% of the old price high" is
`handleLow >= 0.85 * cupHigh`. **The unpublished part is the rim detection**: how many bars of pullback
constitute a handle rather than noise, and whether the rim is the highest high on the cup's right side
or the last high before the drift. Two implementations differing only on that will disagree about
whether a handle exists at all. "Drifts downward" is expressible as a negative OLS slope on the handle's
closes, or as IBD's own inverse formulation (wedging = "drift upward along their price lows or sideways
on lows", i.e. a non-negative slope on the handle's *lows*) — IBD's wording points at the lows
specifically, but publishes no slope threshold, so the boundary between "drifts downward" and "sideways
on lows" is unowned. **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone:** the bear-market 20%–30% allowance, whose
stated condition is that "the general market starts a new major uptrend" — a forward-looking judgement
about an index, not a measurement of the stock's bars.

**Spread.** Only one house publishes numbers here at all. IBD's ladder is: first base 20%–25%+, second

stage with a ≥20% separation between bases. Every other authority publishes `null`. The spread,
therefore, is not between houses but between *evidence classes* — IBD's 80% is asserted with no sample,
while Bulkowski, the one house whose entire method is sampling, has measured nothing on this axis.

**Irreconcilable.** No two houses contradict each other numerically, because only one publishes numbers.
The conflict worth recording is a naming collision rather than a numeric one: Weinstein's "Stage 2" and
IBD's "second-stage base" are unrelated quantities — Weinstein's is a state defined by price versus the
30-week MA, IBD's is an ordinal count of bases since the advance began. A stock can be in Weinstein
Stage 2 while forming an IBD fourth-stage base, and any implementation that stores one integer called
"stage" will silently merge them.

**Computability.** Computable from daily OHLCV, with one genuinely undecidable piece. The countable
part: detect bases in sequence; for each breakout at `pivot_i`, compute
`gain_i = (max(High) between breakout i and the start of base i+1 − pivot_i) / pivot_i`; increment the
stage counter when `gain_i >= 0.20`, and treat the next base as base-on-base (same stage) when
`gain_i < 0.20`. The ≥20% separation test is `baseStart_{i+1} high >= 1.20 * pivot_i`. **The
un-computable piece is the reset**, because no reset rule is published: without one the counter
increments monotonically forever and every long-lived stock eventually reads as a tenth-stage base. An
implementation must choose its own reset condition — a common choice is an undercut of the prior base's
low, or a fall below the 200-day line, or a fixed elapsed time — and must state that the choice is its
own and not IBD's. The 80% failure figure is not computable at all in the sense of being *verified*
from bars, because "fail" is undefined in the source; it can only be re-derived under a definition the
implementation invents.

**Spread.** Throwback frequency runs from 58% (Bulkowski's aggregate across 10,305 patterns) to 62%
(cup with handle), 66% (rectangle top), 67% (high tight flag and Adam & Eve double bottom), and 70%–74%
conditioned on above-average breakout volume. Timing is published once: a 6-day climb and a 10-day
round trip on average, all inside a 30-calendar-day definitional window. Only one authority publishes
anything here at all; the five houses that trade breakouts publish nothing.

**Irreconcilable.** The conflict is *within* the one house that measures. Bulkowski's throwbacks page
gives "A high volume (above the 30-day average) breakout throws back 70% of the time"; his volume page
gives "throwbacks occur 74% of the time if the breakout is on above average volume", where that page's
average is over the 5 trading days before the breakout. These are two different measurements — different
studies, different periods, different averaging windows — reported in language that reads as the same
claim. Likewise the throwback performance split is published as 40% vs 29% on one page (n=400 / n=2,767)
and 41.3% vs 26.7% on another (n=9,372 across 841 stocks). No blend is proposed; both pairs stand with
their samples attached, and any citation must carry the sample or it is misquoting.

**Computability.** Fully computable from daily OHLCV, and this is the one measurement in this file that
can be *validated* rather than merely implemented, because the definition is published. For a breakout
at bar `b` with `pivot = P`: a throwback occurred if `min(Low[b+1 .. b+30_calendar_days]) <= P`
(Bulkowski's "return to" arm) — the "or come close to" arm is **not quantified** and must be either
dropped or given an owned tolerance such as 1%. The 30 days are **calendar** days, not bars, so the
window is a date arithmetic operation, not a bar offset (roughly 21 trading days, but not exactly, and
holidays shift it). Throwback height is `(max(High[b .. throwbackStart]) − P) / P`. The
volume-conditioned rates require choosing the same averaging window Bulkowski used for the figure being
reproduced — 30 days for the 70% figure, 5 days for the 74% figure — and mixing them will reproduce
neither. Nothing here needs intraday data.

## The numbers nobody publishes

These are the measurements that every house gestures at and none quantifies. For each, an
implementation has exactly two honest options: choose its own number and say openly that the number is
its own, or refuse to ship the criterion. Silently inventing one and attributing it to a house is the
failure mode this section exists to prevent.

1. **Maximum distance above a moving average at entry.** Minervini, Weinstein and Qullamaggie all
   require price to be *above* a named MA and all describe over-extension as a reason not to buy; none
   publishes a ceiling. The only published extension limit in this entire file is IBD's 5% above the
   *pivot*, which is a different anchor. (§8)

2. **Tightness of a BASE as a magnitude.** IBD publishes 1.5% (or 1%–2%) for the standalone
   three-weeks-tight pattern and, in its dedicated article on tight trading *inside a cup base*,
   publishes no percentage at all — only "Limited weekly trading ranges make for a well-defined
   structure". Minervini's material states outright that the percentages "do not need to be perfect".
   LuxAlgo requires each contraction to be "meaningfully shallower" and does not define "meaningfully".
   The only base-level numbers found are vendor constructions — Deepvue's proprietary RMV threshold
   (published as < 15 on one page and < 10 on another) with a ±4% 5-day band, and a secondary's
   "one-third of the 50-day ATR". (§3)

3. **Volume dry-up threshold from any of the six houses.** One number exists in the whole research —
   "less than 50% of the average volume" — and it comes from the Kacher/Morales lineage via a secondary,
   not from O'Neil, Minervini, Weinstein, Qullamaggie, Darvas or Bulkowski. Also unpublished: how many
   consecutive dry-up bars are required, and whether the average is anchored at the base start or
   trailing. (§4)

4. **The minimum tightness of the final VCP contraction.** Every source supplies an example — 3–8%, 5%,
   6%, 7% — and no source supplies a threshold. This is the criterion that actually distinguishes a
   completed VCP from an incomplete one, and it is the one number the method does not publish. (§6)

5. **The number of contractions *required* (as opposed to typical).** Sources give typical ranges
   (2–3, 2–4, 2–6) and none states a minimum below which the base is rejected. (§6)

6. **The swing-detection lookback that defines a "contraction" or a "pullback" at all.** Nobody
   publishes the fractal/pivot window `k`. Change `k` and the same chart yields three contractions or
   five. Any published contraction count without a stated `k` is not reproducible. (§6)

7. **The handle-detection rule.** The cup's right rim, and therefore the handle's existence, depth and
   duration, all depend on how a rim is identified from bars. Every house states handle *rules* and none
   states how to find the handle. (§10)

8. **The failure rate of a wedging handle.** IBD names the failure mode precisely — handles that "drift
   upward along their price lows or sideways on lows" — and says only that these "have a higher failure
   rate". No rate, no comparison rate, no sample. The rule is stated crisply enough to implement and
   the evidence for it is stated too vaguely to check. (§10)

9. **The bear-market adjustment for the BASE, as an operational rule.** IBD does quantify the bear-market
   allowance for the *handle* — "as deep as 20%-30% in bear markets" — but attaches a forward-looking
   condition ("if the general market starts a new major uptrend") that no bar computation can evaluate
   at signal time. For the base itself, the only quantification found is O'Neil's "1-1/2 to two times
   the market average", which needs a *second* series and a definition of the concurrent
   market-drawdown window — and that window is published by no one. The 40–50% and 75% bear-market base
   depth figures could not be verified against a fetchable primary. (§1, §10)

10. **The base-count reset condition.** IBD publishes precisely how a stage is *counted up* (a ≥20%
    gain, a ≥20% separation) and never publishes what returns a stock to base one. Without it, the
    counter never resets. (§11)

11. **Weinstein's numbers, essentially in full.** Base depth, minimum base duration, buy-stop offset,
    maximum buyable extension above the breakout — all absent. EdgeStacker, having built a runnable
    Weinstein scan, states the position outright: "Stan Weinstein describes this setup without exact
    numbers; this is our stated formalization of it." Every Weinstein number in circulation (2×, 3×,
    150-day, 65-day) is somebody's formalization. (§1, §2, §5, §7)

12. **Whether the breakout must close above the pivot or merely trade through it.** Bulkowski requires
    a close; IBD's "+10 cents" is silent on the bar field. This single unstated choice changes the
    signal set materially and is not settled by any source. (§7)

13. **Whether 52-week highs and lows are taken on intraday extremes or on closes.** Minervini's
    template hangs two of its criteria on these levels and never says which field. (§8)

14. **Any percentage translation of IBD's "+10 cents".** It is a nominal cent amount, worth 0.83% on a
    $12 stock and 0.011% on a $900 one, undefined outside USD. No percentage equivalent is published. (§7)

15. **The slope threshold for a handle that "drifts downward".** IBD requires the drift and names its
    opposite ("sideways on lows" counts as wedging and fails), so the boundary between the two is
    load-bearing — and no source publishes it. The direction is stated; the magnitude by nobody. (§10)

16. **Throwback frequency from any breakout house.** O'Neil/IBD, Minervini, Weinstein, Qullamaggie and
    Darvas all trade breakouts and all build stop rules that presuppose retests; none publishes how
    often a retest occurs or within how long. Bulkowski is the sole source, and he is not one of the
    base-and-breakout houses. (§12)

17. **A measured failure rate by ordinal base number, with a sample.** IBD asserts "as much as 80% of
    fourth-stage bases fail" with no n, no period, and no definition of failure. Bulkowski, whose entire
    method is measurement, has measured nothing on this axis. (§11)

18. **Qullamaggie's consolidation depth and any volume threshold.** He publishes duration
    ("2 weeks to 2 months"), the MAs the consolidation rides, the prior-advance band, position sizing and
    stop rules — and no depth limit and no volume multiple. His stop rule references ATR/ADR without a
    multiplier: "stop should not be wider than the ATR or ADR of the stock". (§1, §4, §5)

19. **Darvas's box dimensions.** The 3-consecutive-day rule defines the box boundaries; no maximum box
    height, no minimum box duration, no breakout offset ("above the high by some points"), and no volume
    multiple are published. (§1, §2, §5, §7)

20. **The vendor defaults that vendors do not disclose.** The Amphibiantrading VCP indicator "Tracks up
    to five contractions (C1 → C5)" and exposes "Maximum Depth" and "Final Contraction Depth" inputs, but
    the script is closed-source and its shipped default values are not published — a screener can be
    reproducing someone's specific thresholds without anyone being able to say what they are. (§6)
