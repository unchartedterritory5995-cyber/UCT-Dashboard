# Vendor Detection Implementations — How Commercial Products Actually Detect Chart Patterns

Scope: **multi-bar chart patterns and base structures only.** Single-candlestick patterns are covered elsewhere and appear here only where a vendor's *entire* pattern offering turns out to be candlesticks (which is itself a finding — see StockCharts).

Purpose: capture what vendors **publish** about detection — parameters, tolerances, confirmation rules, repainting, scoring, and self-reported accuracy — so a competing detector can be measured against shipped behaviour rather than folklore.

## Evidence discipline used throughout

Three labels are applied to every claim:

- **DOCUMENTED** — the vendor's own help/manual describes the behaviour of the product.
- **ASSERTED, NOT MEASURED** — a marketing claim with no stated sample or period.
- **MEASURED** — a number the vendor states it derived over a stated sample and period.

A fourth, critical distinction appears repeatedly: several vendors publish **third-party literature statistics** (Bulkowski, Edwards & Magee, Schabacker) in their own documentation. Those are **not** the vendor's measurements of its own detector and are labelled **CITED THIRD-PARTY LITERATURE**.

## TradingView

TradingView ships **first-party** "Auto Chart Patterns" indicators, each pattern as its own indicator, plus an aggregate. This is the most granular per-pattern parameter documentation of any vendor surveyed — but it publishes almost **no default values**.

- **patterns_detected**: From the *All Chart Patterns* article, 16 patterns, named exactly: "Bullish Flag, Bearish Flag, Bullish Pennant, Bearish Pennant, Double Top, Double Bottom, Triple Top, Triple Bottom, Head And Shoulders, Inverse Head And Shoulders, Rising Wedge, Falling Wedge, Triangle, Rectangle, Cup and Handle, Inverted Cup and Handle". The blog states "There are currently 15 indicators available that define different patterns." (The 16-vs-15 discrepancy is TradingView's own; the KB folder lists 16 individual pattern articles plus the aggregate.) Elliott Wave and Gaps are separate KB articles and separate tools.

- **parameters**:
  - `pivot definition (fixed, not user-exposed)` — 5 bars left / 5 bars right — "Thus, a point will be a 5/5 pivot high if there are no high values 5 bars to the left and 5 bars to the right of it that are higher than this value at this point." — confidence(high)
  - `scan range (fixed, not user-exposed)` — 600 bars — "The indicator searches for patterns on the last 600 bars." — confidence(high)
  - `Patterns` — value: null — "Defines which pattern will be drawn depending on its status. Possible values: All, Awaiting and Reached, Only Awaiting, Last Awaiting." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published. The Triple Top article notes "There are 4 statuses in total."
  - `Price Targets` — value: null — "Defines which price targets will be drawn depending status of the pattern. Possible values: All, Only Awaiting, Awaiting and Reached, None." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `In Progress` — value: null — "Search for emerging patterns." (variant wording on reversal patterns: "Search for patterns that have not been fully formed yet."; on Cup and Handle: "searching for the patterns which are being formed.") — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published, though the blog implies it is a mode you switch on.
  - `Permissible Deviation` (Triangle) — value: null — "The maximum allowable deviation of point 3 from the corresponding line. It is calculated as a percentage of the distance between the triangle lines." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Permissible Deviation` (Rectangle) — value: null — "The maximum allowable deviation of a point from its corresponding line. It is calculated as a percentage of the distance between the lines of the rectangle on the same bar where this point is located." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Permissible Deviation` (Bullish Flag) — value: null — "The maximum allowable deviation of points in the flag from the lines of the parallel channel. Calculated as a percentage of the channel height." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Permissible Deviation` (Double Top) — value: null — "The maximum allowable difference in tops heights…Percent value." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Permissible Deviation` (Double Bottom) — value: null — "The maximum allowable difference in vertex depth…Percent value." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Permissible Deviation` (Cup and Handle) — value: null — "determines the maximum allowable price difference along the edges of the cup." It is "calculated as a percentage of the height of the cup". — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Handle Max Rollback` (Cup and Handle) — value: null — "determines the maximum allowable price reduction in the handle, calculated as a percentage of the height of the cup." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Cup Max Rollback` (Cup and Handle) — **disabled by default** — "determines the maximum allowable decrease in the price in the cup relative to the previous trend."; the checkbox "enables/disables parameter check and trend search before the pattern. The option is disabled by default." — confidence(high). *This is the only explicitly published default value found anywhere in TradingView's chart-pattern KB.*
  - `Max Rollback` (Bullish Flag) — value: null — "Determines the maximum price rollback in the flag channel as a percentage of the height of the flagpole." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Max Rollback` (Bullish Pennant) — value: null — "Determines the maximum rollback of the price of point 3 in the pennant triangle as a percentage of the height of the flagpole." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Trend Height` (Head and Shoulders) — value: null — "Sets the required height of the trend preceding the pattern relative to the height of the head" (percent value) — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Trend Height` (Double Top / Double Bottom / Triple Top / Triple Bottom) — value: null — "Sets the required height of the trend preceding the pattern relative to the height of the pattern itself…Percent value." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Second Top` / `Second Bottom` — value: null — "Determines which patterns to display with respect to whether the second top of the first top is above Higher or below Lower…If Both is selected, all patterns will be displayed." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Tops` (Triple Top) — value: null — "The maximum allowable difference in tops heights." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Intermediate Minima` (Triple Top) — value: null — "The maximum allowable difference in the depth of intermediate minima." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Bottoms` (Triple Bottom) — value: null — "The maximum allowable difference in the depth of bottoms." (Percent value) — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Intermediate Maxima` (Triple Bottom) — value: null — "The maximum allowable height difference of intermediate maxima." (Percentage value) — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Channel Width Ratio Start/End` (Rising Wedge, Falling Wedge) — value: null — "The minimum required channel height difference at the beginning and end of the pattern. Percent value." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `minimum cup width` (Cup and Handle, fixed) — 20 bars — "The minimum width of the cup is 20 bars, and the handle cannot be longer than the cup." — confidence(high)

- **confirmation_rule**: **On close, one bar.** Stated flatly for continuation patterns: "The breakout of the pattern is fixed by the close value." (Rectangle, Rising Wedge, Falling Wedge, Bullish Pennant). For reversal patterns the neckline test is explicit and close-based: Double Top — "The pattern is considered formed when, after the appearance of the second peak, the price (close) falls below the neckline."; Double Bottom — "…the price (close) rises above the neck line."; Triple Top — "…after the appearance of the third peak, the price (close) falls below the neckline."; Triple Bottom — "…after the appearance of the third bottom, the price (close) rises above the neck line."; Head and Shoulders — "The pattern is considered completed only when the price, having formed the right shoulder, falls below the neckline." A **containment constraint** also gates formation: "The intersection of the lines of the triangle with the close value in the interval between points 1 and 5 is not allowed." (analogous wording for rectangle points 1–4, wedge points 1–4, pennant points 2–5) — i.e. no intra-pattern close may pierce the boundary lines. **No multi-bar or percentage penetration filter is published** — one close beyond the line is the whole rule.

- **repainting**: TradingView never uses the word "repaint" in this KB. What it publishes instead is a mechanism description: in In Progress mode "patterns are searched for that have not yet been completed, i.e., their last two points have not yet been found and may not be reversal points" and "The last two points of such a pattern may not be in pivots, and the last price line will be dotted." That is a repainting disclosure by another name — the two right-most anchors are provisional and are drawn dotted. Because pivots are 5/5, a *completed* pattern's final pivot cannot be confirmed until **5 bars after** it prints; that lag is implied by the pivot definition, not stated. TradingView also warns the pattern set itself is not stable: "We may change the patterns these indicator identifies, make sure you consult the corresponding Knowledge base article."

- **published_statistics**: **none published.** No accuracy, hit-rate, or backtest number appears anywhere in the chart-pattern KB or the end-of-beta blog post. TradingView points users at Bar Replay to test manually — "Bar Replay — how and why to test a strategy in the past" — rather than publishing its own numbers.

- **scoring**: **No quality score.** TradingView has no analogue of Autochartist's quality vector or Recognia's 0–5 score. The only ranking dimension is pattern *status* (All / Awaiting and Reached / Only Awaiting / Last Awaiting — "There are 4 statuses in total"), which is a lifecycle state, not a quality measure. Detection is binary: geometry either satisfies the deviation constraints or it does not.

- **notable_gaps**: No Ascending / Descending / Symmetrical triangle **subtypes** — "Triangle" is one undifferentiated indicator, unlike TrendSpider, Autochartist, Finviz and Recognia which all split it. No broadening / megaphone family (TrendSpider has six). No channels as a pattern class (TrendSpider and Autochartist both have three/two). No harmonic-Fibonacci family (Autochartist has six). No flat base, VCP, or ascending base. No volume term anywhere in the detection rules — TradingView's patterns are **pure price geometry**, where Autochartist and Recognia both make volume a scored or advisory component.

**Third-party contrast worth recording.** The most-installed community alternative, *Auto Chart Patterns [Trendoscope®]*, exposes what TradingView hides: a configurable pivot count — "either 5 or 6" — a "Last Pivot Direction" of "up, down, both, or custom", and an explicit repaint policy that TradingView's own tool lacks: "When permitted, repainting only updates a pattern if the new coordinates yield a more geometrically accurate representation," with the feature "off by default." Its taxonomy is also finer — it splits every wedge and triangle into *Expanding* and *Contracting* variants and adds Converging/Diverging Triangle and Ranging Channel. That a community script publishes a stricter repaint contract than the platform vendor is the single sharpest gap in TradingView's disclosure.

## TrendSpider

TrendSpider is the most **parameter-transparent** vendor for structural patterns, and the only one that ships an explicit anti-hindsight mechanism.

- **patterns_detected**: The chart-pattern KB names 13: "Triangle, Ascending", "Triangle, Descending", "Triangle, Symmetrical", "Double Top", "Double Bottom", "Channel, Horizontal", "Channel, Ascending", "Channel, Descending", "Head and Shoulders", "Inverse Head and Shoulders", "Wedge, Rising", "Wedge, Falling", "Cup and Handle". The section index reveals a further **six broadening patterns** with their own articles: "Ascending Broadening", "Asymmetrical Broadening", "Descending Broadening", "Right-Angled and Ascending Broadening", "Right-Angled and Descending Broadening", "Symmetrical Broadening" — **19 structural patterns total**, the largest first-party structural taxonomy surveyed. Separately, and out of scope here: "TrendSpider supports more than 100 different auto-recognized candlestick patterns, including core patterns (described below) and candlestick patterns developed by Rob Smith's The Strat, Tom Bulkowski's The Pattern Site and Newsome Candles."

- **parameters**:
  - `Time Span` — value: null (options "Short Term" or "Long Term") — the docs say it "regulates what kind of patterns should be preferred by adjusting low-level technical inputs for the Recognition logic"; on Cup and Handle, "Short Term to prioritize smaller, faster-forming patterns, or Long Term for larger, more developed formations" — confidence(high). `missing:` the "low-level technical inputs" it adjusts are never enumerated, so the actual tolerances are opaque. Applies to triangles, channels, wedges, cup and handle, double top/bottom, broadening.
  - `Max peak distance, ATR` (Double Top / Double Bottom) — value: null — described as defining "how far your Tops can be from each other in order for the formation to still be valid" — confidence(med). `missing:` no default published. *This is the clearest published example anywhere of an **ATR-normalised** tolerance rather than a percentage one.*
  - `Max peak distance, %` (Double Top / Double Bottom) — value: null — same purpose, percentage-denominated — confidence(med). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Type` (Double Top / Double Bottom) — value: null (options "All" or "Confirmed") — "If set to `Confirmed` then all the patterns which have not been confirmed (see above) will be discarded." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Depth` (Head and Shoulders) — value: null — "Depth represents the number of candles without a second maximum or minimum deviation of the previous pivot." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published. This is TrendSpider's pivot-width equivalent of TradingView's fixed 5/5.
  - `Deviation` (Head and Shoulders) — value: null — "Deviation defines the vertical distance from the previous pivot point." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Back Step` (Head and Shoulders) — value: null — "The back step parameter refers to the number of bars or candles that must pass before the pattern can change direction and draw a new line or swing." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published. Depth + Deviation + Back Step is a **ZigZag swing-detector parameterisation**, which tells you TrendSpider's structural engine is ZigZag-based rather than fractal/pivot-based.
  - `Head Height` (Head and Shoulders) — value: null — "The head height allows users to define the percentage distance between the head and left shoulder." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Bands` (Head and Shoulders, Cup and Handle, Broadening) — value: null (options ATR, Standard Deviation, Constant, Percentage) — "The 'Bands' option allows traders to add various styles of bands around the horizontal levels that coincide with the shoulders and head, as well as the neckline." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published. Bands are a *tolerance envelope made visible*, and the four band types are the four tolerance metrics worth implementing.
  - `Retracement` (Double Top/Bottom, Head and Shoulders, Cup and Handle) — value: null — enables Fibonacci retracement lines on the detected pattern — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Inverted` (Cup and Handle) — value: null — "enables inverse pattern detection" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Analysis Type` (trendline engine, feeds pattern detection) — value: null (options "Original", "Enhanced", "Standard") — "Original" is the "most basic presentation and will show the least number of trend lines"; "Enhanced" "shows more trend lines than the Original setting" with "most trend sensitivity"; "Standard" is a "mix between Original and Enhanced" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Drawing Input` — value: null (options "Wick (H/L)", "Body (O/C)") — "Wick (H/L)" will "draw trend lines based on the absolute highs and lows for each period"; "Body (O/C)" will "draw trend lines based on the opening and closing prices for each period" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published. **A first-class design decision most competitors leave implicit.**
  - `Island (Gaps)` — value: null (options "Respect", "Ignore") — "Respect" "will not draw trend lines across gaps"; "Ignore" "will ignore gaps in price and draw trend lines across them" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published for the mode itself.
  - `ATR for Islands` (gap-size threshold) — **three times ATR(14)** — "default setting is three times the Average True Range (ATR) of a 14-day period" — confidence(high). **This is a genuine published numeric default and the clearest gap-definition threshold in the survey.**
  - `HH/LL Numbers` — value: null — "determine the higher highs and lower lows for each timeframe"; "Higher numbers will yield fewer trend lines and smaller numbers will yield more trendlines" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published. This is TrendSpider's swing-window control, the analogue of TradingView's fixed 5/5.
  - `Lines` — value: null — filters lines on an "ATR factor of themes recent price action" — confidence(med). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Quality` — value: null (options "Most Relevant", "More Lines", "All (Unfiltered)") — "Most Relevant" will "filter out the irrelevant trend lines and display only the top one per cent"; "More Lines" is a "filtered but loose view where the maximum lines that might be relevant shall appear"; "All (Unfiltered)" shows "all the lines generated on the chart as analyzed by the system" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published. **"the top one per cent" is a hard published selectivity number.**
  - `Raindrop / Dynamic Price Range` — Raindrop is a *chart type*, not a pattern detector. "Left Mean" — "It represents the Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) for the first half (left side) of the period."; "Right Mean" — "It represents the Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) for the second half (Right side) of the period."; the body "represents the Volume by Price for each half of the time period (shown as the left and right sides of the period)." The term "Dynamic Price Range" does not appear in TrendSpider's Raindrop documentation — confidence(high). No parameters or defaults are published for it.

- **confirmation_rule**: **On close, exactly one bar, on the pattern's own timeframe.** "Each breakout requires one full candlestick close through a trend line on a given timeframe." Worked example: "a five-minute candlestick must close on the opposite side of a trend line in order for a breakout to be valid." Breakouts are marked "Green Circle: Represents the trendline breaking up" / "Red Circle: Represents the trendline breaking down". For Double Top specifically: "In case if after that the price action goes below the trough, this Double Top is considered to be confirmed." Broadening patterns confirm on "a decisive breakout" — "A breakout above suggests bullish momentum, while a breakout below indicates bearish momentum" — with no numeric threshold published. Detection is also **recency-gated**: "We'll only detect Ascending Triangle which is either still forming or had been just 'played out' recently."

- **repainting**: TrendSpider is the only vendor with a **named anti-hindsight feature**, the *Truth in Analysis Timestamp*, which applies to Trendlines, Auto Fibonacci and Heatmaps. Locked analysis is drawn yellow/blue and is "currently locked at the point in time"; unlocked analysis is drawn red and updates when the user changes their analysis. Users "force-refresh any analysis by moving the cursor over the bottom of the line and clicking the button— Refresh & Lock in the expanded timestamp," and "When you refresh an analysis, it locks the trends at the current point in time until you refresh it again or the analysis becomes stale by moving out of range." For chart patterns the vendor is blunter and states a *limitation* rather than a guarantee: "Chart Patterns painted on charts do not respond to real time updates (i.e., their lines do not extend)". Read together: patterns are computed at analysis time and frozen, and the timestamp is the audit trail proving when. **This is the disclosure model a competing detector should copy** — a per-detection "as of" stamp beats any prose claim of "we don't repaint".

- **published_statistics**: **none published.** No hit rate, no backtest, no sample. The vendor instead publishes an explicit accuracy *disclaimer*: "Think of our Chart Pattern detection as of your fellow advisor. It most cases it will be perfectly right. Sometimes it won't." That is an ASSERTED, NOT MEASURED statement of reliability — and notably an unusually candid one. Marketing claims about pattern *counts* ("more than 100 different auto-recognized candlestick patterns") are counts, not accuracy.

- **scoring**: No per-pattern quality score is published for chart patterns. But TrendSpider **does** publish a user-writable scoring formula language for the **trendline** engine that feeds pattern detection, and its variable set is the most directly implementable scoring vocabulary found anywhere in this survey. Component names verbatim:
  - `length` — "The number of bars covered by the line"
  - `seriesLength` — "The total number of bars in the series"
  - `priceDev25`, `priceDev50`, `priceDev75` — quantile deviations between the trend line and the price midpoint, on a 0-to-1 scale
  - `hits` — "The number of highs or lows touching the trend line"
  - `violations` — "The number of bars that actually cross a trend line"
  - `points` — "The highest highs or lowest lows calculated at the given window size"; `points2x` is the same at double the window size
  - `bounceUp`, `bounceDown` — single-bar touches without adjacent bar contact
  - `peaksUp`, `peaksDown` — touches where price bars exceed **1.5 × ATR(14)** relative to adjacent bars
  
  `hits` versus `violations` is the key pairing: a line's quality is *touches minus crossings*, normalised by length. And `peaksUp`/`peaksDown` gives a published, concrete pivot-significance test — **1.5 × ATR(14) above the adjacent bars** — which is the only hard numeric pivot-prominence threshold any vendor publishes. **Limitation:** "The documentation does not specify default weights, numeric coefficients, or the exact computation method for overall trendline scores." The variables are exposed; the vendor's own weighting is not. So: **ranking machinery exists for trendlines, not for chart patterns**, and the `Quality` filter's only published selectivity figure is "display only the top one per cent".

- **notable_gaps**: No flags, no pennants, no rectangles (it has "Channel, Horizontal" which is geometrically similar but named and treated differently), no triple tops/bottoms. TradingView, Autochartist and Recognia all cover flags/pennants; TrendSpider does not. No harmonic/Fibonacci pattern family as detected patterns (it has automated Fibonacci *retracements*, which is a different thing). No flat base, VCP, or ascending base. No published quality score, so no way to rank a day's detections other than by filtering.

## Autochartist

Autochartist is by a wide margin the **most methodologically explicit** vendor: it is the only one that publishes a decomposed quality vector, a stated forecast construction, and a self-measured performance tool over a named window.

- **patterns_detected**: The user manual states a count without printing the roster in text — "Autochartist identifies 16 different Chart Patterns." and "Autochartist identifies six different Fibonacci patterns." The names are rendered as images in the manual, so the roster below is assembled from the vendor's own prose across pages, **not** from a single enumerated vendor list:
  - Manual intro (vendor, verbatim): "Chart pattern formations such as Triangles, Wedges, Head-and-Shoulders and Channels"; "Fibonacci Retracements, Extensions, ABCD, Gartley and Butterfly patterns"; "Key Levels - the technical analysis of horizontal support and resistance levels".
  - Trending-pattern article (vendor, verbatim): "rising and falling wedges", "channel up and down patterns", "head-and-shoulder patterns with more angle necklines", "some flag formations", and "triangle or rectangle".
  - Key Levels (vendor, verbatim): "There are only two types of Key Levels – Support and Resistance."
  - Market-stages article (vendor, verbatim): "Understanding which stage is most suited for Wedges, Triangles, Channels, and Rectangles, as well as understanding all the patterns Autochartist identifies is key to effective chart pattern analysis and trading" and, on stage 3, distribution "can develop Triangle, Pennant, Double Tops/Bottoms, Triple Tops/Bottoms chart patterns."
  - A review (UNOFFICIAL — EarnForex) enumerates: "Triangles (ascending, descending, and symmetrical)", "Channels (up and down)", "Rectangles", "Wedges (rising and falling – continuation and reversal)", "Head and Shoulders (plus inverse Head and Shoulders)", "Double top and Double bottom", "Triple top and Triple bottom". That sums to exactly 16 (3 triangles + 2 channels + 1 rectangle + 4 wedges + 2 H&S + 2 double + 2 triple), which matches the vendor's stated count.
  - **But the arithmetic match is a coincidence, not a confirmation, and should not be trusted.** The vendor's own prose names **Pennant** and "some flag formations" as patterns it identifies; neither appears in the review's 16. So either the review's roster is wrong, or flags and pennants displace something in it. **The only safe conclusions are: the count 16 is vendor-stated; Triangles, Wedges, Channels, Rectangles, Head-and-Shoulders, Double Tops/Bottoms, Triple Tops/Bottoms, Pennant and flag formations are all vendor-named; and the exact 16-name roster is not publicly established.** Autochartist renders its pattern roster only as images, which is why no vendor-hosted text list exists.
  - Fibonacci family (vendor prose + count of six): ABCD, Gartley, Butterfly, Fibonacci Retracement, Fibonacci Extension, and per a support article "3 Drive, 3 Point Retracement and the 3 Point Extension" — the article-level naming and the manual-level naming do not reconcile cleanly into one list of six, so **the six Fibonacci names are not reliably established.**

- **parameters**: Autochartist exposes **filter** parameters, not detector-tuning parameters. The detector's internal tolerances are never published.
  - `Price Range` — value: null — "One can define a minimum to maximum price range in the text boxes provided, for example to limit penny stocks or very expensive equities." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Direction` — value: null — "Search results can be filtered according to the direction of the possible outcome of price movement after the pattern is identified." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Trend Change` — value: null — "If the forecast price movement after the pattern follows the same direction as the Initial Trend it is known as a continuation pattern. If it goes in the opposite direction to the Initial Trend it is called a reversal pattern." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Minimum Pattern Length` — value: null, denominated in bars — "Pattern length is determined by the number of candles (bars) of the price chart that are contained in the pattern. For example, a pattern length of 30 on a graph with 60 minute bars would mean that the pattern was formed over 30 hours." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Quality sliders` (five, one per indicator) — range 0 to 10 — "Quality is expressed as a number ranging from 0 to 10, where 10 is the best quality and 0 is the poorest. Use the sliders to set the required levels of pattern quality for a search"; "If all the quality indicator filters are set to zero, no results will be filtered out on quality and all results will be returned." — confidence(high). Effective default is therefore 0 (unfiltered).
  - `Key Level minimum touching points` — **3** — "By default, any Key Level will have at least 3 touching points, but this set of radio buttons allows traders to select a higher minimum number of touching points to filter on." — confidence(high). *This is one of the few explicitly published numeric defaults in the entire survey.*
  - `Price Range Forecast width` — one standard deviation — "For advanced users, these ranges are calculated as one standard deviation around the mean of the price movement." over "the past six months of price movement data for a particular instrument for the current day of the week at the current time of day" — confidence(high).
  - `Forecast horizon` — one pattern length — "Let predictionTime be pattern_end_time + pattern_length." — confidence(high).

- **confirmation_rule**: **Breakout of support/resistance, which promotes an emerging pattern to a completed one.** "Emerging chart patterns… represent fully formed patterns that only need price to break through support or resistance in order to complete." and "As soon as price breaks through the anticipated level of support or resistance, the emerging pattern is expired and a completed pattern presented as a trading opportunity." Failure condition is also published: "If a new turning point forms between the support and resistance levels before breaking out, the emerging pattern will expire." Autochartist **does not publish** whether the break is measured on close or intraday, nor a bar count, nor a penetration percentage — a real documentation gap given how specific it is elsewhere. Breakout Strength being "The size of the candle with which the price breaks through a support or resistance level" implies a **single breakout candle**, which weakly suggests bar-level (not multi-bar) confirmation. Lifecycle: a result "expires as soon as an event occurs that makes the expected outcome either unlikely or impossible, or when the expectation was fulfilled and the target reached."

- **repainting**: No use of the term. The lifecycle model substitutes for one: emerging patterns are provisional by construction and *expire* rather than being silently redrawn — "Many emerging patterns do not complete however, and this usually means that the price has not continued moving in the direction of support or resistance where the breakout for completion was expected." Expired opportunities are retained in a separate "Expired Trading Opportunities" pane rather than deleted, and every result carries "The time when the pattern was identified" plus "Age in candles since the pattern was identified." **Retaining expired detections with an identification timestamp is functionally an anti-repaint audit trail** and is the second-best disclosure model after TrendSpider's timestamp.

- **published_statistics**: This is the only vendor in the survey with a **self-measured** statistic, and it is scrupulously scoped.
  - Sample/period: "This tab provides detailed statistics on the overall accuracy of Autochartist for the preceding 6-month period, as well as specific statistics on how Autochartist performs on particular symbols, time granularities, patterns and directions."
  - The metric's definition, which is the important part: "Disclaimer: These statistics are not a measure of profitability, but simply count the quantity of identified opportunities that reach the forecast level within a predefined time." — i.e. **hit-rate-to-forecast-level within one pattern length**, not P&L, not risk-adjusted.
  - Also self-measured: "Our Favorites - Autochartist provides a list of currently active trading opportunities that have had a high probability of success over the past 6 months." and "All Autochartist opportunities are evaluated against past performance with respect to the pattern type, the instrument and the time of day it was identified." Note the conditioning set: **pattern type × instrument × hour of day**.
  - Support portal confirms breakdowns: "factual information on the success rate of Autochartist forecasts over the last 12 months" with breakdowns by asset class, breakout vs emerging, pattern type, symbol, direction and hour of day. **The vendor states two different windows in two different documents — "the preceding 6-month period" (manual) and "over the last 12 months" (support portal). Both are quoted above; do not average or pick one.**
  - **No headline percentage is published in any vendor-hosted page I could fetch.** The numbers live behind the live Performance Statistics tab. Broker marketing pages assert a ~70% figure; I could not fetch one to quote it verbatim, so **no such number is recorded here.** A review (UNOFFICIAL — EarnForex) describes the tool's colour-coding rather than a claim: "A success rate of 70% or higher is highlighted in light green color, while a success rate below 60% is highlighted in light pink color" — that is a UI threshold, not a measured accuracy.
  - Explicit scope limit on the quality score, which must not be mistaken for accuracy: "Disclaimer: Visual Quality is not an assessment of the efficacy of the Chart Pattern, nor the possible profit or loss one can realize from specific trading opportunities."

- **scoring**: Five components, each 1–10, arithmetically averaged. This is the most directly copyable scoring design in the survey. Component names and definitions, verbatim:
  - **Clarity** — "Pattern clarity is calculated as a value between 1 and 10 of the average direction and distance a high or a low of a candle is away from virtual pattern-point lines"; user-facing gloss: "Indicates the level of 'noise' in the price graph. If there are many spikes in the price movements the clarity will be low."
  - **Initial Trend** — "Initial Trend is calculated as a value between 1 and 10 of the length of the initial trend as a proportion of the length of the first leg of the pattern"; gloss: "The strength of the trend that precedes a pattern. A long, consistent Initial Trend will have a higher rating for this indicator."
  - **Uniformity** — "Pattern Uniformity is calculated as a value between 1 and 10 of the average time-distance between identified touching points of the pattern support and resistance lines"; gloss: "If the points touching the support and resistance lines are more evenly spaced over time, the pattern will have a better Uniformity rating."
  - **Breakout Strength** — "Breakout Strength is only applicable to completed patterns and is a value between 1 and 10 of the size of the breakout bar in relation to the average bar sizes"; gloss: "The size of the candle with which the price breaks through a support or resistance level".
  - **Volume (Volume Increase)** — "It is a value between 1 and 10 representing the average volume increase of the volume in the last leg of the pattern, when compared to the volume of the rest of the pattern"; "Volume is only applicable to exchange-traded instruments (equities, ETFs)."
  - **Overall Quality** — "Pattern Overall Quality is calculated as a value between 1 and 10 and is the arithmetic average of all the other quality indicators", excluding volume for OTC instruments and breakout for emerging patterns. The manual restates the range as "a number ranging from 0 to 10" — **the vendor states 1–10 in the knowledgebase and 0–10 in the manual; both are quoted, unreconciled.**
  - Fibonacci patterns use a **different three-component vector**: "Clarity", "Time Symmetry" ("The degree to which time differences between turning points follow the same Fibonacci ratios found in price levels") and "Price Symmetry" ("Price Symmetry is the degree to which the identified ratios match the theoretical Fibonacci ratios, within some margin of error. Therefore, the smaller the margin of error, the higher the Price Symmetry.").
  - Key Levels use a **single** component: "a measure of the number of points where the price graph touches the support or resistance level and thus confirms it."

- **notable_gaps**: No cup and handle (TradingView, TrendSpider and ChartMill all have it). No broadening family. No flat base / VCP / ascending base. Detector-side tolerances are entirely unpublished — you can filter on quality but you cannot tune what counts as a pattern, unlike TrendSpider and TradingView. Close-vs-intraday breakout semantics are undocumented, which is a surprising hole given the rest of the disclosure.

## Recognia / Trading Central

Recognia (now part of Trading Central) is the pattern engine white-labelled into Fidelity, Schwab, IBKR and many brokers. Its public documentation is **educational rather than algorithmic**: it publishes a great deal of pattern *literature* and almost nothing about its own detector.

- **patterns_detected**: Vendor-stated count, verbatim: "over 60 technical events such as Head and Shoulders, Breakouts, Shooting Stars, Hammers, Gravestones, Triangles, Flags, Pennants and more"; Fidelity's description says it uses "over fifty analysis techniques". Events are grouped into four classes: "Classic Patterns", "Short-term Patterns", "Indicator events", "Oscillator events". Named in the IBKR guide: Classic Patterns — Double Top, Flag, Head and Shoulders Top; Short-term Patterns — Shooting Star, Engulfing Line, Hammer, Gravestone; Indicator events — "Double moving average crossovers", "Triple moving average crossovers"; Oscillator events — MACD, RSI, Fast Stochastic. The 2009 *Understanding Classic Chart Patterns* monograph documents nine classic patterns in depth, which is the closest thing to a structural roster: "Head and Shoulders Top", "Head and Shoulders Bottom", "Symmetrical Triangle", "Ascending Triangle", "Descending Triangle", "Double Tops", "Double Bottoms", "Triple Top", "Triple Bottom". **No single vendor page enumerates all 60+ events**, so the full roster is not publicly established.

- **parameters**: **No user-exposed detection parameters are published.** Recognia's product is a notification feed, not a tunable detector; brokers surface filters (event type, horizon, instrument), not tolerances. What the monograph publishes are *advisory* criteria from the literature and from Recognia's advisory board — these describe what the firm believes a valid pattern looks like, and are the best available proxy for its internal thresholds:
  - `minimum reversal points, symmetrical triangle` — 4 — Elaine Yager, "a member of Recognia's Board of Advisors, notes that the pattern should display two highs and two lows, all touching the trendline as - a minimum of four reversal points is necessary to draw the two converging trendlines." — confidence(med — advisory-board opinion, not stated as the algorithm)
  - `triangle duration` — under three months — "It may take up to one month to form and it usually forms in less than three months." — confidence(med — literature restatement)
  - `neckline penetration` — 1% to 3% — Murphy "applies the 1 to 3% penetration criterion (see Edwards and Magee above)"; Edwards and Magee "call a close below the neckline break of approximately 3% of the price of the stock the 'breakout' or 'confirmation' of the head and shoulders top." — confidence(med — CITED THIRD-PARTY LITERATURE, not stated as Recognia's rule)
  - `two-day rule` — 2 successive closes — Murphy "confirms the neckline break with the 'two-day rule,' the requirement of the two successive closes below the neckline." — confidence(med — CITED THIRD-PARTY LITERATURE)
  - `double top peak tolerance` — 3% — "The second rally back to the previous high (plus or minus 3%) should be on lower volume than the first."; and "Analysts advise that if the second peak exceeds the first by more than 3%, the pattern may not be a double top." For double bottoms: "if [the second bottom differs by] more than 3% or 4%, the pattern may not be a double bottom." — confidence(med — CITED THIRD-PARTY LITERATURE)
  - `double top intervening trough depth` — at least 10%, 15% or 20% depending on authority — "a reaction of at least 10% should follow"; "there should be at least a 15% decline between the two tops, on diminishing activity"; "Other analysts maintain that the decline registered between the two tops should be at least 20% and the peaks should be spaced at least a month apart." — confidence(med — CITED THIRD-PARTY LITERATURE, three conflicting values published side by side)
  - `volume` — required for a valid breakout — Yager "strongly advises that volume is a prerequisite for a valid breakout." — confidence(med — advisory-board opinion)

- **confirmation_rule**: The product-level rule published by IBKR is that events are scored "upon their confirmation", but the *definition* of confirmation is never given in product docs. The monograph's position, from cited literature, is a close-based neckline violation with a 1–3% penetration filter and optionally Murphy's two-successive-closes rule, plus volume expansion. Recognia's own confirmation threshold is **not published**. Target price uses the classic measure rule, worked verbatim for a symmetrical triangle: "assume the highest high of the pattern occurs at 100 and the lowest low at 80. The height of the pattern is 20 (100 - 80 = 20). The apex of the triangle occurs at 90. The pattern has an upside breakout. Using the measuring rule, the target price is 110 (90 + 20 = 110)." Two target refinements are worth copying. First, the target is always a floor, not a point estimate — "This target price of 50 is only a guide… Experts remind us that this target is a minimum target. Prices will often move beyond that". Second, the vendor publishes a **damped variant for weak patterns**: "Given the sometimes weak performance of the double top, Bulkowski suggests dividing the height in half before subtracting from the breakout point. In the above example, this would mean a target price of 215 (230 - 15 = 215)." Recognia's advisory board also separates the two uses of the height measurement — "Yager uses two measurements, one to confirm the formation of the pattern and one to compute a target price" — with the target measured from the right shoulder rather than the neckline break, and "This calculation should be done only when the price has penetrated the neckline."

- **repainting**: **Nothing published.** No page located uses the term or describes whether an event notification can be revised or withdrawn. Because events are delivered as timestamped *notifications* rather than as chart drawings, the architecture is structurally repaint-resistant — a sent alert is a historical fact — but the vendor never claims this. This is a genuine documentation gap for the most widely white-labelled engine in the survey.

- **published_statistics**: **None about its own detections.** This is the survey's most important negative finding, because Recognia's documentation is *full* of statistics that are easy to misattribute. Every number in *Understanding Classic Chart Patterns* is CITED THIRD-PARTY LITERATURE, not a Recognia measurement:
  - "because his statistics show that the pattern has a 93% success rate, there's no need to wait for a confirmed breakout" — Bulkowski, head and shoulders top.
  - "Bulkowski calculates failure rates ranging between 2% and 6% for symmetrical triangles after a valid breakout."
  - "Bulkowski, however, warns that only 55% of developing descending triangles actually prove to be bearish. However, if investors wait for a valid breakout, then the success rate increases to 96%."
  - "Bulkowski estimates the double top has a failure rate of 65%. If an investor waits for the breakout, however, the failure rate declines to 17%."
  - "Bulkowski estimates the double bottom has a failure rate of 64%, which he terms surprisingly high. If an investor waits for a valid breakout, however, the failure rate declines to 3%."
  - "Bulkowski estimates that in 68% of double bottom patterns, price will throwback to the breakout price." and "Bulkowski estimates that the average time for prices to return to the breakout price is 11 days. Throwbacks that occur 30 days after the breakout are not throwbacks at all, but simply normal price fluctuations."
  - "Bulkowski calculates that the measure rule is not completely reliable for the triple top, estimating that nearly 50% of [patterns fail to reach target]"; triple bottom — "Bulkowski estimates the failure rate to be a low 4%, assuming that an investor waits for the upside [breakout]" and "Bulkowski estimates that 70% of triple [bottoms see a pullback]".
  - "Schabacker estimates that probably not more than a third of them signal reversal and that most patterns which an investor might call a double top are not in fact that formation."
  - None of these carry Recognia's sample or period; all are attributed to named authors. The monograph's framing is explicit: "Recognia provides you with a balanced perspective from a number of published experts in the field of technical analysis, including Edwards and Magee, Bulkowski, Murphy and Schabacker."
  - The only Recognia-measured number located is an **engagement**, not accuracy, statistic, and it is ASSERTED with no sample or period: "users who had access to Recognia's Technical Insight traded 61% more than those with no access."

- **scoring**: A single scalar, 0–5, assigned at confirmation. Verbatim: "Events are assigned a score upon their confirmation - the value is assigned differently depending on the type of event. For example, for classic patterns, the value depends on how symmetrical the pattern is and how closely it matches the textbook definition. For indicator and oscillator events, the value is based on the amount of penetration." So the two named components for classic patterns are **symmetry** and **conformance to textbook definition** — a much coarser decomposition than Autochartist's five, and the component weights are not published.

- **notable_gaps**: No cup and handle in any located roster. No wedges named (TradingView, TrendSpider, Autochartist, Finviz all have them). No channels named. No broadening family. No flat base / VCP. The deeper gap is disclosure, not coverage: for the engine embedded in the most retail brokerages, **zero detection parameters and zero self-measured accuracy statistics are public.**

## TC2000

TC2000 ships **no multi-bar chart-pattern detector at all.** It sells a formula language, PCF, and manual drawing tools. The interesting question is therefore not "what does it detect" but "what can PCF express" — and the answer is a sharp, reusable constraint.

- **patterns_detected**: **No structural chart patterns or bases.** No triangle, cup-and-handle, double bottom, head-and-shoulders, flag, wedge, rectangle, channel or base appears anywhere in the help site. There is no pattern-recognition section, no pattern condition category, no pattern indicator, across all eight top-level help sections. What exists (out of scope, but it establishes the language's reach) is three published candlestick formula tables — *Basic Candle Formulas Table*, *Bullish Candlestick Patterns Formulas Table* (~33 patterns), *Bearish Candlestick Patterns Formulas Table* — plus 67 pre-built indicator templates.

- **parameters**: PCF is a language, so the parameters are language primitives. TC2000 publishes almost no defaults; the built-in indicators table says so outright — "The document does not specify default values for any parameters; only parameter definitions and types are provided."
  - `XUP(v, w, x)` — **x = 1** — "Returns true when v is less than w at x bars ago and v is greater than or equal to w now"; "x defaults to 1 if not supplied" — confidence(high)
  - `XDOWN(v, w, x)` — **x = 1** — "Returns true when v is greater than w at x bars ago and v is less than or equal to w now"; "x defaults to 1 if not supplied" — confidence(high)
  - `CountTrue(b, x)` — value: null — "Will return the number of times Boolean formula was true in the period" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `SinceTrue(b, x)` — value: null — "returns -1 if Boolean Formula was not true in period, or bars since it was true, 0 being the current bar, and period-1 the max it will return" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published. **This is the single most important primitive for approximating structure in PCF** — it is the only way to recover *how long ago* something happened.
  - `TrueInRow(b, x)` — value: null — "Will return a value between 0 and period that is how many times the Boolean formula was true in a row" — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `MAX(w, x)` / `MIN(w, x)` — value: null — "Returns the highest value of w over the most recent x bars." / "Returns the lowest value of w over the most recent x bars." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `SUM(w, x)` — value: null — "Adds together the value of w over the most recent x bars." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default published.
  - `C(z)`, `H(z)`, `L(z)`, `O(z)`, `V(z)` (bar offsets) — value: null, no maximum published — "Close or Current price of the specified bar", "High price of the specified bar", "Low price of the specified bar", "Open price of specified bar", "Volume for the specified bar" — confidence(high)
  - compressed range forms `MAXHx.z`, `MINLx.z`, `AVGHx.z`, `AVGLx.z` — value: null — syntax table gives `MAXwx.z` where "w=O, H, L, C, or V; x=Period; z=Offset" — confidence(med-high). `missing:` "No maximum Period or Offset values are specified in the document."
  - `GREATEST(v, w, …)` — "Allows an unlimited number of arguments separated by commas and returns the argument with the highest value." — confidence(high); `LEAST` is the mirror.
  - boolean-to-numeric coercion — `(b)` returns **-1 if true, 0 if false**; `ABS(b)` returns **1 if true, 0 if false** — confidence(high)
  - `Flex Condition` ordering — **default = in order** — "The default mode is for the conditions to pass in order (e.g. step 1, 2, 3, 4, etc). Uncheck the tickbox if you do not want them to pass in order." — confidence(high)
  - `Flex Condition` time span — value: null (documentation example uses 30 bars, not stated as a product default) — "done in number of bars so that it can be applied in any timeframe" — confidence(med). `missing:` no default published. Requires "Premium or Premium+ service level".
  - Full operator surface (confidence high): math `* / + - \ ^ MOD`; relational `> >= < <= = <>` plus `XUP`/`XDOWN`; logical `AND OR NOT NAND NOR XOR XNOR`; math functions `ABS SQR LOG CLG EXP SGN GREATEST LEAST MAX MIN SUM`; trig and hyperbolic families; `IIF(b,t,f)`.

- **confirmation_rule**: **None published, because nothing is detected.** For user-built conditions, the Condition Editor's "True Field" offers "Now", "x bars ago", "within last x bars", "x bars in a row" (confidence med). The closest thing to a published trigger rule is for Flex Conditions: "The last condition of a flex condition must always be passing on the last bar of the flex period to be true." (confidence high). Breakout is entirely user-defined; TC2000 publishes no breakout definition. Pre/post-market scanning is supported — "scanning can be done on the pre/post market data" (confidence med).

- **repainting**: **Nothing published.** The help site never uses the term, and the pre/post-market article says nothing about bars still forming. One structural signal, flagged as inference not vendor text: the syntax table defines `C(z)` as "Close or Current price of the specified bar" — **one symbol serves both the settled close and the live price**, so any intraday condition on the current bar necessarily re-evaluates as the bar forms. TC2000 neither names nor addresses this.

- **published_statistics**: **none published.** No accuracy, hit-rate or backtest figure exists on the help site or homepage. The only quantified marketing claim is about speed, not accuracy, and is **asserted, not measured**: "In 1995, EasyScan® could scan a thousand stocks in 2-seconds. Imagine what it can do today." Likewise "Testing millions of data points for your conditions on-the-fly" and "If you can think it, you can build a condition" — both asserted, no sample.

- **scoring**: **No pattern quality scoring** — there are no patterns to score. Ranking is by WatchList column sort, or by any user-written PCF used as a sortable column. No published composite score and no component names.

- **notable_gaps**: The decisive gap is not product strategy but **language expressiveness**, and it generalises:
  - **No loops, no iteration, no recursion, no arrays** — confirmed by the complete absence of such constructs from the syntax table.
  - **No index-returning function.** `MAX`/`MIN`/`MAXH`/`MINL` return the extreme's *value*, never *which bar it occurred on*. This is the core blocker: you cannot locate a pivot's position, so you cannot measure spacing between two peaks, fit a neckline, or compute converging trendline slopes.
  - **No pivot / fractal / swing-point function, no ZigZag, no trendline, no linear regression or slope function.**
  - Therefore **not expressible**: triangles and wedges (need two fitted converging lines), head and shoulders (needs three ordered, index-located pivots plus a sloping neckline), channels, rectangles, true cup-and-handle geometry.
  - **What *is* partially expressible**, stated precisely rather than overclaimed: a pivot high is testable as `H = MAXH20`, an idiom TC2000 uses in its own published Belt Hold formula — verbatim: `H = MAXH10 AND O > H1 AND O - C >= .7 * (H - L) AND H - L >= 1.2 * (AVGH10 - AVGL10)`. Composed with `SinceTrue(H = MAXH20, 100)` you recover *bars since* a 20-bar swing high — a crude pivot position. `TrueInRow`/`CountTrue` support consolidation and tightness counting. Ordered Flex Conditions give a multi-criteria structural sequencer, the most promising primitive for approximating a base. Brute force is available in principle — with no loop, a double bottom could be written by enumerating `L1…Ln` explicitly — but it is combinatorially impractical and still cannot fit a line.
  - TC2000's published candle formulas do reach back multiple bars, which bounds what the language demonstrably does: Breakaway (bearish) spans 5 bars — `ABS(C4 - O4) > .5 * (H4 - L4) AND C4 > O4 AND C3 > O3 AND L3 > H4 AND C2 > C3 AND C1 > C2 AND C < O AND L < H4 AND H > L3` (confidence med-high).

The EasyScan Condition Library's in-product contents are not published, so an undocumented pattern condition inside the UI cannot be fully ruled out, though silence across all eight help sections makes it unlikely.)*

  - The Predefined Scans library's pattern-shaped entries are indicator- or gap-defined, not geometric: New 52-Week Highs, Strong Volume Gainers, Bullish 50/200-day MA Crossovers, Bullish MACD Crossovers, Oversold with an Improving RSI, Moved Above Upper Bollinger Band, Moved Above Upper Price Channel, Moved Above Upper Keltner Channel, Improving Chaikin Money Flow, New CCI Buy Signals, Parabolic SAR Buy Signals, Stocks in a New Uptrend (Aroon), Stocks in a New Uptrend (ADX), Gap Ups, Breakaway Gap Ups, Runaway Gap Ups, **Island Bottoms** and the bearish mirrors including **Island Tops**. *Island Bottoms/Tops are the only genuinely multi-bar structural formations in the whole library, and they are gap-defined rather than geometry-defined.*
  - **No "ChartScan" feature and no Recognia chart-pattern partnership appears in current official documentation.** The docs corpus returned "I cannot find information about a Recognia partnership or a Recognia chart-pattern recognition product". Web search was unavailable to check press releases or archives, so the honest status is: **absent from current official documentation; historical status unverified.**

- **parameters**:
  - all 61 predefined patterns — **no parameters at all, boolean only** — "All patterns accept `true`/`false` values with operators: `is`, `is not`, `=`, `!=`" — confidence(high)
  - `Uptrend` / `Downtrend` EMA — **5**, fixed and not adjustable — "a security is considered to be in an uptrend if the midpoint of the candlestick is above its 5-day exponential moving average." — confidence(high)
  - P&F reversal — **3-box**, exclusive — "traditional scaling with a 3-box reversal"; no alternative scaling is supported for P&F scans — confidence(high)
  - `P&F Box Count` — value: null (documentation example `[P&F Box Count > 10]`) — confidence(med). `missing:` no default published.
  - `Long Body` / `Short Body` / `Doji` / `Marubozu` / `Star` / `Spinning Top` thresholds — value: null — **thresholds are not published on the syntax page**; definitions are hyperlinked out — confidence(high). `missing:` the numeric body/range ratios are not in the scan reference.
  - `Max(nPeriod, exp)` — value: null — "The highest value of the expression over the specified time period", e.g. `[Max(253,close) > 99.9]` — confidence(high)
  - `Min(nPeriod, exp)` — value: null — "The lowest value of the expression over the specified time period" — confidence(high)
  - `Count Up(nPeriods, exp)` / `Count Down` — **exp default = Close** — "The number of periods in the specified timeframe where the value has increased" / "…decreased" — confidence(high)
  - `Streak Up(exp)` / `Streak Down(exp)` — **exp default = Close** — "The number of periods in a row where the value has increased" / "…decreased" — confidence(high)

  - `PctDiff(exp1, exp2)` — "The percentage difference between two expressions" — confidence(high)

  - `Slope(nPeriods, exp)` — value: null — listed under Trend indicators — confidence(high). **The one structural primitive StockCharts has that TC2000 lacks.**
  - date modifiers — **trading days, not calendar days** — "today's, yesterday's, n days ago (e.g. 3 days ago), this week's, last week's, n weeks ago (e.g. 2 weeks ago), this month's, last month's, n months ago (e.g. 4 months ago)"; "These date modifiers refer to trading days, not calendar days, so a clause with a '5 days ago' modifier is looking at data from 5 trading days earlier." — confidence(high).   - maximum lookback and maximum clauses per scan — **not published** — confidence(med)
  - The complete technical-function set is only **10 functions**: AbsVal, Count Down, Count Up, Max, Min, PctChange, PctDiff, PctRelative, Streak Down, Streak Up.

- **confirmation_rule**: Bar granularity is **daily, weekly and monthly only** — intraday bars are not supported by the scan engine (confidence high). Intraday freshness: "When a StockCharts member runs a scan based on the 'Last Intraday Update', the scan engines will use a 'most recent' data point that is somewhere between 5 and 30 seconds old." First update of the day is "a minute after the market opens, 9:31 AM Eastern Time." Predefined patterns evaluate per bar as booleans with **no separate breakout-confirmation stage**; breakout must be hand-coded. StockCharts' own documented recipe asks four questions — "Has consolidation occurred? / Was there an uptrend prior? / Has a breakout happened? / Did volume increase sharply?" — and publishes the clauses verbatim: consolidation `[today's max(20,close) < today's close * 1.03]` with `[today's min(20,close) > today's close * 0.97]` (a ±3% range test), the alternative `[max(10,close) < SMA(10,close)*1.01]`, prior uptrend `[today's close > today's SMA(50,close)]`, breakout `[today's close > yesterday's close * 1.06]`, volume `[today's volume > yesterday's SMA(20,volume) * 1.5]`, and 52-week high `[today's high > yesterday's daily max(253,high)]`. **This is the most explicit published numeric definition of a consolidation-and-breakout scan from any vendor surveyed** — ±3% over 20 bars for the base, +6% close for the breakout, 1.5× 20-day average volume for confirmation.

- **repainting**: **Not addressed by name anywhere.** The vendor never uses the term. The relevant documented mechanic — the inference is ours, not the vendor's — is that scans run against a daily bar whose data point is "somewhere between 5 and 30 seconds old" during the session, so a pattern boolean on today's unfinished bar can flip repeatedly intraday and settle differently at the close. StockCharts states the data mechanic and draws no conclusion from it.

- **published_statistics**: **none published**, and uniquely among the vendors surveyed StockCharts affirmatively disclaims effectiveness: "StockCharts.com makes no claims about the effectiveness of these scans for trading purposes. These scans should only be used for educational purposes; they are intended to help you develop your own personal trading strategy." No accuracy figure, no sample, no period, no backtest; users are pointed at the Starting Date Adjuster to test for themselves. This is a **cleaner posture than an unmeasured marketing number** — an explicit refusal to claim.

- **scoring**: **No pattern quality scoring.** Patterns are boolean — present or absent, with no strength, confidence or grade. Adjacent ranking mechanisms exist but are not documented as pattern-quality components: **SCTR** (StockCharts Technical Rank, available as a scan property), **P&F Box Count** (a raw count, `[P&F Box Count > 10]`), and the **Chande Trend Meter**.

- **notable_gaps**: StockCharts publishes an **explicit exclusion list**, which is the single most useful artifact found in this survey because it is a vendor naming its own structural blind spots: "Anchored VWAP, Momentum, Moving Average Ribbon, Pivot Points, RS-Ratio and RS-Momentum, RVOL - Price Plot, RVOL - Time of Day, Volume By Price, VWAP, and ZigZag" are unavailable for scans. **ZigZag and Pivot Points — the two primitives required for any swing-structure detection — are specifically named as unavailable.** That is dispositive. Consequently not expressible: head and shoulders, cup and handle, OHLC triangles and wedges, flags, pennants, channels, rectangles, ascending or descending bases, or any pattern needing a fitted trendline or a located pivot. Also: no loops, no arrays, no index-returning function (`Max`/`Min` return values only — the same blocker as TC2000); no intraday bars at all, so no intraday base or breakout detection; only 10 technical functions in the entire language. `Slope(nPeriods, exp)` gives a scalar trend measure over a window — StockCharts' own consolidation scan uses it as a flatness test, `[yesterday's Slope(20) < 0.3]` with `[yesterday's Slope(20) > -0.3]` — which is enough to detect flatness or tightness but **not** enough to fit two converging boundary lines, since you cannot regress over a chosen subset of pivots.

**The shared root cause across TC2000 and StockCharts, and the most transferable finding in this document:** both engines are *per-bar boolean evaluators over a fixed window*. Neither has a loop, and neither has a function that returns **where** an extreme occurred. Pattern geometry requires locating pivots and fitting lines between them, and a value-returning `MAX` over a window cannot do that however the expression is composed. That one missing primitive — not product strategy — is why neither vendor detects triangles, and it is why Point & Figure is the only route either takes: P&F discretises price onto a grid, which converts geometry into an exact combinatorial match.

## Trade Ideas

**Trade Ideas publishes more about *how* it detects patterns than any other vendor here** — a turning-point count, a volume-confirmation algorithm, an explicit fast-versus-confirmed trade-off, a Z-score quality definition, and percentile cutoffs. It is the single best model for a competing detector's disclosure.

- **patterns_detected**: The geometric family, tagged internally "Geometric Pattern" and "Volume Confirmed", named exactly with alert codes: "Broadening bottom [GBBOT]", "Broadening top [GBTOP]", "Triangle bottom [GTBOT]", "Triangle top [GTTOP]", "Rectangle bottom [GRBOT]", "Rectangle top [GRTOP]", "Double bottom [GDBOT]", "Double top [GDTOP]", "Inverted head and shoulders [GHASI]", "Head and shoulders [GHAS]". Adjacent structural and channel alerts: "Consolidation [C]", "Channel breakout [CHBO]", "Channel breakdown [CHBD]", "Channel breakout (confirmed) [CHBOC]", "Channel breakdown (confirmed) [CHBDC]", the "5/10/15/30 minute consolidation breakout [CBO5, CBO10, CBO15, CBO30]" family with matching breakdowns, "Crossed above resistance [CAR]" / "(confirmed) [CARC]", "Crossed below support [CBS]" / "(confirmed) [CBSC]", "Crossed daily highs resistance [CDHR]", "Crossed daily lows support [CDLS]", "Check mark [CMU]" / "Inverted check mark [CMD]", plus the 1/2/5/10/15/30/60-minute opening-range breakout family and 5/15/30/90-minute linear-regression trend alerts. Vendor count claim: "Trade Ideas Users have over 500 Alerts and Filters at their disposal to create customized scans." On the *filter* side only "Consolidation [ConDays]" and "Position in Consolidation [RCon]" are structural — there are no double-top or H&S filters.

- **parameters**: The richest published parameter set in the survey.
  - `turning points required` — **5** — H&S: "A head and shoulders pattern is defined by exactly 5 consecutive turning points."; Rectangle: "We report a rectangle pattern after seeing at least 5 consecutive highs and lows."; Triangle: "It takes at least 5 consecutive highs and lows in this pattern before we report it."; Broadening: same wording — confidence(high). **Note the difference: H&S is *exactly* 5, the others *at least* 5.**
  - `"approximately the same price" tolerance` — **adaptive, no number published** — "Our definition of \"approximately the same price\" depends on the size of the pattern and the volatility of the stock." — confidence(high). `missing:` the adaptation function is not published — but the *statement that tolerance scales with pattern size and volatility* is itself the design guidance.
  - `alert-specific quality filter` (all Geometric Pattern alerts) — optional, blank by default — "This filter is located immediately to the right of the corresponding alert and is always optional. You can decide to leave it blank if you want to receive more alerts, or you can fill it in to restrict the number of alerts you receive." — confidence(high)
  - `hours-traded filter` — value: null; published guidance value **7.5** — "If you want to see stock patterns that lasted for a whole day, a good estimate is 7.5 hours, 6.5 for normal hours plus 1 for the pre and post market." and "Volume is the major factor in this filter." — confidence(high)
  - `Consolidation quality` — **default 2**, minimum 2, "tight" at ≥5, maximum 10 — "2 is the lowest quality that the alerts server ever reports. That is the default. 5 is the cutoff for a \"tight\" consolidation… 10 is the best quality reported by the alerts server. 10 means that the top and bottom of the trading range, as reported in the consolidation alert, are the same." — confidence(high)
  - `Channel Breakout (confirmed) quality` — minimum **1.0**, "briskly" at ≥**5.0**, "very few" above **10.0** — "A value of 1.0 is the minimum that the alerts server ever reports. Leaving this field blank, or setting it to 1.0, shows all alerts. Alerts with values of 5.0 or higher as marked as moving \"briskly\" in the description field. There is no upper limit to this value, but very few alerts have a value above 10.0." — confidence(high)
  - `Consolidation filter [ConDays] lookback` — **40 trading days** — "This looks at the daily candles for the previous 40 trading days. This does not look at today's data." and "The longest consolidation we can report is 40 days." — confidence(high)
  - `ConDays suggested settings` — min 7 / max 3 / max 25 — "Adjust the minimum value to 7 to see only stocks with a strong consolidation pattern." / "Set the maximum value to 3 to see only stocks which have not had any significant consolidation." / "you may want to set your maximum to 25 days or lower to see more ordinary patterns." — confidence(high)

- **confirmation_rule**: **Volume-confirmed turning points, print-driven, not bar-close.** The algorithm is published verbatim: "You need significant volume below a high price, just to set a baseline. Then you need significant volume near or at the high price to define the high price. Finally, you need significant volume below the price, again, to show that the trend has reversed itself and the price has turned around. Lows are defined similarly." Pattern completion: Triangle — "The pattern is complete when the price breaks through its support level."; Rectangle — "The only way to confirm a rectangle pattern is for the price to move up and down through the entire range of the rectangle." The **fast-versus-confirmed trade-off is stated explicitly**, which no other vendor does: "This alert attempts to identify the same chart patterns as its confirmed counterparts. The primary difference is that this alert attempts to notify the user as quickly as possible, while the confirmed alert waits until the chart pattern is clearer. As a trade-off for being notified sooner, the user may receive some false signals." With the timescale quantified: "Very roughly speaking this alert is on the same time-scale as a one minute chart, and the confirmed version is on the same time-scale as a 15 minute chart." And confirmed breakout requires a pattern, not a single print: "an alert does not occur every time a single print is outside of the channel. An alert only occurs when there is a recognizable pattern of price, time, and volume." Re-evaluation cadence: "On average the software reevaluates each consolidation every 15 minutes, but the exact time depends on how quickly the stock is trading."

- **repainting**: The word is not used, but Trade Ideas publishes three directly relevant behaviours, together the most concrete repaint-adjacent disclosure after TrendSpider's timestamp:
  - Non-lookahead, stated flatly: "This looks at the daily candles for the previous 40 trading days. **This does not look at today's data.**"
  - **Boundaries drift instead of firing** — the honest description of what a competing detector will also do: "If the stock price moves just slightly outside of the range of the consolidation, the software may just increase the size of the channel. In this case another consolidation alert will eventually occur, but it will be labeled as \"decaying\"."
  - Timestamp imprecision is disclosed rather than hidden: "As a result of the smoothing and confirmation, the times are not as precise as the prices."

- **published_statistics**: **No measured accuracy statistic for the pattern alerts.** Two things must be kept strictly apart.
  - **(a) OddsMaker is a user-run backtester, not a published result set.** Its component names are published — Profit Factor ("calculated by dividing the sum of all profits by the sum of all losses"), total number of trades, Win Rate, Average Winner vs Average Loser, Strategy Return, Projected Annual Return, Buying Power, Drawdown, Equity Curve, plus a colour-coded Calendar and an Optimization Tab. Its published **limits** are the real numbers and they are the most useful part: "Typically, there are 64 days worth of history in the database."; "The OddsMaker will list the first 100 trades per day. If set to use the entire available backtesting period, this equates to 6200 trades."; "The OddsMaker does not look at any data for the current trading day. It does not backtest in the premarket or postmarket session."; "Once it enters a position for a specific stock symbol, it will not look at that stock again for the rest of the trading day."; "Our backtesting tool… is based on the open, high, low, and close data of 1-minute candles, not on tick-by-tick data. Because of this, you are likely to see discrepancies between testing results and actual trading."; "The OddsMaker will use historical data for the exit price and doesn't factor in the spread."
  - **(b) A vendor blog post carrying uncited percentages — ASSERTED, NOT MEASURED, and to be treated with active suspicion.** The official Trade Ideas blog post *Chart Pattern Recognition at Scale* (2025-10-08) states: "Statistical analysis shows that computer-identified patterns with proper volume divergence confirmation produce 65-70% success rates, compared to 45-50% for manually identified setups." It also asserts "stocks with increasing institutional ownership show 40% higher pattern success", "Volume requirements can exceed 150% of the 20-day average during breakout attempts", and "breakouts must hold above resistance for at least two consecutive sessions". **Flag these hard**: no sample, no period, no methodology, no source; the post does not claim these describe Trade Ideas' own engine (it says "computer-identified patterns" generically); and the "two consecutive sessions" rule **directly contradicts the product documentation**, which is print-driven and intraday. This is marketing copy sitting on the same domain as excellent technical documentation — a good reminder that vendor-hosted is not the same as vendor-documented.

- **scoring**: Two published quality scales with named mechanics, and one of them is a genuine trap.
  - **Geometric Pattern alerts — the "quality" column is AGE, not goodness**: "The quality value on a scan using the 'Head and Shoulders' alert defines the number of hours since the measured pattern has been established." (identical wording on Double Top, Rectangle Top, Triangle Top, Broadening Top). Anyone benchmarking against Trade Ideas who reads that column as pattern quality will be comparing the wrong quantity.
  - **Consolidation / Channel Breakout — a Z-score**: "Internally, when the alerts server examines a stock it produces a Z-score describing the error in the consolidation pattern. This is a statistical measure comparing the ideal pattern, a range of $0, to the actual range, taking the volatility into account. Larger errors translate to lower quality." Drivers: "The more time and volume in a consolidation, the higher the quality. For a given stock, the smaller the range, the higher the quality. For different stocks, historical volatility is used to make the quality scales match." **A volatility-normalised Z-score against an ideal zero-width range is the most rigorous published base-tightness metric in this survey.**
  - **Percentile cutoffs** (the only published percentile calibration anywhere): "A value of 4 shows the most extraordinary 1/3 of the alerts. A value of 10 ensures only the top 1% of the alerts will be triggered."

- **notable_gaps**: No **Wedge** of any kind, no **Flag or Pennant**, no **Cup and Handle**, no **Diamond**, no **Rounding Bottom**. No **Ascending/Descending Triangle as distinct types** — only a symmetric-style "Triangle top"/"Triangle bottom" defined as "a series of lower highs and higher lows". No sloped **Channel Up/Down** as patterns; Trade Ideas' "Channel" is the horizontal consolidation channel. Triple and quadruple tops are folded into Double Top: "This alert can also report triple tops, quadruple tops, etc. In these cases, the alert description states the number of highs."

## MetaStock

MetaStock ships **no native structural pattern recognition**. Its native pattern functions are candlesticks; structural detection is sold as paid third-party add-ons. Its unique contribution to this survey is that **it is the only vendor that explicitly and clearly documents repainting.**

- **patterns_detected**:
  - **Native: none structural.** The formula language's built-in pattern functions are single- to three-bar candlesticks only — Bearish harami, Bearish harami cross, Big black candle, Big white candle, Black body, Bullish harami, Bullish harami cross, Dark cloud cover, Doji, Doji star, Engulfing bullish line, Evening doji star, Evening star, Falling window, Gravestone doji, Hammer, Hanging man, Inverted black hammer, Inverted hammer, Long legged doji, Long lower shadow, Long upper shadow, Morning star, Piercing line, Rising window. **No head-and-shoulders, triangle, wedge, flag, cup, rectangle or double-top function exists in the language.** MetaStock 20 bundles a candlestick engine free — "INCLUDED FOR FREE - Greg Morris Japanese Candle Pattern Recognition ($399 Value)" — again candles, not structure.
  - **Add-on: Chart Pattern Recognition by John Murphy** ($299; components "Explorations, Indicators, Experts"; EOD, 11.0+; SKU F-018-053) — "CPR will label each of the nine different patterns as they occur within your charts": Reversal — "Head and Shoulders", "Inverse Head and Shoulders", "Triple Tops", "Triple Bottoms", "Double Tops", "Double Bottoms"; Continuation — "Symmetrical Triangles", "Ascending Triangles", "Descending Triangles".
  - **Add-on: Fulgent Chart Pattern Engine by Ed Downs** ($1,495; "Style: Pattern Recognition"; version 17.5; SKU 3PC-ADD-CPE) — "Scans and identifies 12 pattern types": "Trend Line Breaks", "Trend Line Reversals", "Support/Resistance Breaks", "Support/Resistance Reversals", "Consolidations" (Rectangle, Triangle, Wedge, Broadening, Flag), "Fibonacci Retracements" (38%, 50%, 62%), "Cup and Handle", "Gaps" (Breakaway, Exhaustion, Measured), "Saucers & Rounded Tops", "Head and Shoulders", "Tops & Bottoms", "Volume Climax & Trend".
  - Other pattern-adjacent add-ons in the same catalogue: Silas Peters' 3S Code ($497, "W Pattern, M Pattern, 123 Tops, 123 Bottom"), Rick Saddler's Patterns for Profit ($799), Red Rock Pattern Strategy ($299), Around the Horn Pattern Scans ($299), Fulgent AI ($149/mo or $1609.20/yr), Fulgent WaveTrader ($499, Elliott Wave).

- **parameters**: The formula language is where structural parameters live. Exact signatures verbatim from the official Formula Primer:
  - `zig( DATA ARRAY, MINIMUM CHANGE, DIFF_METHOD )` — value: null — "MINIMUM CHANGE is the amount required for a reversal and DIFF_METHOD is how the change is to be calculated."; "Valid DIFF_METHODs are PERCENT and POINTS (these can be abbreviated as % and $)." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default reversal amount published.
  - `peak( Nth, DATA ARRAY, % MINIMUM CHANGE )` — value: null — "Using the Zig Zag function, this calculates the value DATA ARRAY had at a peak. How many peaks back to reference is contained in the Nth parameter." — confidence(high). `missing:` no default.
  - `peakbars( Nth, DATA ARRAY, % MINIMUM CHANGE)` — value: null — "calculates how many periods have passed since a peak in the specified DATA ARRAY has occurred." — confidence(high). **This is the index-returning function TC2000 and StockCharts both lack**, and it is exactly why MetaStock's language can express structure that theirs cannot.
  - `trough( Nth, DATA ARRAY, % MINIMUM CHANGE )` and `troughbars( Nth, DATA ARRAY, % MINIMUM CHANGE)` — value: null — confidence(high). `missing:` no defaults.
  - illustrative reversal amount — **ten percent**, explicitly illustrative not a default — "The following chart shows a stock and the Zigzag indicator with a ten percent filter." — confidence(high)
  - `CPR (Murphy) parameters` — **none published** — confidence(high). `missing:` no thresholds, tolerances or settings appear on the product page.
  - `CPE (Fulgent) parameters` — user-configurable but unspecified — "User-configurable colored highlights over the patterns" / "User-defined interface to activate specific patterns in the Expert Advisor" — confidence(high). `missing:` no values published.
  - MetaStock publishes an explicit warning that **no universal reversal amount exists** — worth quoting to anyone tempted to hard-code one: "they require a predetermined reversal amount. A value that is perfect for one chart may not find anything on another chart, or perhaps worse, be too sensitive and bounce around when the prices are actually flat and consolidating."

- **confirmation_rule**: For the formula language, **none — the user writes it.** MetaStock's Primer II teaches DIY pivot detection precisely because the built-ins are insufficient: "The Peak() and Trough() functions will find these types of pivots, but they require a predetermined reversal amount… A more creative solution is called for." Its worked alternative is a bar-count rule on daily bars — `H > Ref( HHV( H, 40), -1) AND H > Ref( H, +1)` — and it offers the asymmetric variant "look for a bar that has four lower values to the left and one lower value to the right." *(That asymmetry — more bars required on the left than the right — is a deliberate latency-versus-stability trade and is the sharpest published alternative to TradingView's symmetric 5/5.)* For CPR, patterns are reported before completion: "You can also use CPR to generate daily trading alerts with patterns that aren't fully formed." Breakout significance is delegated to human judgement, not a rule: "Was the breakout from the pattern significant enough to warrant a trade?" CPE works on "any time frame including weekly, daily and intraday bars."

- **repainting**: **MetaStock is the only vendor in this survey that explicitly and unambiguously documents repainting**, in its own Formula Primer, and it names the downstream blast radius:
  - "The Zigzag's plot is a line moving up and down in sync with the values it is plotted on, however, the last segment of the line is estimated. The direction of the last part of the line is determined by assuming that the plot will continue in the same direction."
  - "Notice the last part of the line is dipping downward even though the security has not lost ten percent of its value since the last peak. The Zigzag is anticipating the continued downward movement."
  - "During the previous 3-week period, the prices never dropped the required ten percent. As a result, when the prices started rising again, the line corrected to show it as one continuous upward progression."
  - The warning, in full: "Understanding how and why the Zigzag does this is important because several functions in the MetaStock formula language are based on this indicator. These additional functions include Peak(), Trough(), and Divergence(), etc. … because they are based on the Zigzag, the last value is always uncertain. These functions have many uses in examining historical data, but **you should be careful with any buy and sell decisions based directly on them. Tomorrow, the signal might not be there.**"
  - **Direct consequence:** any structural pattern built on `peak()`/`trough()` inherits Zig Zag repainting. Since TrendSpider's Depth/Deviation/Back Step parameterisation is a ZigZag swing detector, the same caution transfers to it — and TrendSpider does not say so.

- **published_statistics**: **none published.** Every quantitative-sounding claim is either throughput or unsupported assertion:
  - **Asserted, not measured**: "Numerous studies have repeatedly shown that these patterns have excellent predictive value." (Murphy CPR page — no studies named, no sample, no period.)
  - **Asserted, not measured**: "this incredible new tool rivals the best pattern recognition experts in the industry."
  - **Asserted, not measured**: "JCPR delivers true Japanese methodology, advanced filtering, and unmatched accuracy in pattern recognition." — "accuracy" with no number attached at all.
  - **Award, not accuracy**: "This product has won prestigious Technical Analysis of Stocks and Commodities Readers' Choice Awards." — a readers' poll, not a measurement.
  - **Throughput, not accuracy** (the only real numbers): "The Engine is capable of scanning charts at a rate exceeding 50 per second, which means you can scan the S&P 500 in about 10 seconds – or the Russell with 2,000 stocks in under a minute." Plus provenance claims: "based on 17 years of pattern research by Nirvana Systems, Inc.", "Over the past 22 years, Mr. Downs has led his team…", "Currently, in its 6th revision".

- **scoring**: **No numeric pattern-quality score** from MetaStock or either add-on. CPR substitutes human commentary — "Each pattern is computer-verified and supplemented with John Murphy's own expert commentary. Simply click your mouse on the identified pattern to read the specific details on how he feels this pattern rates" — and produces unexplained projections: "the reports will give you price projections where the security price will move within a specified period of time". CPE substitutes a visual encoding: "all patterns will be detected and shown, each with a color code that shows the zone over which the pattern formed" — a location code, not a quality score.

- **notable_gaps**: **Natively, everything structural** — no head and shoulders, triangle, wedge, flag, cup, rectangle, channel or double top ships in the box; only candlesticks do. Murphy CPR: no wedges, flags/pennants, cup and handle, rectangles, channels or broadening — nine patterns, EOD only, 11.0+. Fulgent CPE: no double/triple tops as named types (folded into "Tops & Bottoms"), no separately-named inverse head and shoulders, and the catalogue lists compatibility with **version 17.5 only** — a real deployment gap against current MetaStock 20.

### Table stakes — what every detecting vendor has

**Important framing:** two of the ten vendors, **TC2000 and StockCharts, detect no OHLC chart patterns at all.** For them the answer to every row is architectural, not commercial — neither language has a loop or a function that returns *where* an extreme occurred, so pattern geometry is inexpressible. "Every vendor" is therefore vacuous, and table stakes are computed over the **eight vendors that actually detect structural patterns** (TV, TS, AC, CM, FV, TI, MS-via-add-on, RC).

- **Double Top — 8 of 8.**
- **Double Bottom — 8 of 8.**
- **Triangle, in some form — 8 of 8** (though only 6 of 8 split it into ascending/descending/symmetrical; TradingView and Trade Ideas ship one undifferentiated triangle).
- **Head and Shoulders and Inverse Head and Shoulders — 7 of 8**, missing only from ChartMill.

That is the entire table-stakes set: **three families, four to five named patterns.** Anything below that line is not competitive; anything above it is optional.

Two further near-universals worth noting because they are infrastructure rather than patterns: **horizontal support/resistance levels (6 of 8)** and **fitted trendlines (5 of 8)**. Several vendors sell these as products in their own right, and every geometric detector needs them internally anyway.

### Differentiators — what only one or two vendors have

- **Island Top / Island Bottom — StockCharts only (1).** Ironically the vendor that refuses chart patterns ships the one gap-defined structural formation nobody else does.
- **Harmonic patterns (ABCD, Gartley, Butterfly) — Autochartist only (1)**, with its own three-component quality vector (Clarity, Time Symmetry, Price Symmetry) rather than the five-component chart-pattern one.
- **Rounding Bottom / Saucer — MetaStock only (1)**, and only through the $1,495 Fulgent add-on.
- **Inverted Cup and Handle — TradingView and TrendSpider (2).**
- **Wedge as an undifferentiated converging pattern — Finviz and MetaStock/Fulgent (2).**
- **Consolidation / squeeze base as a first-class named detector — ChartMill and Trade Ideas (2).** This is the most commercially interesting gap: it is the closest anyone comes to detecting a *base* rather than a *shape*, and both implementations are well documented (ChartMill's Squeeze Play family "as defined by John Carter"; Trade Ideas' volatility-normalised Z-score against an ideal zero-width range).
- **Just outside the line at 3:** Cup and Handle (TradingView, TrendSpider, MetaStock+) and Broadening (TrendSpider, Trade Ideas, MetaStock+). Both are more differentiator than table stake.

### What no vendor detects — build-from-scratch territory

Not one of the ten vendors detects any of the following, under any name:

- **Flat Base**
- **Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP)**
- **Ascending Base**
- **High Tight Flag** (ChartMill publishes an article; no vendor ships a detector)
- **Darvas Box**
- **Diamond** (several vendors write about it; none detect it)
- **Base-on-base / Power Play**
- **Handle-less cup**
- **Pocket pivot as a detected event** — ChartMill uses it only as a bonus term inside its Setup Rating: "More Bonus points are given for recent pocket pivots."

**This is the single most actionable finding in the document.** The entire O'Neil/CANSLIM base vocabulary — the structures a swing-trading screener most wants — is *completely absent from every commercial vendor surveyed*. What vendors sell is **classical chart-pattern geometry** (triangles, H&S, double tops); what base-oriented traders actually want is **contraction-and-tightness structure over weeks to months**. Those are different problems, and the market has not solved the second one. Any competing detector that ships flat base, VCP, or ascending base is not matching a vendor feature — it is entering unoccupied territory, and there is no vendor benchmark to measure against.

## Synthesis: what to take from vendor truth

**1. Nobody publishes a measured accuracy statistic for their own detector — with one carefully-scoped exception.** Across ten vendors, exactly one publishes a self-measured number: Autochartist, whose Performance Statistics tool covers "the overall accuracy of Autochartist for the preceding 6-month period" (the support portal says "over the last 12 months" — the vendor contradicts itself and both are recorded here), broken down by asset class, pattern type, symbol, direction and hour of day. Even that is scoped with unusual honesty: "These statistics are not a measure of profitability, but simply count the quantity of identified opportunities that reach the forecast level within a predefined time." Everything else framed as accuracy is marketing — Recognia's monograph statistics are all **cited third-party literature** (Bulkowski, Edwards & Magee, Schabacker), Trade Ideas' "65-70% success rates" blog claim has no sample and contradicts its own documentation, and MetaStock's "unmatched accuracy" carries no number at all. **A competing detector that publishes a real hit-rate with a stated sample and window would be, on this evidence, roughly alone.**

**2. The confirmation rule is nearly always "one close beyond the line", and the vendors that deviate say so loudly.** TradingView: "The breakout of the pattern is fixed by the close value." TrendSpider: "Each breakout requires one full candlestick close through a trend line on a given timeframe." StockCharts' hand-coded recipe: a single `today's close > yesterday's close * 1.06`. The deviations are the interesting part — Trade Ideas is print-driven rather than bar-close and publishes the fast-versus-confirmed trade-off explicitly ("As a trade-off for being notified sooner, the user may receive some false signals", one-minute versus fifteen-minute timescale), while the classical literature Recognia cites wants a 1–3% penetration filter plus Murphy's "two-day rule" of two successive closes. **No vendor ships the percentage-penetration filter the literature recommends.** That is an available, cheap differentiator: a penetration threshold and a two-close option, both exposed.

**3. Tolerance is where the vendors actually differ, and three distinct denominators are in use.** TradingView normalises deviation against *pattern geometry* ("a percentage of the distance between the triangle lines", "a percentage of the height of the cup"). TrendSpider offers **both** ATR and percentage for the same knob (`Max peak distance, ATR` and `Max peak distance, %`) and four band types (ATR, Standard Deviation, Constant, Percentage). Trade Ideas normalises against **volatility and pattern size together** — "Our definition of \"approximately the same price\" depends on the size of the pattern and the volatility of the stock" — and scores tightness as a volatility-normalised Z-score against an ideal zero-width range. The published numeric anchors worth stealing outright: TrendSpider's pivot-prominence test of **1.5 × ATR(14)** relative to adjacent bars, its gap threshold of **three times ATR(14)**, TradingView's **5/5 pivot** and **20-bar minimum cup**, Trade Ideas' **5 turning points** (exactly 5 for H&S, at least 5 for the rest) and **40-day consolidation lookback**, Autochartist's **3 minimum touching points** for a key level, and StockCharts' **±3% over 20 bars** consolidation test.

**4. Repainting is the industry's blind spot, and the two vendors who address it do it in opposite ways.** Six of ten vendors say nothing at all. MetaStock is the only one that admits it outright, and names the blast radius — "because they are based on the Zigzag, the last value is always uncertain… Tomorrow, the signal might not be there." TrendSpider is the only one that engineers against it, with the **Truth in Analysis Timestamp** and Refresh & Lock. TradingView discloses it obliquely, by drawing provisional anchors dotted. Note the transitive risk nobody states: TrendSpider's Depth/Deviation/Back Step is a **ZigZag parameterisation**, so MetaStock's warning applies to it too, and TrendSpider does not say so. Meanwhile a 5/5 pivot cannot be confirmed until five bars after it prints — a structural latency TradingView documents implicitly and never names. **A per-detection "as of" timestamp plus an explicit provisional/confirmed flag would beat every vendor's disclosure here, and it is nearly free to implement.**

**5. Copy Autochartist's scoring shape, and Trade Ideas' scoring rigour.** Autochartist is the only vendor publishing a decomposed, arithmetically-averaged quality vector — Clarity, Initial Trend, Uniformity, Breakout Strength, Volume, each 1–10 with a stated computation, averaged into Overall Quality, with volume excluded for OTC and breakout excluded for emerging patterns. Recognia's 0–5 is a single scalar over "how symmetrical the pattern is and how closely it matches the textbook definition". ChartMill's Setup Rating adds the *context* components the geometry-only scores miss ("Whether or not volatility is decreasing", "Whether or not there is a support zone below the current price"). TrendSpider publishes the raw ingredients (`hits`, `violations`, `priceDev25/50/75`, `peaksUp/peaksDown`) but not the weights. And Trade Ideas publishes the only percentile calibration anywhere — "A value of 4 shows the most extraordinary 1/3 of the alerts. A value of 10 ensures only the top 1%." **A trap to avoid: Trade Ideas' "quality" column on its geometric pattern alerts is not quality at all — it is "the number of hours since the measured pattern has been established."** Benchmark against the wrong column and every comparison is meaningless.

**6. Watch for name collisions before comparing vendors.** Finviz's "Wedge" is everyone else's symmetrical triangle. Finviz's "Triangle Ascending" is internally `wedgeresistance`. StockCharts' "Triangle" and "Double Top" exist only on a Point & Figure grid and will never match an OHLC detection. Trade Ideas' "Channel" is a horizontal consolidation, not a sloped channel. ChartMill's "Multiple Top" may or may not include triple tops — the vendor does not say. And ChartMill, TrendSpider and Finviz all publish educational articles about patterns they do not detect. **Article existence is not detector existence, and a same-named row in the table above is not always the same object.**
