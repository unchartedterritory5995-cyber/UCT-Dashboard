## Cup with Handle

- **origin / source_name**: William J. O'Neil, *How to Make Money in Stocks*; taught continuously in IBD's *Investor's Corner* / *How To Buy Stocks* columns. Sources 1, 2, 4, 5, 25, 26 above.
- **definition**: A U-shaped price consolidation following a prior advance, whose right side recovers toward the old high and is then interrupted by a short, shallow, downward-drifting pullback (the handle) that shakes out weak holders immediately before the breakout. IBD's own framing of the base concept: *"The base is a place where the stock pauses to rest and get ready for the next move up."* (source 4). IBD names it as the first of *"seven bases"*, alongside *"the saucer with handle, double bottom, base on base, flat base, ascending base and high, tight flag"* (source 4) — note that list omits the square box and the cup-without-handle.
- **criteria**:
  - base length, minimum — 7 weeks — *"A proper cup base needs to span a minimum of seven weeks, while a flat base can be as short as five weeks."* (source 2) — confidence: high
  - base length, observed range — 7 to 65 weeks — *"They range from seven weeks to as long as 65 weeks in length, with many forming over three to six months."* (source 1) — confidence: high
  - how weeks are counted — first down-closing week through the week before breakout — *"Count the weeks in the base starting with the first week that closes down through the week prior to the breakout."* (source 1) — confidence: high
  - base depth, normal range — 12% to 33% — *"The size of the decline, or correction, in a cup base should generally be between 12% and 33%."* (source 2) — confidence: high
  - base depth, bear-market allowance — up to 50% — *"In bear markets, cups can run as deep as 50%."* (source 6) — confidence: high
  - base depth, relative to market — 1.5x to 2.5x the index decline — *"It's normal for growth stocks to create cup patterns during intermediate declines in the general market and to correct 1-1/2 to 2-1/2 times the market averages."* (source 6) — confidence: high
  - handle position within the base — upper half — *"A proper handle forms in the upper half of a base and drifts slightly downward along its price lows."* (source 2) — confidence: high
  - handle position relative to the old high — within 15% — *"Handle should form in the upper half of the cup, and within 15% of the old price high"* (source 26, IBD handout, provenance unverified) — confidence: med
  - handle drift direction — downward along the lows; an upward drift is a defect — *"A proper handle forms in the upper half of a base and drifts slightly downward along its price lows."* (source 2) and, of a specific failed-looking setup, *"handle with an upward drift, a negative"* (source 5) — confidence: high
  - handle depth — 10% to 12% — *"depth of the handle should be 10%-12%"* (source 25, IBD handout, provenance unverified) — confidence: med
  - handle length, minimum — value: null — no IBD source fetched in this session states a minimum handle length in weeks. `missing:` an IBD-published minimum handle duration (Bulkowski, a *different* authority, publishes "1 week minimum with no maximum"; do not import that number into an IBD rule set).
  - volume behavior inside the base and handle — must ease — *"Volume should mostly ease until the breakout"* and *"Turnover fell sharply as it shaped a handle."* (source 2) — confidence: high
  - accumulation vs distribution weeks — value: null — *"You prefer to see weeks of accumulation outnumber weeks of distribution in any base"* (source 12) states a direction but publishes no ratio. `missing:` a published minimum accumulation:distribution week ratio, and IBD's definition of an accumulation week in OHLCV terms.
  - pivot / buy point — high of the handle + $0.10 — *"10 cents above the peak in the handle"* (source 25) — confidence: med for the exact wording, high for the rule, which is corroborated in IBD editorial for every other base (sources 8, 11, 17)
  - breakout volume threshold — at least 40% above the 50-day average — *"the stock should clear the buy point in at least 40% above its 50-day average."* † (source 11 — stated for the flat base, but IBD applies the same threshold across bases) and *"at least a 40% increase above the stock's average daily volume"* (source 26) — confidence: high on 40%, med on the exact denominator wording
  - breakout volume, upper phrasing — 40% to 50% above average — *"At least 40%-50% above average"* (source 25) — confidence: med
  - buy zone above the pivot — 5% — *"Ideally, the stock will quickly clear the 5% buy zone and create a cushion of gains."* (source 4) and *"up to 5% above the ideal buy point"* (source 25) — confidence: high
  - prior uptrend required before the base — at least 30% — *"Prior uptrend of at least 30%"* (source 25) and, for the cup specifically, *"first leg should be up at least 30%"* (source 30, third-party summary of O'Neil's book) — confidence: med (no IBD editorial column fetched here states this number)
  - shape smoothness — no weekly swings of 10–15%+ — *"Cups should show smooth action on the left and right sides of the pattern. There shouldn't be jagged edges or many wild weekly swings of 10% to 15% or more."* (source 3) — confidence: high
  - symmetry — down weeks left ≈ up weeks right — *"The base should be symmetrical in shape; the number of down weeks on the left side should roughly match the number of up weeks on the right side."* (source 3) — confidence: high
- **measured_performance**: **none published by IBD.** IBD's only quantitative claim about bases in general is a provenance claim with no sample size and no benchmark: *"IBD research on market winners going back to 1880 shows stocks that form correctly shaped bases have the best chance of success."* (source 3) — no n, no period breakdown, no comparison population, so it is not a usable statistic. The only measured numbers found are Thomas Bulkowski's, on **his** definition, not IBD's: *"Overall performance rank (1 is best): 3 out of 39"*, *"Break even failure rate: 5%"*, *"Average rise: 54%"*, *"Throwback rate: 62%"*, *"Percentage meeting price target: 61%"*, based on *"913 perfect trades"* (source 27). Bulkowski's page as fetched did not state the date range of the 913 trades. **Base-rate warning:** Bulkowski publishes no benchmark — no all-stocks-over-the-same-window comparison — so "5% break-even failure rate" and "54% average rise" are unanchored. A 54% average rise over an unstated holding horizon in a sample of *perfect* (hand-selected) patterns is not comparable to any buy-and-hold control, and none is given.
- **invalidation**: wide-and-loose price action (jagged edges, weekly swings of 10–15%+); a handle that forms in the lower half of the base; a handle that drifts *upward* (source 5 calls this explicitly *"a negative"*); a handle whose peak sits more than 15% below the old high; volume that fails to dry up into the handle; a breakout on inadequate volume; a base that is a third- or fourth-stage structure (see base counting below); breaking out while the market is not in a confirmed uptrend. IBD also lists *"recent excessive stock splits or share offerings"* as a disqualifier of the stock, not the shape (source 3). Post-entry, IBD's hard invalidation is a price rule, not a shape rule: *"Get out if a stock drops 8% below your buy point."* (source 4).
- **detection_notes**: Computable from daily OHLCV.
  - Resample daily → weekly (Mon-anchored) bars; IBD counts and measures bases on the weekly chart. Base-length counting needs the weekly close direction (`first week that closes down`), not just the high/low.
  - Left rim = a swing/pivot high on weekly bars with N left / N right bars. Base low = the minimum weekly low between the left rim and the right side. Depth = `(left_rim_high - base_low) / left_rim_high`.
  - Cup shape (U not V) needs a curvature test: fit a quadratic or compare the time spent within the bottom X% of the range against a V-shaped reference; a raw min/max pair cannot distinguish a U from a V.
  - Symmetry primitive: count down-closing weeks left of the base low vs up-closing weeks right of it, and compare their counts — IBD's symmetry rule is stated in *weeks*, not in price.
  - Handle: a second, later swing high (the right rim) followed by a short pullback. Handle depth = `(right_rim_high - handle_low) / right_rim_high`. Handle position test = `handle_low > base_low + 0.5 * (right_rim_high - base_low)` (upper half). Handle-vs-old-high test = `right_rim_high >= 0.85 * left_rim_high`.
  - Handle drift direction: linear regression slope over the handle's daily *lows* — IBD's rule is specifically "along its price lows", so fitting closes gets the wrong answer on a handle whose closes chop.
  - Volume dry-up: ratio of mean handle volume to mean base volume, and to the 50-day average. IBD names no threshold, so this is a *relative* primitive with a tunable, not a published rule.
  - Breakout: `close > pivot` (pivot = handle high + 0.10) with `volume >= 1.40 * SMA50(volume)`. The $0.10 is a nominal, unadjusted-dollar constant — on a $12 stock it is 0.8% and on a $600 stock it is 0.017%, so it does not scale and must not be converted to a percentage.
  - Buy zone: `pivot <= price <= pivot * 1.05`; "extended" is `price > pivot * 1.05`.
  - Weekly 10-week MA and daily 50-day MA for support tests; 40-week / 200-day for the long-term line.
  - **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone**: the prior-uptrend-of-30% rule is computable, but the base-counting gate that depends on it (see below) is *not* — IBD's handout says not to start counting bases until *"quarterly earnings and sales start growing by at least 25%"*, which requires fundamentals. Relative Strength Rating, Accumulation/Distribution Rating, Composite Rating, IBD's institutional-sponsorship screens, and the "excessive share offerings" disqualifier all require data outside OHLCV.
- **conflicts**:
  - Cup **minimum length**: IBD says 7 weeks in sources 1 and 2 (*"a minimum of seven weeks"*), but IBD's own cup-without-handle column says *"A cup can be as short as six weeks."* (source 6). Both recorded; not reconciled.
  - Cup **depth ceiling**: 33% (source 2, *"between 12% and 33%"*) vs 30–35% (source 6, *"no deeper than 30% to 35%"*) vs 15–30% (source 25 handout, *"15%-30%"*). Three different IBD-attributed ranges. All recorded.
  - **Handle depth**: IBD handout 10–12% (source 25) vs AAII's rendering of the pattern — *"not more than one-third of the cup's depth, but at most, one-half"* (source 28), which on a 30% cup permits a 10–15% handle and on a 12% cup permits only 4–6%. AAII's rule is *proportional*; IBD's is *absolute*. Both recorded.
  - **Handle length**: IBD publishes no minimum in anything fetched here. AAII: *"one to four weeks"* (source 28). Bulkowski: *"1 week minimum with no maximum, forming in the upper half of the cup"* (source 27). Three positions, recorded separately.
  - **Breakout volume**: IBD 40% above the 50-day average (source 11); IBD handout *"At least 40%-50% above average"* (source 25); AAII *"At breakout, the minimum volume typically looked for is that which is 50% above the 50-day average volume."* (source 28). **And IBD's own editorial contradicts its own threshold**: source 5 describes a valid breakout that *"cleared that buy point on a barely sufficient 38% increase in volume"* — 38% is below the published 40% floor and IBD called it sufficient.
  - **Breakout trigger level**: IBD triggers on the *handle* high + $0.10. AAII, describing the same pattern name, triggers on the cup rims: *"The breakout takes place when the price exceeds the highs of the left and right sides of the cup; most use the closing price as the relevant price value."* (source 28) — a materially higher trigger, and a *closing*-price trigger rather than an intraday one.
  - **Cup duration**: Bulkowski *"From 7 to 65 weeks (allow variations)"* (source 27) matches IBD's source-1 range exactly, which is unusual and suggests Bulkowski adopted O'Neil's bounds rather than measuring them independently.

## Cup without Handle (cup base)

- **origin / source_name**: IBD, "Cup Patterns Without Handles Can Still Go On To Be Winners" (source 6). Treated by IBD as a distinct entry case of the cup, not a separate base in the "seven bases" list.
- **definition**: The same U-shaped consolidation, but the stock takes out the left-side high without pausing to build a handle. Entry is therefore at the left rim rather than at a handle high.
- **criteria**:
  - base depth — no deeper than 30% to 35% — *"The correction in a cup should generally run no deeper than 30% to 35%."* (source 6) — confidence: high
  - base depth, bear market — as deep as 50% — *"In bear markets, cups can run as deep as 50%."* (source 6) — confidence: high
  - base length, minimum — 6 weeks — *"A cup can be as short as six weeks. Most outstanding cup patterns will last between three and six months."* (source 6) — confidence: high — **note this contradicts the 7-week minimum in sources 1 and 2**
  - depth relative to the index — 1.5x to 2.5x — *"It's normal for growth stocks to create cup patterns during intermediate declines in the general market and to correct 1-1/2 to 2-1/2 times the market averages."* (source 6) — confidence: high
  - pivot / buy point — left-side high + $0.10 — *"The [buy point] is 10 cents above the left-side high of the base."* † (source 6) — confidence: high on the rule, med on wording (extractor elided the linked term)
  - shape — rounded, U not V — the column *"emphasizes selecting patterns with 'rounded bottoms' and U-shaped formations rather than V-shaped ones"*; no numeric curvature threshold is published — value: null — `missing:` a published quantitative U-vs-V test (e.g. a maximum permitted fraction of the base's total decline occurring in any single week).
  - breakout volume — value: null in this column specifically; the column as fetched stated no volume percentage. `missing:` a volume threshold stated in the cup-without-handle column itself. The 40%-above-50-day rule from source 11 is IBD's general threshold but was stated for the flat base.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: V-shaped rather than rounded bottom; depth beyond 30–35% outside a bear market; asymmetric or jagged sides; taking out the left-side high on weak volume. IBD frames the no-handle entry as acceptable *"especially if the stock takes out the left-side high in heavy trade"* — i.e. the missing handle raises the volume bar rather than lowering it.
- **detection_notes**: Same primitives as the cup with handle, minus the handle block. The distinguishing test is negative: a cup whose right side reaches within some tolerance of the left rim *without* a qualifying pullback. That "without" is the hard part — it requires a pullback detector with an explicit minimum magnitude and duration so that a 2-day, 1.5% wobble is not classified as a handle. IBD publishes no such minimum, so the boundary between "cup with handle" and "cup without handle" is **not** determinable from published rules; it is a tunable. Buy point = weekly (or daily) left-rim swing high + 0.10. Everything here is daily-OHLCV computable.
- **conflicts**: IBD internal — 6-week minimum here vs 7-week minimum in sources 1 and 2; 30–35% depth ceiling here vs 12–33% in source 2 and 15–30% in the source-25 handout.

## Saucer with Handle (saucer base)

- **origin / source_name**: IBD, sources 7 and 8. Listed by IBD as one of the "seven bases" (source 4).
- **definition**: A longer, shallower, more gradual cousin of the cup — the decline is slow enough that the base flattens out and stretches, giving a saucer rather than a cup profile. IBD: *"the saucer is a shallower pattern than the cup."* (source 7). A handle may or may not form: *"Sometimes a stock will tack on a [handle] to its saucer base. Other times it's in a hurry, and the stock breaks out to [new highs] without sketching a handle."* † (source 7)
- **criteria**:
  - base length, minimum — 7 weeks — *"A proper saucer takes at least seven weeks to develop."* (source 7) — confidence: high
  - base length, range — 7 weeks to a year or more — *"Saucers form over a period of seven weeks to a year or more."* (source 8) — confidence: high
  - base depth, typical — about 12% to 20% — *"You might see a correction of about 12% to 20%, while a typical cup can run as deep as 35%."* (source 7) — confidence: high
  - base depth, maximum — up to 30% — *"they can correct up to 30%, or as much as a solid cup pattern."* (source 8) — confidence: high — **directly at odds with the 12–20% figure in source 7**
  - pivot / buy point with a handle — handle high + $0.10 — *"The buy point was 37.43, or 10 cents above the high of the handle."* (source 8) — confidence: high
  - handle volume — must be light, drifting lower — *"The handle drifted lower in light volume, a bullish sign."* (source 8) — confidence: high
  - symmetry — weeks left = weeks right — *"Altria's base showed nice symmetry. The number of weeks on the left side of the pattern equaled the number of weeks on the right side."* (source 8) — confidence: high (stated as praise of one example, not as a hard rule)
  - chart timeframe — weekly or monthly — *"saucer bases can be so long that they're only visible on a weekly or monthly chart."* (source 8) — confidence: high
  - handle depth / handle length / handle position — value: null — no saucer-specific handle geometry is published in either column. `missing:` whether the cup-with-handle handle rules (upper half, 10–12%, within 15% of the old high) transfer to a saucer, and if so unchanged.
  - breakout volume — value: null — IBD explicitly softens the rule here rather than stating one: *"Sometimes volume can kick in a bit late with a [breakout], but the move still works out."* † (source 7). `missing:` a stated saucer breakout volume threshold.
- **measured_performance**: none published. IBD asserts without measurement that saucers are *"capable of producing strong gains similar to shorter patterns such as the cup-with-handle and flat bases."* (source 8) — an equivalence claim with no sample, no period and no comparison statistic behind it.
- **invalidation**: Shorter than seven weeks; a decline too abrupt to be a saucer (which pushes it into cup classification, not into failure); depth beyond the 30% ceiling of source 8. No faulty-saucer taxonomy is published the way faulty handles are for the cup.
- **detection_notes**: Weekly (and for the longest cases monthly) resampling is mandatory — IBD says outright the structure is invisible on shorter timeframes, so a daily-bar-only detector will systematically miss saucers, not merely mis-score them. Primitives: long-lookback pivot high (left rim), minimum over a 7-to-52+ week window, depth ratio, and a **flatness/curvature discriminator** to separate saucer from cup. The natural discriminator is the ratio of depth to duration (a saucer is shallow-and-long, a cup is deep-and-short) or the second derivative of a quadratic fit — but IBD publishes no cutoff, so the saucer/cup boundary is a tunable, not a rule. Symmetry primitive: week count left of the low vs week count right of it. Handle block as per the cup, if present. All daily-OHLCV computable given weekly resampling; nothing here needs fundamentals.
- **conflicts**: **IBD against itself, in the same base name**: source 7 says *"about 12% to 20%"* and source 8 says *"up to 30%, or as much as a solid cup pattern."* Both recorded verbatim above; a detector cannot satisfy both, and averaging them would produce a number neither column published.

## Double Bottom (the "W")

- **origin / source_name**: IBD, sources 9 and 10; also sources 1, 25, 26. Listed among the "seven bases" (source 4).
- **definition**: Two down legs forming a W, where the second leg dips below the first, shaking out holders who bought the first low, with the buy point at the middle peak of the W. IBD: *"The double-bottom consists of two down legs."* (source 9)
- **criteria**:
  - base length, minimum — 7 weeks — *"Double bottoms must also be at least seven weeks in length."* (source 1) — confidence: high
  - second leg must undercut the first — required — *"The low of the second leg should fall slightly beneath the low of the first leg."* (source 9) — confidence: high
  - undercut magnitude, tolerated — 5% to 10% — *"Undercutting the first low by 5% to 10% or so is also acceptable."* (source 9) — confidence: high
  - pivot / buy point — middle peak of the W + $0.10 — *"The [buy point] will be located at the top right side of the second down leg, and should equal the middle peak of the W plus a dime."* † (source 9); corroborated in source 10: *"In a double bottom, the ideal time to buy shares is when the stock crosses 10 cents above the middle peak between the two lows."* — confidence: high
  - handle variant — buy point moves to the handle — *"As with a cup base, a [handle] can form on a [double bottom]. If that turns out to be the case, the buy point would then be 10 cents above the top of the handle."* † (source 9) — confidence: high
  - early entry ("shakeout plus three") — first low + 3 points — *"Add three points to the price of the first low."* (source 10) — confidence: high
  - early entry, high-priced stocks — first low + at least 6 points — *"When the stock is higher-priced, say 80 to 100 a share, add at least six points to the first low."* (source 10), with IBD's stated rationale *"to keep the 'plus three' part of the pattern at a reasonable proportion to the base."* — confidence: high
  - base depth, maximum — **two incompatible published values**: *"Max 30%"* (source 26 handout) and *"40% or less"* (source 25 handout) — confidence: med on both, and they cannot both be the rule
  - prior uptrend — 30% or more — *"30% or more"* (source 25) — confidence: med
  - breakout volume — surge required, no percentage stated in the column — value: null in source 9: *"Whether a stock breaks out from a handle or the top of the right side, look for a surge in [volume] as it clears the entry."* † `missing:` a double-bottom-specific volume percentage. Source 25 gives *"At least 40%-50% above average"* at med confidence.
  - buy zone — up to 5% above the pivot — *"up to 5% above the ideal buy point"* (source 25) — confidence: med for this base specifically, high as IBD's general rule (source 4)
  - relative frequency — less common than the cup — *"It tends to occur less frequently than the cup base"* (source 9) — no count or rate is published — value: null — `missing:` a published frequency ratio.
- **measured_performance**: none published. Source 9's only number is a single named example (SolarWinds, breakout "the last week of October 2011", then *"140% over the next 11 months"*) — n=1, no benchmark, not a statistic.
- **invalidation**: A second low that does **not** undercut the first (this is the defining feature, not a preference); an undercut far beyond 5–10%; a first pullback that is too shallow — source 5 flags exactly this in Regeneron: *"first pullback appears too shallow"*, with no numeric threshold given for "too shallow"; fewer than seven weeks; late-stage.
- **detection_notes**: Daily-OHLCV computable. Primitives: two swing lows (pivot lows with left/right bar confirmation) separated by an intervening swing high; `low2 < low1` with `(low1 - low2) / low1 <= 0.10` for the tolerated-undercut band; middle peak = the max high between the two lows; pivot = middle_peak_high + 0.10; base start = the pre-base swing high; depth = `(base_start_high - min(low1, low2)) / base_start_high`; weekly resample for the 7-week minimum. The optional handle rides on the right side after the middle peak is exceeded and reuses the cup handle primitives. **Trap for the implementer:** the "plus three points" and "plus six points" early entries are absolute dollars, not percentages, and IBD explicitly ties the choice of 3 vs 6 to the stock's *price level* ("80 to 100 a share") — so this rule is price-level-dependent and does not survive a naive percentage conversion. Also note the two published prices IBD names (80 and 100) do not define a function, only two sample points, so anything between and beyond them is undefined. Nothing here needs fundamentals except the prior-uptrend/base-count gates.
- **conflicts**: **Depth ceiling within IBD-derived material**: 30% (source 26) vs 40% (source 25). Also, the classic technical-analysis "double bottom" (Bulkowski, Edwards & Magee) requires the two lows to be at *roughly the same price* and treats an undercut as a flaw; IBD **requires** the undercut (*"should fall slightly beneath"*). Same pattern name, opposite requirement on the defining feature. Recorded, not reconciled.

## Flat Base

- **origin / source_name**: IBD, sources 11, 12, 13; also sources 1, 25, 26. One of the "seven bases" (source 4).
- **definition**: A tight sideways consolidation, shallow by construction, that typically forms *after* a prior breakout as a later-stage rest. IBD: *"a flat base consists of a stock price decline of less than 15%"* (source 12), and it *"tend[s] to form as a second or third base in a stock's major advance."* (source 11)
- **criteria**:
  - base length, minimum — 5 weeks — *"It needs at least five weeks to form. Shorter patterns are not adequate enough to flush out weak or impatient shareholders."* (source 11), corroborated *"they must take shape over a minimum of five weeks"* (source 13) — confidence: high
  - base depth, maximum — 15% — *"The stock's price declines no more than 15% from its intraday peak to intraday trough."* (source 11); *"The correction within a flat base should be no more than 15% from its intraday high to the bottom of the pattern."* (source 13); *"a flat base consists of a stock price decline of less than 15%"* (source 12) — confidence: high
  - base depth, alternate published band — 10% to 15% — *"Still, look for consolidation areas that are at least five weeks long and fall no more than 10% to 15%."* (source 1) — confidence: high; this is a *tighter* ceiling than the plain 15%
  - depth is measured intraday-to-intraday — high-to-low, not close-to-close — *"from its intraday peak to intraday trough"* (source 11) — confidence: high — this is one of the few places IBD is explicit about which price series to measure
  - how weeks are counted — from the first down week — *"Usually, start counting with the first down week on a weekly chart."* (source 11) — confidence: high
  - pivot / buy point — highest price in the pattern + $0.10 — *"The optimal [buy point] is determined by finding the highest price in the pattern and adding 10 cents to it."* † (source 11); *"To get the proper buy point, add 10 cents to the highest price in the pattern"* (source 13); *"The typical buy point is the price high made before the decline, plus 10 cents."* (source 12) — confidence: high
  - breakout volume — at least 40% above the 50-day average — *"the stock should clear the buy point in at least 40% above its 50-day average."* † (source 11) — confidence: high on the number, med on wording
  - base stage — usually second or third, rarely first — *"Flat bases tend to form as a second or third base in a stock's major advance, following a [cup-with-handle], or another, larger base. Rarely, flat bases are the first base."* † (source 11) — confidence: high
  - trigger for the flat base to begin — a prior advance of 20%+ — *"usually occurs after a stock has advanced 20% or more off a cup with handle pattern"* (source 30, third-party summary of O'Neil's book, **not** IBD editorial) — confidence: low
  - tightness — required, unquantified — *"Flat bases should show tight trading."* (source 3) — value: null — `missing:` a published numeric tightness measure (e.g. a maximum average weekly range as a percentage, or a maximum weekly close-to-close dispersion).
  - support behavior — 10-week line — *"The 10-week moving average served as a support level."* (source 12, describing an example) — confidence: high as an observation, not published as a requirement
- **measured_performance**: none published. Source 11's example (Tractor Supply, *"correcting 14% over eight weeks before breaking out on high volume"*) and source 12's (Herbalife, *"the correction in the base was only 12%"*) are single instances used to illustrate the depth rule, not performance data.
- **invalidation**: A decline deeper than 15% intraday-to-intraday (this converts it to a different base type, not merely a weak flat base); fewer than five weeks (*"Shorter patterns are not adequate enough to flush out weak or impatient shareholders."*); loose rather than tight trading; late-stage. Note that "rarely the first base" is a *frequency* statement, not a disqualifier — a first-stage flat base is unusual, not invalid.
- **detection_notes**: Fully daily-OHLCV computable and the cheapest of all the IBD bases to detect. Primitives: weekly resample; base high = max of daily `high` over the window; base low = min of daily `low`; depth = `(base_high - base_low) / base_high <= 0.15`; window length >= 5 weeks, counted from the first down-closing week; pivot = `base_high + 0.10`; breakout = `close > pivot and volume >= 1.40 * SMA50(volume)`. The depth rule is explicitly intraday-extreme based, so using weekly closes will under-measure depth and admit bases that IBD would reject. A "tightness" score (mean weekly `(high-low)/close`, or the standard deviation of weekly closes normalized by price) is *required in practice* but is a tunable, since IBD publishes no threshold for it. Base-stage classification requires the base count, which requires the prior breakout history (computable) and, per the source-25 handout, quarterly earnings growth (**not** computable from OHLCV).
- **conflicts**: IBD internal — plain 15% ceiling (sources 11, 12, 13) vs *"no more than 10% to 15%"* (source 1). Third-party O'Neil summary gives *"does not correct more than 10% to 15%"* (source 30), matching source 1 rather than IBD's own more common 15%.

## Ascending Base

- **origin / source_name**: William J. O'Neil, quoted directly by IBD in source 14; MarketSmith (William O'Neil + Co.) in source 23. One of the "seven bases" (source 4).
- **definition**: A three-step staircase that forms in the middle of an existing advance, in which the stock is repeatedly knocked back by general-market weakness but each setback bottoms higher than the last. O'Neil, quoted verbatim by IBD: *"Ascending bases ... occur midway along a move up after a stock has broken out of a cup-with-handle or double-bottom base."* (source 14)
- **criteria**:
  - number of pullbacks — exactly three — *"There are three healthy price pullbacks of 10% to 20%, with each low higher than the preceding one."* (source 14) — confidence: high
  - pullback depth — 10% to 20% each — same quote as above (source 14); corroborated *"three 10-20% pullbacks"* (source 23) — confidence: high
  - higher lows — required — *"with each low higher than the preceding one"* (source 14) — confidence: high
  - higher highs — required, three of them, each a new high — *"The base shows three advances to new highs, with each high rising above the previous high."* (source 14) — confidence: high
  - base length — 9 to 16 weeks — *"Ascending bases are shaped by three pullbacks with higher highs and higher lows over a nine- to 16-week span."* (source 1); corroborated by IBD's example commentary *"within the usual time range of nine to 16 weeks"* (source 14) and by MarketSmith *"usually lasts for 9-16 weeks"* (source 23) — confidence: high
  - placement in the advance — midway, after a prior base breakout — O'Neil quote above (source 14); MarketSmith adds a number: *"midway along a move up after a stock has run up at least 20% off an earlier base"* (source 23) — confidence: high for the placement, med for the 20%
  - cause of the pullbacks — general-market declines — *"Each of the pullbacks usually occurs because the general market is declining at that time."* (source 14) — confidence: high
  - pivot / buy point — high from which the third pullback began + $0.10 — *"when the stock price rises ＄0.1 higher than the high point from which the third pull back began"* (source 23) — confidence: med (MarketSmith, O'Neil-affiliated, not IBD editorial)
  - breakout volume — at least 40% above average — *"increased volume at least 40% larger than average"* (source 23) — confidence: med
- **measured_performance**: none published. IBD's column illustrates with Titanium Metals (*"sketched an ascending base that lasted 15 weeks"*) — n=1.
- **invalidation**: Fewer or more than three pullbacks; any pullback deeper than 20% or shallower than 10%; a lower low or a failure to make a new high on any of the three advances; a duration outside 9–16 weeks; occurring at the *start* rather than midway through an advance (an ascending base is by construction never a first-stage base — it requires a prior base breakout). O'Neil's causal claim (pullbacks driven by market declines) is an explanation, not a testable disqualifier.
- **detection_notes**: Daily-OHLCV computable, and the most mechanically specifiable of all the IBD bases — it is a strict alternation with monotonicity constraints, which makes it the best candidate for a precise detector.
  - Primitives: an alternating sequence of pivot highs and pivot lows (with left/right bar confirmation) over a 9–16 week window; three (high, low) pairs; assert `high1 < high2 < high3` and `low1 < low2 < low3`; assert each `(high_i - low_i) / high_i` in `[0.10, 0.20]`.
  - Pivot = `high3 + 0.10` where `high3` is the high from which the *third* pullback began — note this is the high *before* the third low, not the highest point of the structure after it.
  - Weekly resample for the 9–16 week duration test; the pullback depths should be measured on daily extremes.
  - The "midway along a move up" and the MarketSmith "at least 20% off an earlier base" conditions require the prior base's pivot, so this base cannot be detected in isolation — it needs the base-count/breakout history chain.
  - **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone**: nothing structural, but the "each pullback occurs because the general market is declining" attribution requires index data (cheap to add — a market index series — but it is a second data source, and O'Neil states it as a cause, never as a required condition, so railing on it would over-refuse).
- **conflicts**: None found. This is the only base in the taxonomy where every fetched source — IBD editorial, IBD's O'Neil quote, MarketSmith, and the base-counting column — gives identical numbers (3 pullbacks, 10–20%, 9–16 weeks). Notable by contrast with the cup and the saucer.

## High, Tight Flag

- **origin / source_name**: William J. O'Neil / IBD, sources 15 and 16; also source 1. One of the "seven bases" (source 4).
- **definition**: A near-vertical doubling (the flagpole) followed by a brief, shallow, tight sideways consolidation (the flag), from which the stock resumes. IBD calls it rare and explicitly high-risk.
- **criteria**:
  - flagpole gain — 100% to 120% — *"The stock begins by climbing 100% to 120% in four to eight weeks."* (source 15); *"a stock must gain 100% to 120% in a span of four to eight weeks"* (source 16); *"The flagpole is shaped by a rapid run-up of 100% to 120% in only four to eight weeks."* (source 1) — confidence: high
  - flagpole duration — 4 to 8 weeks — same three quotes above — confidence: high
  - flag depth — 10% to 25% — *"Next, the stock corrects 10% to 25% in three to five weeks."* (source 15); source 16 renders the same rule differently: *"usually 10% to 20% or sometimes 25%"* — confidence: high
  - flag duration — 3 to 5 weeks — *"in three to five weeks"* (source 15); *"in the next three to five weeks"* (source 16); *"The flag then forms with three to five weeks of tight sideways trading."* (source 1) — confidence: high
  - flag character — tight sideways — *"three to five weeks of tight sideways trading"* (source 1) — value: null on the tightness measure — `missing:` a numeric definition of "tight" for the flag (a maximum weekly range, or a maximum close-to-close dispersion).
  - flag volume — dries up — *"volume generally dries up. Big fund managers are holding the stock"* (source 16) — confidence: high on the direction, no percentage published
  - pivot / buy point — the peak of the flagpole — *"when the stock clears the peak of the flagpole in big turnover"* (source 16) — confidence: high. Note this column does **not** state the +$0.10; the third-party O'Neil summary does: *"the high of the pattern plus 10 cents"* (source 30) — confidence: low for the dime on this base specifically.
  - breakout volume — at least 40% above average — *"meaning daily volume that's at least 40% above average"* (source 16) — confidence: high
  - risk characterization — high-risk, many fail — *"High, tight flags are high-risk bases. Many fail."* (source 16) — value: null on the failure rate — `missing:` the number behind "many". IBD publishes the warning without the statistic.
  - upside claim — 200%+ — O'Neil, quoted by IBD: *"Many stocks can skyrocket 200% or more off this pattern."* (source 15) — this is an **assertion with no sample, no period and no rate**; "many" is not a frequency.
- **measured_performance**: **none published as a statistic.** IBD publishes only worked examples, all winners, which is a selection-biased set by construction. From source 15, on Taser International: *"In July 2003, Taser broke out and advanced about 98% in four weeks."*; *"The stock corrected 24% in just under three weeks."*; *"In mid-September 2003, Taser cleared the pattern's 27.07 buy point and advanced more than 150% in seven weeks."*; *"After the more than 150% rise, a new 25%-deep flag formed."*; *"Taser cleared the pattern at 69.45 in late November, and this time there was no hitch in the advance...rose more than 450% in five months."*; *"An investor who caught both rides could've bagged a more than 1,300% gain in seven months."* Source 16 names Google (2004), Qualcomm (1999), JinkoSolar (2010), NQ Mobile (2013), JDS Uniphase (1999) and American Dairy (2009) as instances, again all as successes. **These are anecdotes with n=1 each and no base rate.** IBD's own text says "Many fail" — so the published examples are, by IBD's own admission, drawn from the surviving tail. Do not treat the 200%/450%/1,300% figures as expectancy.
- **invalidation**: A flagpole gain below 100% or built over more than 8 weeks; a flag correction deeper than 25%; a flag that runs longer than 5 weeks (it becomes a different, deeper base); loose rather than tight flag action; volume that does not dry up in the flag; a breakout on under-40% volume. IBD attaches an unusual meta-invalidation: it labels the whole pattern high-risk rather than labelling individual instances faulty.
- **detection_notes**: Daily-OHLCV computable, and the flagpole leg is trivially so.
  - Flagpole: over a rolling 4–8 week (20–40 trading day) window, `(high_end / low_start) - 1 >= 1.00` and `<= 1.20`. The bounds are on the *rise*, so `low_start` must be the run's origin low, not an arbitrary window edge — a swing-low anchor is required or the detector will fire on any 40-day window inside a longer trend.
  - Flag: from the flagpole peak, a 3–5 week (15–25 day) consolidation with `(pole_high - flag_low) / pole_high` in `[0.10, 0.25]`; tightness via mean daily `(high-low)/close` or the width of the flag's high/low channel relative to the pole; volume ratio of the flag window vs the pole window (must be < 1, no published threshold).
  - Pivot = the flagpole peak (source 16's rule). If the +$0.10 is adopted, flag it as coming from a third-party summary, not from IBD editorial.
  - Breakout: `volume >= 1.40 * SMA50(volume)`.
  - **Rarity is the operational problem, not detection.** IBD calls the pattern rare; a screener that emits high-tight flags at any meaningful daily rate is almost certainly mis-detecting, and the natural failure mode is a loose flagpole anchor. A rate check against IBD's own characterization ("rare") is the cheapest sanity rail, and it needs no extra data.
  - Nothing here requires fundamentals or sponsorship data.
- **conflicts**: IBD internal, minor — flag depth *"10% to 25%"* (source 15) vs *"usually 10% to 20% or sometimes 25%"* (source 16). Both recorded. The buy-point definition also differs by source: IBD editorial says the flagpole peak with no dime (source 16); the third-party O'Neil summary says pattern high plus 10 cents (source 30).

## Base on Base

- **origin / source_name**: IBD, source 18 (definition tied to the base count) and source 4 (listed among the "seven bases"); MarketSmith India, source 24. This is the one IBD "base" that is defined by its *relationship to a prior base*, not by its own shape.
- **definition**: A second base that forms directly on top of a first, because the breakout from the first failed to produce a full advance — typically because the general market turned down. IBD's definition is arithmetic, not geometric: *"If the gain is less than 20% and the stock forms another base, it's a base-on-base pattern and counted as one stage."* (source 18)
- **criteria**:
  - defining condition — prior breakout gained less than 20% — *"A breakout needs to produce a gain of at least 20% in order to be counted as one stage."* / *"If the gain is less than 20% and the stock forms another base, it's a base-on-base pattern and counted as one stage."* (source 18) — confidence: high
  - base-count treatment — the pair counts as ONE stage — same quote (source 18) — confidence: high; corroborated by the handout *"If a stock advances less than 20%, then forms another base, it's all counted as one consolidation"* (source 25)
  - second base depth — ideally 10–15%, rarely over 20% — *"Shallow depth (ideally 10–15%, rarely more than 20%)."* (source 24, MarketSmith India, O'Neil-affiliated but not IBD editorial) — confidence: med
  - how far the second base may sink into the first — value: null — no fetched source states this verbatim with a number. IBD's rule as summarized (not as a quote I obtained) is that the second base's low may slip into the first but ideally not by much. `missing:` a published maximum overlap, expressed either as a percentage of the first base's range or as a price level (e.g. "must stay above the first base's midpoint").
  - support — 10-week line — *"The best Base-on-Base patterns have clear support, often near the 10-week line."* (source 24) — confidence: med
  - volume — contraction in the base, spikes on up days — *"Volume contraction during the base, followed by spikes on up days."* (source 24) — confidence: med, no percentages
  - entry — clearing the second base on above-average volume — *"Enter as the stock clears the second base on above-average volume."* (source 24) — confidence: med. Note "above-average", not the 40% figure IBD uses elsewhere.
  - minimum weeks for each of the two bases — value: null — no fetched source publishes a duration rule for the base-on-base as such. `missing:` whether each constituent base must independently satisfy its own type's minimum (5 weeks flat, 7 weeks cup), or whether the combined structure has its own minimum.
  - stop — 7–8% — *"Use O'Neil's 7–8% stop-loss rule."* (source 24) — confidence: med; IBD editorial states it as *"Get out if a stock drops 8% below your buy point."* (source 4) — confidence: high
- **measured_performance**: none published. Source 24's headline calls it *"The Silent Power Pattern of Market Leaders"* with no supporting statistic. IBD's source 18 mentions Ambarella (AMBA) as an instance with no outcome figure.
- **invalidation**: The prior breakout produced 20% or more (then it is not a base-on-base at all — it is two separate stages); a second base that sinks materially into the first (unquantified, see the `missing:` note above); a second base that is deep rather than shallow.
- **detection_notes**: Daily-OHLCV computable, but **only as a stateful, sequential detector** — it cannot be recognized from a single window of bars. Required: the pivot of the previous base, the maximum price achieved after that pivot, and then the geometry of the new base. Primitives: `prior_pivot`; `max_close_after_prior_pivot`; test `(max_after - prior_pivot) / prior_pivot < 0.20`; then run the normal flat/cup detector on the subsequent window and test that the new base's low sits above (or not far below) the prior base's high. Weekly resampling for the base geometry; 10-week MA for the support test. The 20% test is on the *gain from the pivot*, not on the gain from the base low — using the low inflates it and will silently reclassify base-on-bases as separate stages. This base's whole function is bookkeeping: it exists to stop the base count from incrementing, so a detector that finds the shape but does not feed the count has implemented half of it.
- **conflicts**: MarketSmith India's entry volume rule (*"above-average volume"*) is looser than IBD's 40%-above-50-day standard used for every other base. Both recorded. No numeric conflict on the 20% rule itself — it is the most consistently stated number in the entire taxonomy.

## Square Box

- **origin / source_name**: William J. O'Neil, *How to Make Money in Stocks*, 4th edition (2009) — IBD states the provenance explicitly: O'Neil *"first explained the pattern in the fourth edition of his book 'How to Make Money in Stocks' in 2009."* (source 17). IBD calls it *"a relatively new pattern among the lineup of bases defined by IBD"*. It is **absent** from IBD's own "seven bases" list in source 4.
- **definition**: A short, shallow, boxy sideways consolidation that forms after a stock has already moved up out of an earlier base. IBD: *"Note how the chart action has a square, boxy look."* (source 17)
- **criteria**:
  - base length — 4 weeks minimum, 7 weeks maximum — *"The square box just takes four weeks to develop and does not exceed seven weeks."* (source 17) — confidence: high. This is the **only IBD base with a published maximum duration**; every other base has a floor and no ceiling.
  - base depth, maximum — 15% — *"With a square box, a stock corrects no more than 15%."* (source 17) — confidence: high
  - pivot / buy point — the structure's high + $0.10 — *"You get the buy point by adding a dime to Deere's 79.66 high reached on Nov. 9, 2010, or 79.76."* (source 17) — confidence: high (stated through a worked example, and the arithmetic is shown)
  - tilt — sideways preferred over up or down — value: null on any angle threshold. IBD's characterization is *"square, boxy look"* (source 17). `missing:` a published maximum slope for the box's highs or lows.
  - support — 10-week moving average — *"The stock found support at its 10-week moving average as it consolidated."* (source 17) — confidence: high as an observation of the example, not stated as a requirement
  - breakout volume — above average, no percentage stated — *"Volume came in above average that week, even if it dipped slightly from the prior week's level."* (source 17) — value: null on a percentage — `missing:` a square-box-specific volume threshold. Note IBD accepted a breakout week whose volume *fell* versus the prior week, provided it was above average — a materially weaker bar than the 40%-above-50-day rule.
  - placement — after a prior base breakout, typically late-stage — IBD's own example is graded down for this: *"This base probably is best seen as a third-stage pattern, meaning it was actually a riskier, later-stage structure."* (source 17) — confidence: high
- **measured_performance**: none published. Source 17's Deere example (*"consolidated for four weeks, just meeting the time requirement"*) is n=1, and IBD itself grades the example as a riskier third-stage base.
- **invalidation**: Shorter than four weeks or longer than seven (**the only IBD base you can invalidate by being too long**); a correction deeper than 15%; a pronounced upward or downward tilt rather than boxy sideways action; late-stage placement raises risk without formally disqualifying it.
- **detection_notes**: Daily-OHLCV computable and mechanically the simplest base in the taxonomy — it is a flat base with a hard duration ceiling.
  - Primitives: weekly resample; window length in `[4, 7]` weeks; depth = `(max(high) - min(low)) / max(high) <= 0.15`; pivot = `max(high) + 0.10`.
  - The "boxy" / no-tilt requirement needs a slope test on the weekly highs and on the weekly lows (two linear fits, both near zero) — but with **no published threshold**, so it is a tunable. A `flat` classification that only checks depth will admit tilted structures IBD would not call a square box, and — because a 15% depth cap over a 7-week window is a very common shape — the tilt test is what does most of the discriminating work here. Without it, this detector will fire constantly.
  - The four-to-seven-week window overlaps the flat base's five-week-minimum window, so square box and flat base are **not mutually exclusive** on published rules alone. A 5-, 6- or 7-week sub-15% consolidation satisfies both definitions. Precedence between them is not published — `missing:` IBD's tiebreak.
  - Base-stage grading requires the base count (see below).
- **conflicts**: Internal to IBD's own material: the square box does not appear in IBD's "seven bases" enumeration (source 4) even though IBD publishes a dedicated column defining it (source 17). Its breakout volume standard as demonstrated (above average, down week-on-week) is weaker than the 40%-above-50-day standard IBD applies to the flat base (source 11) — a base of essentially the same shape and depth.

## Pivot / buy point, the 5% buy zone, and "extended"

- **origin / source_name**: IBD across sources 4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 17, 22, 25.
- **definition**: The pivot (buy point) is the specific price at which the base's overhead supply is cleared. It is defined per base type, always as a *named structural high plus ten cents*, and buying is permitted only in a narrow band above it.
- **criteria**:
  - the dime — $0.10 above the structural high, on every base — cup-with-handle *"10 cents above the peak in the handle"* (source 25); cup-without-handle *"10 cents above the left-side high of the base"* † (source 6); double bottom *"the middle peak of the W plus a dime"* † (source 9); flat base *"add 10 cents to the highest price in the pattern"* (source 13); saucer *"10 cents above the high of the handle"* (source 8); square box *"adding a dime to Deere's 79.66 high"* (source 17) — confidence: high
  - buy zone — 5% above the pivot — *"Ideally, the stock will quickly clear the 5% buy zone and create a cushion of gains."* (source 4); *"up to 5% above the ideal buy point"* (source 25) — confidence: high
  - "extended" — value: null in IBD editorial fetched here — no column I reached defines the word "extended" in a quotable sentence with its number. The operative rule is the 5% zone above; "extended" is its complement. `missing:` an IBD sentence that states the extended threshold in its own words. Note: source 22, IBD's own selling column, contains **no** mention of the 5% zone or of "extended" at all.
  - breakout volume — at least 40% above the 50-day average — *"the stock should clear the buy point in at least 40% above its 50-day average."* † (source 11); *"at least a 40% increase above the stock's average daily volume"* (source 26); *"At least 40%-50% above average"* (source 25) — confidence: high on 40%, med on whether the ceiling phrasing is 40% or 50%
  - stop loss — 8% below the buy point — *"Get out if a stock drops 8% below your buy point."* (source 4) — confidence: high
  - profit target — 20% to 25% — *"A gain of 20% to 25% from the proper entry point is an attractive profit-taking target for most growth stocks."* (source 22) — confidence: high
  - eight-week hold exception — 20%+ in three weeks or less — *"any stock that surges that fast in three weeks or less should be held at least eight weeks."* (source 22) — confidence: high
  - climax-top warning — 25% to 50%+ in three weeks or less — *"After a prolonged advance, shares soar 25% to 50% or more in three weeks or less. That's the hallmark of a climax top."* (source 22) — confidence: high
- **measured_performance**: none published for the pivot rule itself, the 5% zone, the 8% stop or the 20–25% target. IBD publishes these as discipline, never as backtested edges, and states no sample behind any of them.
- **invalidation**: Buying above pivot × 1.05.
- **detection_notes**: `pivot = structural_high + 0.10`; `in_buy_zone = pivot <= price <= pivot * 1.05`; `extended = price > pivot * 1.05`. The `structural_high` differs per base and must come from the base detector, not from a generic "recent high". The $0.10 is unadjusted nominal dollars and does not survive a split adjustment — a historical bar series adjusted for splits will place the dime at the wrong relative distance for every pre-split base, which quietly shifts the pivot. IBD's own square-box example shows this: it prices JDS Uniphase's buy point *"adjusted for two 2-for-1 stock splits"*, i.e. IBD adjusts the *level* but the dime was applied at the unadjusted price. Everything else here is daily-OHLCV computable.
- **conflicts**: The 40% breakout volume floor is contradicted by IBD's own editorial judgment in source 5, which describes a breakout on *"a barely sufficient 38% increase in volume"* as valid — 38% < 40%. AAII sets the same threshold at 50% (source 28). Bulkowski's cup-with-handle page publishes no volume identification guideline at all.

## Market direction gate and the Follow-Through Day

- **origin / source_name**: William J. O'Neil / IBD. The Follow-Through Day is O'Neil's; the Market Pulse is IBD's daily publication of it. Sources 19, 20, 21, 3; third-party test in source 29.
- **definition**: The "M" of CAN SLIM. No base breakout is taken unless the general market is in a confirmed uptrend, and an uptrend is confirmed by a Follow-Through Day — a strong, higher-volume advance by a major index on Day 4 or later of a rally attempt off a low.
- **criteria**:
  - why the gate exists — three of four stocks follow the market — *"You really only want to be buying stocks while the market is rising, because three out of four stocks tend to follow the general trend."* (source 21); *"three of four stocks tend to follow the market's trend"* (source 20) — confidence: high on the claim, but this is an **assertion**: no sample size, no period, no methodology and no base rate is published anywhere I could reach
  - the three market states — *"market in confirmed uptrend, uptrend under pressure and market in correction"* (source 20) — confidence: high
  - hard prerequisite for any buy — *"Make sure the market is in a confirmed uptrend."* (source 3) — confidence: high
  - what counts as a correction — a 3% to 5%+ drop from the recent high — *"a drop of 3% to 5% or more from the indexes' recent high"* (source 20) — confidence: high
  - rally attempt, Day 1 — the first higher close after a sell-off — *"After a long, hard sell-off in the market, watch first for a higher close."* (source 19) — confidence: high
  - earliest valid follow-through — Day 4 — *"The follow-through signal takes place on Day 4 or thereafter of the rally attempt."* (source 19) — confidence: high
  - best window — Days 4 through 7 — *"Generally, the best follow-throughs take place on Days 4 through 7 of a rally attempt."* (source 19) — confidence: high
  - required index gain — at least 1% to 1.25% — *"Generally, the gain should be at least 1% to 1.25%."* (source 19) — confidence: high — **this is materially lower than the 1.7% figure widely attributed to IBD**, and lower than the 1.5%–2% often quoted by third parties
  - volume requirement — higher than the prior session, average not required — *"Volume must rise vs. the prior session; it doesn't need to be above average."* (source 19) — confidence: high. This is the rule most often mis-stated elsewhere as requiring above-average volume.
  - which index — value: null — source 19 as fetched does not restrict the signal to a named index. `missing:` IBD's published list of qualifying indexes and whether a follow-through on one index alone confirms.
  - distribution-day threshold that flips the state back — value: null — *"Too many distribution days can add up to an eventual market correction"* (source 20) states the mechanism without the count. `missing:` the number of distribution days, and the rolling window over which they are counted, and how they expire.
  - late follow-throughs — permitted — IBD cites *"a Day 15 follow-through on Jan. 8"* that preceded a *"15-year high by April"* (source 19) — confidence: high, so the Days 4–7 window is a preference, not a boundary
  - historical claim — *"Since the early 1900s, no bull market has ever started without a follow-through day."* (source 19) — confidence: high that IBD published it, but this is a **necessary-condition claim with no false-positive rate attached**, and IBD immediately concedes *"The follow-through signal isn't perfect. Nothing is."* A signal that fires on every bull market start tells you nothing about how often it fires when no bull market follows, and IBD publishes no such count.
- **measured_performance**: **IBD publishes none.** The only measured test found is third-party: Quantifiable Edges (source 29) tested Dec 1971 through Jan 11 2008 (37 years) and found *"35 of 64 possible Follow Through Days were successful for a success rate of 54.7%"* at a 1% threshold, and *"29 of 52 possible Follow Through Days being labeled 'successful'. This equals a 55.7% success rate"* at a 1.7% threshold. **That study publishes no base rate** — no random-entry or all-days control — so 54.7% is not comparable to anything, and in particular is not demonstrated to beat a coin flip or a buy-and-hold control over the same period. The study author contrasts his figure with an IBD-claimed 70–80% success rate but does not source that IBD claim to a document, and I could not locate an IBD publication of it. Do not carry the 70–80% number forward.
- **invalidation**: A follow-through that occurs before Day 4; an advance on lower volume than the prior session; a rally attempt that undercuts its Day 1 low before the follow-through arrives (widely stated, but **I found no verbatim IBD sentence for it in this session** — `missing:` IBD's own wording of the undercut-resets-the-count rule).
- **detection_notes**: Computable from daily index OHLCV — which is a **second data series** beyond the stock's own bars, and is the one part of the O'Neil framework that no per-symbol detector can produce.
  - Primitives: for a major index, detect a sell-off low; Day 1 = the first higher close after it; count sessions from Day 1; on Day >= 4, test `(close / prev_close) - 1 >= 0.010` (or 0.0125 for the upper bound of IBD's stated band) **and** `volume > prev_volume`.
  - Note the volume test is a strict comparison against the *prior session*, not against a moving average — implementing it as `volume > SMA50(volume)` is wrong in IBD's own words and will both miss valid follow-throughs and admit invalid ones.
  - The gain test is on the index close-to-close percentage; the band 1% to 1.25% is stated as a minimum with a range, not as two thresholds, so it needs a single chosen value plus a note that IBD publishes a band.
  - **Not computable at all from published rules**: the distribution-day count that ends a confirmed uptrend. IBD publishes the mechanism and not the number, so the "uptrend under pressure" and "market in correction" transitions cannot be reproduced from the sources reached here. Anyone implementing the Market Pulse states will be inventing that threshold, not deriving it.
- **conflicts**: **The required index gain is the sharpest conflict in this entire document.** IBD's own column says *"at least 1% to 1.25%"* (source 19). The threshold universally attributed to IBD by third parties is 1.7%, and Quantifiable Edges explicitly tested 1.7% as "IBD's updated threshold" (source 29). Both are recorded. A detector built on 1.7% will find roughly 20% fewer signals than one built on IBD's published 1%–1.25% (64 vs 52 candidates over the same 37 years, per source 29) — so this is not a cosmetic disagreement, it changes the population.

## Base counting and stage degradation

- **origin / source_name**: William J. O'Neil, *How to Make Money in Stocks*, quoted directly by IBD; IBD sources 18, 3, 17; the IBD-derived handout source 25.
- **What IBD publishes about how the stage number is determined:**
  - the counting unit — *"A breakout needs to produce a gain of at least 20% in order to be counted as one stage."* (source 18) — confidence: high
  - what happens below 20% — *"If the gain is less than 20% and the stock forms another base, it's a base-on-base pattern and counted as one stage."* (source 18) — confidence: high; the handout renders the same rule as *"If a stock advances less than 20%, then forms another base, it's all counted as one consolidation"* and states the positive form as *"Each base should form at least 20% above the buy point of the base that preceded it"* (source 25) — confidence: med
  - when the count resets — bear markets zero it — *"Bear markets reset the base count back to zero"* (source 25) — confidence: med. IBD's editorial equivalent (that a decline severe enough to undercut the most recent base resets the count) appeared in search summaries but I obtained **no verbatim IBD sentence** for it — value: null — `missing:` IBD's own wording and its definition of the undercut that triggers the reset.
  - where a first-stage base can come from — after an IPO or a long decline. I obtained no verbatim quote for this — value: null — `missing:` IBD's own sentence defining a first-stage base.
  - a prerequisite that is not price-based at all — *"Don't count bases until quarterly earnings and sales start growing by at least 25%"* (source 25) — confidence: med. **If this is IBD's rule, base counting is not a chart operation.** It is gated on fundamentals, and a purely OHLCV base counter is computing a different quantity than IBD's.
- **What IBD publishes about how the odds change by stage:**
  - the preference — *"It's usually best to buy when a stock breaks out from a first-stage or second-stage base."* (source 18) — confidence: high
  - the same preference, with the reason stated as risk — *"Prefer stocks that are a first- or second-stage base, because later bases tend to be risky."* (source 3) — confidence: high
  - O'Neil's own rule, quoted verbatim by IBD — *"Sell when your stock makes a new high in price off a third- or fourth-stage base. The third time is seldom a charm in the market. By then, an advancing stock has become too obvious, and almost everyone sees it."* (source 18) — confidence: high
  - the mechanism IBD offers — *"First- and second-stage bases tend to produce larger gains than a third- or fourth-stage bases. A growth story tends to draw the most investors early in its run. After a while, the growth fades, as does enthusiasm about the stock."* (source 18) — confidence: high
  - IBD applying the grade in practice — of its own square-box example: *"This base probably is best seen as a third-stage pattern, meaning it was actually a riskier, later-stage structure."* (source 17) — confidence: high
- **The measured degradation curve — value: null.** This is the central finding of this section and it is negative. Across every IBD source reached, **IBD publishes no win rate, no average gain, no failure rate and no sample size for any base stage.** The degradation is asserted four ways ("usually best", "tend to be risky", "tend to produce larger gains", "seldom a charm") and quantified zero ways. There is no first-stage-vs-third-stage expectancy figure, no n, and no period. `missing:` for the stage rule to be computable as an expectancy adjustment rather than a filter, IBD would have to publish, per stage, the number of breakouts studied, the period, the success definition, the resulting rate, and the unconditional breakout base rate over the same population.
- **The one quantitative provenance claim IBD does make**, and why it is not a statistic: *"IBD research on market winners going back to 1880 shows stocks that form correctly shaped bases have the best chance of success."* (source 3). It names a start year and nothing else — no count of stocks, no count of bases, no success definition, and critically **no comparison population**, since the study is described as being of *winners*. A study of winners can establish what winners looked like; it cannot establish the probability that something that looks like that becomes a winner. Every base-shape rule in this document inherits that limitation.
- **detection_notes**: The base count is a **stateful sequence over a symbol's whole price history**, not a property of a window, and it is the single most expensive thing in this taxonomy to compute correctly.
  - Required primitives: an ordered list of prior base pivots; for each, the maximum price reached before the next base began; the test `(max_after_pivot_i - pivot_i) / pivot_i >= 0.20` to increment the stage, else merge into the prior stage as a base-on-base; a reset condition on a severe decline; and a bear-market detector for the handout's reset rule.
  - The 20% is measured **from the pivot**, not from the base low. Measuring from the low inflates every advance and will systematically over-count stages, pushing first-stage bases into third-stage grades and suppressing exactly the setups IBD wants bought.
  - The reset rule is the fragile part: IBD publishes the *existence* of a reset but, in the sources reached, not a computable trigger. Any implementation is choosing a threshold IBD did not publish.
  - **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone**: the handout's *"Don't count bases until quarterly earnings and sales start growing by at least 25%"* requires quarterly fundamentals. The bear-market reset requires index data. And IBD's broader stage judgment in practice leans on Relative Strength Rating, Accumulation/Distribution Rating and sponsorship data, none of which are in a bar series.
  - Because there is no published expectancy curve, a base count can be used as a **filter** ("prefer stages 1–2") exactly as IBD states it, but it **cannot** be used as a weight or a probability multiplier without inventing numbers IBD never published.
- **conflicts**: The 20% rule is stated identically by IBD editorial (source 18) and the handout (source 25) — no conflict on the number. The conflict is one of *kind*: IBD editorial presents base counting as a chart operation, while the handout gates it on 25% quarterly earnings and sales growth. Those are not the same rule, and only one of them can be implemented from bars.

## Cross-cutting notes for implementers

1. **IBD's numbers disagree with IBD's numbers more often than they disagree with rivals.** Within IBD's own columns: the cup minimum is 6 weeks in one place and 7 in two others; cup depth is 12–33%, 30–35%, and 15–30% in three places; saucer depth is 12–20% in one column and up to 30% in another; flat base depth is 15% in three columns and 10–15% in a fourth; double-bottom depth is 30% in one handout and 40% in the other; and a 38% volume increase is called "barely sufficient" against a published 40% floor. None of these are averaged or reconciled above; both values are recorded in every case.
2. **Only one base in the taxonomy is internally consistent across every source: the ascending base** (3 pullbacks, 10–20% each, 9–16 weeks, higher highs and higher lows). It is also, not coincidentally, the most mechanically specifiable.
3. **Nothing in this taxonomy carries a published win rate.** The only measured numbers anywhere in this file are Bulkowski's cup-with-handle statistics (5% break-even failure rate, 54% average rise, 61% target-hit, 62% throwback, 913 perfect trades) and Quantifiable Edges' follow-through-day success rates (54.7% on 64 signals, 55.7% on 52 signals, Dec 1971–Jan 2008) — **and neither publishes a base rate**, so neither can be read as an edge.
4. **The $0.10 pivot offset is nominal and does not scale.** It is 0.8% of a $12 stock and 0.017% of a $600 stock, and it interacts badly with split-adjusted history.
5. **Three rules in the framework are not computable from a stock's own daily OHLCV**: the market-direction gate (needs index bars), the distribution-day count that ends an uptrend (IBD publishes no threshold), and the base-count start condition (the handout gates it on 25% quarterly earnings and sales growth). A screener that implements the shapes but not these has implemented the shapes only, and O'Neil ranks the market gate — the "M" — as the framework's most important element.
