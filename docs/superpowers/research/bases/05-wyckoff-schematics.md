# Richard D. Wyckoff and the Modern Wyckoff Method — Accumulation / Distribution Schematics

Research file for a multi-bar **base-structure** screener. Scope is the multi-week trading-range
structure. Single-, two- and three-bar candlestick formations are out of scope and are not
discussed here except where a Wyckoff event is *defined* by a single bar's spread/volume/close
relative to its neighbours (e.g. the Selling Climax), which is unavoidable.

## Measured performance: the global finding (read this before any single entry)

**No source fetched publishes a win rate, failure rate, hit rate, expectancy, or backtest for any
Wyckoff event or for the schematics as a whole.** Not one. Every "measured_performance" field below
therefore reads *none published*, and that is the honest finding, not a gap in the search.

Three specific cautions:

1. A web-search snippet surfaced a claim of a "baseline failure rate of 20-25%" for springs rising
   to "55-70%", and "success probability increases from 65% to 80-85%" under certain secondary-test
   conditions, attributed loosely to Wyckoff/crypto trading pages. I fetched the page most likely to
   carry it (S12) and it publishes **no** such numbers — the fetched text contains no failure rates,
   probabilities, sample sizes, or study periods. **Treat those percentages as unsourced. Do not use
   them.** They have no named sample, no period, and no base rate.
2. S2/S8 state a *reward-to-risk* rule that is frequently misread as a win rate: "Wyckoff believed
   that profit potential should be at least three times the risk... Using such a risk-reward ratio,
   it would be possible to make profits 50% of the time and still make money." (S8). That is an
   arithmetic **assertion about position sizing**, not a measured hit rate, and it comes with no
   sample and no benchmark.
3. S7 quotes the method's own epistemology, which is why numbers are scarce: *"**Rule One:** Don't
   expect the market to behave exactly the same way twice. The market is an artist, not a computer.
   It has a repertoire of basic behavior patterns that it subtly modifies, combines and springs
   unexpectedly on its audience."* (S7, quoting Hutson/Weis/Schroeder). S7 adds: "Instead of
   steadfast rules, Wyckoff advocated broad guidelines when analyzing the stock market. Nothing in
   the stock market is definitive."

Consequence for a screener: **the Wyckoff corpus supplies a grammar, not thresholds.** Almost every
criterion below is comparative ("wider spread than", "less volume than the prior") with no published
constant. Where a real number exists, it is quoted; there are only about a dozen in the entire
corpus and most of them are *illustrative examples on a $50 stock*, not thresholds.

# ACCUMULATION EVENTS

## Preliminary Support (PS)

- **origin / source_name**: S2 (Bogomazov/Lipsett, StockCharts ChartSchool, "Accumulation: Wyckoff
  Events"); S1 (Pruden & von Lichtenstein, Schematic 1 annotations); S3 (Wyckoff SMI glossary).
- **definition**: The first appearance of meaningful demand inside a prolonged decline; the earliest
  labelled event of the schematic. Phase A (the stopping phase). S2: "**PS—preliminary support**,
  where substantial buying begins to provide pronounced support after a prolonged down-move. Volume
  increases and price spread widens, signaling that the down-move may be approaching its end."
  S1 (Schematic 1 side-bar) is near-identical: "(PS) preliminary Support is where substantial buying
  begins to provided pronounced support after a prolonged downmove Volume and spread widen and
  provide a signal that the downmove may be approaching its end."
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend is an extended decline — value: null — "after a prolonged down-move" (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` a published definition of "prolonged" (bar count, % decline, or trend-line age).
  - volume expands on the bar/cluster — value: null — "Volume increases and price spread widens" (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` the comparison window and the multiple (vs what average, over how many bars).
  - price spread widens — value: null — "Volume and spread widen" (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` spread baseline and multiple.
  - demand visibly halts the decline, even temporarily — value: null — "an action in which important demand comes in to stop the down move even temporarily" (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` an operational definition of "stop" (n bars without a lower low? a close above the prior bar's high?).
  - PS is frequently at the same price as the eventual LPS — value: null — "Preliminary Support and the Last Point of Support (LPS) often occur at the same price level." (S3) — confidence med — `missing:` a tolerance band; "same level" is unquantified.
  - PS may be absent — value: null — "The (PS) is sometimes obscured due to the violence of the price action and sometimes it is virtually missing. But usually on accumulation some form of (PS) is present." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` nothing; this is an explicit optionality statement.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: If the decline resumes and makes sustained new lows with no subsequent SC/AR/ST,
  the point was simply a pause in the downtrend and the PS label is void. Reclassifies as: an
  ordinary reaction inside a downtrend; or, if the subsequent action is climactic, the "PS" is
  retroactively re-labelled as the SC itself and a *new* PS is sought earlier.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — pivot-low detection; `spread_ratio = (high-low) / SMA(high-low, W)`;
  `vol_ratio = volume / SMA(volume, W)`; a prior-downtrend test (e.g. close below a long SMA, or a
  descending sequence of pivot lows). **PS is the only accumulation event that is *not*
  event-sequence-dependent** — it depends on a *trend*, not on a prior labelled event. In practice
  it is almost always labelled retrospectively, once the SC and AR exist, so a live detector will
  emit it as a *candidate* and confirm it later. Not computable from daily OHLCV: "substantial
  buying" as an attribution of who is buying. `W` is a free parameter no source specifies.
- **conflicts**: **Material.** S3 (Wyckoff SMI) does *not* treat PS as a support point preceding the
  SC — it treats PS as a **rally** and as a *species of* selling climax: "Preliminary Support (PS):
  is a form of a selling climax... The first important rally in a downtrend is the (PS) it may occur
  after the first oversold condition." S2/S1 treat PS as a distinct, earlier *support/absorption*
  event that precedes a separate SC. Record both. A detector built on S2 looks for a wide-spread
  high-volume *down* bar with support; a detector built on S3 looks for the first important *rally*.
  Separately, **S9 (Wyckoff Analytics' own page) names this event "Preliminary Supply (\"PS\")"
  inside the *accumulation* schematic** — "Our Type 1 Accumulation Schematic, shows us four (4)
  distinct steps at the beginning: 1. Preliminary Supply (\"PS\"); (2) Selling Climax (\"SC\");
  3 Automatic Rally (\"AR\"); 4 Secondary Test (\"ST\")." Almost certainly a typo for "Preliminary
  Support", but it is what the page says, and it collides head-on with the distribution-side PSY.

## Selling Climax (SC)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1 (Schematic 1 annotation); S3.
- **definition**: The volume/spread climax that ends the decline; the low of the SC is one of the two
  anchors of the trading-range floor. Phase A. S2: "**SC—selling climax**, the point at which
  widening spread and selling pressure usually climaxes and heavy or panicky selling by the public
  is being absorbed by larger professional interests at or near a bottom. Often price will close
  well off the low in a SC, reflecting the buying by these large interests."
- **criteria**:
  - occurs only after an extended decline — value: null — "This situation occurs only after a move has been in effect for some time." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` "some time" in bars.
  - extreme spread expansion — value: null — "This panic selling creates an extreme expansion of the price spread and an expansion of the volume" (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the multiple and the baseline window.
  - volume expansion — value: null — same quote (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` multiple/baseline.
  - **the spread+volume expansion is mandatory** — value: null — "If it does NOT HAVE THIS IT IS NOT A SELLING CLIMAX." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the threshold that "this" must exceed.
  - close well off the low — value: null — "Often price will close well off the low in a SC" (S2); "the ability of price to close off the low and up on the day" (S4) — confidence med — `missing:` how far off the low (S2 says "often", not always; no fraction published).
  - may span more than one bar — value: null — "this action may occur over one day or over several days" (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` a cap on "several".
  - the SC is not universal — value: null — "The Wyckoff principle of the selling climax does not always occur at the end of every decline." (S3); S2: "Sometimes the downtrend may end less dramatically, without climactic price and volume action." — confidence high.
  - an SC does not prove accumulation — value: null — "The presence of a Wyckoff principle like a selling climax (SC) doesn't confirm that accumulation is occurring in the TR, but it does strengthen the case for it. However, it may be accumulation, redistribution or nothing." (S1) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price continues down through the SC low on comparable or greater volume without
  an AR — the event was a mid-trend capitulation, not a climax. If a *later* bar has greater spread
  and volume at a lower low, the SC label moves to that bar. S3 warns the SC only stops the move:
  "All that the (ST) of the (SC) does is STOP the DOWNMOVE."
- **detection_notes**: Requires — `vol_ratio` vs a stated window; `spread_ratio`; `close_position =
  (close - low) / (high - low)`; a multi-bar aggregation mode (S3 permits the climax to span days,
  so a single-bar detector will systematically miss multi-day climaxes); prior-downtrend test.
  **Not sequence-dependent on a prior labelled event** (only on a downtrend), though S2's schematic
  places it after PS. Not computable from daily OHLCV: "panicky selling by the public" vs
  "absorbed by larger professional interests" — this is an attribution of counterparties, invisible
  in OHLCV. S6 gives the useful *inverse* fingerprint: high volume with a *narrowing* range and only
  marginal new low is "inharmonious", i.e. stopping action — "Note how the highest volume week of
  decline is only marginally down (and the weekly range narrows from the prior week). This is
  inharmonious action of price and volume." (S6). That is computable and is arguably a *better*
  stopping-action detector than the wide-spread definition.
- **conflicts**: S3 defines SC purely as an intensity extreme ("A situation characterized by the
  highest intensity of speculative supply occurring within a downtrend") and hard-gates it on
  spread+volume expansion. S2 softens this by allowing the whole of Phase A to occur "without
  climactic price and volume action", in which case no SC exists at all. S4 (Fraser) in practice
  labels the SC from "very large spikes in volume and an acceleration of the stock price in to a
  low" — adding a *velocity* condition (acceleration) that neither S2 nor S3 states.

## Automatic Rally (AR)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S3.
- **definition**: The reflexive bounce off the SC that occurs because supply is exhausted, not
  because demand is strong. Its high sets the trading-range ceiling. Phase A. S2: "**AR—automatic
  rally**, which occurs because intense selling pressure has greatly diminished. A wave of buying
  easily pushes prices up; this is further fueled by short covering. The high of this rally will
  help define the upper boundary of an accumulation TR."
- **criteria**:
  - immediately follows the SC — value: null — "Following the Selling Climax one of two things may happen an Automatic rally (AR) or a lateral move." (S3) — confidence high.
  - **duration a few days to about a week** — value: **~3–7 sessions** — "Generally, the (AR) lasts for only a few days to about a week." (S3) — confidence med — this is one of the very few duration numbers in the corpus; note "generally", and note it is an assertion, never measured.
  - the high defines TR resistance — value: null — "The high of this rally will help define the top of the trading range." (S1) — confidence high.
  - a *lateral* move instead of a rally is bearish — value: null — "If there is a simple lateral move the stock is FAR MORE LIKELY to continue the downtrend than if there is a good rally." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` a rule separating "rally" from "lateral" (a % move? a close above the SC high?). S3 elsewhere sizes the lateral alternative as "a couple of days, or perhaps for as much as a couple of weeks".
  - short covering contributes — value: null — "further fueled by short covering" (S2) — confidence low for detection — not observable in OHLCV.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price fails to advance meaningfully off the SC and instead drifts sideways then
  lower — reclassifies as S3's "lateral move" branch, and the SC is downgraded to a mid-trend event.
  If the "AR" runs far beyond a week and keeps making higher highs, the structure is not a trading
  range and the whole schematic is void.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — SC already detected (**strictly sequence-dependent**); pivot-high
  detection within a bounded forward window; a bar-count window (the only place a published duration
  exists — 3 to ~7 sessions per S3, but S2/S1 publish none). The AR high becomes `tr_resistance`, a
  persistent state variable every later event reads. Note that the AR is also the structure's **first
  Change of Character**: it is the first higher high after a run of lower highs, which is directly
  computable from pivot sequences.
- **conflicts**: S3 gives a duration ("a few days to about a week"); S1/S2 give none. S1 and S2 both
  say the AR high "help[s] define" the boundary — i.e. it is one input, not the definition. S3 makes
  the *absence* of a good AR a strong bearish signal; S2 does not mention this branch at all.

## Secondary Test (ST)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S3.
- **definition**: The return to the SC area to re-measure supply at that price. Phase A (and repeated
  through Phase B). S2: "**ST—secondary test**, in which price revisits the area of the SC to test
  the supply/demand balance at these levels. If a bottom is to be confirmed, volume and price spread
  should be significantly diminished as the market approaches support in the area of the SC. It is
  common to have multiple STs after a SC."
- **criteria**:
  - revisits the SC price area — value: null — "revisit the area of the Selling Climax" (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` a tolerance band around the SC low.
  - volume significantly diminished vs the SC — value: null — "volume and price spread should be significantly diminished" (S2); "especially by the Decreased Volume" (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the ratio. "Significantly" is never quantified anywhere in the corpus.
  - spread narrowed vs the SC — value: null — "a narrowing of spread and decreased volume" (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` the ratio.
  - **stops at or above the SC low** — value: null — "generally stopping at or above the same price level as the SC" (S2) — confidence high — this one IS directly computable as a boolean (`st_low >= sc_low`) even though no tolerance is published.
  - an ST below the SC low is a warning — value: null — "If the ST goes lower than that of the SC, one can anticipate either new lows or prolonged consolidation." (S2) — confidence high.
  - multiple STs are normal — value: null — "It is common to have multiple STs after a SC." (S2); "There may be repeated secondary tests depending upon the ability of the professionals to absorb the supply" (S3) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: An ST on *expanding* volume and *widening* spread denies the test — supply is
  still present. S3: a test that fails re-opens the downtrend and the "stock may go through
  redistribution, accumulation, or a trading range in which nothing of importance is going on."
  A deep ST that closes and stays below the SC low reclassifies the SC as a failed climax.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — SC and AR already detected (**sequence-dependent on both**);
  proximity test to `sc_low`; `vol_ratio_vs_event = st_volume / sc_volume`; `spread_ratio_vs_event`;
  a *repeat* counter (multiple STs must not each reset the state machine). Note the ST is the first
  event whose criteria are stated as ratios **against a specific prior event**, not against a rolling
  average — so the detector must retain the SC's own volume and spread, not just a window mean.
- **conflicts**: S3 tightens the sequencing — "Secondary Test (ST): Immediately follows the (AR)" —
  where S2 allows STs scattered through Phase B ("There are usually multiple STs during Phase B").
  S2's "at or above the same price level as the SC" is a floor rule; S3 states no such price floor,
  only that "There should be less selling than on the Selling Climax."

## Spring — and the three spring types (#1, #2, #3)

- **origin / source_name**: S3 (Wyckoff SMI glossary — the **only** source found that publishes the
  numbered spring taxonomy in full, and the only one with a numeric penetration example); S2; S1;
  S4/S5 (Fraser, who uses "Spring #2" in live analysis).
- **definition**: A penetration below trading-range support that quickly reverses back inside; the
  Phase C test of remaining supply. S2: "A 'spring' takes price below the low of the TR and then
  reverses to close within the TR; this action allows large interests to mislead the public about
  the future trend direction and to acquire additional shares at bargain prices." S1: "A spring is a
  price move below the support level of a trading range that quickly reverses and moves back into
  the range. A spring is an example of a 'bear trap'... In reality, though, the drop marks the end of
  the downtrend, thus 'trapping' the late sellers, or bears." S3 roots it historically: "a spring is
  a refinement of Mr. Wyckoff's concept of a Terminal Shake-Out and grew out of that concept."
- **criteria**:
  - price penetrates TR support — value: null — "a penetration below a previous support area" (S3) — confidence high.
  - price closes back inside the TR — value: null — "reverses to close within the TR" (S2) — confidence high — this is the single most computable Wyckoff criterion in the corpus: `low < tr_support AND close > tr_support`.
  - occurs late in the TR — value: null — "Springs or shakeouts usually occur late within a TR" (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` "late" as a fraction of TR duration.
  - **penetration depth separates a spring from a terminal shakeout** — value: **on a $50 stock, ~¾ to 1½ points ⇒ spring; 4 to 5 points ⇒ terminal shakeout** — "in a $50 dollar stock if the drive into new low ground is 4 or 5 points and then it turns around, we would call that a terminal shake-out. However, if it reacted or penetrated ¾ or a point, a point or a point and a ½ or a much shorter penetration we would call this a spring." (S3) — confidence med — **this is the only penetration-depth number in the entire Wyckoff corpus I found.** Note it is stated as an *example on one price*, not as a percentage rule; naively converted it implies roughly 1.5–3% for a spring and 8–10% for a terminal shakeout, but **S3 never states a percentage and I am not asserting one.**
  - the depth of penetration and the volume on it measure supply — value: null — "The extent of supply, or the strength of the sellers, can be judged by the depth of the price move to new lows and the relative level of volume on that penetration." (S1) — confidence high.
  - **#1 spring — overwhelming supply, bearish** — value: null — "The #1 SPRING has OVERWHELMING SUPPLY which is indicated by a EXTREME PRICE WEAKNESS and heavy selling. The PRICE and VOLUME characteristics are that there is a WIDE SPREAD and HEAVY INCREASE in VOLUME... The stock goes through the T/R on the DOWN SIDE and continues DOWN" (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the volume multiple that counts as "HEAVY INCREASE".
  - **#2 spring — some supply, must be tested** — value: null — "SOME INCREASE in PRICE WEAKNESS... and SOME INCREASE in the VOLUME over the GENERAL level of TRADING. SUPPLY is not absent, it is NOT OVERWHELMINGLY ABUNDANT." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the band separating "some" from "heavy"/"none". S3 subdivides further: a #2 whose supply is absorbed is a "#2 plus SPRING, or a 23 spring"; one whose supply persists is a "#2 minus SPRING, or a 21 spring" with "the SAME effect as the #1 SPRING".
  - **#3 spring — no supply, most bullish** — value: null — "No INCREASE in SUPPLY can COME OUT on the DRIVE into NEW LOW GROUND we call this a #3 SPRING. The #3 Spring is evidenced by lack of a INCREASE in the general level of trading. Any INCREASE in volume if at all is very, very MINOR. And there is a lack of IMPORTANT PRICE WEAKNESS as it goes into NEW LOW GROUND and the SPREAD does NOT materially WIDEN." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the volume ceiling.
  - **the boundaries between types are explicitly undefined** — value: null — "It is VERY IMPORTANT to understand that there is NO CLEAR CUT LINE of demarcation between a #1 spring and a #2 spring, a #2 spring and a #3 spring. The CRITICAL FACTOR is NOT the TERMINOLOGY, the CRITICAL FACTOR is YOUR UNDERSTANDING of the relationship of SUPPLY to DEMAND" (S3) — confidence high — **this is the source itself refusing to publish thresholds. Any type-1/2/3 classifier is inventing them.**
  - a #2 spring requires a secondary test — value: null — "both have supply and both must be tested by a secondary test" (S3) — confidence high.
  - springs are **not required** — value: null — "springs and terminal shakeouts are not required elements: Accumulation Schematic 1 depicts a spring, while Accumulation Schematic 2 shows a TR without a spring." (S2) — confidence high.
  - light supply on the break clears the way; heavy supply means renewed decline — value: null — "If the amount of supply that surfaces on a break of support is very light (low volume), it will be an indication that the way is clear for a sustained advance. Heavy supply here will usually mean a renewed decline. Moderate volume here may mean more testing of support and to proceed with caution." (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` all three volume bands.
- **measured_performance**: none published. (See the global note: the circulating "20-25% baseline
  failure rate" for springs is **not** in any source fetched, has no sample, and must not be used.)
- **invalidation**: Supply persists after the penetration — price keeps making lower lows on
  expanding volume. That converts the label from a bullish #3/#2-plus spring to a **#1 or #2-minus
  spring**, which S3 says has "the SAME effect as the #1 SPRING": a resumption of the decline
  requiring "EXTENSIVE further PREPARATION". Note the reclassification is *within* the spring
  taxonomy — S3 does not call a failed spring "not a spring". A spring is also invalidated as a
  *Phase C* event if the subsequent SOS never arrives; S4 states the relabelling rule explicitly:
  "we would relabel Phase C and D as a continuation of Phase B, with the final testing still ahead."
- **detection_notes**: Requires — established `tr_support` (from SC low and ST lows) and
  `tr_resistance` (from AR high) — so the spring is **strictly sequence-dependent**: it cannot be
  detected without Phase A having been detected first, because "below support" is meaningless
  without a support level. Primitives: penetration test (`low < tr_support`); close-back-inside test
  (`close > tr_support`, per S2 — note S2 says *close* within the TR, which is stricter and more
  computable than S1's "quickly reverses and moves back into the range"); penetration depth in
  points and as a fraction of TR height; `vol_ratio` on the penetration bar(s) vs the TR's own
  average volume (S3's phrase is "over the GENERAL level of TRADING", i.e. the TR baseline, **not** a
  fixed lookback SMA — this matters); `spread_ratio` vs the TR average spread; TR-elapsed-fraction
  for the "late in the TR" condition. **The #1/#2/#3 classification is not computable from published
  criteria** — S3 explicitly denies that demarcation lines exist. Any implementation must pick its
  own cut-points and label them as house parameters, not Wyckoff.
- **conflicts**: **S3 vs S2 on what "spring" names.** S3 makes *spring* a shallow penetration and
  *terminal shakeout* a deep one, distinguished by depth (the $50/4-5-points example). S2 makes the
  terminal shakeout a matter of *intensity*, not depth: "A terminal shakeout at the end of an
  accumulation TR is like a spring on steroids." **S5 (Fraser) vs S2 on the Phase C alternative:**
  Fraser makes Spring and LPS the two mutually exclusive forms of the Phase C test — "There are two
  types of tests; the Spring (or Shakeout) and the Last Point of Support (LPS)... The Spring is
  easier to identify because the Spring makes a minor new price low. An LPS makes a higher low and
  then turns upward... One or the other will occur" (S5) — whereas S2 puts the LPS in Phase D, after
  the SOS. See the LPS entry; this is the sharpest disagreement in the corpus.

## Terminal Shakeout (TSO) and ordinary Shakeout (OS)

- **origin / source_name**: S3 (the only source giving both a definition and the distinction); S2
  (mentions terminal shakeout only in passing).
- **definition**: A sharp thrust through support to force out weak holders. The **terminal**
  shakeout sits at the *end of the accumulation TR* (Phase C); the **ordinary** shakeout sits
  *inside an existing uptrend* and is therefore not a Phase-C event at all. S3: "Terminal Shake-Out
  (TSO): is a sharp downward thrust through a previous support area... It is executed for the
  purpose of buying all the stock possible from weak or vulnerable holders. It is PRECEDED by a
  TRADING RANGE or a SUPPORT LEVEL or at the end of ACCUMULATION area. It is FOLLOWED by an
  attempted to begin the markup phase of the cycle."
- **criteria**:
  - sharp downward thrust through prior support — value: null — verbatim above (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` "sharp" as a spread or velocity number.
  - **TSO is preceded by a TR; OS is preceded by an up-move** — value: null — "the DIFFERENCE between the Terminal Shakeout and a ordinary Shakeout is that the ordinary Shakeout occurs in an UPWARD trend. The Terminal Shakeout occurs at the END of the ACCUMULATION area and at the END of a TRADING RANGE or a SUPPORT AREA." (S3) — confidence high — **this is a context test, not a bar test: the two are indistinguishable bar-for-bar.**
  - OS has no extensive prior preparation — value: null — "a sharp DOWNWARD THRUST occurring in an UPWARD TREND without extensive previous preparation" (S3) — confidence high.
  - OS volume is unconstrained — value: null — "characterized by PRICE WEAKNESS and usually an INCREASED VOLUME... However, the VOLUME maybe HIGH, MEDIUM or LOW." (S3) — confidence high — **note this criterion is deliberately non-discriminating and therefore useless as a filter.**
  - a shakeout with supply must be tested — value: null — "When there is SUPPLY on the Shakeout itself it must be tested by a SECONDARY TEST. The secondary test should have a NARROW SPREAD and a DECREASED VOLUME compared to that of the ORDINARY SHAKEOUT." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the ratios.
  - the depth threshold vs a spring — value: **4–5 points on a $50 stock** — see the Spring entry's verbatim quote (S3) — confidence med.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: If the markup does not follow, the "terminal" label is wrong by definition —
  S3 defines it as "FOLLOWED by an attempted to begin the markup phase". Reclassifies to: a #1
  spring (supply overwhelming), or, if the structure was an uptrend all along, an ordinary shakeout.
- **detection_notes**: Requires everything the Spring requires, **plus** a prior-trend classifier to
  separate TSO from OS. Sequence-dependent: TSO needs a detected TR; OS needs a detected uptrend and
  is explicitly *not* part of the accumulation state machine. Because S3 says OS volume "maybe HIGH,
  MEDIUM or LOW", **the ordinary shakeout has no volume signature at all** and is effectively
  undetectable except by context. S2 additionally notes shakeouts recur after markup begins
  ("Shakeouts may also occur once a price advance has started"), which means a shakeout detector
  will fire in Phase E and must not be allowed to reset the state machine to Phase C.
- **conflicts**: S2 collapses the distinction that S3 makes central. S2 treats "spring or shakeout"
  as an interchangeable pair throughout and gives the terminal shakeout no separate criteria beyond
  "a spring on steroids". S3 gives it a distinct definition, a distinct location, a distinct depth,
  and a *third* variant (the ordinary shakeout) that S2 mentions only in a single clause.

## Test (of the spring / of the shakeout)

- **origin / source_name**: S2 (which gives "Test" its own bullet, separate from ST); S3; S4.
- **definition**: The low-volume revisit that confirms the spring or shakeout removed the supply.
  Phase C, after the spring. S2: "**Test**—Large operators always test the market for supply
  throughout a TR (e.g., STs and springs) and at key points during a price advance. If considerable
  supply emerges on a test, the market is often not ready to be marked up. A spring is often followed
  by one or more tests; a successful test (indicating that further price increases will follow)
  typically makes a higher low on lesser volume."
- **criteria**:
  - **makes a higher low than the spring** — value: null — "typically makes a higher low on lesser volume" (S2) — confidence high — computable as a boolean (`test_low > spring_low`), though "typically" leaves it non-mandatory.
  - **volume less than the spring's** — value: null — same quote (S2); "Volume will diminish on the LPS test as compared to the prior low." (S4) — confidence high — `missing:` the ratio; the comparison is event-to-event, not vs a window.
  - considerable supply on the test denies markup — value: null — "If considerable supply emerges on a test, the market is often not ready to be marked up." (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` "considerable".
  - a low on very high volume will be retested later — value: null — "When a low is made on very high volume, expect a price retest at a later date." (S4) — confidence med — this is a *forecast rule*, useful for a screener's forward-looking watchlist rather than a classifier.
  - the test's job is to prove absorption — value: null — "An LPS or a Spring are doing the same type of work; which is to test a prior high volume price low." (S4) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: A test that undercuts the spring low, or that arrives on volume equal to or
  greater than the spring's, denies it. Reclassifies the spring to a #2-minus / #1 spring and returns
  the structure to Phase B.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — spring already detected (**strictly sequence-dependent; the test
  is meaningless without a specific prior low to test**); retention of the spring's low *and* its
  volume as state; a forward search window (unpublished — no source states how many bars a test may
  lag the spring). Fully computable from daily OHLCV given the prior event: `test_low > spring_low`
  AND `test_volume < spring_volume`. This is, along with the spring's close-back-inside test, the
  most mechanically checkable rule in the corpus.
- **conflicts**: S2 keeps "Test" and "ST" as separate labelled bullets while admitting STs *are*
  tests ("Large operators always test the market for supply throughout a TR (e.g., STs and
  springs)"). S3 does not list a standalone "Test" — it folds the concept into "Secondary Test",
  which is why S3's ST can appear both after the AR and after a spring. For a state machine this
  means the *same* bar pattern may be labelled ST (S3) or Test (S2) depending on which event it
  follows — the label is a function of position, not of bar shape.

## Sign of Strength (SOS) / Jump Across the Creek (JAC)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1 (which attributes the "jump" metaphor to Robert G. Evans); S3;
  S4/S5.
- **definition**: The wide-spread, high-volume advance that carries price through the resistance at
  the top of the TR. Ends Phase C / defines Phase D. S2: "**SOS—sign of strength**, a price advance
  on increasing spread and relatively higher volume. Often a SOS takes place after a spring,
  validating the analyst's interpretation of that prior action." S1: "Jump Across the Creek (JAC) is
  a continuation of the creek analogy of jumping resistance and is a good sign if done on good spread
  and volume – a sign of strength (SOS)." S3 merges them: "The Sigh of Strength and the Crossing of
  the Creek are often two ways of looking at the SAME ACTION."
- **criteria**:
  - spread widens on the advance — value: null — "an advance on good (increasing) spread and volume" (S1); "WIDENING SPREAD" (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` multiple and baseline.
  - volume increases on the advance — value: null — same (S1/S3) — confidence high — `missing:` multiple and baseline.
  - penetrates outer resistance — value: null — "A jump is a relatively wider price-spread move made on comparatively higher volume that penetrates outer resistance or support." (S1) — confidence high — computable once `tr_resistance` exists.
  - it must clear the *creek*, not merely rise — value: null — "the CREEK is WHERE the BOY JUMPED. Applying this to the market, the CREEK is WHERE the VOLUME CAME IN, where the EFFORT the PUSH the POWER came in." (S3, quoting Evans) — confidence med — **this is circular for a detector**: the creek is defined as the place the jump happened.
  - it must clear the prior UT highs — value: null — "the stock price has the power to jump above the prior UT highs and the price holds above the Accumulation area" (S4) — confidence med — computable, and stricter than "above TR resistance".
  - a wide-spread, high-volume *reaction* after the supposed SOS cancels it — value: null — "What if the stock has a possible (SOS) indicated by a widening spread and increasing volume on the move up and is then followed by good supply on possible (LPS) indicated by wide spread and high volume on the reaction? This CANCELS the probability of the fist action being a (SOS)" (S3) — confidence high — a genuine, checkable invalidation rule.
  - Evans' timing caveat — value: null — "when the Wave reaches a resistance point and is unable to penetrate it fairly quickly (Mr. Evans' 'go and go now' observation), it has a tendency to return to the trading range." (S11) — confidence med — `missing:` "fairly quickly" in bars.
  - S2 distinguishes SOS volume from breakout volume — value: null — "the CLIMAX VOLUME is stopping a trend which is out in open territory... ABSORBTION VOLUME however, occurs with a WIDENING SPREAD and INCREASED VOLUME as the stock is breaking through a previous SUPPLY AREA" (S3) — confidence high — **same bar signature, different meaning, separated only by location relative to the TR.**
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price falls back into the TR on wide spread and high volume — S3's explicit
  cancellation rule above. Reclassifies as: a failed rally inside Phase B; if the failure comes from
  *above* resistance, the same bar pattern is an **Upthrust (UT)**, i.e. a distribution event. This
  is the single most consequential ambiguity in the whole schematic: **SOS and UT are the same bar
  shape at the same location, separated only by what happens next.**
- **detection_notes**: Requires — `tr_resistance`; `spread_ratio` and `vol_ratio` (baselines
  unpublished); prior-event memory (S2 ties the SOS's meaning to a preceding spring: "Often a SOS
  takes place after a spring, validating the analyst's interpretation of that prior action").
  **Sequence-dependent** in the sense that its *significance* requires Phase C, though the bar
  pattern alone is detectable without it. A robust implementation must emit SOS only provisionally
  and confirm it with the subsequent LPS/BU behaviour — because the cancellation rule (S3) and the
  UT alternative both depend on later bars. Not computable from daily OHLCV: the creek's freehand
  position — S3 says outright "there is no one exact way of drawing these creeks."
- **conflicts**: S6 (Fraser) gives a directly contrary volume observation for the post-accumulation
  advance: "During the jump out of the Accumulation range, the price bars are wide and closing near
  the high of the weekly range. The Effort of volume is actually declining with each weeks advance.
  This seems to defy the market wisdom that price should rise on expanding volume... This is a large
  Result with modest Effort which is considered bullish early in a new bull market." So Fraser
  accepts a *declining-volume* markup as bullish, while S1/S2/S3 all require increasing volume for
  the SOS. A volume-expansion gate would reject Fraser's own example.

## Last Point of Support (LPS)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S3; S4/S5 (Fraser, who places it differently — see conflicts).
- **definition**: **S2/S1/S3 version:** the low of the reaction that follows an SOS, on reduced
  spread and volume — Phase D. S2: "**LPS—last point of support**, the low point of a reaction or
  pullback after a SOS. Backing up to an LPS means a pullback to support that was formerly
  resistance, on diminished spread and volume. On some charts, there may be more than one LPS,
  despite the ostensibly singular precision of this term." **S5 version:** the Phase C alternative to
  a spring — "The Last Point of Support is the final drive toward the bottom of the Accumulation area
  before the uptrend begins... The Spring is easier to identify because the Spring makes a minor new
  price low. An LPS makes a higher low and then turns upward."
- **criteria**:
  - follows an SOS — value: null — "the low point of a reaction or pullback after a SOS" (S2) — confidence high (**S2/S1/S3 branch only**).
  - spread narrows vs the SOS — value: null — "Should have a LACK OF SUPPLY indicated by a RELATIVE NARROWING of the SPREAD and a DECREASE IN THE VOLUME. The comparison is between the up move constituting the (SOS) and the REACTION FOLLOWING it, the (LPS)." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the ratios; note S3 names the comparison basis explicitly (the SOS, not a window).
  - volume decreases vs the SOS — value: null — same (S3) — confidence high.
  - pullback lands on former resistance — value: null — "a pullback to support that was formerly resistance" (S2) — confidence high — computable: `lps_low ≈ tr_resistance`.
  - **Fraser branch: LPS makes a higher low, no new low** — value: null — "If a new low is made, expect a Spring. If a new low is not made, a Last Point of Support (LPS) is expected." (S4) — confidence high — cleanly computable and **mutually exclusive with a spring**.
  - the LPS often lands at the PS / SC level — value: null — "In the case of a longer-term count involving multiple P&F phases, the LPS often appears at the original level of preliminary support or the SC. When the LPS occurs at either of these levels, this tends to validate the count." (S2); "Preliminary Support and the Last Point of Support (LPS) often occur at the same price level." (S3); "The first LPS goes almost exactly to the Buying Climax low... SCLX=LPS." (S5) — confidence high — `missing:` tolerance band.
  - multiple LPSs are normal — value: null — "there may be more than one LPS, despite the ostensibly singular precision of this term" (S2); Fraser labels a first, second and third LPS in one chart (S5) — confidence high.
  - stop placement — value: null — "place a stop under the low of the LPS" (S5) — confidence high — an explicit, computable invalidation level.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: A reaction on *wide* spread and *high* volume — S3: "This CANCELS the probability
  of the fist action being a (SOS) and the stock will probably continue in the trading range for
  additional testing." So a failed LPS retro-invalidates the SOS, not just itself. Price below the
  LPS low is the practical stop (S5). If price returns deep into the TR, S4's relabelling rule
  applies: Phases C and D become a continuation of Phase B.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — **SOS already detected (S2/S1/S3 branch), or a Phase-A trading
  range (Fraser branch)**. Either way, **strictly sequence-dependent**. Primitives: pivot-low;
  event-relative `spread_ratio` and `vol_ratio` against the retained SOS bar(s); proximity test to
  `tr_resistance` (S2 branch) or to `tr_support` / prior pivot lows (Fraser branch); a
  higher-low boolean vs the prior structural low. **A detector must choose a branch and say so** —
  the two branches place the LPS on opposite sides of the SOS in time and at opposite ends of the TR
  in price. Note S5 also reports an LPS on *very high* volume being bullish ("Note the very high
  volume on the low bar, this is absorption and good demand. This is the low bar of the LPS"), which
  directly contradicts S3's "DECREASE IN THE VOLUME" requirement.
- **conflicts**: **The largest conflict in the corpus.** (a) *Phase placement:* S2 (Bogomazov/
  Pruden) puts LPS in **Phase D, after the SOS**; S5 (Fraser) puts LPS in **Phase C, as the
  alternative to a spring, before the SOS** — "Either a Spring or a Last Point of Support arrive in
  Phase C which is the last test of the Support area prior to the markup out of the Accumulation.
  One or the other will occur." (b) Fraser is internally inconsistent within a single article: the
  same piece (S5) also says "It is after the SOS where the Wyckoffian will look for the LPS." Both
  usages are recorded; neither is merged. (c) *Volume:* S3 requires decreased volume at the LPS; S5
  presents very high volume at the LPS as evidence of absorption. Record both.

## Back-Up (BU) / Backing Up to the Edge of the Creek (BUEC)

- **origin / source_name**: S2 (crediting Robert Evans); S1; S3; S4/S5.
- **definition**: The pause/pullback immediately after the jump (SOS) that tests the former
  resistance from above. Phase D. S2: "**BU—'back-up'**. This term is short-hand for a colorful
  metaphor coined by Robert Evans... Evans analogized the SOS to a 'jump across the creek' of price
  resistance, and the 'back up to the creek' represented both short-term profit-taking and a test for
  additional supply around the area of resistance. A back-up is a common structural element preceding
  a more substantial price mark-up, and can take on a variety of forms, including a simple pullback
  or a new TR at a higher level."
- **criteria**:
  - immediately follows the jump — value: null — "A backup is a test that immediately follows the jump" (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` "immediately" in bars.
  - relatively narrow spread — value: null — "a relatively narrow price-spread reaction or rally on comparatively lighter volume that tests and confirms the legitimacy of the preceding jump action." (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` ratios.
  - comparatively lighter volume — value: null — same (S1) — confidence high.
  - the backup is where you buy, not the breakout — value: null — "The Wyckoff method instructs you to buy after a backup following an upward jump (a sign of strength)... Also according to Wyckoff, you should not buy breakouts because that would leave you vulnerable to swift moves in the opposite direction if the breakout turned out to be false." (S1) — confidence high — **a hard, explicit entry rule; it means the screener's alert should fire on the BU, not on the breakout bar.**
  - the BU is normally a potential LPS — value: null — "The Backup to the edge is normally a potential Last Point of Support (LPS)." (S3) — confidence high.
  - the BU may itself become a new TR — value: null — "can take on a variety of forms, including a simple pullback or a new TR at a higher level" (S2) — confidence high — **a detector must tolerate a BU that lasts weeks.**
  - the BU should show shrinking volume and spread to prove absorption — value: null — "The Backups should have the signature of shrinking volume and spread to prove absorption has taken place. If weakness persists with widening volume and price spread, then we would conclude that more supply is arriving which could drive prices downward." (S4) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price falls back *through* the creek and stays inside the TR. S3's imagery is
  explicit: "every so often that little old boy scout sort of drowns." Reclassifies the SOS as an
  Upthrust and the whole TR as potential distribution — S4's relabelling rule: "relabel Phase C and
  D as a continuation of Phase B."
- **detection_notes**: Requires — SOS/JAC already detected (**strictly sequence-dependent**);
  `tr_resistance` retained as the level being tested from above; narrow-spread and low-volume tests
  measured *against the SOS bars*; and a variable-length window, because S2 explicitly permits the BU
  to be an entire new trading range. Practically, a BU detector is a "pullback-to-breakout-level
  holds" test — the most conventional and most computable structure in the whole method. Not
  computable from daily OHLCV: which of the several rally peaks the freehand "creek" runs through.
- **conflicts**: S1 frames the BU as a *test that confirms the jump*, i.e. a validation step; S2
  frames it as *profit-taking plus a supply test*, i.e. partly mechanical. S3 equates BU with a
  potential LPS; S2 keeps BU and LPS as separate bullets while describing them almost identically
  ("Backing up to an LPS means a pullback to support that was formerly resistance"). For detection
  purposes BU and the S2-branch LPS are the same object under two names.

# DISTRIBUTION EVENTS

## Preliminary Supply (PSY)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1 (Schematics 2 and 3 annotations); S3.
- **definition**: The first meaningful supply entering after a pronounced advance. Phase A of
  distribution. S2: "**PSY—preliminary supply**, where large interests begin to unload shares in
  quantity after a pronounced up-move. Volume expands and price spread widens, signaling that a
  change in trend may be approaching." S1: "(PSY) Preliminary Supply… is where substantial selling
  begins to provide pronounced resistance after an upmove. Volume and spread widen and provide a
  signal that the upmove may be approaching its end."
- **criteria**:
  - follows a pronounced up-move — value: null — "after a pronounced up-move" (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` "pronounced" as bars or %.
  - volume expands — value: null — "Volume expands and price spread widens" (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` multiple/baseline.
  - spread widens — value: null — same (S2) — confidence high.
  - the later LPSY often returns to the PSY price — value: null — "The price reached on the (LPSY) after a sign of weakness very often is in the same area reached on the (PSY)." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` tolerance. S1 confirms this on the Nokia case: "the price level stopped in July near the same 500 level as did the earlier preliminary supply (PSY) in March-April."
  - PSY marks where distribution may have begun — value: null — "Analytically the significance of the (PSY) is that if the entire area is distribution. That distribution may have begun in the area of the (PSY)." (S3) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: The advance resumes on expanding volume and makes sustained new highs with no BC
  or AR — the "PSY" was a routine reaction inside markup. Reclassifies as a re-accumulation pause.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — prior-uptrend test; `vol_ratio`; `spread_ratio`; pivot-high
  detection. **Not sequence-dependent on a prior labelled event** (trend-dependent only) — PSY, like
  PS, is the entry point of the state machine. Not computable from daily OHLCV: "large interests
  begin to unload" as an attribution.
- **conflicts**: **Material and directly detection-breaking.** S2/S1 place PSY *before* the BC and
  make it a supply-at-a-high event. **S3 makes PSY a *reaction* that follows a lesser buying climax:**
  "Usually there's some form of a Buying Climax which stops the move and that is followed by a
  REACTION, (PSY), which has pronounced WEAKNESS or volume or preferably both (wide spread & high
  volume). The buying climax (BC), is part of the Preliminary Supply (PSY) this NORMALLY HAS LESS
  VOLUME THAN THAT ON THE FINIAL BUYING CLIMAX." Under S2 the PSY bar is a *high*; under S3 the PSY
  is a *down-move* from a preliminary, lower-volume climax that precedes the final BC. A detector
  written to S2 will label a high; a detector written to S3 will label a decline. Record both.
  S3 adds a further variant: "sometimes the (PSY) and the (BC) occur on the largest and longest leg
  of an upwards thrust of the uptrend" — i.e. they can coincide on one bar.

## Buying Climax (BC)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S3.
- **definition**: The volume/spread climax that ends the advance; its high anchors the TR ceiling.
  Phase A. S2: "**BC—buying climax**, during which there are often marked increases in volume and
  price spread. The force of buying reaches a climax, with heavy or urgent buying by the public being
  filled by professional interests at prices near a top. A BC often coincides with a great earnings
  report or other good news, since the large operators require huge demand from the public to sell
  their shares without depressing the stock price."
- **criteria**:
  - marked increase in volume — value: null — "marked increases in volume and price spread" (S2); "The buying climax has increased volume and a widening spread as it moves up." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` multiple/baseline.
  - widening spread — value: null — same (S2/S3) — confidence high.
  - buying exhausts itself — value: null — "The buying gradually builds up & builds up and finally comes in with a RUSH and EXHAUSTS itself on the buying climax." (S3) — confidence high.
  - **often coincides with good news / an earnings report** — value: null — "A BC often coincides with a great earnings report or other good news" (S2) — confidence high — **explicitly NOT computable from OHLCV; requires a news or earnings-calendar join.**
  - followed by either an AR or a lateral move — value: null — "Following a buying climax one of two things can occur, either a (AR) or a lateral move." (S3) — confidence high.
  - the AR after a BC has no fixed signature — value: null — "That (AR) may have increased volume, heavy volume, or no volume. It may have wide price spread, or relatively narrow price spread." (S3) — confidence high — **another deliberately non-discriminating criterion.**
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price consolidates briefly then breaks to new highs on expanding volume with no
  AR and no ST failure — reclassifies as a markup continuation / re-accumulation. If a later bar
  posts greater spread and volume at a higher high, the BC label moves.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — prior-uptrend test; `vol_ratio`; `spread_ratio`; pivot-high;
  optionally `close_position` (S2 does not state the mirror of the SC's "close well off the low",
  which is an asymmetry in the published definitions). **Not sequence-dependent** on a prior labelled
  event. The earnings-coincidence criterion needs an external calendar; flag it as out-of-band for
  a pure OHLCV screener. S1 adds a structural asymmetry worth encoding: "it is useful to remember
  that distribution is generally accomplished in a shorter time period than accumulation" — so a
  distribution detector should tolerate shorter TRs than an accumulation detector. S3 quantifies
  this loosely: "MAJOR distribution occurs in only a few weeks or perhaps a few months very rarely
  over a several year period", against accumulation which "takes MUCH LONGER, sometimes over many
  years."
- **conflicts**: See PSY — S3 makes the BC "part of the Preliminary Supply (PSY)" and posits *two*
  buying climaxes (a preliminary one with less volume, then "the FINIAL BUYING CLIMAX"), whereas
  S2/S1 recognise one BC preceded by a separate PSY.

## Automatic Reaction (AR, distribution)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S3.
- **definition**: The reflexive sell-off after the BC; its low sets the TR floor. Phase A. S2:
  "**AR—automatic reaction**. With intense buying substantially diminished after the BC and heavy
  supply continuing, an AR takes place. The low of this selloff helps define the lower boundary of
  the distribution TR."
- **criteria**:
  - follows the BC — value: null — "Following the Buying Climax is the Automatic Reaction" (S3) — confidence high.
  - **duration measured in days** — value: null — "As with the Automatic Rally, the time factor here is generally measured in days." (S3) — confidence med — `missing:` a bar count. S3 gives the accumulation AR "a few days to about a week" but does **not** repeat that number here; I am not transferring it.
  - the low defines TR support — value: null — "The low of this selloff will help define the bottom of the Trading Range (TR)." (S1) — confidence high.
  - its extent depends on how completely demand is exhausted — value: null — "The extent of the reaction depends on how completely the demand is exhausted and how extensive the first wave of short selling is." (S3) — confidence high — not computable.
  - it is limited by the absence of prior preparation — value: null — "by the fact that the REACTION does not have any significant preparation in advance to sustain it." (S3) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: No meaningful reaction occurs — S3's lateral-move branch — in which case the BC
  is downgraded and the uptrend likely continues.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — BC already detected (**strictly sequence-dependent**); pivot-low
  in a bounded forward window; the resulting low is stored as `tr_support` for every later event.
  This is also the distribution structure's first Change of Character (first lower low after a run of
  higher lows) — computable from pivot sequence alone.
- **conflicts**: S3 gives a duration unit ("measured in days"); S1/S2 give none. S2 says the AR
  "helps define" the boundary; S1 says the same. No source states the AR must undercut a prior
  pivot low.

## Secondary Test (ST, distribution)

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1.
- **definition**: The return to the BC area to re-measure demand. Phase A, and repeated in Phase B.
  S2: "**ST—secondary test**, in which price revisits the area of the BC to test the demand/supply
  balance at these price levels. For a top to be confirmed, supply must outweigh demand; volume and
  spread should thus decrease as price approaches the resistance area of the BC. An ST may take the
  form of an upthrust (UT), in which price moves above the resistance represented by the BC and
  possibly other STs before quickly reversing to close below resistance. After a UT, price often
  tests the lower boundary of the TR."
- **criteria**:
  - revisits the BC price area — value: null — "revisit the area of the Buying Climax" (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` tolerance band.
  - volume and spread decrease into resistance — value: null — "volume and spread should be diminished as the market approaches the resistance area of the BC" (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` ratios.
  - **an ST may itself be a UT** — value: null — "An ST may take the form of an upthrust (UT)" (S2) — confidence high — **this means ST and UT are not mutually exclusive labels, which a state machine must permit.**
  - after a UT-form ST, price tests the TR low — value: null — "After a UT, price often tests the lower boundary of the TR." (S2) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: An ST on expanding volume that holds above the BC high and continues — demand is
  still dominant; the TR is re-accumulation, not distribution.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — BC and AR already detected (**strictly sequence-dependent**);
  proximity test to `bc_high`; event-relative volume and spread ratios; and a *disambiguation* step
  against UT (does the bar exceed `tr_resistance` and close back below?). Because S2 permits an ST to
  be a UT, the detector should emit both labels rather than choosing.
- **conflicts**: S1's Schematic-2/3 annotation is the mirror of the accumulation ST and adds no
  UT-form clause; S2 explicitly adds it. S3's glossary has no separate distribution-ST entry, only
  the generic "Secondary Test" written for accumulation.

## Upthrust (UT)

- **origin / source_name**: S1; S3; S2 (folded into ST/Phase C); S4.
- **definition**: A penetration above TR resistance that immediately fails and reverses back inside;
  the bull-trap mirror of the spring. Phase B or Phase C. S1: "An upthrust is the opposite of a
  spring. It is a price move above the resistance level of a trading range that quickly reverses
  itself and moves back into the trading range. An upthrust is a 'bull trap' – it appears to signal
  a start of an uptrend but in reality marks the end of the up move. The magnitude of the upthrust
  can be determined by the extent of the price move to new highs and the relative level of volume on
  that movement."
- **criteria**:
  - moves above a prior supply level and does not hold — value: null — "An Upthrust is a sharp price movement ABOVE a prior supply level which does NOT HOLD, but immediately reacts below that previous level." (S3) — confidence high.
  - reverses back into the TR — value: null — "quickly reverses itself and moves back into the trading range" (S1) — confidence high — computable: `high > tr_resistance AND close < tr_resistance`.
  - **narrow spread with increased volume** — value: null — "Usually on the Upthrust the spread will be narrow and the volume will be increased, this is evidence of the supply overcoming demand." (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` the spread and volume thresholds; note this is an *effort-without-result* signature, not the wide-spread signature of an SOS.
  - the effort/result arithmetic — value: **10,000 shares per $1, then 20,000 shares per $1 (or per $0.50)** — "Suppose a stock moves up from $50.00 to $51.00 and it takes 10,000 shares to do it and then moves to $52.00 and it takes 20,000 shares to do it. The volume, the supply has increased in strength relative to demand. If the volume doubles the price progress should be double and when it does not the inference maybe drawn that the SUPPLY is OVERCOMING the DEMAND. Suppose a stock moves up one point on 10,000 shares and then moves up a ½ point on 20,000 shares, here the narrowing of the spread and the supply coming in to overcome the demand is much more emphatic." (S3) — confidence med — **these are illustrative figures defining a *ratio test* (points-per-share-of-volume), not a threshold. The usable primitive is `price_progress / volume` declining leg-over-leg.**
  - confirmation is in the promptness of the reaction — value: null — "The confirmation that it is an Upthrust is in the promptness and in the manner in which it reacts" (S3) — confidence high — `missing:` "promptly" in bars.
  - **the UT is simultaneously a SOW and an LPSY** — value: null — "The Upthrust itself is the sign of weakness (SOW) and the Last Point of Supply (LPSY) all in the same action. It is normally followed by a more important (SOW) and a (LPSY)." (S3) — confidence high — **major sequencing consequence; see conflicts.**
  - UTs occur in Phase B, not only Phase C — value: null — "There are usually multiple STs during Phase B, as well as upthrust-type actions at the upper end of the TR." (S2, of *accumulation*); "Note the two Upthrusts (UT) which appear to breakout of the trading range and then, suddenly, fail back into the range." (S4, in Phase B of an *accumulation*) — confidence high — **UTs appear inside accumulation ranges too and are not by themselves bearish.**
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price holds above resistance and continues — the bar was an SOS, not a UT.
  S2 warns this repeatedly traps shorts: "the 'smart money' repeatedly stops out traders who initiate
  such short positions with one UT after another, so it is often safer to wait until Phase D and an
  LPSY."
- **detection_notes**: Requires — `tr_resistance` established (**strictly sequence-dependent on
  Phase A**); penetration + close-back-inside test; `spread_ratio` (S3 wants *narrow*, which is the
  opposite of the SOS test) and `vol_ratio` (increased); a promptness window (unpublished). The
  points-per-volume ratio from S3 is genuinely computable: compare `(leg_high - leg_low) / leg_volume`
  across consecutive up-legs. **Critical ordering note: a UT bar and an SOS bar can be identical on
  the penetration bar itself. Only the following bars separate them.** Any live detector must emit
  "penetration event, direction unresolved" and resolve on the next N bars.
- **conflicts**: **S3 vs S3 (internal).** The glossary says the UT has "narrow" spread and
  "increased" volume; S11, from the same institution, states "The definition of an upthrust is
  reduced spread and volume, with the index or stock reacting back to the average level of closes."
  Increased volume vs reduced volume — the same body publishes both. Record both; do not merge.
  **S3 vs S2 on sequencing:** S3 collapses UT, SOW and LPSY into one action; S2/S1 sequence them as
  three separate events (UT in Phase C, SOW in Phase C/D, LPSY in Phase D). A state machine built on
  S2 will wait for three events where S3 says one has already occurred.

## Upthrust After Distribution (UTAD)

- **origin / source_name**: S1 (Schematic 3, point 11); S2; S3 (which transmits **Robert Evans'
  original rule verbatim, including the one duration number in the corpus**).
- **definition**: The definitive, late-stage test of new demand above TR resistance — the
  distribution counterpart of the spring/terminal shakeout. Phase C. S2: "**UTAD—upthrust after
  distribution.** A UTAD is the distributional counterpart to the spring and terminal shakeout in the
  accumulation TR. It occurs in the latter stages of the TR and provides a definitive test of new
  demand after a breakout above TR resistance. Analogous to springs and shakeouts, a UTAD is not a
  required structural element."
- **criteria**:
  - occurs in the latter stages of the TR — value: null — "usually occurs in the latter stages of the TR" (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` "latter stages" as a fraction of TR length.
  - moves into new high ground above TR resistance — value: null — "moved into new high ground" (S3); "a pronounced move upward, breaking through the highs of the TR" (S1) — confidence high.
  - **on increasing volume with relative narrowing of spread** — value: null — "Should the move to new high ground be on increasing volume and 'relative narrowing spread' and then return to the average level of closes of the TR, this would indicate lack of solid demand and confirm that the breakout to the upside did not indicate a TR of accumulation, but rather a formation of distribution." (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` the volume multiple and the spread narrowing ratio.
  - **returns to the average level of closes of the TR** — value: null — same quote (S1); "to then return to the AVERAGE level of closes" (S3) — confidence high — **this IS computable exactly: `mean(close)` over the TR bars. It is the most precisely specified price target in the whole corpus and it is not a round number, it is a computed statistic.**
  - "relative" is relative to volume — value: null — "we now say that it has moved into new high ground on a 'relative' narrowing of the spread, we have added this word relative because a widening or a narrowing of the spread is RELATIVE to the VOLUME. The spread MUST BE COMPARED to the VOLUME." (S3) — confidence high — so the test is `spread / volume`, not spread alone.
  - **Evans' original rule required a 4-to-12-week lateral move — and modern SMI removed it** — value: **4 to 12 weeks** — "in Mr. Evan's original rules he stated: it moves sideways for a period of four to twelve weeks. However, to put these constraints on the rule has proved to limit the understanding of this excellent rule. Therefore we have modified the rule and have used: 'The building of a potential cause'." (S3) — confidence high — **the single most useful number in this file for a screener, and it is published together with its own retraction.**
  - light-volume breakout with no follow-through, or heavy volume with a breakdown, both confirm distribution — value: null — "If this breakout occurs on light volume with no follow through or on heavy volume with a breakdown back into the centre of the trading range, then this is more evidence that the TR was Distribution not Accumulation." (S1) — confidence high — **note this criterion accepts *either* volume extreme, so volume alone cannot discriminate.**
  - UTAD is optional — value: null — "a UTAD is not a required structural element: the TR in Distribution Schematic #1 contains a UTAD, while the TR in Distribution Schematic #2 does not." (S2); "you must avoid looking for examples, or expecting examples of the (UTAD) to occur all the time, they simply DO NOT." (S3) — confidence high.
  - demand may be too weak for a true UTAD — value: null — "Often demand is so weak in a distribution TR that price does not reach the level of the BC or initial ST. In this case, Phase C's test of demand may be represented by a UT of a lower high within the TR." (S2) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price holds above the TR and continues higher on good spread — the move was a
  genuine breakout and the TR was accumulation, not distribution. S1 states the confirmation
  condition as the *return* to the average level of closes; absent that return, the UTAD is not
  confirmed.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — `tr_resistance`, `tr_support` and **`tr_mean_close = mean(close)`
  over the TR window** (the confirmation target); penetration above resistance; `spread/volume`
  ratio vs the TR baseline; TR-elapsed-fraction for "latter stages"; and, if implementing Evans'
  original rule, a **TR duration filter of 4–12 weeks (≈20–60 daily bars)** — with a flag noting SMI
  itself abandoned that window. **Strictly sequence-dependent**: requires the full Phase A boundary
  set plus a Phase B of sufficient length. Weekly resampling is natural here given the weeks-scale
  duration rule. Not computable from daily OHLCV: "weak holders of short positions surrendering their
  positions to stronger players" (S1) — a positioning claim.
- **conflicts**: S3 and S1 both state the "return to the average level of closes" confirmation. S2
  omits it entirely and defines UTAD only by location and function. S3 explicitly **rejects** Evans'
  4-to-12-week constraint that S3 itself quotes — so the same page carries both the number and its
  repudiation. S2 additionally permits a "UT of a lower high within the TR" to serve as Phase C when
  price never reaches the BC level, which is not a UTAD at all under S1's rule (no new high ground).

## Sign of Weakness (SOW) / Falling Through the Ice

- **origin / source_name**: S1 (Schematic 2, point 10); S2; S3.
- **definition**: The wide-spread, high-volume decline to or through TR support that establishes
  supply's dominance. Phase C or D. S2: "**SOW—sign of weakness**, observable as a down-move to (or
  slightly past) the lower boundary of the TR, usually occurring on increased spread and volume. The
  AR and the initial SOW(s) indicate a change of character in the price action of the stock: supply
  is now dominant." S1: "(SOW) Sign of Weakness… at point 10 will usually occur on increased spread
  and volume as compared to the rally to point 9. Supply is showing dominance. Our first 'fall on the
  ice' holds and we get up try to forge ahead."
- **criteria**:
  - down-move to or slightly past TR support — value: null — "a down-move to (or slightly past) the lower boundary of the TR" (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` how far "slightly past" may go.
  - increased spread and volume **compared to the preceding rally** — value: null — "on increased spread and volume as compared to the rally to point 9" (S1) — confidence high — **note the comparison basis is the prior rally leg, not a rolling average.**
  - it need not break the ice — value: null — "The SOW may or may not 'fall through the ice'" (S1) — confidence high.
  - **must be confirmed, denied, or left in doubt by the subsequent rally** — value: null — "any possible or potential (SOW) must be confirmed, denied or left in doubt by the SUBSEQUENT rally... The critical thing is NOT HOW FAR the stock rallies, the critical thing is HOW it RALLIES." (S3) — confidence high — **the SOW is never confirmable on its own bar; it is confirmed only by the LPSY that follows.**
  - confirmation = the rally shows narrowing spread, decreased volume, and a lower top — value: null — "If it rallies with a gradual decrease in demand, evidenced by a narrowing spread and decreased volume, and with a lower top this COMPARATIVE lack of DEMAND would PROVE and CONFIRM that the previous reaction was a (SOW)." (S3) — confidence high.
  - denial = the rally shows good/increased volume and wide spread — value: null — "should demand still be strong on that rally as evidenced by good or increased volume as well as wide or widening spread, it is usually best to regard such action as a DENIAL of the (SOW)" (S3) — confidence high.
  - a TR ends with either a SOS+LPS or a SOW+LPSY — value: null — "a stock may remain in the T/R indefinitely until it has a (SOS) followed by a (LPS) indicating an Upward move, or a (SOW) followed by a (LPSY) indicating a Downward move." (S3) — confidence high — **the cleanest terminal-condition statement in the corpus.**
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: The subsequent rally comes on widening spread and increasing volume to a higher
  high — S3's explicit DENIAL branch. Reclassifies the decline as a Phase B swing or, if the TR was
  accumulation, as a spring/LPS.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — `tr_support`; the *prior rally leg's* spread and volume retained as
  the comparison basis (**not** a rolling window — S1 is explicit); a forward confirmation window for
  the subsequent rally. **Strictly sequence-dependent both backward (needs the TR) and forward
  (needs the next rally to confirm).** A screener can therefore only ever emit a *provisional* SOW.
- **conflicts**: S3 collapses UT into SOW+LPSY (see UT entry), so under S3 an SOW may already have
  occurred at the moment a UT is labelled; S2 sequences them separately. S2 places the "initial
  SOW(s)" alongside the AR as the change-of-character marker; S1 places the SOW at point 10, i.e.
  in Phase C after Phase B's standoff.

## Last Point of Supply (LPSY)

- **origin / source_name**: S1 (Schematics 2 and 3, points 11/13/15/17); S2; S3.
- **definition**: The feeble rally after an SOW; the last distribution before markdown, and the
  preferred short entry. Phase D. S2: "**LPSY—last point of supply**. After testing support on a
  SOW, a feeble rally on narrow spread shows that the market is having considerable difficulty
  advancing. This inability to rally may be due to weak demand, substantial supply or both. LPSYs
  represent exhaustion of demand and the last waves of large operators' distribution before markdown
  begins in earnest."
- **criteria**:
  - **must be preceded by an SOW** — value: null — "The reaction preceding the (LPSY) must be a (SOW)." (S3) — confidence high — **a hard ordering constraint, stated as "must".**
  - **the full required sequence** — value: null — "The (LPSY) is proceeded by the (PSY), (BC), (AR), (ST), (SOW) and then the (LPSY) occurs." (S3) — confidence high — **the only place in the corpus where a complete mandatory event chain is written out.**
  - narrow spread on the rally — value: null — "a feeble rally on narrow spread" (S2); "a feeble rally attempt on narrow spread shows us the difficulty the market is having in making a further rise" (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` ratio.
  - **volume may be light OR heavy** — value: null — "Volume may be light or heavy, showing weak demand or substantial supply." (S1) — confidence high — **explicitly non-discriminating on volume; only spread and failure-to-advance matter.**
  - **often stops just below the halfway point** — value: **½ (50%) of the preceding down-leg** — "The (LPSY) rally will often stop just below the ½ way point. It is NOT particularly important where a (LPSY) stops on the rally, however, it very often turns in the vicinity of that that ½ way point." (S3) — confidence med — computable: `halfway = (leg_high + leg_low)/2`. Note the source hedges twice ("often", "NOT particularly important"), so this is a tendency, not a gate. S3's halfway arithmetic is spelled out: "if a stock moves down from $30 to $21 a distance of nine points. The half-way-point would be at half of that distance and is $4 ½ points added to $21 gives a half-way-point of $25 ½."
  - often returns to the PSY or BC price area — value: null — "Very often the (LPSY) will come in the same area as the (PSY) or the (BC)." (S3) — confidence med — `missing:` tolerance.
  - after the ice breaks, the LPSY is a rally thwarted at the ice from below — value: null — "after a break through the ice, a rally attempt is thwarted at the ice's surface (now resistance). The rally meets a last wave of supply before markdown ensues." (S1) — confidence high — computable: `rally_high ≈ tr_support` (former support, now resistance).
  - multiple LPSYs — value: null — S1 marks LPSYs at points 11, 13, 15 and 17 across Schematics 2 and 3 — confidence high.
  - if the SOW is denied, take no position — value: null — "When this occurs DO NOT take a SHORT POSITION. It is better to miss a move then to run a great risk of being wrong." (S3) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: The rally comes on widening spread and increased volume and exceeds the prior
  high — the SOW is denied and no LPSY exists. Reclassifies the TR toward accumulation.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — SOW already detected and *confirmed* (**strictly sequence-dependent
  on the longest chain in the corpus: PSY → BC → AR → ST → SOW → LPSY**); the prior down-leg retained
  to compute the halfway point; `tr_support` retained as the former-support-now-resistance level;
  narrow-spread test. Because S1 says volume "may be light or heavy", **do not gate the LPSY on
  volume** — that would reject half the published cases. This is the clearest example in the corpus
  of a criterion that must be left out of a detector precisely because the source refuses to
  constrain it.
- **conflicts**: S3 requires the full six-event chain; S2 requires only "After testing support on a
  SOW". S1's Schematic 2 places an LPSY at point 11 *before* the ice breaks and another at point 13
  *after* it — two structurally different LPSYs sharing one label. S3's UT entry says a UT is itself
  an LPSY, which would let an LPSY precede any SOW — contradicting S3's own "must be a (SOW)" rule
  in the LPSY entry. Record all three.

# PHASES

## Phase A — Accumulation

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S9 (Wyckoff Analytics, "the stopping phase").
- **definition**: The stopping of the prior downtrend and the establishment of the TR boundaries.
  S2: "Phase A marks the stopping of the prior downtrend. Up to this point, supply has been dominant.
  The approaching diminution of supply is evidenced in preliminary support (PS) and a selling climax
  (SC)... The lows of the SC and the ST and the high of the AR set the boundaries of the TR."
  S9: "Collectively, these four elements form our 'ideal' version of Phase A—the stopping phase."
- **criteria**:
  - contains PS, SC, AR, ST — value: null — S9 enumerates "four (4) distinct steps at the beginning" — confidence high.
  - the SC/ST lows and AR high define the TR — value: null — "The lows of the Selling Climax (SC) and the Secondary Test, and the high of the Automatic Rally (AR) initially set the boundaries of the trading range." (S1) — confidence high — **directly computable; this is the definition of `tr_support` and `tr_resistance`.**
  - **Phase A may complete without climactic action** — value: null — "It is also possible that Phase A can end without dramatic changes in spread and volume. However, it is usually better if it does, in that more dramatic selling will generally clear out all the sellers and pave the way for a more pronounced and sustained markup." (S1) — confidence high.
  - **in re-accumulation, PS/SC/ST are absent** — value: null — "In a re-accumulation TR (which occurs during a longer-term uptrend), the points representing PS, SC and ST are not evident in Phase A. Rather, in such cases, Phase A resembles that more typically seen in distribution." (S2) — confidence high — **a detector that requires PS+SC+ST will find zero re-accumulation bases.**
  - Phase A is COCH 1 — value: null — "The beginning of the structure is marked by a series of events that represent an initial change of character ('COCH 1')... For all structures, COCH 1 is what stops the prior movement of price." (S9) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price breaks decisively below the SC/ST lows on expanding volume — the downtrend
  was not stopped and the "TR" was a pause. Reclassifies as continued markdown; a new Phase A must
  be sought lower.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — trend classification (prior downtrend); the four events above;
  pivot detection. Phase A is the **only phase detectable without prior phase state**, and it is what
  bootstraps `tr_support`/`tr_resistance` for everything downstream. Re-accumulation handling is a
  separate code path (S2). Weekly resampling helps identify "prolonged down-move".
- **conflicts**: S9 labels the first element "Preliminary Supply ('PS')" inside the accumulation
  schematic where S2/S1 say "Preliminary Support". S4 (Fraser) begins Phase A at the SC and does not
  require a PS at all: "Look for very large spikes in volume and an acceleration of the stock price
  in to a low which marks the start of the Accumulation range. This is the Selling Climax (SCLX).
  After the Automatic Rally and Secondary Test, Phase A is complete."

## Phase B — Accumulation

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S3 (TR behaviour); S4.
- **definition**: The cause-building consolidation. S2: "Phase B serves the function of 'building a
  cause' for a new uptrend... In Phase B, institutions and large professional interests are
  accumulating relatively low-priced inventory in anticipation of the next markup."
- **criteria**:
  - supply and demand in equilibrium, no decisive trend — value: null — "In Phase B, Supply and Demand on a major basis are in equilibrium and there is no decisive trend." (S1) — confidence high.
  - **early swings wide and volume high/erratic; late swings narrow and volume dries up** — value: null — "In the early stages of Phase B, the price swings tend to be rather wide, and volume is usually greater and more erratic. As the TR unfolds, supply becomes weaker and demand stronger as professionals are absorbing supply. The closer you get to the end or to leaving the TR, volume tends to diminish." (S1); S3's TR entry says the same at length — confidence high — **this is computable as a *trend in* volatility and volume across the TR (regression slope of `spread` and `volume` vs bar index), which is a far better Phase-B signature than any single-bar rule.**
  - **may take a year or more** — value: **≥ 1 year, sometimes** — "The process of institutional accumulation may take a long time (sometimes a year or more)" (S2) — confidence med — a duration *ceiling illustration*, not a floor: "sometimes" means the corpus publishes **no minimum TR duration for accumulation anywhere.** `missing:` a minimum bar count for a valid Phase B — this is the single most consequential unpublished number for a screener, since it decides what counts as a base at all.
  - multiple STs and upthrust-type actions occur — value: null — "There are usually multiple STs during Phase B, as well as upthrust-type actions at the upper end of the TR." (S2) — confidence high — **UTs inside an accumulation Phase B are normal and must not flip the state machine to distribution.**
  - Phase B is volatile and discouraging — value: null — "The B Phase is a volatile and aimless trading range which discourages stock holders to the point they give up and sell their shares." (S4) — confidence high.
  - support/resistance lines contain the action — value: null — "Support and resistance lines, (shown as horizontal lines A, B, C, and D on the Accumulation Schematic 1) usually contain the price action in Phase B" (S1) — confidence high — note S1's schematic uses **four** lines (A and B for support, C and D for resistance), i.e. support and resistance are *bands*, not lines. S9 agrees: "areas (not exact lines, but ranges) of support and resistance".
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: A decisive breakdown below the TR on expanding volume with no recovery — the TR
  was distribution or a mere pause. Note S3: "the T/R maybe accumulation, distribution, or nothing."
- **detection_notes**: Requires — Phase A complete (**sequence-dependent**); the contraction trend in
  `spread` and `volume` over the TR (the strongest computable Phase B signal); a swing counter; a
  duration counter. Not computable: "institutions are accumulating" — the attribution. **The most
  important detection consequence is negative: no bar-level rule identifies Phase B. It is
  identified by a *trend across many bars*, so a per-bar classifier cannot express it.**
- **conflicts**: S1 draws four boundary lines; S2 draws two (support, resistance). S2 mentions the
  duration ("a year or more"); S1 gives none.

## Phase C — Accumulation

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S9; S4/S5.
- **definition**: The decisive test of remaining supply. S2: "It is in Phase C that the stock price
  goes through a decisive test of the remaining supply, allowing the 'smart money' operators to
  ascertain whether the stock is ready to be marked up." S9: "COCH 2, begins the structural area
  identified as Phase C. Phase C is a point at which (a) we change from a testing phase; and (b)
  transition to a rising price period in which Demand generally overcomes Supply."
- **criteria**:
  - contains a spring/shakeout, **or** a test higher in the range — value: null — "The stock may begin to come out of the TR on the upside with higher tops and bottoms or it may go through a downside spring or shakeout, breaking previous supports. This latter test is preferred, given that it does a better job of cleaning out remaining supply from weak holders and creates a false impression as to the direction of the ultimate move." (S1) — confidence high.
  - **without a spring, Phase C is hard to identify** — value: null — "the testing of supply can occur higher up in the TR without a spring or shakeout; when this occurs, the identification of Phase C can be challenging." (S2) — confidence high — an unusually candid admission of non-detectability.
  - the SOS after the spring validates the analysis — value: null — "The appearance of a SOS shortly after a spring or shakeout validates the analysis." (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` "shortly" in bars.
  - **you cannot know the TR is accumulation until Phase C** — value: null — "Until this testing process, we cannot be sure the TR is accumulation and must wait to take a position until there is sufficient evidence that mark-up is about to begin." (S1) — confidence high — **this is the method's own statement that a Phases-A-and-B-only detector cannot classify a base.**
  - first entry at the shakeout, better entry at the test — value: null — "The shakeout at point 8 on our Schematic 1 represents our first prescribed place to initiate a long position. The secondary test at point 10 is better, since a low volume pullback and a specific low risk stop or danger point at point 8 gives us greater evidence and more confidence to act." (S1) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Supply persists after the test; price re-enters and stays in the range. S4's
  relabelling rule is explicit: "If weakness persists with widening volume and price spread, then we
  would conclude that more supply is arriving which could drive prices downward. If this second
  scenario unfolds then we would relabel Phase C and D as a continuation of Phase B, with the final
  testing still ahead." **Phase labels are retractable; a state machine must support rollback.**
- **detection_notes**: Requires — Phase A boundaries and a developed Phase B (**strictly
  sequence-dependent**); spring detection OR the higher-low variant (Fraser's LPS); TR-elapsed
  fraction. **Because S2 concedes the no-spring variant is "challenging" to identify, a screener
  should treat spring-present Phase C as detectable and spring-absent Phase C as low-confidence.**
- **conflicts**: **S5 (Fraser) vs S2 (Bogomazov) on what Phase C contains** — Fraser: "There are two
  types of tests; the Spring (or Shakeout) and the Last Point of Support (LPS)... One or the other
  will occur." Bogomazov/S2: Phase C contains the spring; the LPS belongs to Phase D after the SOS.
  Also S4 places the *end* of Phase C at the jump — "After a Test of the Spring, price jumps the
  overhead line of Support (blue line). This concludes the final testing of Phase C" — putting the
  SOS/jump at the Phase C/D boundary, whereas S1 puts the SOS as the thing that "will bring us into
  Phase D" and S2 places SOSs *within* Phase D.

## Phase D — Accumulation

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1; S4.
- **definition**: Demand's consistent dominance; the advance out of the range with pullbacks that
  hold. S2: "If we are correct in our analysis, what should follow is the consistent dominance of
  demand over supply. This is evidenced by a pattern of advances (SOSs) on widening price spreads and
  increasing volume, as well as reactions (LPSs) on smaller spreads and diminished volumes. During
  Phase D, the price will move at least to the top of the TR."
- **criteria**:
  - advances on widening spread and increasing volume — value: null — verbatim above (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` ratios.
  - reactions on smaller spreads and diminished volumes — value: null — same (S2) — confidence high.
  - **price moves at least to the top of the TR** — value: null — "During Phase D, the price will move at least to the top of the TR." (S2) — confidence high — computable: `high >= tr_resistance`.
  - if the pattern fails, do not add and consider closing — value: null — "If this pattern does not occur, then we are advised not to add to our position and look to close our original position until we have more conclusive evidence that markup is beginning." (S1) — confidence high.
  - Phase D ends after the second SOS/BUEC pair — value: null — "Phase D concludes after SOS 2 and BUEC 2." (S4) — confidence med — Fraser's chart-specific reading, not a general rule.
  - entries at the BUEC and at the more important support/resistance point — value: null — "this opportunity comes at point 12 on the 'pullback to support' after 'jumping resistance'... Another similar opportunity comes at point 14, a more important point of support and resistance." (S1) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: A reaction on wide spread and high volume that re-enters the TR. Per S3 this
  cancels the SOS; per S4 it relabels Phases C and D as Phase B.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — Phase C complete (**strictly sequence-dependent**); alternating
  leg classification (advance vs reaction) with spread/volume characterisation per leg; comparison
  to `tr_resistance`. A practical implementation is a **leg-alternation test**: for consecutive
  swings, `up_leg.spread_avg > down_leg.spread_avg AND up_leg.vol_avg > down_leg.vol_avg`. That is
  computable from daily OHLCV plus pivot detection and captures S2's criterion directly.
- **conflicts**: S1 places the LPS entries at points 12 and 14 within Phase D (consistent with S2);
  S5 (Fraser) puts the *first* LPS in Phase C. See LPS.

## Phase E — Accumulation

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1.
- **definition**: Markup; price leaves the TR. S2: "In Phase E, the stock leaves the TR, demand is in
  full control and the markup is obvious to everyone. Setbacks, such as shakeouts and more typical
  reactions, are usually short-lived."
- **criteria**:
  - price is out of the TR and demand controls — value: null — "the stock leaves the TR and demand is in control" (S1) — confidence high.
  - setbacks are unpronounced and short-lived — value: null — "Setbacks are unpronounced and short lived." (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` duration or depth.
  - **new higher TRs (re-accumulation / "stepping stones") may form at any point** — value: null — "New, higher-level TRs comprising both profit-taking and acquisition of additional shares ('re-accumulation') by large operators can occur at any point in Phase E. These TRs are sometimes called 'stepping stones' on the way to even higher price targets." (S2) — confidence high — **a screener must expect its own accumulation detector to re-fire inside Phase E; that is correct behaviour, not a false positive.**
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price re-enters and stays inside the old TR — markup failed; the TR was not
  accumulation.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — Phase D complete (**sequence-dependent**); a sustained-close-above
  test. Phase E's practical value for a screener is the stepping-stone rule: a fresh, shorter TR
  above a prior base is a *confirming* count, not a new independent base (see Cause and Effect).
- **conflicts**: None material found.

## Phase A — Distribution

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1.
- **definition**: Stopping of the prior uptrend; TR boundaries set by BC high and AR low. S2:
  "Phase A in a distribution TR marks the stopping of the prior uptrend. Up to this point, demand has
  been dominant and the first significant evidence of supply entering the market is provided by
  preliminary supply (PSY) and the buying climax (BC). These events are usually followed by an
  automatic reaction (AR) and a secondary test (ST) of the BC, often upon diminished volume."
- **criteria**:
  - contains PSY, BC, AR, ST — value: null — verbatim above (S2) — confidence high.
  - **may terminate without climactic action** — value: null — "the uptrend may also terminate without climactic action, instead demonstrating exhaustion of demand with decreasing spread and volume; less upward progress is made on each rally before significant supply emerges." (S2) — confidence high — **the non-climactic variant is defined by *decreasing thrust per rally*, which is computable (S3's "Thrust" measure: "the price difference between consecutive tops in up trends").**
  - **in re-distribution, Phase A may look like accumulation** — value: null — "In a redistribution TR within a larger downtrend, Phase A may look more like the start of an accumulation TR (e.g., with climactic price and volume action to the downside)." (S2) — confidence high — **a symmetric trap to the re-accumulation case: Phase A alone cannot tell accumulation from redistribution.**
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price breaks above the BC high on expanding volume and holds — the uptrend was
  not stopped.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — prior-uptrend test; the four events; pivot detection. Like
  accumulation Phase A, this is the **bootstrap phase and is not event-sequence-dependent**. The
  non-climactic variant needs a *thrust series*: `thrust[i] = pivot_high[i] - pivot_high[i-1]`,
  tested for decline. That is computable and is materially different from any volume rule.
- **conflicts**: See PSY — S3 orders BC-then-PSY where S2/S1 order PSY-then-BC.

## Phase B — Distribution

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1.
- **definition**: Cause-building for the downtrend. S2: "The function of Phase B is to build a cause
  in preparation for a new downtrend. During this time, institutions and large professional interests
  are disposing of their long inventory and initiating short positions in anticipation of the next
  markdown."
- **criteria**:
  - same structural points as accumulation Phase B, with the tilt toward supply — value: null — "The points to be made here about Phase B are the same as those made for Phase B within Accumulation, except clues may begin to surface here of the supply/demand balance moving toward supply instead of demand." (S1) — confidence high.
  - large interests are net sellers — value: null — "the large interests are net sellers of shares as the TR evolves, with the goal of exhausting as much of the remaining demand as possible" (S2) — confidence high — **not computable from OHLCV.**
  - SOWs come with significantly increased spread and volume — value: null — "SOWs are usually accompanied by significantly increased spread and volume to the downside." (S2) — confidence high — `missing:` ratios.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: The range resolves upward on good spread and volume — it was re-accumulation.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — distribution Phase A complete (**sequence-dependent**); the same
  contraction/expansion trend measures as accumulation Phase B, but with the asymmetry that
  distribution ranges are shorter (S1: "distribution is generally accomplished in a shorter time
  period than accumulation"). **Accumulation Phase B and distribution Phase B are not separable by
  their own bars** — only by what the prior trend was and by what Phase C does.
- **conflicts**: None material beyond the shared Phase B ambiguity.

## Phase C — Distribution

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1 (Schematics 2 and 3 give two different Phase C forms).
- **definition**: The test of remaining demand — via UT/UTAD (Schematic 3) or via an SOW that breaks
  the Phase B standoff (Schematic 2). S1: "One of the ways Phase C reveals itself after the standoff
  in Phase B is by the 'sign of weakness' (SOW) shown at point 10 on Schematic 2... Looking now at
  Schematic 3, Phase C may also reveal itself by a pronounced move upward, breaking through the highs
  of the TR. This is shown at point 11 as an 'Upthrust After Distribution' (UTAD)."
- **criteria**:
  - Schematic 2 form: an SOW on increased spread and volume that breaks the standoff — value: null — "This SOW is usually accompanied by significantly increased spread and volume to the downside that seems to break the standoff in Phase B." (S1) — confidence high.
  - Schematic 3 form: a UTAD above the TR highs — value: null — verbatim above (S1) — confidence high.
  - **a third, unschematised variant exists** — value: null — "A third variation not shown here in schematic form would be an upthrust above the highs of the trading range with a quick fall back into the middle of the TR, but where the TR did not fully represent distribution. In this case, the TR would likely be too wide to fully represent distribution and there would be a lack of concentrated selling except in the latter portions of the TR." (S1) — confidence high — `missing:` "too wide" as a number.
  - a UT of a lower high can serve when demand is very weak — value: null — "Phase C's test of demand may be represented by a UT of a lower high within the TR." (S2) — confidence high.
  - aggressive shorts here get stopped out repeatedly — value: null — "the 'smart money' repeatedly stops out traders who initiate such short positions with one UT after another, so it is often safer to wait until Phase D and an LPSY." (S2) — confidence high — **a qualitative statement about failure frequency with no measurement behind it; treat as an assertion, not a hit-rate.**
- **measured_performance**: none published. (The "repeatedly stops out" claim above is the closest
  the corpus comes to a frequency statement and it carries no sample, period, or base rate.)
- **invalidation**: Price holds above the TR after the upthrust — demand was real; the TR was
  accumulation. Or the SOW is denied by a strong subsequent rally (S3's denial rule).
- **detection_notes**: Requires — distribution Phase A and B (**strictly sequence-dependent**);
  **at least three alternative detectors** (SOW form, UTAD form, low-high UT form), which is why
  distribution Phase C has materially lower detectability than accumulation Phase C. `tr_mean_close`
  is needed for the UTAD confirmation.
- **conflicts**: S1 offers two schematics with *different* Phase C events (SOW vs UTAD) and names a
  third variant it declines to schematise. S2 presents only the UT/UTAD form in its Phase C prose.
  A detector implementing only S2 will miss S1's Schematic 2 entirely.

## Phase D — Distribution

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1.
- **definition**: Supply clearly dominant; the LPSY rallies. S2: "During Phase D, price travels to or
  through TR support. The evidence that supply is clearly dominant increases either with a clear
  break of support or with a decline below the mid-point of the TR after a UT or UTAD."
- **criteria**:
  - price travels to or through TR support — value: null — verbatim above (S2) — confidence high — computable.
  - **or declines below the mid-point of the TR after a UT/UTAD** — value: **the TR mid-point (50% of TR height)** — "a decline below the mid-point of the TR after a UT or UTAD" (S2) — confidence high — **computable exactly: `(tr_resistance + tr_support)/2`. One of the few explicit geometric levels in the corpus.**
  - multiple weak rallies (LPSYs) occur — value: null — "There are often multiple weak rallies within Phase D; these LPSYs represent excellent opportunities to initiate or add to profitable short positions." (S2) — confidence high.
  - short entries at points 13, 15, 17 — value: null — "Our best opportunities are at points 13, 15, and 17 as represented on our Schematics 2 and 3." (S1) — confidence high.
  - add only if already profitable — value: null — "It is important that additional short positions be added or pyramided only if our initial positions are in profit." (S1) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price recovers above the TR mid-point and then above resistance on good volume —
  supply was not dominant.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — Phase C complete (**sequence-dependent**); `tr_midpoint`;
  `tr_support`; rally-leg weakness characterisation. The midpoint rule is the single cleanest
  computable phase-transition trigger anywhere in the corpus and should be implemented literally.
- **conflicts**: S1's Phase D language ("a break through the 'ice' or ... a further SOW into the TR
  after an upthrust") matches S2's, but S1 does not state the midpoint rule; S2 adds it. Record S2
  as the source of the midpoint number.

## Phase E — Distribution

- **origin / source_name**: S2; S1.
- **definition**: Markdown. S2: "Phase E depicts the unfolding of the downtrend; the stock leaves the
  TR and supply is in control. Once TR support is broken on a major SOW, this breakdown is often
  tested with a rally that fails at or near support."
- **criteria**:
  - price out of the TR, supply in control — value: null — "the stock or commodity leaves the TR and supply is in control." (S1) — confidence high.
  - the breakdown is retested by a failing rally — value: null — "this breakdown is often tested with a rally that fails at or near support. This also represents a high-probability opportunity to sell short." (S2) — confidence high — computable: `rally_high ≈ tr_support` from below. Note "high-probability" is asserted with no number.
  - rallies are feeble — value: null — "Rallies are usually feeble." (S1) — confidence high.
  - climactic action later may start a new TR — value: null — "After a significant down-move, climactic action may signal the beginning of a re-distribution TR or of accumulation." (S2) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Price recovers back inside the TR and holds — the breakdown was a spring, and the

- **detection_notes**: Requires — Phase D complete (**sequence-dependent**); `tr_support` retained as

- **conflicts**: None material found.

# CONCEPTS

## The Composite Man / Composite Operator (C.O.)

- **origin / source_name**: Richard D. Wyckoff, as quoted by S2 from *The Richard D. Wyckoff Course
  in Stock Market Science and Technique*, section 9, pp. 1-2; S1 (which uses "Composite operator");
  S6 (Fraser, "C.O.").
- **definition**: A heuristic personification of all large-operator activity, used so that the
  analyst reads the chart as one coherent campaign rather than as noise. Not an event; a framing
  device that underlies every event's *interpretation*. Wyckoff, quoted in S2: *"…all the
  fluctuations in the market and in all the various stocks should be studied as if they were the
  result of one man's operations. Let us call him the Composite Man, who, in theory, sits behind the
  scenes and manipulates the stocks to your disadvantage if you do not understand the game as he
  plays it; and to your great profit if you do understand it."*
- **criteria**:
  - the Composite Man plans, executes and concludes campaigns — value: null — "The Composite Man carefully plans, executes, and concludes his campaigns." (S2) — confidence high — not computable.
  - he advertises by creating the appearance of a broad market — value: null — "attracts the public to buy a stock in which he has accumulated a sizeable line of shares by making many transactions involving many shares, in effect advertising his stock by creating the appearance of a 'broad market.'" (S2) — confidence high — the *only* partially computable trace is a rise in turnover with little net price progress.
  - whether the moves are real or artificial does not matter — value: null — Wyckoff, quoted in S2: it doesn't matter if market moves "are real or artificial; that is, the result of actual buying and selling by the public and bona fide investors or artificial buying and selling by larger operators." — confidence high.
  - accumulation = absorption = removing stock from the market — value: null — "Absorption is the activity of building a stock position, and thus removing stock from the marketplace." (S6) — confidence high.
  - the ascending-bottoms logic derives from the Composite Operator — value: null — "The logic for ascending bottoms amid descending peaks is rooted in the concept of the Composite operator... as the trading range proceeds, the new schematic reveals that fewer and fewer sellers remain to propel stocks downward in price. As a consequence, the downwaves become shorter and shorter in length (the bottoms rise)" (S1) — confidence high — **this IS computable: a rising sequence of pivot lows inside a TR.**
- **measured_performance**: none published; the Composite Man is explicitly a heuristic ("Wyckoff
  proposed a heuristic device", S2), not a testable claim.
- **invalidation**: Not applicable — it is a framing device, not an event. It cannot be falsified by
  price action.
- **detection_notes**: **Not computable from OHLCV, and this is the honest answer.** The only
  operationalisable residue is S1's *Accumulation Gradient of Rising Bottoms* — a new schematic
  Pruden and von Lichtenstein introduced in this very paper to fill what they call "an obvious gap in
  the conceptual body of the Wyckoff Method" ("there are currently two Schematics for distribution,
  but only one Schematic for accumulation"). Its detector is simple and worth implementing:
  *ascending pivot lows inside a TR whose pivot highs are flat or descending.* Everything else about
  the Composite Man — intent, campaign, manipulation — is unobservable in bars.
- **conflicts**: S1 uses "Composite operator" / "composite man" interchangeably; S2 uses "Composite
  Man"; S6 uses "Composite Operator (C.O.)". Same concept, three names — a naming hazard for any
  glossary. S1 additionally grounds the concept in Cusp Catastrophe Theory ("the Delay Rule and the
  Maxwell Rule followed by the Maxwell Principle"), which no other source mentions.

## The Law of Effort versus Result

- **origin / source_name**: S2 (Wyckoff Law #3); S1; S3; S6 (the richest worked example).
- **definition**: Volume is effort, price movement is result; divergence between them warns of a
  trend change. Applies at every point in the schematic, not at one event. S2: "**3. The law of
  effort versus result** provides an early warning of a possible trend change in the near future.
  Divergences between volume and price often signal a change in the direction of a price trend."
- **criteria**:
  - high volume with narrow range after a rally = distribution — value: null — "when there are several high-volume (large effort) but narrow-range price bars after a substantial rally, with the price failing to make a new high (little or no result), it suggests that big interests are unloading shares in anticipation of a change in trend." (S2) — confidence high — computable: `vol_ratio` high AND `spread_ratio` low AND no new high.
  - high volume with little price change at TR support = absorption, bullish — value: null — "If we observe this price-volume behavior in a reaction to support in an accumulation trading range, this indicates absorption of supply by large interests, and is considered bullish." (S2) — confidence high — **note the identical bar signature is bearish at resistance and bullish at support: location decides the sign.**
  - **the worked arithmetic** — value: **2-point spread on 10,000 shares, then ½-point spread on 20,000 shares** — "If a stock has been moving up every day with a two point spread every day with ten thousand shares and it breaks into the high ground with twenty thousand shares and a half point spread for a couple of days straight we know supply is coming in and is overcoming the demand. This is an effort that is not having an proportionate result" (S3) — confidence high — **the usable primitive is `spread / volume` per bar, compared leg-over-leg. S3 gives the numbers as an example, not a threshold.**
  - large result on modest effort is bullish early in a bull market — value: null — "This is a large Result with modest Effort which is considered bullish early in a new bull market." (S6) — confidence high — **this inverts the naive "breakouts need volume" rule and is the reason a volume-expansion gate on the SOS is dangerous.**
  - inharmonious action can also appear as decelerating price on rising volume — value: null — "There is a three week dive to a new low in November 2008. Each week is on high volume and note that the spread of each bar is less than into the climax low in October... Effort in volume is not Resulting in dramatically in lower prices, again this is inharmonious. This is early evidence of stopping action" (S6) — confidence high — computable on weekly bars.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Effort and result in proportion ("harmonious") means no warning — the trend
  continues. S3: "if you have an effort expressed the result should be in proportion to the effort."
- **detection_notes**: Requires — `spread/volume` ratio per bar and per swing leg; a leg-segmentation
  primitive (pivots); and, critically, **a location test** (near support vs near resistance vs open
  territory), because the same divergence has opposite meanings in different places. Weekly
  resampling is used by S6 throughout and is arguably the natural timeframe for this law. Fully
  computable from daily OHLCV once legs and TR boundaries exist — this is the most implementable of
  the three laws.
- **conflicts**: S6's bullish "declining volume on the markup" reading directly opposes the
  volume-expansion requirement stated for SOS in S1/S2/S3. Both are recorded; the reconciliation
  S6 offers is that after complete absorption there is little stock left to trade, so volume need not
  expand. A detector cannot serve both rules simultaneously without a mode switch.

## The Law of Cause and Effect and the Point-and-Figure Horizontal Count

- **origin / source_name**: S2 (Wyckoff Law #2 and the "Wyckoff Point and Figure (P&F) Count Guide"
  — the most detailed published procedure found); S1; S3; S7 (a second worked example with
  different numbers); S5/S6 (Fraser's practical use).
- **definition**: The horizontal extent of the TR is the *cause*; the subsequent move is the
  *effect*. S2: "Wyckoff's 'cause' can be measured by the horizontal point count in a Point and
  Figure chart. The 'effect' is the distance price moves corresponding to the point count." S3:
  "in order to have an effect you must first build a cause. The effect will be in direct proportion
  to the cause and cannot be separated from the cause."
- **criteria**:
  - **box size by price band** — value: **0.5–1 point for low-priced stocks; 5 points for stocks over $200; 100 points for the DJIA** — "for low-priced stocks, the box size could be 0.5 to 1 point, whereas for high-priced (> $200) stocks, a box size of 5 points would be more appropriate. The box size for the Dow Jones Industrial Average could be 100 points." (S2) — confidence high — note "could be", i.e. a guideline.
  - **the count is taken at the LPS level, right to left** — value: null — "After identifying a sign of strength (SOS) towards the right side of the TR on the bar chart, locate the last point at which support was met on a reaction—the last point of support (LPS). Locate this point on your P&F chart also and count from right to left at the price level of the LPS" (S2) — confidence high — **the count is sequence-dependent: it cannot be taken until an SOS and an LPS exist.**
  - **never add a partial phase** — value: null — "In moving to the left, turn to your bar chart and divide the area of accumulation into phases, adding one complete phase at a time... Never add only part of a P&F phase to your count." (S2) — confidence high.
  - **P&F phases are NOT Phases A–E** — value: null — "Note that P&F phases are NOT the same as Phases A – E used in the analysis of trading ranges described in previous sections" (S2) — confidence high — **a naming trap that will silently corrupt an implementation.**
  - **the projection arithmetic** — value: **columns × box size × reversal metric** — "tally the number of columns at the level of the count line, multiply that sum by 100 (the box size) and 3 (the reversal metric), then add this product to the count line (producing the maximum price objective), the low of the trading range (minimum price objective) and the half-way point." (S2) — confidence high — fully computable.
  - **a second worked example with different numbers** — value: **20 columns × 10 points × 3 = 600, target 670 + 600 = 1270; 42 columns × 3 × 10 = 1260, target ≈1930** — "Based on 10 points per box and a 3-box reversal setting, the projected advance would be 600 points (20 x 10 x 3 = 600) and the target would be around 1270 (670 + 600 = 1270). The second pattern is much bigger and extends some 42 columns for a projected advance of 1260 points (42 x 3 x 10)." (S7) — confidence high.
  - column-count guidance — value: **ten columns as the wide/narrow divide** — "a long base extending over ten P&F columns would project a relatively high target upon a breakout. Conversely, a narrow base covering just six columns would project a relatively low target... An extended top covering over ten P&F columns would project a much deeper decline than a narrow top extending less than ten columns." (S7) — confidence med.
  - **targets are not reversal points** — value: null — "Price targets derived from Wyckoff P&F counts represent points where you should 'stop, look and listen.' These targets should never be looked upon as exact points of where a trend will change" (S2) — confidence high.
  - stepping-stone counts confirm the original — value: null — "Very often, this will produce a 'stepping stone confirming count' of the original P&F count... as the price target projected from the stepping-stone TR approaches that of the original TR, the upward or downward trend may be ready to resume." (S2) — confidence high.
  - a spring may itself serve as the LPS for counting — value: null — "A spring may also serve as the LPS from the perspective of the P&F count. Usually a spring is followed by an SOS, and the low of the reaction following that SOS is also a valid LPS." (S2) — confidence high.
  - **the reward/risk gate** — value: **3:1** — "Estimated upside profit potential is at least three times the loss if the initial stop-loss were hit" (S2, Buying Test 9); "Should never be less than 3:1, This means that for every point below the purchase price the stop is placed, three points of profit are anticipated. So for a stop that is placed three points below the purchase price, nine points of profit are anticipated." (S3) — confidence high.
- **measured_performance**: **none published.** No source reports how often P&F counts are reached,
  by how much they overshoot or undershoot, or over what sample. S7 concedes only qualitatively:
  "Some counts fall short of their targets, while some counts exceed their targets." That is the
  entire published evidence base for the method's price-objective machinery.
- **invalidation**: A count is not invalidated by price action in any published way — S2 explicitly
  demotes targets to attention-zones. The count *procedure* is invalidated by counting a partial
  phase or by counting from a level other than the LPS.
- **detection_notes**: Requires — P&F construction from daily OHLCV (box size and reversal are free
  parameters, though S2 gives price-banded guidance); a column counter at a specified price line;
  and **the LPS and SOS must already be detected**, so the count is at the very end of the
  sequence-dependency chain. Fully computable in principle. Two traps: (a) box size materially
  changes the count, and S2's guidance is "could be", not a rule — so the projection is not a
  well-defined function of the price series alone; (b) "P&F phases" must not be conflated with
  Phases A–E. Also note S2's smaller-box rule for stepping stones: "it is preferable to use a smaller
  box size to measure P&F counts within the former."
- **conflicts**: S2 counts at the **LPS** level and adds the product to three anchors (count line =
  maximum, TR low = minimum, halfway = conservative). S7 counts the pattern's **full column width**
  from entry column to exit column and adds to a single anchor. These are different procedures
  yielding different numbers on the same chart. S1 says only "could be projected by using the
  Wyckoff point and figure method" without specifying either. Record all three.

## Trading-Range Boundaries: the Creek, the Ice, Support and Resistance

- **origin / source_name**: S1 (Robert G. Evans' metaphors, transmitted by Pruden); S3 (the fullest
  definitions); S2; S11.
- **definition**: The TR's ceiling and floor, drawn as *freehand wavy lines* through rally peaks (the
  creek) and reaction lows (the ice) — deliberately not horizontal lines. S1: "The 'CREEK' is an
  analogy to a wavy line of resistance drawn loosely across rally peaks within the trading range.
  There are of course minor lines of resistance and more significant ones that will have to be
  crossed before the market's journey can continue onward and upward." S1: "The ice… is an analogy to
  a wavy line of support drawn loosely under reaction lows of the Trading Range. A break through the
  ice will likely be followed by attempts to get back above it. A failure to get back above firm
  support may mean a 'drowning' for the market."
- **criteria**:
  - creek = the flow of supply across the top of the TR — value: null — "relates to the flow of supply across the top of the trading range. The creek itself is a wiggly, squiggly trend line drawn freehand through the tops of the rallies within that trading range." (S3) — confidence high — **explicitly freehand; not a computable object as defined.**
  - ice = former support at the bottom of the TR that becomes resistance — value: null — "The ICE is the FORMER SUPPORT AREA at the BOTTOM of the T/R which BECOMES a SUPPLY AREA. The ICE is shown by drawing a wiggley trendline across the various support points at the bottom of the range." (S3) — confidence high.
  - **minor vs major creek** — value: null — "Minor Creek: lower branch, The minor creek very often occurs in the lower or the middle part of the T/R but is still somewhere in the T/R." / "Major Creek: upper branch, The jump of the major creek is a larger move and it very often carries the stock above the old supply levels in the T/R often into new high ground." (S3) — confidence high — **there are at least two creeks per TR; a single resistance level under-models the structure.**
  - the creek is where the volume came in — value: null — "the CREEK is WHERE the VOLUME CAME IN, where the EFFORT the PUSH the POWER came in." (S3) — confidence med — circular but suggests a *volume-weighted* resistance level rather than a price extreme.
  - **there is no exact way to draw it** — value: null — "It is important to recognize that there is no one exact way of drawing these creeks." (S3) — confidence high — **the source disclaiming determinism.**
  - boundaries are bands, not lines — value: null — "areas (not exact lines, but ranges) of support and resistance" (S9); S1's Schematic 1 uses four lines: "Lines A and B … define support of the Trading Range. Lines C and D… define resistance of the Trading Range." — confidence high.
  - the Boy Scout gets two chances — value: null — "The Boy Scout has two chances to get back above the ice (i.e., creating a bullish 'Spring' situation). On the first upward rally the Boy Scout may fail to regain a footing above the ice. If so, then he will sink lower into the river in order to gather strength to try and rally once and crack the ice. If on this second attempt, the Boy Scout again fails to penetrate above the ice, he would be most likely to sink downward and drown" (S1) — confidence high — **two failed recovery attempts is a countable, computable rule.**
  - **trend magnitude scale** — value: **minor ≈ up to 10%; intermediate ≈ 15–20%; major > 25%** — "MINOR TRENDS: ... are moves of up to approximately ten percent of the price of the stock. Usually they last up to a couple of days to a couple of weeks" / "INTERMEDIATE TRENDS: ... are movements of around fifteen to twenty percent of the stock. They usually run for a couple of weeks to a couple of months" / "MAJOR TREND: ... is a movement of over twenty-five percent of the price of the stock. Usually major trends will last for several months or perhaps much longer" (S3) — confidence high — **the only percentage scale in the corpus, and it is about trends, not events; useful for sizing what a base must precede.**
  - **the halfway point as a measuring tool** — value: **50% of the leg** — "if a stock moves from $50 to $56 the distance of six points and then reacts, the half-way-point would be half of that six points or at $53." (S3); "Wyckoff used the 50% retracement level as his line in the sand for pullbacks. A shallower pullback shows relatively weak selling pressure and underlying strength, while a deeper pullback reflects relatively strong selling pressure and underlying weakness." (S8) — confidence high — computable. S3 adds: "Always calculate Half-way-points mathematically do not guess, because your eyes will lead you astray."
  - the thrust measure — value: null — "Thrusts are used to measure each drive up & drive down... The thrust is the price difference between consecutive tops in up trends, or between consecutive bottoms in down trends." (S3) — confidence high — computable from pivots.
- **measured_performance**: none published.
- **invalidation**: Not an event; boundaries are revised as new pivots form. A boundary is
  "invalidated" only in the sense that a sustained close beyond it with follow-through redefines the
  structure.
- **detection_notes**: **This is the load-bearing primitive for the entire method and the sources
  refuse to specify it.** Required: pivot-high and pivot-low detection with some lookback; a
  clustering step to build support and resistance *bands* (S9: ranges, not lines; S1: two lines each
  side); optionally a second, higher "major creek" level; `tr_mean_close` for the UTAD rule;
  `tr_midpoint` for the distribution Phase D rule; the halfway point per leg. **Explicitly not
  computable as defined:** the freehand wavy creek and ice (S3 says there is no exact way to draw
  them). Any implementation must substitute a deterministic proxy (e.g. pivot-high cluster, or a
  volume-weighted level per S3's "where the volume came in") and disclose the substitution. Note
  also S3's warning that the TR itself may mean nothing: "the T/R maybe accumulation, distribution,
  or nothing, nothing being an area in which no one is preparing for a large move."
- **conflicts**: S1/S3 insist the boundaries are wavy freehand lines through peaks and troughs;
  S2's schematics and most modern renderings draw them as horizontal lines anchored to specific
  events (SC/ST lows, AR high). These give different answers on any sloping range. S1 additionally
  presents a whole schematic built on *non-horizontal* boundaries — "The Accumulation Gradient of
  Rising Bottoms" — in which the support boundary rises through the TR by construction. Any detector
  assuming a horizontal floor will miss that schematic entirely.

## The schematic as a state machine

### Ordering — Accumulation

```
[prior downtrend]
   │
   ├─ PS      (optional; often absent or obscured — S3)      ─┐
   ├─ SC      (optional in the sense that Phase A may end     │  PHASE A
   │           "without climactic action" — S1/S2)            │  ("the stopping phase" — S9)
   ├─ AR      (MANDATORY — it is what sets tr_resistance)     │  COCH 1
   └─ ST      (MANDATORY — with SC low it sets tr_support)   ─┘
   │
   │  ►► tr_support, tr_resistance now exist. NOTHING BELOW IS DETECTABLE WITHOUT THEM.
   │
   ├─ PHASE B: multiple STs; upthrust-type actions at the top of the TR (S2);
   │           swings narrow and volume dries up toward the right edge (S1/S3).
   │           MANDATORY as a phase; no single mandatory event inside it.
   │
   ├─ PHASE C  (COCH 2 — S9). EXACTLY ONE OF:
   │     ├─ Spring (#1 / #2 / #3) or Terminal Shakeout   [new low made]     — S2, S5
   │     ├─ Test of the spring (required after a #2 spring — S3; optional after #3)
   │     └─ LPS at a higher low                          [no new low made]  — S5 (Fraser only)
   │     …or the "challenging" no-spring variant higher in the range (S2).
   │     ►► OPTIONAL: "springs and terminal shakeouts are not required elements" (S2).
   │
   ├─ SOS / Jump Across the Creek   (MANDATORY to leave the TR upward — S3:
   │     "a stock may remain in the T/R indefinitely until it has a (SOS) followed by a (LPS)")
   │
   ├─ PHASE D: LPS / BU / BUEC, possibly several (S2: "more than one LPS")
   │     ►► At least one LPS/BU is MANDATORY per S3's SOS-then-LPS terminal rule.
   │     ►► SOS ⇄ LPS may alternate: Fraser labels SOS 1/BUEC 1 then SOS 2/BUEC 2 (S4).
   │
   └─ PHASE E: markup; re-accumulation "stepping stone" TRs may nest here (S2) —
        a nested TR restarts the whole machine one level up.
```

**Rollback is mandatory, not optional.** S4: "we would relabel Phase C and D as a continuation of
Phase B, with the final testing still ahead." A Wyckoff state machine must be able to *un-label*
phases when the confirming action fails. S3 supplies the specific rollback trigger: a wide-spread,
high-volume reaction after a supposed SOS "CANCELS the probability of the fist action being a (SOS)".

### Ordering — Distribution

```
[prior uptrend]
   │
   ├─ PSY     (optional; and see the PSY↔BC ordering conflict — S2 vs S3)  ─┐
   ├─ BC      (optional — the uptrend "may also terminate without           │  PHASE A
   │           climactic action" — S2)                                      │
   ├─ AR      (MANDATORY — sets tr_support)                                 │
   └─ ST      (MANDATORY — with BC high sets tr_resistance;                ─┘
               "An ST may take the form of an upthrust (UT)" — S2)
   │
   ├─ PHASE B: net selling; SOWs on increased spread/volume; shorter than accumulation (S1)
   │
   ├─ PHASE C — ONE OF (S1 gives two schematics plus a third unschematised variant):
   │     ├─ UTAD above TR highs, returning to the TR's average level of closes  (Schematic 3)
   │     ├─ UT of a lower high, when demand never reaches the BC level          (S2)
   │     └─ SOW that breaks the Phase B standoff, no upthrust at all            (Schematic 2)
   │     ►► OPTIONAL: "a UTAD is not a required structural element" (S2)
   │
   ├─ SOW  (MANDATORY before any LPSY — S3: "The reaction preceding the (LPSY) must be a (SOW)")
   │     ►► must be CONFIRMED by the next rally (narrowing spread, decreased volume, lower top)
   │        or DENIED (widening spread, increased volume) — S3
   │
   ├─ PHASE D: LPSY, usually several (S1 marks points 11, 13, 15, 17).
   │     Trigger: price to/through tr_support, OR below tr_midpoint after a UT/UTAD (S2)
   │
   └─ PHASE E: markdown; the breakdown is retested by a rally failing at tr_support from below (S2);
        later climactic action may start a re-distribution TR or an accumulation (S2)
```

S3 states the **only fully-written mandatory chain** in the whole corpus, and it is the distribution
one: *"The (LPSY) is proceeded by the (PSY), (BC), (AR), (ST), (SOW) and then the (LPSY) occurs."*

### Mandatory vs optional — summary

| Event | Accumulation | Distribution | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| PS / PSY | **optional** | **optional** | S3: PS "sometimes it is virtually missing"; S2 omits PS/SC/ST entirely in re-accumulation |
| SC / BC | **optional** | **optional** | S1/S2: Phase A can end "without dramatic changes in spread and volume" |
| AR | **mandatory (de facto)** | **mandatory (de facto)** | It is the event that defines one TR boundary; without it there is no range |
| ST | **mandatory (de facto)** | **mandatory (de facto)** | Sets the other boundary; S3 sequences it "Immediately follows the (AR)" |
| Phase B | **mandatory** | **mandatory** | It *is* the cause; without it there is no effect to project (Law 2) |
| Spring / Shakeout | **optional** | — | S2, explicitly: "not required elements" |
| Test of spring | mandatory after a #2 spring | — | S3: "both must be tested by a secondary test" |
| UT / UTAD | — | **optional** | S2, explicitly: "not a required structural element" |
| SOS / SOW | **mandatory** | **mandatory** | S3: the TR ends only via SOS+LPS or SOW+LPSY |
| LPS / LPSY | **mandatory** | **mandatory** | S3, same rule; both sources treat multiples as normal |
| BU / BUEC | optional (often merged with LPS) | — | S2 treats BU and LPS as near-synonyms |

### Sequence-dependency — which events cannot be found alone

**Bootstrap events (need only a prior *trend*, not a prior *event*):** PS, SC, PSY, BC.
Everything else on this list **requires a previously-detected event or boundary** and therefore
cannot be emitted by a per-bar classifier:

- **AR / AR(dist)** — need SC / BC.
- **ST (both sides)** — need SC+AR / BC+AR (compared *against the climax's own* volume and spread, not a rolling window).
- **Spring, Terminal Shakeout, UT, UTAD** — need `tr_support` / `tr_resistance`, which need Phase A. "Below support" is undefined without support.
- **Test of the spring** — needs the spring's specific low *and* its specific volume.
- **SOS** — needs `tr_resistance`; its *meaning* needs Phase C.
- **LPS / BU / BUEC** — need the SOS (S2 branch) or the TR floor (Fraser branch).
- **SOW** — needs `tr_support`, **and** needs the *following* rally to confirm or deny it. It is the one event that is sequence-dependent in both directions.
- **LPSY** — needs a confirmed SOW; S3 demands the full six-event chain.
- **All phases B, C, D, E (both sides)** — each needs its predecessor.
- **The P&F count** — sits at the very end: it needs the SOS *and* the LPS before it can be taken.

Three further consequences worth stating plainly:

1. **Several event pairs are bar-identical and separable only by what follows.** SOS vs UT at
   resistance; spring vs #1-spring-failure at support; climax volume vs absorption volume (S3 says
   both have "WIDENING SPREAD and INCREASED VOLUME" and differ only by location). A live detector
   must emit provisional labels and resolve them later, or it will be wrong roughly half the time by
   construction.
2. **The same bar signature reverses sign with location.** High volume + narrow spread is bullish
   absorption at TR support and bearish distribution at TR resistance (S2). Location is therefore not
   a filter on top of the pattern — it is *part of* the pattern.
3. **Phase B cannot be detected from any single bar.** It is defined by a *trend across the range* —
   swings narrowing, volume drying up (S1/S3). This is the strongest argument for implementing
   Wyckoff as a range-level regression rather than as a bar-level pattern matcher.

### Detection primitives required, consolidated

Trading-range boundary detection (bands, not lines) · pivot highs/lows with a lookback · penetration
of a boundary followed by a close back inside · volume ratio **against a named prior event** (not
only against a rolling window) · spread ratio against a named prior event · `spread / volume` per bar
and per leg (Effort vs Result) · `close_position = (close-low)/(high-low)` · swing-leg segmentation
and per-leg aggregation · `tr_mean_close` (UTAD confirmation) · `tr_midpoint` (distribution Phase D)
· halfway point per leg (LPSY, 50% retracement) · thrust series `pivot[i] - pivot[i-1]` (non-climactic
Phase A) · regression of spread and volume against bar index across the TR (Phase B) · weekly
resampling (S6 works almost entirely in weekly bars) · P&F construction with a price-banded box size
· **a state machine with memory of every prior event's price, volume and spread, and the ability to
roll back labels.**

**Not computable from daily OHLCV under any implementation:** who is buying or selling (public vs
professional — this attribution appears in the definition of PS, SC, BC, PSY, and the Composite Man);
the freehand creek and ice (S3: "no one exact way of drawing these creeks"); the news/earnings
coincidence that S2 makes part of the BC definition (needs an external calendar); short-covering as a
component of the AR; and every "quality" judgement the sources rest on — "significantly diminished",
"considerable supply", "feeble", "sharp", "prolonged", "pronounced", none of which is ever given a
number anywhere in the corpus.
