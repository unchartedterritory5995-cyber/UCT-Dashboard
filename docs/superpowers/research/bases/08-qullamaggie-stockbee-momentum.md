# PART A — KRISTJAN KULLAMÄGI ("Qullamaggie")

Kullamägi publishes **three** setups and says so explicitly: `"3 setups only (and a few varations of
those setups)"` (FAQ). The "few variations" are named but **never enumerated with distinct numeric
parameters** anywhere on his own site — this is the single biggest gap in his published material and
is recorded per-setup below.

## Breakout

- **origin / source_name**: Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie), qullamaggie.com — "3 TIMELESS setups that have made me TENS OF MILLIONS!" plus the FAQ page for ADR/risk mechanics.

- **definition**: A momentum leader that has already made a large, fast advance, then pulls back and
  consolidates in an orderly way with higher lows and a tightening range while riding rising
  short-term moving averages, and then expands range out of that consolidation. Source's framing:
  `"An orderly pullback and consolidation with higher lows and tightening range in the consolidation
  phase."` The base is defined by *contraction against a rising MA*, not by a geometric shape (no
  cup, no handle, no neckline).

- **criteria**:
  - `universe / leadership rank — top 1–2% of stocks by return over 1-month, 3-month and 6-month windows — "Scan for the 1 or 2% of stocks that are up the most over these 3 timeframes 1-month 3-month 6-month" — confidence: high`
  - `prior move magnitude — 30% to 100%+ — "A big move higher sometime in the past 1-3 months. This move can be anywhere from 30-100%+ and usually lasts a few days to a few weeks." — confidence: high`
  - `prior move lookback window — within the past 1 to 3 months — "A big move higher sometime in the past 1-3 months." — confidence: high`
  - `prior move duration — "a few days to a few weeks" (no day count published) — "usually lasts a few days to a few weeks" — confidence: high (as a qualitative statement); value: null as a number — missing: an explicit minimum and maximum number of bars for the impulse leg`
  - `consolidation length — 2 weeks to 2 months — "The consolidation phase is usually 2 weeks to 2 months." — confidence: high`
  - `consolidation shape — higher lows plus tightening range — "with higher lows and tightening range in the consolidation phase" — confidence: high`
  - `consolidation shape, quantified — value: null — missing: the number of contractions, the required percentage narrowing per contraction, or a maximum permitted depth. Kullamägi publishes NO numeric contraction schedule. (Contrast Minervini's VCP, which does — see conflicts.)`
  - `moving-average support during the base — rising 10-day and 20-day, sometimes 50-day — "During the consolidation the stock price 'surfs' the rising 10- and 20-day. and sometimes toe 50-day moving average." (typo "toe" is in the retrieved source text) — confidence: high`
  - `which MA gates which variant — value: null — missing: an explicit mapping from consolidation length to the specific MA (10 vs 20 vs 50) that must be the support. He names all three in one sentence and never separates them by setup variant.`
  - `entry trigger — high of the first 1-minute, 5-minute or 60-minute candle of the session — "Enter on the opening range highs. The opening range highs can be the highs of the first 1-minute candle, the 5-minute candle or the 60-minute candle" — confidence: high`
  - `entry timing philosophy — on the breakout, never before it — "I buy breakouts as they break out on the daily chart — I don't anticipate breakouts" — confidence: med (secondary transcription, source 26)`
  - `stop placement — low of the day — "Stop is always lows of the day and stop should not be wider than the ATR or ADR of the stock" — confidence: high`
  - `stop width ceiling — 1x ADR (or ATR) of the stock — "Stop should not be wider than the ATR or ADR of the stock, to prevent the risk/reward mechanics getting out of whack. So if the ADR of the stock is 5%, your stop shouldn't be wider than 5%." — confidence: high`
  - `ADR definition — average of (High/Low) over the trailing 20 sessions, expressed in percent — "It's the average daily range in % over the past 20 sessions. 100*((H0/L0+H1/L1+H2/L2+H3/L3+H4/L4+H5/L5+H6/L6+H7/L7+H8/L8+H9/L9+H10/L10+H11/L11+H12/L12+H13/L13+H14/L14+H15/L15+H16/L16+H17/L17+H18/L18+H19/L19)/20-1)" — confidence: high`
  - `ADR% MINIMUM to qualify a candidate — value: null — missing: a published minimum ADR threshold in Kullamägi's own words. He publishes the FORMULA and uses ADR as a STOP-WIDTH CEILING, but nowhere on qullamaggie.com does he state a minimum ADR% for candidate selection. Third-party sources circulate "ADR% above 5-6" (breakoutshappen.com) and "ADR above 5%" (tikamalma.substack.com, confirmed by that page to be the AUTHOR'S own choice, not a quote). Do not attribute 5% to Kullamägi.`
  - `liquidity / dollar-volume minimum — value: null — missing: any published dollar-volume, average-volume, market-cap or share-price floor. His own pages give none. He instead says position size and risk "Depends on the liquidity of the stock" without publishing the threshold. Third-party numbers in circulation CONFLICT with each other: $100,000,000 dollar volume (deepvue.com) vs $3,000,000 dollar volume (tikamalma.substack.com, author's own choice). Neither is his.`
  - `risk per trade — 0.25% to 1% (article) — "My risk on most trades is usually 0.25-1%. I rarely risk more than 1% of my account on any trade." — confidence: high`
  - `risk per trade — 0.3% to 0.5% typical, rarely >1% (FAQ) — "Depends on the liquidity of the stock and conviction on the trade. Most of the time 0.3-0.5%. Rarely more than 1%." — confidence: high — NOTE: the article and the FAQ give different lower bounds (0.25% vs 0.3%). Recorded as published; not reconciled.`
  - `position size — 5% to 25% of account, most around 10–15% — "Generally 5%-25% with most being around 10-15%. Depends on liquidity of the stock and conviction" — confidence: high`
  - `overnight concentration ceiling — 30% of account in any one name — "I don't believe you should ever have more than 30% of your account over night in any stock or ETF." — confidence: high`
  - `first profit-take — sell 1/3 to 1/2 after 3–5 days, then stop to breakeven — "You should sell 1/3 to 1/2 of the position after 3-5 days, and then move the stop to break even." — confidence: high`
  - `remainder management — trail with the 10-day or 20-day MA — "The rest of the position should be trailed with the 10- or the 20-day moving average." — confidence: high`
  - `which trailing MA — 10-day for fast movers, 20-day for slow movers — "I usually use the 10-day for faster-moving stocks, and the 20-day for slower-moving stocks" — confidence: med (secondary transcription, source 26)`
  - `stop order type — market, never limit — "I always use market stops, never limit stops." — confidence: high`
  - `market-regime gate — value: null — missing: a published index-level filter. He states only "In a bullish market it is very common to get moves that are 10-20x+ your initial risk." A widely-repeated rule that the index 10-day MA must be above its 20-day MA appears only in third-party write-ups (breakoutshappen.com) and could not be traced to his own text.`

- **measured_performance**: **none published as a setup statistic.** What IS published are *account*
  results, which are a different thing: FAQ gives win rate `"25% in 2019"`, CAGR 2013–2019 `"268%"`,
  and largest drawdown `"50% in 2014"`; the Chat With Traders transcript adds `"In 2020, I had insane
  returns, and only had a 35% win rate. In 2019, my win rate was 25%."` These are whole-account,
  all-three-setups, discretionary-execution numbers over a stated calendar period — they are **not**
  a measured hit rate for the Breakout pattern, and they carry **no base rate and no benchmark**. A
  25% win rate is uninterpretable without the payoff distribution he does not publish; the only
  payoff hint is the assertion `"In a bullish market it is very common to get moves that are 10-20x+
  your initial risk."`, which is an assertion, not a measurement over a sample.

- **invalidation**: Price takes out the low of the entry day (hard stop). A required stop wider than
  1x the stock's ADR disqualifies the trade before entry, because the R:R "mechanics" break. Beyond
  that he publishes no structural invalidation (no maximum base depth, no volume-dry-up requirement,
  no "undercut of the 50-day kills it" rule). A close below the trailing 10-/20-day MA ends the
  remainder of the position.

- **detection_notes**:
  - Daily-computable primitives: (a) percent change over 21 / 63 / 126 trading bars and a
    cross-sectional percentile rank to get the top 1–2%; (b) max percent gain over any window inside
    the trailing 1–3 months to test the "30–100%+ in a few days to a few weeks" impulse; (c)
    higher-lows detection over the consolidation window (swing-low sequence, monotone non-decreasing);
    (d) range contraction — rolling (High−Low)/Close or true range, tested for a downward trend across
    the base; (e) SMA(10), SMA(20), SMA(50) with a rising-slope test and a "price hugging the MA"
    proximity band; (f) ADR% exactly as `100*(mean(High_i/Low_i, i=0..19) − 1)` over the trailing 20
    bars — note this is a **ratio-average**, NOT `mean((H−L)/C)` and NOT Wilder ATR; getting this
    wrong changes the stop-width ceiling.
  - Consolidation window is a **range** (2 weeks to 2 months ≈ 10 to 44 trading bars), so the
    detector must sweep base lengths rather than test one fixed lookback.
  - **HARD INTRADAY CONSTRAINT — the entry trigger is NOT computable from daily OHLCV.** The trigger
    is the high of the first 1-minute / 5-minute / 60-minute bar of the session. From daily bars you
    can only produce a *candidate list* the evening before; the actual buy price is an intraday
    opening-range high. Equally, the stop ("lows of the day") is an intraday running low, not the
    daily bar's low as known at the close. Any daily-only backtest of this setup is testing a
    different strategy.
  - The "1/3 to 1/2 after 3–5 days" partial and the "trail with the 10-/20-day" remainder ARE
    daily-computable once you accept a fixed entry-price proxy.
  - Not computable at all from OHLCV: nothing else — this setup is purely technical, no fundamental
    gate.

- **conflicts**:
  - **vs. O'Neil / IBD**: O'Neil's canonical cup-with-handle base runs roughly 7 weeks to 65 weeks and
    the buy point is a prior-high pivot with a named volume surge; Kullamägi's base is 2 weeks to 2
    months and the buy point is an *opening-range high*, an intraday construct O'Neil's daily
    framework has no analogue for. O'Neil's 8% maximum loss rule is a fixed percentage; Kullamägi's
    stop ceiling is stock-specific (1x ADR) and therefore varies per name. Both recorded; not averaged.
  - **vs. Minervini**: Minervini's VCP specifies a *counted* sequence of volatility contractions with
    a shrinking percentage per contraction and a Trend Template with eight explicit numeric
    conditions (including price within 25% of the 52-week high and 30%+ above the 52-week low).
    Kullamägi publishes **no contraction count, no trend template, and no 52-week-position filter**.
    They describe visually similar structures with entirely different levels of specification.
  - **vs. Bonde (Momentum Burst)**: Kullamägi's base is 2 weeks–2 months; Bonde's pre-breakout
    consolidation is `"3 to 20 days"`. Kullamägi holds the remainder for weeks or months on a trailing
    MA; Bonde exits within 3–5 days. Kullamägi says he does not anticipate breakouts; Bonde's
    Anticipation setup exists specifically to enter *before* the breakout. These are direct,
    irreconcilable disagreements about the same structure.

## Episodic Pivot (EP) — Kullamägi's version

- **origin / source_name**: Kristjan Kullamägi, qullamaggie.com — "3 TIMELESS setups…" and the
  dedicated article "How to master a setup: Episodic Pivots". (Kullamägi credits the concept to
  Bonde; Bonde's own version is documented separately in Part B and differs materially.)

- **definition**: A large gap up on huge volume out of a *dormant* multi-month base, driven by a
  genuine surprise — earnings, guidance, or news — in a stock the market was not already positioned
  in. The base structure is the *absence* of prior trend: `"The best EPs are on stocks ones that have
  gone sideways for 3-6 months or more."` He calls it the least frequent of his three setups and the
  best risk/reward.

- **criteria**:
  - `gap size — 10% or more — "Gap up 10%+ Big volume. If the volume is not there in premarket, it needs to come in at the open." — confidence: high`
  - `opening volume — the stock should trade its average daily volume within the first 15–20 minutes — "massive volume near the open, ideally the stock should trade the average daily volume the first 15-20 minutes or even quicker." — confidence: high`
  - `prior base — sideways for 3–6 months or more — "The best EPs are on stocks ones that have gone sideways for 3-6 months or more." — confidence: high`
  - `prior base, disqualifier — must NOT have already rallied over the past 3–6 months — "It's best if the stock has not rallied over the past 3-6 months. If the stock has already made a big, multi month move into the gap-up, is it really a surprise to the market?" — confidence: high`
  - `"gone sideways" quantified — value: null — missing: a maximum permitted range width or maximum percent advance over the 3–6 month base for it to count as "sideways". He gives the duration but never the tightness.`
  - `fundamental gate — mid/high double-digit to triple-digit EPS and revenue growth plus a significant beat — "There has to be big growth numbers, preferably mid/high or even triple digit EPS and revenue growth and a significant beat to analyst expectations." — confidence: high`
  - `fundamental gate, restated — "triple digit year over year (YoY) earnings and sales growth but mid/high double digits works really well too" plus "a big analyst beat" and "a big guidance higher" — confidence: high`
  - `"significant beat" quantified — value: null — missing: a numeric surprise percentage or a minimum guidance-raise magnitude. He publishes no threshold for what makes a beat "significant".`
  - `entry trigger — high of the first 1-, 5-, or 60-minute candle — "Enter opening range highs. ORH can be the highs of the first 1-, 5-, or 60-minute candle. The stop is at the lows of the day." — confidence: high`
  - `entry refinement — wait for the first 1-minute candle to form, then buy the 1-minute highs, optionally adding on the 5-minute highs — "I will wait for the first 1-minute candle to form" / "buy the 1-minute highs" / "on the 5-minute highs also" — confidence: high`
  - `stop placement — lows of the day — "Stop is always at the lows of the day" — confidence: high`
  - `stop width ceiling — 1x, maximum 1.5x, the ADR or ATR — "your stop is no more than 1x, or maximum 1.5x the average daily range or the average true range." — confidence: high — NOTE: this is a LOOSER ceiling than the Breakout setup's 1x. Recorded as two different published numbers, not reconciled.`
  - `trailing exit — 10-day or 20-day MA once price exceeds the initial stop — "Trail your stop with the 10- or 20-day moving average once they surpass your initial stop." — confidence: high`
  - `first profit-take on EP specifically — value: null — missing: whether the "sell 1/3 to 1/2 after 3-5 days" rule from the Breakout article also applies to EP. The EP article publishes only the trailing rule.`
  - `seasonality / frequency — value: null for a count — his qualitative statement is that EPs "mostly occur during a concentrated period of 3-4 weeks every quarter" — missing: a published count of qualifying EPs per season (Bonde DOES publish one — see Part B, a point of contrast).`
  - `learning curve — 3–4 earnings seasons — "It will take you 3-4 earnings seasons to get good at trading EP, 5-6 if you are a moron." — confidence: high (this is a pedagogical claim, not a screening criterion)`
  - `liquidity / price floor — value: null — missing: any published minimum price, volume or dollar volume for an EP candidate.`

- **measured_performance**: **none published.** He asserts EP is `"the one you can get the best
  risk/reward"` and the most infrequent, but publishes no win rate, average gain, failure rate,
  sample size, or period for this setup. The account-level 25%/35% win rates from the FAQ and
  transcript are whole-account figures spanning all three setups and must not be attached to EP. **No
  base rate is published** — in particular, the unconditional forward return of *all* 10%+ gaps is not
  given, so the edge of the fundamental and dormancy filters is unquantified.

- **invalidation**: The stock had already made a big multi-month move into the gap (kills the
  "surprise" premise). Volume fails to arrive at the open when it was absent premarket. A required
  stop wider than 1.5x ADR/ATR. Price takes out the low of the day.

- **detection_notes**:
  - Daily-computable: gap percent as `Open_t / Close_{t-1} − 1 ≥ 0.10`; a dormancy test over 63–126
    trailing bars (e.g. `Close_{t-1} / Close_{t-126} − 1` bounded near zero, plus a low
    high-minus-low range across the window); relative volume as `Volume_t / SMA(Volume, N)`;
    SMA(10)/SMA(20) for the trail; ADR% per the 20-bar formula above for the stop ceiling.
  - Requires a **non-OHLCV data join**: earnings dates, EPS and revenue YoY growth, analyst-estimate
    surprise, and guidance changes. None of the fundamental gate is derivable from bars. Earnings-date
    proximity is a mandatory primitive here.
  - **HARD INTRADAY CONSTRAINT.** Two separate parts fail on daily bars: (1) the entry trigger is the
    first 1-/5-/60-minute bar high; (2) the volume criterion is explicitly *time-of-day resolved* —
    "average daily volume within the first 15–20 minutes" cannot be evaluated from a daily bar at all,
    even after the close, because a daily bar has no intraday volume profile. A daily-bar screen can
    produce the gap+dormancy+growth candidate set but cannot evaluate the volume-pace criterion or the
    entry.
  - Premarket data is also implied ("if the volume is not there in premarket") — another non-daily feed.

- **conflicts**:
  - **vs. Bonde's EP** (same name, different setup): Kullamägi requires a **10%+ gap**; Bonde's
    scanning threshold is a **4% move** (`"c/c1>1.04"`) or an **8% move** with a 300%-of-average
    volume surge, and separately a **$5 point move** for high-priced names. Kullamägi enters at the
    opening-range high after the first candle forms; Bonde says `"Most EP where I made big money I
    bought in pre market"` using at-the-market orders. Kullamägi trails a 10-/20-day MA; Bonde's stop
    is `"1 dollar below the gap low"` or the 2-day low. Both recorded.
  - **vs. O'Neil / IBD**: IBD's comparable pattern (the "power earnings gap" / buyable gap-up) sits
    inside a *constructive* prior uptrend near the 52-week high; Kullamägi's EP explicitly requires the
    opposite — a stock that has NOT rallied for 3–6 months. This is a direct inversion of the prior-trend
    requirement, not a difference of degree.
  - **vs. Minervini**: Minervini's Trend Template demands the stock be in a Stage 2 uptrend with price
    above a rising 200-day MA. A Kullamägi EP candidate that has "gone sideways for 3-6 months or
    more" will routinely fail that template on the entry day. Both recorded.

## Parabolic Short

- **origin / source_name**: Kristjan Kullamägi, qullamaggie.com — "3 TIMELESS setups that have made me TENS OF MILLIONS!".

- **definition**: The mirror image of his long setups — a stock that has gone vertical for several
  consecutive sessions, far enough from its short-term moving averages that mean reversion to them is
  the trade. This is an *extension* structure, not a base structure; it is included because it is one
  of his three named setups and because its target is defined by the same 10-/20-day MAs that gate his
  long setups.

- **criteria**:
  - `prior move, large cap — up 50% to 100%+ in a few days or weeks — "A stock up 50-100%+ in a few days or weeks (if larger cap) or 300-1000%+ (if smaller cap)." — confidence: high`
  - `prior move, small cap — up 300% to 1000%+ over the same window — same quote as above — confidence: high`
  - `large-cap / small-cap boundary — value: null — missing: the market-cap or share-price level that separates "larger cap" from "smaller cap". Without it the 50–100% vs 300–1000% branch is not computable.`
  - `consecutive up days — 3 to 5 or more in a row — "The stock should be up 3-5+ days in a row." — confidence: high`
  - `prior move window — "a few days or weeks" — value: null as a number — missing: an explicit maximum bar count for the parabolic leg.`
  - `extension above the moving averages — value: null — missing: a published percentage or ATR-multiple distance above the 10-/20-day MA that qualifies the stock as "parabolic". He defines the MAs as the TARGET, never as an entry-qualifying distance threshold.`
  - `entry trigger — the opening range low of the 1-minute or 5-minute candle — "When you think you have identified a candidate you can short on the opening range lows (1-minute, 5-minute candles)." — confidence: high`
  - `stop placement — high of the day, or a reclaim of VWAP after a VWAP failure — "The stop is highs of the day or if VWAP fail, a reclaim of the VWAP." — confidence: high`
  - `target — the 10-day and 20-day moving averages — "The target area is the 10- and 20-day moving averages, that's where these stocks usually bounce." — confidence: high`
  - `risk/reward profile — 5x to 10x — "This setup is more like 5-10x risk reward" — confidence: high — NOTE: this is an ASSERTED payoff shape, not a measured expectancy over a sample.`
  - `win rate — value: null — his only statement is "If you wait for the perfect setups your win rate is probably going to be higher", which is explicitly hedged ("probably") and unquantified — missing: a win rate with a sample size.`
  - `borrow / shortability filter — value: null — missing: any published rule about locate availability or borrow cost, which is the practical gate on this setup.`

- **measured_performance**: **none published by Kullamägi.** A third-party systemization (source 28,
  Niv Goren / Stonks Capital, clearly secondary) reports a backtest with **CAGR 27.7%, max drawdown
  −20.9%, 1,869 trades from end-2007 to publication**. That author explicitly withholds the rules
  used (`"I'm also available for any question regarding the set up if it doesn't include me revealing
  the exact rules I used."`), so the result is **not reproducible and not attributable to
  Kullamägi's published criteria**, and **no benchmark or base rate for short-side returns over the
  same period is given**. It is recorded only so it is not later mistaken for his number.

- **invalidation**: Price makes a new high of day (stop). A VWAP failure that is then reclaimed. The
  implicit invalidation is entering too early: `"Patience is your edge. Let the early shorts get
  burned."` (recorded from the secondary systemization, source 28, which attributes it to him).

- **detection_notes**:
  - Daily-computable: percent change over N trailing bars with N swept over "a few days or weeks";
    a consecutive-up-close counter (`Close_t > Close_{t-1}` streak ≥ 3); SMA(10) and SMA(20) as the
    target level; distance-above-MA as `Close/SMA − 1` if you choose to supply the extension
    threshold he does not publish.
  - The large-cap/small-cap branch needs a market-cap join (shares outstanding × price) — not
    derivable from OHLCV alone — AND a boundary value that is not published.
  - **HARD INTRADAY CONSTRAINT, the most severe of the three setups.** Three separate components are
    intraday-only: (1) the entry is the opening-range LOW of the first 1-/5-minute candle; (2) the
    stop is the running high of the day; (3) **VWAP is a volume-weighted intraday construct that
    cannot be computed from a daily OHLCV bar at all** — there is no daily-bar approximation of "VWAP
    fail then reclaim". A daily-bar system can generate the parabolic candidate list and nothing else.
  - Borrow availability is a broker-feed item, not a bar item.

- **conflicts**:
  - **vs. O'Neil / IBD and Minervini**: both are long-only growth frameworks whose sell rules treat a
    climax/parabolic run as an *exit* signal for a long position (O'Neil's "climax top" — accelerating

    short entry with a defined MA target. Same recognized structure, opposite prescribed action. Both

  - **vs. Bonde**: Bonde's published short-side material is a mirror of his long scans (4% breakdown

    a moving-average target. Not a contradiction so much as a coverage gap on Bonde's side; recorded
    so the two are not conflated.

## Momentum Burst

- **origin / source_name**: Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee), stockbee.blogspot.com — "How to Identify good
  momentum burst and make millions" (2014), "Guidelines to find good momentum burst setups" (2016),
  "Swing trading using momentum burst moves of 3 to 5 days" (2017), "My exit guidelines for momentum
  burst swing trades" (2018).

- **definition**: The core organizing claim of Bonde's whole method — stocks do not trend smoothly,
  they advance in short, sharp bursts separated by dead periods. The tradeable structure is a **range
  contraction followed by a range-expansion day**: an orderly consolidation of narrow-range, low-volume
  bars, ended by a single wide-range, high-volume day that closes near its high. `"Momentum burst is
  the tendency of stocks to move in sharp 3 to 5 days moves of 8% to 40%."`

- **criteria**:
  - `burst magnitude — 8% to 40% — "Momentum burst is the tendency of stocks to move in sharp 3 to 5 days moves of 8% to 40%." — confidence: high`
  - `burst duration — 3 to 5 days — same quote — confidence: high`
  - `burst magnitude for high-priced stocks — a 5 to 25 dollar move if the stock is priced above 40 — "5 to 25 dollar burst if priced higher than 40" — confidence: high`
  - `burst magnitude, alternate published figure — 8% to 20% or more — "The stock experiences a rapid price increase or decrease, often ranging from 8% to 20% or more." (Bonde's own X/Twitter account, read via search-result text rather than a direct fetch of the post) — confidence: med — NOTE: this CONFLICTS with his blog's 8–40% upper bound. Both recorded, not averaged.`
  - `pre-breakout consolidation length — 3 to 20 days — "The stock will have 3 to 20 days consolidation prior to range expansion day" — confidence: high`
  - `day before the breakout — narrow range day or negative day — "The day prior to range expansion day will be narrow range day or negative day" — confidence: high; and "day before breakout should be narrow range day or negative day" — confidence: high`
  - `pre-breakout bar structure — a series of narrow range days — "The stock will have series of narrow range days prior to breakout" — confidence: high`
  - `"narrow range" quantified — value: null — missing: a numeric definition of a narrow-range day (e.g. NR4/NR7, or range < X% of the 20-day average range). Bonde names the condition repeatedly and never publishes the arithmetic.`
  - `breakout-day range — must expand — "stock should have range expansion on breakout day" — confidence: high`
  - `"range expansion" quantified — value: null — missing: an explicit multiple of the prior average range. The 4% close-to-close move (see the 4% Breakout section) is his operational PROXY for range expansion, but he never states that range expansion itself equals 4%.`
  - `breakout-day volume — higher than the previous day — "On breakout day volume is higher than previous day" — confidence: high`
  - `breakout-day close location — at or near the high — "On breakout day stock closes at or near its high for the day (preferred)" — confidence: high`
  - `consolidation volume — orderly and lower — "volume during consolidation should be preferably orderly and lower" — confidence: high`
  - `pre-breakout cleanliness — no big range moves or breakdowns before the breakout — "pre breakout there should not be many big range moves or breakdowns" — confidence: high`
  - `prior leg quality — linear and orderly — "stock should have linearity in prior uptrend before the consolidation period" / "Stock should have linear and orderly move" — confidence: high`
  - `"linearity" quantified — value: null — missing: an R² of the log-price regression, a maximum drawdown inside the leg, or any other computable definition of "linear".`
  - `extension filter — first or second breakout at the start of an uptrend preferred — "Stock is not extended. First or second breakout at start of an up trend is preferred" — confidence: high`
  - `volatility disqualifier — avoid erratic movers — "A very volatile stock exhibiting drunken man walk kind moves should be avoided" — confidence: high (qualitative; value: null — missing: a volatility ceiling)`
  - `float preference — below 25 million good, below 10 million explosive — "Low float below 25 million is good. Below 10 million float leads to explosive moves" — confidence: high`
  - `price preference — sub-$5 stocks make the most explosive moves — "Low priced stocks (below 5 dollar) tend to make very explosive moves of 40% kind in 3 to 5 days" — confidence: high`
  - `entry scan (percentage variant) — close ≥ 1.04× prior close with volume up and above 100,000 — "c/c1>1.04 and v>v1 and V>100000" — confidence: high`
  - `entry scan (dollar variant) — close minus open ≥ $0.90 with volume above 100,000 — "c-o>=.90 and v>100000" — confidence: high — NOTE: this variant is close-minus-OPEN, not close-minus-prior-close. Do not substitute one for the other.`
  - `entry timing — as soon as the stock appears in the scan, any time from the open to just before the close — "Entry in this kind of trade happens as soon as stock shows up in my scan. That can be anywhere from market open to just before close." — confidence: high`
  - `stop placement — low of the entry day, or half the day's range depending on entry — "The stop is low of the entry day as this is momentum burst trade." and "Stop is low of the entry day or half the days range depending on where I enter." — confidence: high`
  - `stop width preference — value: null — missing: a quoted threshold. The retrieved text paraphrases a preference for stops under 4% and ideally below 2% but does not return it inside quotation marks; do not use those numbers as published.`
  - `time-based exit — exit on the 3rd or 5th day if the stock has not moved — "Exit on 3rd or 5th day (for $ b/o) if stock does not move much post entry. This indicates momentum burst got aborted after one day move" — confidence: high`
  - `scheduled partial exit — at least 50% of the position at the close of day 3 or day 5 — "Exit at least 50% of position on third or fifth day at close. 80% of trades would be 3 days hold." — confidence: high`
  - `fast-mover partial exit — if the stock gains 8%+ the same or next day, sell 50% and trail 25–50 cents below the high — "After your entry next day or same day if the stock goes up 8% or more exit 50% of the position and move stop 25 to 50 cents below the high of the day." — confidence: high`
  - `profit protection from day 3 — trail 25 to 50 cents below the high — "By third day the moment stocks goes up start protecting profit by moving stop 25 to 50 cents below the high." — confidence: high`
  - `gap-up exit — exit premarket or at the open on a 20%+ gap after entry — "Exit a stock in pre market or at open if it gaps up 20% or more after your entry next day or third day." — confidence: high`
  - `extended-hold rule — after day 3, trail the low of the day each day — "After 3rd day if you want to keep holding keep moving stop to low of day everyday after that." — confidence: high`
  - `breakeven rule — value: null — the retrieved 4%-breakout page paraphrases moving the stop to breakeven on a 4–5% gain but does not return it as a quote — missing: the rule in quoted form.`

- **measured_performance**: **none published as a measured statistic with a sample.** The one
  quantitative distributional claim is `"80% of trades would be 3 days hold."` — a claim about his own
  trade population with **no stated sample size, no date range, and no base rate**. `"8 to 40%"` and
  `"3 to 5 days"` are asserted properties of the population of momentum bursts, not a measured win
  rate or expectancy. No failure rate is published. The frequently-cited framing that this method
  "trained $100M traders" appears only in third-party write-ups and is a claim about individuals, not
  a setup statistic.

- **invalidation**: The stock is up 3 days in a row into the breakout (no contraction to expand from).
  The stock is extended — past the first or second breakout of the trend. Wide/erratic ranges or a
  breakdown inside the consolidation. Failure to follow through by day 3 aborts the burst. Price takes
  the low of the entry day.

- **detection_notes**:
  - Daily-computable primitives: close-to-close percent change (`C/C1 − 1 ≥ 0.04`); close-minus-open
    in dollars (`C − O ≥ 0.90`); volume vs prior day and vs an absolute floor; a narrow-range detector
    over the trailing 3–20 bars (you must supply the definition Bonde does not publish — NR4/NR7 or a
    range-percentile test); a range-contraction trend across the base; close-location-in-range
    (`(C − L)/(H − L)`) for "closes at or near its high"; a consecutive-up-days counter for the "not up
    3 days in a row" filter; a linearity score over the prior leg (again, your definition, not his).
  - Non-OHLCV joins required: **float** (shares outstanding minus insider/restricted holdings) for the
    25M/10M preference. Not derivable from bars.
  - **Intraday status — partial.** The *signal* is a daily-bar condition and IS computable from daily
    OHLCV after the close. But Bonde's stated entry is same-session (`"anywhere from market open to
    just before close"`), which means the historical fill price is intraday and unknowable from a daily
    bar; a daily-only backtest must substitute the close (or next open) and will misstate both entry
    and the "low of the entry day" stop. The 25-to-50-cent trailing stop and the 20% gap-up exit are
    likewise intraday executions. **Verdict: signal = daily; execution = intraday.**
  - Note the two volume conventions (hundreds vs raw shares) across his posts.

- **conflicts**:
  - **vs. Kullamägi**: holding period 3–5 days vs weeks-to-months on a trailing MA. Consolidation
    3–20 days vs 2 weeks–2 months. Bonde actively prefers sub-$5 stocks and sub-10M floats; Kullamägi
    trades liquid leaders and sizes 10–15% of account per position, which is impossible in a 10M-float
    sub-$5 name at his account size. Both recorded.
  - **vs. O'Neil / IBD**: IBD screens out low-priced stocks (a minimum share price around $15 is the
    long-standing IBD guidance) and requires institutional sponsorship; Bonde explicitly says the
    *most explosive* moves come from sub-$5, sub-10M-float names — the exact population IBD excludes.
    Direct opposition on universe selection. Also, O'Neil's base is measured in weeks-to-months with a
    prior-high pivot; Bonde's is 3–20 days with no pivot concept at all — his trigger is a
    *magnitude of today's move*, not a price level.
  - **vs. Minervini**: Minervini requires a Stage 2 uptrend above a rising 200-day MA before any VCP
    entry. Bonde's momentum burst requires only "an established uptrend" with no MA test published,
    and his 3–20 day consolidation is far shorter than a VCP's typical multi-week contraction sequence.

## 4% Breakout (and the "$ Breakout" / Dollar Breakout variant)

- **origin / source_name**: Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee), stockbee.blogspot.com — "How to use the 4%
  breakout scan to make money" (2015) and "My process loop to trade 4% b/o and $ b/o" (2017).

- **definition**: The operational scan that finds momentum-burst candidates. It is a **range-expansion
  detector, not a pattern matcher**: it takes every stock that moved 4%+ on rising volume today, then
  the *structure* filter (orderly prior consolidation) is applied by eye to the survivors. `"The 4%
  breakout is a range expansion scan which works best on small and mid caps and lower priced stocks."`
  The `$ breakout` is the same idea for higher-priced stocks where 4% is too large a bar.

- **criteria**:
  - `4% breakout scan — close ≥ 1.04× previous close AND volume > previous day's volume AND volume ≥ 100,000 — "c/c1>=1.04 and v>v1 and v>=100000" — confidence: high`
  - `4% breakout scan, earlier-post form — "c/c1>1.04 and v>v1 and V>100000" — confidence: high — NOTE: strict > vs >= differs between posts; immaterial in practice but recorded.`
  - `dollar breakout scan — close minus open ≥ $0.90 AND volume > 100,000 — "c-o>=.90 and v>100000" — confidence: high`
  - `universe — US Common Stocks in Telechart — "you have to run it on US Common Stocks in Telechart" (paraphrase returned; the universe restriction is stated) — confidence: med`
  - `intended outcome — a 3 to 5 day burst of 8% to 20% — "3 to 5 days momentum burst kind of move of 8% to 20%" — confidence: high — NOTE: 8–20% here vs 8–40% in the momentum-burst posts. Both recorded.`
  - `structural filter 1 — not up 3 days in a row prior to the breakout day — "stock should not be up 3 days in a row prior to breakout day" — confidence: high`
  - `structural filter 2 — narrow range day prior to the breakout — "stock should have narrow range day prior to breakout" — confidence: high`
  - `structural filter 3 — a period of range contraction before the breakout day — "stock should have a period of range contraction prior to breakout day" — confidence: high`
  - `structural filter 4 — orderly first up leg — "stock should have orderly first up leg prior to breakout" — confidence: high`
  - `structural filter 5 — an orderly pullback or consolidation before the breakout day — "orderly pullback or consolidation before the breakout day" — confidence: high`
  - `stop — low of the breakout day / low of entry day — "The stop is low of breakout day." and "the low of entry day" — confidence: high`
  - `exit rules — as per Momentum Burst above; the process-loop post restates them — "Exit at least 50% of position on third day at close", "After your entry next day or same day if the stock goes up 8% or more exit 50% of the position", "Exit a stock in pre market or at open if it gaps up 20% or more", "If a stock does not follow through in 3 days exit or move stops to break even" — confidence: high`
  - `hold length — 3 to 5 day hold — "3 to 5 day hold trade" — confidence: high`
  - `number of candidates per day — value: null — missing: a published count. He says only that "the scan gives you many candidates". (Contrast Modified Double Trouble, where he DOES publish 0–5.)`
  - `price floor / market cap — value: null — missing: an explicit minimum share price or cap. He asserts the scan "works best on small and mid caps and lower priced stocks" without a boundary.`

- **measured_performance**: **none published.** The only figure attached to the scan is an
  illustrative single name (`OME` cited as having "made 25% move in last 10 days") — an anecdote, not
  a statistic, with n=1. **No win rate, no base rate, no sample.** Critically, the base rate that
  matters here is not published anywhere: what fraction of *all* stocks that close +4% on rising
  volume go on to make an 8–20% move in 3–5 days? Without it, the structural filters' contribution is
  unquantified — and Bonde himself concedes the raw scan output is mostly noise ("the scan gives you
  many candidates but you must only select the ones where…").

- **invalidation**: Up 3 days in a row into the signal. No prior range contraction. Erratic/wide prior
  leg. No follow-through within 3 days. Low of the breakout day is taken out.

- **detection_notes**:
  - Fully daily-computable as a *screen*: `C/C1 ≥ 1.04`, `V > V1`, `V ≥ 100000`; and for the dollar
    variant `C − O ≥ 0.90`, `V > 100000`. The dollar variant needs the **open**, so an OHLCV feed
    missing the open cannot compute it.
  - The structural filters need: a consecutive-up-days counter; a narrow-range-day test (definition
    not published — supply your own); a range-contraction trend over the base; a linearity/orderliness
    score for the first leg. Three of these four have no published arithmetic, so any implementation
    is *your* operationalization of his eye, and should be labelled as such.
  - Volume convention: this post uses raw share counts (100,000). His older Telechart posts use
    hundreds. Do not port `V >= 1000` from a 2007 post into a raw-share screen.
  - **Intraday status — the screen is daily; the execution is not.** He runs the scan repeatedly
    intraday `"from market opening onwards"` to enter the same session. An end-of-day implementation
    that enters at the next open is a materially different strategy and should be labelled as a
    deviation, not an implementation.

- **conflicts**:
  - **vs. Kullamägi**: Kullamägi's breakout trigger is a *price level* cleared intraday (the opening
    range high) after a 2-week-to-2-month base; Bonde's trigger is a *daily percentage magnitude*
    (+4%) with no price level at all. Two incompatible definitions of "breakout".
  - **vs. O'Neil / IBD**: O'Neil's breakout is defined by clearing a pivot from a named base pattern
    on a volume surge (IBD's long-standing guidance is roughly 40%+ above average volume) and warns
    against buying more than ~5% past the pivot. Bonde's 4% scan has no pivot, no base-pattern name,
    and a volume test that is merely "higher than yesterday" — a far weaker volume bar. Recorded both.

## Double Trouble (and Modified Double Trouble / MDT)

- **origin / source_name**: Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee), stockbee.blogspot.com — "How to setup Double
  Trouble Scan and chart template" (2018-04), "Modified Double Trouble" (2007-06), "How to setup MDT
  scans and charts" (2018-03).

- **definition**: A **longer-horizon momentum universe filter**, not an entry pattern. "Double
  Trouble" isolates stocks that have roughly doubled from their 52-week low — Bonde's "anchored
  momentum" — and then waits for a multi-month base near the highs to break out. He credits the idea
  to Richard Love's *Superperformance Stocks* and Darvas's *How I Made 2 Million Trading Stocks*. The
  base structure here is explicitly a **multi-month consolidation near the high**, which is a *longer*
  structure than anything else in Bonde's toolkit.

- **criteria**:
  - `Double Trouble scan — current close divided by the 252-day low ≥ 1.80 — "c/minl252>=1.80" — confidence: high`
  - `sort column — "c/minl252" — confidence: high`
  - `lookback — 252 trading days (52 weeks) — implied by the formula's minl252 term — confidence: high`
  - `naming vs threshold — the setup is named for DOUBLING but the published threshold is 1.80, i.e. +80% from the 52-week low, not +100% — "c/minl252>=1.80" — confidence: high — NOTE: an 80% threshold under a "double" label. Do not "correct" it to 2.00.`
  - `base requirement after qualification — a base of a few months near the high — "base of few months near high" — confidence: med (short quoted fragment returned from the blog-search page rather than a full sentence on the post itself)`
  - `base requirement, quantified — value: null — missing: an exact number of days/weeks for the "few months" base, a maximum permitted depth, or a tightness criterion.`
  - `Modified Double Trouble (MDT) ranking formula — relative rank on the 135-day average close — "Calculate relative rank using : ((C - AVGC135 ) / AVGC135)" — confidence: high`
  - `MDT universe cut — top 2% by that rank — "Take the top 2% stocks ranked by the above scan." — confidence: high`
  - `MDT tighter universe cut — top 1% for fewer candidates — "If you want even lesser number of candidates take 1% only in step 2." — confidence: high`
  - `MDT combined entry scan — prior-month change < 10%, today ≥ +4%, volume ≥ 1000 (hundreds ⇒ 100,000 shares) and rising — "(100 * (C1 - C22) / C22) < 10 AND ( 100 * (C - C1) / C1) >= 4 AND V >= 1000 AND V > V1" — confidence: high`
  - `MDT candidate count — 0 to 5 per day at the 2% cut; 0 to 3 at the 1% cut — "You will get 0 to 5 candidates on daily basis. If you want even lesser number of candidates take 1% only in step 2. In that case you will get 0 to 3 candidates on daily basis." — confidence: high`
  - `MDT initial stop — the 3-day low — "Initial stop at 3 days low." — confidence: high`
  - `MDT trailing rule — after a 20% gain, trail with a 10% stop — "Trail after 20% profit with 10% stop." — confidence: high — NOTE: this is a MUCH longer-horizon exit than the 3–5 day momentum-burst schedule. The two coexist in Bonde's material as different trade types.`
  - `MDT standalone scans (2018 post) — bullish "c/avgc126>=1.19", bearish "c/avgc126<=.81" — confidence: high — NOTE: the 2018 MDT scan uses a 126-day average close and a ±19% band, which is a DIFFERENT formulation from the 2007 post's 135-day rank. Both published; not reconciled.`
  - `universe size at the Double Trouble threshold — value: null for a rule — the blog-search page returned "total of 457 stocks up 100% plus from their 260 days low" as a point-in-time observation on one date, and a paraphrase of "around 240 stocks" typically qualifying; neither is a criterion — missing: any stated expected universe size as a rule.`

- **measured_performance**: **none published.** The MDT candidate counts (`"0 to 5"`, `"0 to 3"`) are
  *throughput* figures, not performance. No win rate, average gain, or failure rate is published for
  Double Trouble or MDT, and no base rate is given for how often stocks that are +80% off a 52-week
  low go on to break out of a multi-month base successfully. A candidate count is not a hit rate and
  must not be read as one.

- **invalidation**: For the long side, failure of the base breakout; Bonde notes the symmetric case
  explicitly — a base *breakdown* in a Double Trouble name is a short signal. MDT: the 3-day low is
  taken out; or the pre-entry filter `(C1 − C22)/C22 < 10%` fails, i.e. the stock is already extended
  over the prior month.

- **detection_notes**:
  - Fully daily-computable. Primitives: rolling 252-bar minimum of the low (`minl252`); the ratio
    `C / minl252`; a 135-bar and a 126-bar simple average of the close; cross-sectional percentile rank
    to take the top 1–2%; 22-bar percent change for the "not extended over the last month" filter;
    daily percent change; volume vs prior day and an absolute floor.
  - **Volume-unit trap**: the 2007 MDT scan writes `V >= 1000` in Telechart's hundreds convention
    (= 100,000 shares). Implementing it as 1,000 raw shares makes the filter vacuous.
  - The "multi-month base near the high" leg of Double Trouble needs a base detector (distance from
    the 52-week/N-day high, range width over the base, duration) — but Bonde publishes no numbers for
    it, so the detector's parameters are yours.
  - **No intraday requirement.** This is the only setup in this file that is fully specifiable and
    executable on daily bars end to end (entry on the +4% day, stop at the 3-day low, trail at 20%/10%).
    Note the entry day's stop still references a level (3-day low) knowable at the close.

- **conflicts**:
  - **vs. O'Neil / IBD**: O'Neil warns against chasing extended stocks and defines the buy point as a
    pivot at most ~5% above the base high. Double Trouble deliberately *starts* by requiring the stock
    to be +80% off its 52-week low — a population O'Neil's extension rules would flag as late — though
    the "base near the high" step partially re-imposes an O'Neil-like discipline. Recorded both.
  - **vs. Bonde's own Momentum Burst**: MDT trails after a 20% gain with a 10% stop, implying a
    multi-week-to-multi-month hold; Momentum Burst exits at least half the position on day 3. The same
    author publishes two exit regimes that are an order of magnitude apart in horizon. Recorded, not
    reconciled.
  - **vs. Minervini**: Minervini's Trend Template requires price to be at least 30% above the 52-week
    low and within 25% of the 52-week high. Double Trouble's 1.80× requirement is far stricter on the
    low-side test (+80% vs +30%) and imposes **no** high-side proximity test at all in the scan itself.
    Different numbers for the same concept; both recorded.

## Episodic Pivot — Bonde's version

- **origin / source_name**: Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee), stockbee.blogspot.com — "What are Episodic
  Pivots and how to find them" (2010-02) and "My process flow for Episodic Pivots (EP)" (2014-07).
  Bonde is the originator of the term; Kullamägi's version (Part A) is a derivative with different
  numbers. Additional Bonde EP material was read via the community mirror qullamaggie.net (source 27)
  and is flagged inline.

- **definition**: A stock that begins a sustained multi-month rally on the day of a genuine surprise —
  most often an earnings surprise in a neglected name. Grounded explicitly in the academic
  Post-Earnings-Announcement Drift literature: Bonde cites Ball and Brown (1968) and describes stocks
  that `"start their rally on earnings day and continue it for months or sometimes 3-4 quarters."`
  The relevant "base" is the *dormancy* before the event, not a chart pattern.

- **criteria**:
  - `identification scan (2010, Telechart) — "((C - C1) >= 5 AND V > 10000 AND C >= 62.50 AND V > V1) OR ((( 100 * (C - C1) / C1) >= 8 AND V > 3000 AND (100 * V / AVGV100) >= 300) AND C > 1)" — confidence: high — reads as: a $5+ point move on 1,000,000+ shares in a stock ≥ $62.50 with rising volume, OR an 8%+ move on 300,000+ shares where volume is ≥ 300% of the 100-day average, in a stock above $1. Volume terms are in Telechart hundreds.`
  - `identification scan (2014 process flow) — close ≥ 1.04× prior close, volume > 3× the 50-day average, volume ≥ 300,000 — "c/c1>1.04 and v>3*avgv50.1 and v>=300000" — confidence: high — NOTE: the move threshold DROPPED from 8% (2010) to 4% (2014) while the volume-surge multiple stayed at 3x. Both published; recorded, not reconciled.`
  - `after-hours screen — movers up 4%+ on 50,000 volume — "Analyse all after hours movers up 4% plus on 50k volume to see if there is stock with neglect+ game changing earnings" — confidence: high`
  - `pre-market screen — up at least 2% on 50,000+ volume — "Run IB scanner to find stock up on 50k plus volume and up at least 2%" — confidence: high`
  - `volume surge, qualitative — 10x or more vs average — "10 times or more compared to average volume" — confidence: med (quoted fragment; the sentence context was summarized)`

  - The 2014 scan is run repeatedly *during* the session ("multiple times"), so the candidate set is
    time-of-day dependent even before the entry question.

    dormant, no-coverage EP candidate typically fails it. Recorded both.

## Anticipation

- **origin / source_name**: Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee), stockbee.blogspot.com — "How to find
  Anticipation setups" (2015-02) and "How to find the best anticipation setups" (2017-10).

- **definition**: The inverse of the 4% Breakout in timing. Instead of buying the range-expansion day,
  you identify the *contraction* that precedes it and enter before or at the very start of the
  expansion, so the stop is only a few cents away. The structure is a short, quiet, low-volume,
  low-volatility consolidation inside an established move. This is Bonde's most explicitly
  base-structure-oriented setup — the base *is* the signal, with no trigger bar required.

- **criteria**:
  - `consolidation length — 3 to 10 days — "3 to 10 days consolidation/pullback" — confidence: high`
  - `narrow-range bar series — a series of low-range bars in the last 5 to 10 days — "series of low range bars in last 5 to 10 days" and "series of narrow range days in pullback/consolidation" — confidence: high`
  - `volatility state — extremely low; a stock quiet over the last 5–10 days is the ideal candidate — "A stock with low volatility in last 5 to 10 days is ideal candidate as part of a continuation setup." — confidence: high`
  - `volume during the pullback — low — "low volume pullback" — confidence: high`
  - `orderliness — no 4% breakdown anywhere in the pullback or consolidation — "orderly pullback with no 4% b/d during the pullback or consolidation" — confidence: high — NOTE: this is a rare case where Bonde gives the disqualifier a NUMBER (4% down day) rather than leaving it qualitative.`
  - `recent momentum filter — not up 3 days in a row — "not up 3 days in a row" — confidence: high`
  - `prior leg — linear first leg if trading it as a continuation setup — "linear first leg if looking as continuation setup" — confidence: high`
  - `momentum state — extremely low momentum phase, or a shallow pullback within an established momentum phase — described as such in both posts — confidence: high — value: null as a number — missing: a numeric definition of "extremely low momentum phase".`
  - `extension filter — first or second anticipation setup in an established trend is best — "First or second anticipation setup in established trend is best." — confidence: high`
  - `disqualifier — extended stocks — "Avoid Anticipation setups on extended stocks...Extended trends needs to be avoided" — confidence: high — value: null — missing: a numeric definition of "extended" (distance from the rally start, number of prior breakouts, or distance above an MA).`
  - `entry — before the breakout, or a few cents above yesterday's action — "enter before breakout or entering with a order few cents above yesterday's action" — confidence: high`
  - `risk at entry — a few cents, or less than 2% — "Ideal entry is where you risk just few cents or less than 2%" — confidence: high`
  - `alternative screen — Bollinger Band squeeze, but he prefers eyeballing narrow-range series — "You can look for stocks with bollinger band squeeze" with the stated preference for looking for a series of narrow-range consolidation periods instead — confidence: med (the preference statement was returned as paraphrase; the BB-squeeze mention is his)`
  - `BB squeeze parameters — value: null — missing: the Bollinger Band period, standard-deviation multiple, and squeeze threshold. He names the tool and publishes no settings.`
  - `daily throughput — 5 to 10 good setups per day found in about 20 minutes; 1–3 quality candidates from 100–300 screened; only 3 to 5 tracked — "Only 3 to 5 quality ideas should be tracked" — confidence: med (the 5–10 / 20-minute / 100–300 / 1–3 figures were returned as paraphrase, not inside quotes) — value: null for those four figures — missing: them in quoted form.`
  - `stop placement — value: null — missing: an explicit stop rule for Anticipation specifically. He specifies the RISK ("few cents or less than 2%") but not the level. The 2017 post was checked directly for a stop formula and does not publish one.`
  - `scan formula — value: null — missing: any PCF/scan syntax. Both Anticipation posts describe the criteria in prose only; unlike every other Bonde setup, no scan is published.`

- **measured_performance**: **none published.** The daily throughput figures (5–10 setups, 1–3 quality
  candidates) are counts of *candidates*, not outcomes. No win rate, average gain, failure rate,
  sample size, or period is given, and no base rate is offered for how often a 3–10 day narrow-range
  consolidation actually produces a momentum burst. Bonde's own framing is that this is `"a skill
  which requires some minimum level of competency"` developed by studying historical setups — i.e. he
  explicitly positions it as discretionary pattern recognition rather than a measured system.

- **invalidation**: Any 4% down day inside the consolidation. The stock being up 3 days in a row. The
  stock being extended — past the first or second anticipation setup of the trend. A non-linear or
  erratic first leg. Rising volume or expanding range during what should be a quiet period.

- **detection_notes**:
  - Daily-computable primitives, and this setup is unusually well-suited to them: rolling true range
    or (H−L)/C percentile over the trailing 5–10 bars to find the quiet window; a count of bars in the
    3–10 day consolidation; a 4%-down-day detector over the base window (`C/C1 − 1 ≤ −0.04`) as a hard
    disqualifier; volume vs its own trailing average during the base; a consecutive-up-days counter;
    a linearity score on the prior leg; Bollinger Band width as an alternative squeeze detector (with
    parameters you must choose).
  - The **entry is a resting limit/stop order a few cents above the prior day's high** — a level fully
    derivable from the previous daily bar. This is the one entry in Bonde's toolkit that is specified
    against yesterday's daily bar rather than today's intraday tape.
  - **Intraday status — the lightest of any setup here.** Signal, entry level, and the "few cents"
    risk are all computable from daily OHLCV. What is *not* published is the stop level, so the exit
    side has to be borrowed from his momentum-burst rules (low of entry day) with that borrowing
    labelled as an inference, not a quote. Fill realism still requires knowing whether the resting
    order was actually touched intraday, which a daily bar's high does answer (touch, though not
    queue position).
  - Note the asymmetry worth flagging: Anticipation is the setup with the *most* daily-computable
    entry and the *fewest* published numbers. Its two most load-bearing terms — "narrow range" and
    "extended" — have no arithmetic anywhere in his writing.

- **conflicts**:
  - **vs. Kullamägi, head-on**: `"I buy breakouts as they break out on the daily chart — I don't
    anticipate breakouts"` (Kullamägi, source 26) versus a Bonde setup whose entire purpose is to
    `"enter before breakout"`. Same market structure, opposite entry doctrine. This is the sharpest
    single disagreement between the two authors and must not be reconciled.
  - **vs. Bonde's own 4% Breakout**: the 4% scan requires the range-expansion day to have *happened*;
    Anticipation requires it to have *not yet* happened. He runs both concurrently. Recorded as two
    coexisting entry doctrines by one author.
  - **vs. O'Neil / IBD**: IBD doctrine is to buy at or just above the pivot on a volume surge and
    treats buying below the pivot as premature — there is no IBD sanction for pre-breakout entry.
    Minervini likewise buys the pivot of the final contraction. Bonde's Anticipation deliberately
    front-runs that pivot to cut the stop from percent-scale to cents-scale. Both recorded.

## Market Monitor (breadth-based market timing gate)

Not a chart pattern — a regime filter Bonde applies above every setup above. Included because the
brief asks for his breadth-based market timing thresholds, and because in his framework the same base
structure is tradeable or untradeable depending on these readings.

- **origin / source_name**: Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee), stockbee.blogspot.com — "MM" page and "How to
  use market breadth to avoid market crashes" (2011-08).

- **definition**: `"Stockbee Market Monitor is a breadth-based market timing tracker. Extremely
  positive/negative breadth is used for market timing."` It counts, across the US common-stock
  universe, daily 4%+ moves up and down, quarterly 25%+ moves up and down, and tracks T2108. Its
  logic is **contrarian at the extremes**: `"Extremely bearish breadth= bullish and start of a bounce
  or bottom"` and `"Short term extremely bullish breadth= pullback"`.

- **criteria**:
  - `component scan — daily count of stocks up 4%+ and down 4%+ on rising volume — same construction as the 4% Breakout scan — confidence: high`
  - `component scan — count of stocks up 25% and down 25% in a quarter (~60 trading days) — confidence: high (component identified) — value: null for the exact PCF — missing: the quoted 25%-in-a-quarter scan syntax.`
  - `component — T2108, the percentage of NYSE stocks above their 40-day moving average; readings below 20 lead to bottoms — "% of stocks above 40 day moving average. Readings below 20 lead to bottoms" — confidence: high`
  - `primary indicator — the market is bullish when the 25%-up-in-a-quarter count exceeds the 25%-down-in-a-quarter count, bearish when reversed — confidence: high (relationship published) — value: null for a numeric crossover margin — missing: any buffer or hysteresis around the crossover.`
  - `extreme-bearishness buy zone — the 25%-up-in-a-quarter count below 300 — "number of stocks up 25% in Q below 300" — confidence: high`
  - `deeper extreme — a reading below 200 described as producing "extremely powerful" rallies — confidence: med — value: null for the number 200 — missing: the 200 threshold inside a quoted sentence; the fetch returned it adjacent to, not inside, the quote.`
  - `daily 4%-up band labels — "normal buying pressure", "high buying pressure money flowing in to market", "very high buying pressure (normally seen at beginning of a bullish turn from bearish phase)", "extreme buying pressure" — confidence: high for the LABELS — value: null for the four band boundaries — missing: the boundary counts inside quoted sentences. The retrieval returned them as rendered headings (up to 300 / 300–500 / 500–1000 / 1000+) rather than as quoted text. The ordinal STRUCTURE is published and usable; the specific boundary numbers must be re-verified against the raw page before being coded.`
  - `10-day breadth ratio, bullish side — "good for swing trading on long side" at the high end — confidence: high for the LABEL — value: null for 2.0 — missing: the 2.0 threshold inside a quoted sentence.`
  - `10-day breadth ratio, bearish side — "signals start of a bearish move after a bull move has been in progress" at the low end — confidence: high for the LABEL — value: null for 0.50 — missing: the 0.50 threshold inside a quoted sentence.`
  - `tops — explicitly not signalable — "For tops there are no reliable indicators" — confidence: high — this is an important published NEGATIVE claim: he asserts the tool is asymmetric and works at bottoms, not tops.`
  - `sustained-selling signal — "a series of 700 plus days on 4% plus down movements likely indicates development into a larger correction" — confidence: med (returned as paraphrase around a partial quote) — value: null for 700 — missing: the threshold in quoted form.`

- **measured_performance**: **none published by Bonde.** No forward-return study, no sample, no
  benchmark accompanies any threshold on his pages. The claim `"Readings below 20 lead to bottoms"` is
  stated with **no base rate** — how often T2108 goes below 20 without a bottom following is not
  given, and that is the number that would determine whether the signal has an edge. A third-party
  dashboard (github.com/dcimring/stockbee-dashboard) claims to classify regimes with "thresholds based
  on forward returns"; that is someone else's backtest, not Bonde's published statistic, and its
  numbers must not be attributed to him.

- **invalidation**: By his own statement, the framework does not identify tops. Applying it
  symmetrically is a misuse he explicitly warns against.

- **detection_notes**:
  - Fully daily-computable across a universe, and cheap: for each name, `C/C1 − 1 ≥ 0.04` and
    `≤ −0.04` with `V > V1`; the 60-bar 25% move counts; `Close > SMA(Close, 40)` aggregated across
    NYSE common stocks for T2108. The primitives are cross-sectional aggregates, not per-name
    patterns — a different query shape from every other setup in this file.
  - The universe definition matters more than the arithmetic: Bonde runs "US Common Stocks" in
    Telechart (a curated list excluding ETFs, funds and ADR duplicates). A raw all-listings universe
    will inflate every count and invalidate all published thresholds. T2108 specifically is
    NYSE-only. Any implementation must state its universe or the numbers are not comparable to his.
  - **No intraday requirement**, though he notes sub-200 readings often occur `"intra day for few
    hours or minutes"` and are rare at the close — so an EOD implementation will see fewer extremes
    than an intraday one.

- **conflicts**:
  - **vs. Kullamägi**: Kullamägi publishes **no** market-regime filter on his own site — only the
    qualitative `"In a bullish market it is very common to get moves that are 10-20x+ your initial
    risk."` The widely-repeated "index 10-day above 20-day" rule attributed to him is third-party
    (breakoutshappen.com) and could not be traced to his writing. Bonde, by contrast, makes regime the
    top of his funnel. Recorded as an asymmetry, not as agreement.
  - **vs. O'Neil / IBD**: IBD's market-direction call is a *follow-through day* — a specific
    price-and-volume event on an index on day 4 or later of a rally attempt — i.e. a **trend-following
    confirmation**. Bonde's Market Monitor is a **contrarian breadth extreme** ("extremely bearish
    breadth = bullish"). These are opposite epistemologies about the same question, and Bonde's
    version fires *before* IBD's by construction. Both recorded.
  - **vs. Minervini**: Minervini uses index trend and distribution-day counts alongside the breadth of
    leading stocks setting up. No numeric collision with Bonde's thresholds is published on either
    side; recorded as non-overlapping rather than conflicting.

## Cross-cutting notes for implementers

1. **The intraday wall.** Five of the eight setups cannot be triggered from daily OHLCV: Kullamägi's
   Breakout, EP and Parabolic Short (opening-range highs/lows on 1-/5-/60-minute bars, running
   day-low stops, and — uniquely for the short — VWAP, which has no daily-bar analogue), Bonde's EP
   (pre-market and after-hours entries and screens), and Bonde's 4% Breakout / Momentum Burst at the
   *execution* layer (the daily signal is computable; the same-session fill is not). Only **Double
   Trouble / MDT** and **Anticipation** are specifiable end-to-end on daily bars — and Anticipation
   has no published stop.

2. **ADR is not ATR and not mean-range.** Kullamägi's published formula averages the *ratio* `H/L`
   over 20 sessions and subtracts 1. Implementing it as Wilder ATR, as `mean((H−L)/C)`, or over a
   different window silently changes his single hardest numeric rule (the stop-width ceiling).

3. **Telechart volume units.** Bonde's pre-2010 scans express volume in hundreds; his later posts use
   raw shares. `V >= 1000` and `v >= 100000` mean the same thing in different posts.

4. **Where the two authors' numbers are simply absent.** Kullamägi publishes no ADR% minimum, no
   dollar-volume floor, no market-cap floor, no contraction count, no market-regime filter, and no
   per-variant mapping of consolidation length to moving average — despite all six being widely
   attributed to him by third parties with **mutually inconsistent** values ($100M vs $3M dollar
   volume; 5% vs 5–6% ADR). Bonde publishes no numeric definition of "narrow range", "range
   expansion", "linearity", or "extended" — the four terms his setups lean on hardest.

5. **Nothing in this file is a measured setup statistic.** Across all eight setups plus the Market
   Monitor, there is not one published win rate, average gain, or failure rate computed over a stated
   sample of *setup instances*. Every number that looks like performance is one of: an account result
   over a calendar year (Kullamägi's 25%/35% win rate, 268% CAGR, 50% drawdown), a candidate-throughput
   count (Bonde's 0–5/day, 20–25/season, 5–10/day), a claim about a trade-population distribution with
   no sample stated (Bonde's "80% of trades would be 3 days hold"), a single-name anecdote (OME +25%,
   one EP "up over 70 times"), or a third-party backtest whose rules the author withheld (the
   parabolic-short 27.7% CAGR / 1,869 trades). **No base rate accompanies any of them.**
