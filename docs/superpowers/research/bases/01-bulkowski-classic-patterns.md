## READ THIS BEFORE USING ANY NUMBER IN THIS FILE

1. **Fidelity caveat.** Pages were retrieved through a fetch-and-extract pass. Discrete statistics (rank, failure rate, average rise/decline, throwback/pullback rate, target rate, sample size) are read off a compact stats block and are treated as **high** confidence. The wording inside `"..."` for identification guidelines was requested as *short* quoted phrases and may be **abridged** by the extraction pass; those are marked **med** confidence for exact wording even when the substance is certain. Nothing was invented, rounded, or inferred anywhere in this file. Where a page publishes no number for a stated criterion, the entry says `value: null` and names what is missing.
2. **What "average rise/decline" actually measures.** Glossary: ultimate high = `"The highest peak before price declines by at least 20%, measured from the high to the close."` Ultimate low = `"The lowest valley before price rises at least 20%, measured from the low to the close."` So every "average rise: 45%" figure in this file is **breakout → the highest point reached before a 20% reversal**. It is a look-ahead maximum with **no holding period and no exit rule**. It is not a strategy return.
3. **There is no published base rate anywhere.** `id75.html` compares patterns only to each other: `"This is list of chart patterns showing the performance and failure rate rank of each chart pattern by its average rise in a bull market."` No page fetched compares a pattern's average rise to buy-and-hold, to the S&P 500, or to a random-entry control. Every rise/decline number in this file is therefore **unbenchmarked**, and the ranks are ordinal within Bulkowski's own set only.
4. **"Break even failure rate" definition.** Glossary: `"The percentage of patterns that fail to rise (upward breakout) or decline (downward breakout) by at least 5%."` FAQ, in his own words: `"if a double bottom fails to see price rise more than 5% (before it reverses and closes below the bottom), then I consider that a failure."` A 15% "failure rate" therefore means 85% of confirmed patterns moved at least 5% in the breakout direction at some point before a 20% reversal — a very low bar, not a win rate.
5. **Sample provenance.** FAQ: `"My tests often involve hundreds of stocks (from 500 to thousands) and years of daily price data."` and `"If you don't use the same data, you'll get different results."` He states he has manually reviewed `"more than 38,500 chart patterns"`. Most per-pattern stats blocks are stamped **updated 8/26/2020** (Encyclopedia 3rd ed. data); busted-pattern pages are stamped **12/28/2020**; the ugly-double-bottom page carries a newer window (**July 1991 to July 2025**) and a **41**-pattern rank denominator instead of 39. The rank denominators are not consistent across the site (36 / 39 / 41).
6. **"Perfect trade"** is the unit of every sample size below. Bulkowski does not define it on the FAQ or glossary pages fetched. `missing: a published definition of "perfect trade" — until then every sample size below is of an undefined unit.`

# Reversal patterns

## Head-and-Shoulders Top

- **origin / source_name**: Thomas N. Bulkowski, *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns* 3rd ed.; web page https://www.thepatternsite.com/hst.html (stats updated 8/26/2020).
- **definition**: A three-peak topping pattern after an uptrend. `"Looks like a head perched atop two shoulders. A three-peak pattern with the middle peak above the others."` A neckline `"Joins the two armpits."`
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Upward leading to the pattern"` — high (the criterion is stated; no minimum trend length or percentage is published). `missing: a minimum prior-advance percentage or bar count.`
  - three peaks, middle highest — value: null — `"A three-peak pattern with the middle peak above the others."` — high. `missing: how far the head must exceed the shoulders. Bulkowski publishes NO threshold. HSTExplained.html gives no percentage either.`
  - shoulder symmetry — value: null — `"The two shoulders should peak near the same price, be nearly the same distance from the head, and look similar"` — med (wording may be abridged). `missing: numeric tolerance for "near the same price" and "nearly the same distance".`
  - volume shape — 61% — `"Highest on the left shoulder followed by the head. Trends downward 61% of the time."` — high. Note this is a **descriptive frequency of observed patterns**, not a filter he says to apply.
  - confirmation — value: null — `"The pattern confirms as a valid one when price closes below an up-sloping neckline"` — high. `missing: the rule for a DOWN-sloping neckline on the top variant (the bottom page states a substitute rule using the armpit; the top page as fetched does not).`
  - measure rule — 51% — `"Compute the height from the head (A ...) to the neckline directly below (B) then multiply it by the above 'percentage meeting price target.'"` — high.
- **measured_performance** (bull market, sample stated): overall performance rank **9 out of 36**; break-even failure rate **19%**; average decline **16%**; pullback rate **68%**; percentage meeting price target **51%**; `"Based on more than 2,800 perfect trades"`. Period: not stated on the page; site-wide stats stamp is 8/26/2020. **No base rate or benchmark is published beside these.** Average decline is measured to the *ultimate low* (see caveat 2).
- **invalidation**: Price never closes below the neckline ⇒ the pattern never confirms and is not counted. After confirmation, a move of less than 5% before reversal counts as a break-even failure (19% of the sample). A close back above the pattern top after a sub-10% decline makes it a **busted** pattern (see the Busted section).
- **detection_notes**: Requires pivot highs (left/right bar confirmation, e.g. 5/5) to nominate three consecutive peaks P1<P2>P3; pivot lows for the two armpits A1, A2; a linear neckline through (A1, A2) extended forward; a close-below-line test on the extended neckline. Also needs: a prior-uptrend test (e.g. slope of a linear fit over N bars before P1, or price vs a rising MA — **the threshold is not published, so this is your invention, not his**); a shoulder-similarity comparison (|P1−P3|/mid as a percentage — **threshold not published**); a distance-symmetry comparison (|bars(P1→P2) − bars(P2→P3)|); a volume-trend linear fit across the pattern for the 61% descriptive statistic. All computable from daily OHLCV. **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone**: nothing here — but note that "look similar (both wide or both narrow)" is a shape judgement with no published metric, so any width proxy (bars between the pivot's own local lows, or ATR-normalised peak width) is a decision you are making, not his.
- **conflicts**:
  - **Bulkowski vs. Bulkowski (edition drift).** Secondary source ChartScout attributes to Bulkowski's 2005 research (814 patterns): `"Fail to drop 5%: 4% (bull), 1% (bear); Fail to drop 10%: 15% (bull), 5% (bear)"`. The 2020 page states break-even failure rate **19%**. Same author, same pattern, same 5% metric, **4% vs 19%**. Both recorded; not reconciled. Secondary attribution — I could not fetch the 2nd edition text directly to verify the 4%.
  - **Bulkowski vs. Bulkowski (symmetry).** The identification guideline says the shoulders `"should peak near the same price ... and look similar"`. His own study page HSTExplained.html says `"The more unsymmetrical a head-and-shoulders top appears, the better it performs."` and reports `"Those patterns with a higher left shoulder see declines that average 25% compared to declines of 20% for higher right shoulders and 19% for even shoulders."` A screener that enforces symmetry is selecting the **19%** bucket, the worst of the three.
  - **Bulkowski vs. LuxAlgo.** LuxAlgo (Jacob Denbrock, 2026-07-21) publishes only the qualitative `"1: H&S = Left Shoulder < Head > Right Shoulder / 2: Left Shoulder ≈ Right Shoulder"` and, per a search-result summary of that page, a "3 to 5 percent" head-above-shoulders threshold. Bulkowski publishes **no** such threshold. Recorded as a third-party number with no Bulkowski counterpart; **do not attribute it to Bulkowski.**

## Head-and-Shoulders Bottom

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/hsb.html.
- **definition**: `"A 3-valley pattern with the middle valley below the others. The pattern should look like an inverted person's head and shoulders, proportional, and not lopsided."`
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Downward leading to the pattern"` — high. `missing: minimum prior-decline percentage or duration.`
  - three valleys, middle lowest — value: null — `"A 3-valley pattern with the middle valley below the others."` — high. `missing: how far the head must undercut the shoulders.`
  - shoulder symmetry — value: null — `"The two shoulders should bottom near the same price, be nearly the same distance from the head, and look similar (both wide or both narrow)."` — med. `missing: numeric tolerance.`
  - volume — 65% — `"Highest on the left shoulder or head, diminished on the right shoulder. Trends downward 65% of the time."` — high (descriptive frequency).
  - confirmation — value: null — `"The pattern confirms as a valid one when price closes above a down-sloping neckline or above the right armpit when the neckline slopes upward."` — high. This is the **dual-rule** the top page lacks.
  - measure rule — 71% — `"Compute the height from the head low (A) to the neckline directly above (B) and then multiply it by the 'percentage meeting price target.'"` — high. Tactic: `"Wait for confirmation before placing a trade."`
- **measured_performance**: overall performance rank **13 out of 39**; break-even failure rate **11%**; average rise **45%**; throwback rate **65%**; percentage meeting price target **71%**; sample **`"3,197 perfect trades"`**. Period not stated on page; site stamp 8/26/2020. No benchmark published.
- **invalidation**: no close above the neckline (or right armpit when the neckline slopes up) ⇒ never a pattern. Post-confirmation rise under 5% ⇒ break-even failure (11%).
- **detection_notes**: Mirror of the top. Pivot lows V1>V2<V3 with left/right bar confirmation; pivot highs for the armpits; neckline linear fit through the two armpits; **branch on neckline slope** — close above the line when the neckline slopes down, close above the right-armpit price when it slopes up. Prior-downtrend test needed. Volume-trend linear fit for the descriptive 65%. All computable from daily OHLCV.
- **conflicts**: The 45% average rise (rank 13/39) is *higher* than the complex variant's 47% at rank 9/39 — i.e. rank and average rise are not monotone with each other across these two pages, so the rank is not a pure function of average rise. No cross-authority conflict captured.

## Complex Head-and-Shoulders Top

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/chst.html.
- **definition**: A head-and-shoulders top with `"Multiple shoulders or heads, but rarely both"`.
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Upward leading to the pattern"` — high.
  - multiplicity — value: null — `"Multiple shoulders or heads, but rarely both"` — med. `missing: the maximum count of shoulders/heads, and the rule that forbids "both".`
  - symmetry — value: null — `"Shoulders peak near same price, similar distance from head"` — med. `missing: tolerance.`
  - volume — value: null — `"Usually higher on the left side"` — med. `missing: a percentage; the simple HST page publishes 61%, this one publishes none in the fetched extract.`
  - neckline — value: null — `"Joins lowest armpits and often nearly horizontal"` — med.
  - confirmation — value: null — `"Price closes below up-sloping neckline or right armpit"` — high.
- **measured_performance**: rank **7 out of 36**; break-even failure rate **18%**; average decline **17%**; pullback rate **66%**; percentage meeting price target **47%**; sample `"More than 650 perfect trades"`. No benchmark.
- **invalidation**: same as HST — no neckline close, no pattern.
- **detection_notes**: Needs a **variable-length peak sequence** (4–6+ pivot highs) rather than a fixed 3, plus a grouping step that assigns pivots to "shoulder" vs "head" roles and a rule rejecting the both-multiple case. Neckline = fit through the *lowest* armpits, not all armpits. This is materially harder than the 3-pivot case and is the main reason a naive H&S detector will double-report a complex pattern as several overlapping simple ones.
- **conflicts**: `BestPatterns.html` ranks complex H&S top the **best** down-breakout pattern on a 1–3 month horizon (−8% / −10% / −10%), while `id73.html` puts it at performance rank 7 among down-breakout patterns and the simple HST at 9. The two ranking systems (ultimate-move vs 1–3 month) do not agree on ordering; both recorded.

## Complex Head-and-Shoulders Bottom

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/chsb.html.
- **definition**: Inverted complex H&S — `"Multiple shoulders or multiple heads but rarely both"`.
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Downward leading to the pattern"` — high.
  - symmetry — value: null — `"Shoulders should bottom near same price"` — med. `missing: tolerance.`
  - volume — 65% — `"Usually higher on left side of pattern"`, with volume trending downward **65% of the time** — high (frequency).
  - neckline — value: null — `"Joins the highest armpits"` — med.
  - confirmation — value: null — `"Price closes above down-sloping trendline or armpit"` — high.
- **measured_performance**: rank **9 out of 39**; break-even failure rate **7%**; average rise **47%**; throwback rate **66%**; percentage meeting price target **71%**; sample **933 perfect trades**. No benchmark.
- **invalidation**: no confirming close ⇒ no pattern; sub-5% rise ⇒ break-even failure (7%, the lowest of the H&S family).
- **detection_notes**: As complex HST, mirrored. Note the neckline joins the **highest** armpits here vs the **lowest** armpits on the top — an asymmetry a shared implementation will get wrong if it just flips signs.
- **conflicts**: none captured.

## Double Top, Adam & Adam

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/aadt.html. The Adam/Eve taxonomy is his own (see DoubleBottomTypes.html).
- **definition**: `"Two distinct tops that look similar, usually twin spikes"` after an uptrend; Adam = narrow one-day-ish spike.
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Upward leading to the pattern"` — high.
  - shape (Adam) — value: null — `"Two distinct tops that look similar, usually twin spikes"` — med. Adam bottoms/tops are described on DoubleBottomTypes.html as `"narrow, often one-day price spikes"`. `missing: a numeric width limit for "narrow".`
  - valley depth between tops — **at least 10%** — `"at least 10%, but allow exceptions"` — high. This is one of the few hard numbers Bulkowski publishes as an identification rule.
  - peak price variation — **usually less than 3%** — `"variation between price peaks is small, usually less than 3%"` — high.
  - separation — value: null — `"usually several weeks apart"` — med. `missing: a bar-count range for the top variant (the Eve & Eve top page gives "2 to 6 week range"; this page gives none).`
  - confirmation — value: null — `"price closes below the valley between the two peaks"` — high.
  - volume — value: null — `"Usually higher on formation of the left peak than the right"` — med. `missing: a ratio or a percentage-of-time figure.`
- **measured_performance**: rank **19 out of 36**; break-even failure rate **25%**; average decline **15%**; pullback rate **64%**; percentage meeting price target **64%**; sample **1,114 perfect trades**. No benchmark.
- **invalidation**: no close below the intervening valley ⇒ not a double top, just two peaks. Valley depth under 10% or peak variation materially over 3% ⇒ fails identification.
- **detection_notes**: Two pivot highs P1, P2 with a pivot low V between them. Computables: `|P1−P2| / max(P1,P2) ≤ 0.03`; `(max(P1,P2) − V) / max(P1,P2) ≥ 0.10`; bar separation between P1 and P2; a peak-width metric to classify Adam vs Eve (bars the peak spends within X% of its high, or ATR-normalised spike width — **the Adam/Eve width cutoff is not published**, so this is your calibration); volume of the P1 window vs the P2 window; close < V for confirmation. All from daily OHLCV.
- **conflicts**: none captured against another authority. Internal: the 10% valley rule is stated as `"at least 10%, but allow exceptions"` — a hard number immediately softened, which makes it unusable as a strict screener gate without deciding the exception policy yourself.

## Double Top, Eve & Eve

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/eedt.html. Described there as `"the classic double top formation"`.
- **definition**: Two `"Rounded and wide looking"` tops at similar price after an uptrend.
- **criteria**:
  - shape (Eve) — value: null — `"Two distinct tops that look similar"`, both `"Rounded and wide looking"` — med. `missing: numeric width/roundness criterion separating Eve from Adam.`
  - valley depth — **at least 10%** — `"At least 10%"` — high.
  - peak price variation — **often less than 3%** — `"Variation between price peaks is small, often less than 3%"` — high.
  - separation — **2 to 6 weeks** (typical range) — `"Several weeks apart with most falling in the 2 to 6 week range"` — high. This is a *descriptive* modal range, not a stated gate.
  - confirmation — value: null — price closes below the valley between the tops — high.
  - volume — value: null — `"Usually higher on formation of the left peak"` — med.
- **measured_performance**: rank **12 out of 36**; break-even failure rate **20%**; average decline **16%**; pullback rate **65%**; percentage meeting price target **43%**; sample **942 perfect trades**. No benchmark.
- **invalidation**: as Adam & Adam.
- **detection_notes**: Same primitives as A&A plus the Eve/Adam width classifier. The 2–6 week separation converts to roughly 10–30 trading bars but **he publishes weeks, not bars** — the conversion is yours.
- **conflicts**: percentage meeting price target is **43%** here vs **64%** for Adam & Adam, while the average declines are nearly identical (16% vs 15%). Two "same family" patterns disagree by 21 points on target achievement with near-identical average moves — recorded, not reconciled.

## Double Bottom, Adam & Adam

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/aadb.html.
- **definition**: `"Two distinct valleys that look similar"`, both narrow/spiky, after a downtrend.
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Downward leading to the pattern"` — high.
  - rise between bottoms — **at least 10%** — `"Rise between bottoms should measure at least 10%"` — high.
  - bottom price variation — **1%** (average, measured) — `"Price variation between bottoms is small"`, average bottom price variation **1%** — high (the 1% is a measured average, not a gate).
  - separation — **median 16 days** — `"Usually several weeks apart"`, median separation **16 days** — high (measured median, not a gate).
  - confirmation — value: null — `"Price closes above the peak between valleys"` — high.
  - volume — value: null — `"Usually higher on formation of first bottom"` — med.
- **measured_performance**: rank **26 out of 39**; break-even failure rate **16%**; average rise **39%**; throwback rate **67%**; percentage meeting price target **73%**; sample **1,154 perfect trades**. Measure rule stated as `"Multiply the pattern height by 73% and add result to breakout price"`. No benchmark.
- **invalidation**: no close above the intervening peak ⇒ not a double bottom. Rise between bottoms under 10% ⇒ fails identification.
- **detection_notes**: Pivot lows V1, V2 with pivot high P between; `(P − min(V1,V2))/min(V1,V2) ≥ 0.10`; `|V1−V2|/min(V1,V2)` compared against the measured 1% average (note: **1% is what he observed, not a threshold he sets** — using it as a gate is your choice); bar separation vs the 16-day median; volume window comparison; close > P for confirmation.
- **conflicts**: Adam & Adam is the **worst** of the four double-bottom variants by rank (26/39) yet has the **second-highest** percentage meeting price target (73%) of the three variants captured. Rank and target-hit rate disagree in direction.

## Double Bottom, Adam & Eve

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/aedb.html.
- **definition**: First bottom narrow/V-shaped (Adam), second wide/rounded (Eve). `"Adam is narrow and V-shaped, Eve is wide and rounded."`
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Downward leading to the pattern."` — high.
  - rise between bottoms — **at least 10%** — `"The rise between bottoms should measure at least 10%,"` with taller patterns performing better — high.
  - bottom price variation — value: null — `"The price variation between bottoms is small."` — med. `missing: a percentage for this variant (Eve & Eve publishes "0% and 6%"; this page does not).`
  - separation — **almost two months** (average) — `"The average separation between bottoms is almost two months."` — high (measured average, not a gate).
  - confirmation — value: null — `"Price closes above the peak between the two valleys"` — high.
  - volume — value: null — `"Usually higher on formation of the left valley."` — med.
- **measured_performance**: rank **17 out of 39**; break-even failure rate **12%**; average rise **43%**; throwback rate **67%**; percentage meeting price target **69%**; sample **1,020 perfect trades**. Page adds: without confirmation `there's a 48% chance price continues lower`. No benchmark.
- **invalidation**: no confirming close ⇒ no pattern; unconfirmed patterns break down ~48% of the time per the page.
- **detection_notes**: Same primitives as A&A, but the Adam-vs-Eve classifier must run **per bottom** and the two must come out *different*. Needs a width/roundness metric per valley. This is the single most under-specified primitive in the double-bottom family: Bulkowski gives only prose (`"narrow, often one-day price spikes"` vs `"wider and more rounded looking"`), never a number. `missing: any numeric definition of Adam vs Eve.`
- **conflicts**: none captured.

## Double Bottom, Eve & Eve

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/eedb.html. Called there `"what most chartists call the classic double bottom"`.
- **definition**: Two wide, rounded valleys near the same price after a downtrend.
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Downward leading to the pattern"` — high.
  - rise between bottoms — **at least 10%** — `"Rise between bottoms should measure at least 10%"` — high.
  - bottom price variation — **0% to 6%** — `"Price variation between bottoms is small, usually between 0% and 6%"` — high. This is the **only** double-bottom page in the set that publishes an explicit variation range.
  - separation — **2 to 7 weeks** (modal range) — `"Twin valleys are several weeks apart with most falling in the 2 to 7 week range"` — high (descriptive).

## Rounding Bottom

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://www.thepatternsite.com/roundb.html (stats updated 8/26/2020; page cites *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns*).
- **definition**: `"Rounded bowl shape, usually over many months"` — a saucer.
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — **67%** — `"Price trends upward to the pattern 67% of the time"` — high. **Note: a "bottom" pattern that is preceded by an *uptrend* two thirds of the time.**
  - shape — value: null — `"Rounded bowl shape, usually over many months"` — med. `missing: any curvature metric, minimum duration in weeks, or maximum allowed depth.`
  - bump — value: null — `"Price shoots up midway through the turn, near the bottom"` — med (a permitted feature).
  - confirmation — value: null — `"Close above the left peak because price on the right might not pause"` — high.
  - measure rule — 65% — `"Compute the height from the left saucer lip to the lowest valley in the pattern then multiply it by the above 'percentage meeting price target.' Add the difference to the right saucer lip"` — high.
- **measured_performance**: rank **7 out of 39**; break-even failure rate **4%** (the lowest in this entire file); average rise **48%**; throwback rate **64%**; percentage meeting price target **65%**; sample **990 perfect trades**. No benchmark. `id73.html` also gives rounding bottom performance **1** for down breakouts.
- **invalidation**: no close above the left lip ⇒ unconfirmed.
- **detection_notes**: Needs a **quadratic (2nd-order polynomial) fit** over a long window with a positive leading coefficient and an R² floor, or a rolling-minimum smoothness test; plus the left-lip pivot high and a close-above test. The left lip is the *earlier* peak, which means the measure rule and the confirmation both reference a level that can be many months back — your pivot lookback must be long (months, not the 5/5 bars used for H&S). **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone**: nothing, but the absence of any published duration bound means the window length is entirely your choice and will dominate the hit count.
- **conflicts**: The **4%** break-even failure rate is an outlier against every other pattern in this file (next best: cup with handle 5%, complex H&S bottom 7%). With no published base rate (caveat 3) there is no way to tell whether 4% reflects the pattern or reflects the fact that a months-long rounded base is, by construction, only identifiable in hindsight after price has already turned up.

## Diamond Top

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://www.thepatternsite.com/diamondt.html.

- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Upward leading to the pattern"` — high.
  - shape — value: null — `"Looks like a diamond, but one usually tilted to the side"`; forms broadening then symmetrical triangle phases — med.
  - touches — value: null — `"Prices will touch each trendline once or twice"` — med. Note this is a **much lower** touch requirement than the triangles (3 and 2).

- **invalidation**: no close outside either trendline ⇒ unconfirmed. Fewer than the required touches, or no broadening-then-narrowing sequence ⇒ not a diamond.
- **detection_notes**: The hardest pattern here to automate. ## Ascending Triangle

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/at.html.
- **definition**: `"Triangular. Prices move between two converging trendlines."` with `"the top trendline is horizontal and the bottom one slopes upward."`
- **criteria**:
  - prior trend — value: null — `"Can be any direction leading to the chart pattern."` — high. **There is no prior-trend requirement**, contrary to how the pattern is usually taught.
  - trendlines — value: null — `"Two trendlines bound prices; the top trendline is horizontal and the bottom one slopes upward."` — high. `missing: how flat "horizontal" must be (max slope) and how steep the rising line may be.`
  - touches — **3 and 2** — `"Price must touch one trendline at least three times, the other at least twice, forming distinct valleys and peaks."` — high. The cleanest published touch rule in the set.
  - white space — value: null — `"Price must cross the pattern from side to side, filling the triangle with price movement, not white space."` — high. `missing: a fill-ratio number.`
  - volume — **at least 78%** — `"Trends downward at least 78% of the time."` — high (frequency).
  - breakout — **63% up**, at **64%** of the distance to the apex — `"Upward 63% of the time and 64% of the way to the triangle apex (for both breakout directions)."` — high.
- **measured_performance**: rank **16 out of 39 (up)** / **30 out of 36 (down)**; break-even failure rate **17% / 38%**; average rise/decline **43% / 13%**; throwback/pullback **64% / 63%**; percentage meeting price target **70% / 44%**; sample `"more than 1,400 perfect trades"`. No benchmark.
- **invalidation**: fewer than 3+2 touches; price fails to cross side to side (too much white space); no close outside a trendline.
- **detection_notes**: Primitives: pivot highs and pivot lows; a horizontal-line fit to the pivot highs with a **max-slope tolerance you must choose** (unpublished); a rising linear fit to the pivot lows with a positive-slope test and R²; a touch counter with a tolerance band (e.g. within k·ATR of the line — the tolerance is unpublished); a fill/white-space metric (fraction of bars whose range intersects the triangle interior); apex computation from the two line intersections and a `bars_to_apex` ratio to test the 64% figure; breakout = close above the horizontal line (`at.html` notes the flat top makes the breakout price trivially known, unlike a symmetrical triangle). All from daily OHLCV.
- **conflicts**: **`at.html` vs `id73.html`.** The pattern page gives rank **16 (up) / 30 (down)**. The alphabetical rank table gives performance **16 (up), 30 (down)** but *failure* rank **27 (up), 35 (down)**. Performance rank and failure rank are different columns and diverge by 11 places — a screener ranking "best patterns" will get a different order depending on which column it reads. Recorded.

Everything else requires you to supply the width bound (e.g. `(highest_high − lowest_low) / lowest_low ≤ X`) and the flatness measure (slope of a linear fit through closes ≈ 0, or ATR-normalised range). Since he explicitly declines to require flat tops/bottoms, a naive two-horizontal-trendline detector is *stricter* than his definition. Treat flat base as a **family** (rectangle bottom is one member) rather than a single detectable shape.
- **conflicts**: The 41%-vs-51% duration finding is the clearest published "longer base ⇒ bigger move" evidence in the set, but it is stated **without a sample size**, so it cannot be weighed against the rectangle-bottom page's own 48% pooled average rise (900+ trades). Both recorded.

This is the single most important entry in this file for a screener, because a screener holds for a **fixed horizon**, not to the ultimate high.

# Cross-pattern findings

## Busted patterns (Bulkowski's own concept)

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/bustedsetup.html, https://thepatternsite.com/BustHST.html, https://thepatternsite.com/BustHSB.html, https://thepatternsite.com/BustDoubleBots.html, https://thepatternsite.com/BustSymTriangles.html. Busted-pattern pages are stamped **updated 12/28/2020**, sourced to *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns, 3rd edition*.
- **definition**: `"price breaks out in one direction and moves less than 10% away from the breakout price, reverses, and then breaks out in the new direction."` Then `"After the pattern busts, price tends to trend in the new direction, setting up a profitable trade."`
- **criteria**:
  - single bust — **10% threshold, both sides** — HST version: price must confirm, `"drop no more than 10%"`, then close above the pattern top and `"continues rising more than 10%"` — high.
  - double bust — **10% threshold again** — the first bust reverses again after `"fails to rise more than 10%"` — high.
  - triple bust — **10% threshold a third time** — `"price fails to drop more than 10% below the bottom"` before reversing again; the pattern continues until price moves more than 10% beyond the boundaries — high.
- **measured_performance** (no sample sizes are published on any busted page — see rule 6):
  - Head-and-shoulders **top**: **32%** bust. Of those: **67%** single, **3%** double, **30%** triple. Single-busted average price rise **67%** vs **45%** for non-busted. `missing: sample sizes; also the "45% for non-busted head-and-shoulders tops" figure is a RISE, which contradicts hst.html's average DECLINE of 16% — the comparison basis is not stated and I could not resolve it.`
  - Head-and-shoulders **bottom**: **16%** bust. Of those: **47%** single, **36%** double, **17%** triple. Single-busted average decline **22%** vs **16%** for non-busted. `missing: sample sizes; the "non-busted head-and-shoulders bottoms average a 16% price drop" phrasing is ambiguous for a bottoming pattern.`
  - Double **bottom**: **20%** bust. Of those: **53%** single, **35%** double, **12%** triple. Single-busted average decline **23%** vs **16%** for non-busted. `missing: sample sizes.`
  - Symmetrical triangle (downward breakouts): **48%** bust. Of those: **67%** single, **6%** double, **28%** triple. Single-busted average rise **52%** vs **34%** for non-busted. `missing: sample sizes.`
- **invalidation**: a move of **more than 10%** in the original breakout direction before reversing ⇒ the pattern did not bust, it worked. A reversal that never closes beyond the opposite side ⇒ not a bust.
- **detection_notes**: A bust is a **state machine over post-breakout bars**, not a shape: (1) confirmed breakout at price B in direction D; (2) track `max_adverse_excursion` in direction D — if it exceeds 10% of B, the bust window closes permanently; (3) a close beyond the opposite boundary of the pattern = bust #1; (4) repeat with the sign flipped for busts #2 and #3. Requires holding the pattern's high and low boundaries as persistent state and evaluating every subsequent bar. Fully computable from daily OHLCV, but it needs **per-pattern persistent state after detection** — most screeners are stateless per-bar scans and cannot express this without a pattern registry.
- **conflicts**: The single-bust "average rise 67% vs 45%" style comparisons are published **without sample sizes and without a benchmark**, and the "non-busted" comparison figures do not match the corresponding pattern pages' own headline numbers (e.g. HST: 45% rise here vs 16% decline on hst.html). Both recorded, neither reconciled. **The bust rates themselves are the more usable fact**: 32% of H&S tops, 48% of down-breakout symmetrical triangles, 20% of double bottoms, and 16% of H&S bottoms reverse on his 10% definition.

## Throwbacks

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/throwbacks.html.
- **definition**: `"After an upward breakout from a chart pattern, price lifts off then falters and returns to the launch price."`
- **criteria**:
  - time window — **30 calendar days** — `"By convention, a throwback occurs within 30 calendar days after the breakout."` — high.
  - shape — value: null — price must rise and *curve back* to the breakout price rather than sliding along it — med. `missing: a minimum rise before the return (the page describes a "lift off" but publishes no distance).`
- **measured_performance**: `"Since year 2000, 58% of 10,305 chart patterns with upward breakouts had throwbacks."` Timing: `"It takes an average of 10 days, total, for price to complete the return trip back to the breakout"`, with price typically climbing for **6 days** before reversing. Performance split: when price stayed at or above the breakout, `"the rise averaged 40%"` (**400 samples**); when price dropped below the breakout, `"the resulting rise averaged just 29%"` (**2,767 samples**). Figures drawn from **3,167 throwbacks out of 10,348 patterns**.
- **invalidation**: a return after 30 days is not a throwback; a slide along the breakout line is not a throwback.
- **detection_notes**: Post-breakout state again: track `max(high)` after the breakout, then a close (or low) back at/below the breakout price within 30 calendar days. The 40%-vs-29% split needs a *third* state — whether the throwback's low undercut the breakout price. Computable from daily OHLCV with a per-pattern registry.
- **conflicts**: **Three different throwback base rates on the same site.** `throwbacks.html`: **58%** of 10,305 patterns. `Volume.html`: overall throwback rate **57.0%**, rising to **74%** when the breakout came on above-average volume (3,749 throwbacks). Per-pattern pages: **52% to 72%**. The 400-sample vs 2,767-sample split is also badly unbalanced — the "40% average rise" arm rests on 400 observations against 2,767. All recorded.

## Pullbacks

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://www.thepatternsite.com/pullbacks.html.
- **definition**: `"After a downward breakout from a chart pattern, price drops but then sometimes curls upward and returns to the breakout price."`
- **criteria**:
  - time window — **30 days** — pullbacks occur within 30 days after the breakout — high.
  - typical depth — **average 9%**, most in the **4–10%** range — high (measured, not a gate).
- **measured_performance**: Since 2000, **58% of 8,765** chart patterns with downward breakouts had pullbacks. Average round trip about **11 days**. After returning to the breakout price, **53%** of stocks continue rising above the pattern while **47%** resume the decline. When price stayed below the breakout during the pullback the subsequent drop averaged **25%**, versus **20%** when it climbed above (sample: **2,738** pullbacks).
- **invalidation**: return after 30 days.
- **detection_notes**: Mirror of the throwback machinery.
- **conflicts**: `pullbacks.html` gives **58%** of 8,765; `Volume.html` gives **59.7%** overall and **68%** on above-average breakout volume. Recorded.

## Volume trend — the finding that contradicts the guidelines

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/Volume.html.
- **definition**: A study of volume trend before the breakout and volume on the breakout day, against post-breakout performance.
- **measured_performance** (all with sample ranges stated on the page):
  - 3-week pre-breakout window, upward breakouts: **rising** volume trend ⇒ **16%** failure rate; **falling** volume trend ⇒ **21%** failure rate — `"31% reduction in failures with rising volume"`. Winners (25%+ gain): **54%** with rising volume vs **50%** with falling. Samples 1,505–2,902 patterns.
  - Downward breakouts: failures **26–30%** regardless of volume trend; winners **28–30%** — no effect.
  - Breakout-day volume, upward breakouts: average gain rises from **36%** (breakout volume 0–10% of average) to **59%** (600–650% of average); failure rate falls from **25%** to **8%** as breakout volume rises.
  - Author's own summary: `"The difference between rising and falling volume means performance difference of 2 percentage points"` and most results are `"not enough that you will see a difference in your trading results."`
- **detection_notes**: Two distinct primitives, and they are not the same thing. (1) **Volume trend inside the pattern** — a linear fit (slope of volume vs bar index) across the pattern's bars, or a first-half/second-half mean ratio. (2) **Breakout-day volume ratio** — `volume[breakout] / SMA(volume, 30)` (the scoring calculator asks for volume vs the **30-day average**). Both from daily OHLCV.
- **conflicts**: **This is the sharpest self-contradiction in Bulkowski's material.** Every pattern page above states that volume `"trends downward"` in 55–86% of patterns, and this is universally read as a *quality filter*. But `Volume.html` measures that **rising** pre-breakout volume produces a **lower** failure rate on up breakouts (16% vs 21%). The pattern pages report a **descriptive frequency of what patterns look like**; the volume study reports **what predicts performance**; the two point in opposite directions and a screener that gates on "volume must recede" is selecting the worse-performing bucket. Recorded. Also note the effect size he himself calls small (2 percentage points on average rise), while the failure-rate effect (16% vs 21%) is proportionally large — the two framings of the same data support opposite conclusions.

## Performance rank tables

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/id73.html (alphabetical rank), https://thepatternsite.com/id75.html (by performance), https://www.thepatternsite.com/rank.html (landing page — the landing page itself carries no table).
- **definition**: `"A performance rank of 1 is best (highest post breakout rise or decline, or lowest failure rate)."` `"This is list of chart patterns showing the performance and failure rate rank of each chart pattern by its average rise in a bull market."`
- **criteria**: **39** patterns ranked for upward breakouts, **36** for downward breakouts. Two separate columns exist — **performance rank** and **failure rank** — and they are NOT the same ordering.
- **measured_performance**: ranks captured (performance up / performance down, then failure up / failure down where they differ): H&S top 9/10, 9/10 · H&S bottom 13/10, 13/10 · cHS top 7/9 · cHS bottom 9/3 · DT A&A 19/21 · DT A&E 10/13 · DT E&A 16/14 · DT E&E 12/11 · DB A&A 26/25 · DB A&E 17/14 · DB E&A 20/12 · DB E&E 5/13 · Triple top 24/23 · Triple bottom 12/16 · Asc triangle perf 16/30, **fail 27/35** · Desc triangle perf 33/15, **fail 34/18** · Sym triangle 36/34 · Rect top perf 4/32, **fail 23/32** · Rect bottom perf 8/14, **fail 18/20** · Rising wedge perf 32/36, **fail 31/36** · Falling wedge perf 31/27, **fail 37/27** · Broadening top perf 22/28, **fail 29/25** · Broadening bottom perf 15/23, **fail 25/24** · Diamond top perf 39/3, **fail 33/3** · Diamond bottom perf 27/1, **fail 15/5** · Rounding bottom 7/1 · Rounding top perf 2/3, **fail 4/12** · Cup with handle 3/2 · Ascending scallop perf 20/22, **fail 11/18** · Descending scallop perf 29/12, **fail 17/8**. **Flags and pennants are not in the table at all.** — confidence: **med** for the table as a whole; the extraction pass may have mis-assigned up/down columns for single-direction patterns such as the H&S top, which has no upward breakout. Where a per-pattern page and this table disagree, **trust the per-pattern page**.
- **detection_notes**: n/a — this is a lookup table, not a detector. If you use ranks as screener weights, pick **one** column (performance or failure) and say which; mixing them silently is how two "top pattern" lists end up disagreeing.
- **conflicts**: (a) Performance rank and failure rank diverge by up to **11 places** (ascending triangle 16 vs 27; falling wedge 31 vs 37; rectangle top 4 vs 23). (b) Rank denominators are inconsistent site-wide: **36**, **39**, and (on udb.html) **41**. (c) `BestPatterns.html` produces a **completely different ordering** using 1/2/3/6-month post-breakout moves over `over 30,000 chart pattern samples` — high and tight flags **22%/21%/21%** first, Eve & Eve double bottom **9%/12%/13%**, ascending/inverted scallop **11%/12%/13%** for up breakouts; complex H&S top **−8%/−10%/−10%**, H&S top **−7%/−8%/−7%**, diamond top **−8%/−7%/−6%** for down breakouts. Under this fixed-horizon measure, the cup with handle disappears entirely (`"performance is poor"`) despite being rank 3 of 39 on the ultimate-move measure. **For a fixed-horizon screener, BestPatterns.html's numbers are the relevant ones and they are an order of magnitude smaller than the headline "average rise" figures.**

## Scoring system (from *Trading Classic Chart Patterns*)

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, *Trading Classic Chart Patterns*; site pages https://thepatternsite.com/scoring.html (landing) and https://thepatternsite.com/scat.html (the ascending-triangle scoring calculator).
- **definition**: `"Various technical and fundamental factors are scored to create a final number...Values above 0 often result in performance that vastly exceeds the result from chart patterns with scores below 0."`
- **criteria** — the calculator's **inputs** are visible even though the point values are not:
  - highest / lowest price in the pattern — value: null — (input to a pattern-height factor) — high that it is an input.
  - 52-week high / low price — value: null — (input to a yearly-range-position factor) — high.
  - shares outstanding — value: null — (input to a market-capitalisation factor) — high.
  - breakout price and breakout direction — value: null — high.
  - trend start duration — value: null — high.
  - overhead resistance present (yes/no) — value: null — high.
  - volume trend direction — value: null — high.
  - breakout volume vs the **30-day average** — **30-day** window — high (the only published window length in the scoring inputs).
  - throwback/pullback expectation — value: null — high.
  - `missing: EVERY point value (+1/0/-1) and EVERY numeric threshold — the pattern-height percentage, the market-cap dollar cutoffs, the yearly-range third boundaries, the volume-ratio cutoff. The site publishes the inputs and the outcome but not the mapping. The book is required.`
- **measured_performance**: `"Patterns with scores above 0 show gains averaging 50% (upward breakouts) or declines averages 23% (downward breakouts). Scores below 0 show gains of 21% and declines of 12%."` **No sample size and no date range are published for this split** (scat.html). Against the ascending triangle's own pooled average rise of 43%, a score>0 subset at 50% and a score<0 subset at 21% is a large spread — but with no sample sizes it cannot be weighed.
- **invalidation**: n/a (a scoring overlay, not a pattern).
- **detection_notes**: Eight of the nine inputs are computable from daily OHLCV plus a shares-outstanding field: pattern height (`(high−low)/breakout_price`), 52-week range position, breakout-day volume / SMA(volume,30), volume trend fit, trend-start duration. **Not computable from daily OHLCV alone**: shares outstanding / market capitalisation (needs a fundamentals feed) and `"overhead resistance"` (needs a resistance-level detector — prior pivot highs or a volume-by-price profile — which Bulkowski does not specify).
- **conflicts**: The site presents the scoring outcome (50%/23% vs 21%/12%) as a large effect while withholding the rules that produce it, and `scoring.html` refers the reader to the book. That means the scoring claim is **unverifiable and unimplementable from published sources** — record it, do not build on it.

## Entry timing

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/BestEntry.html (double bottoms).
- **definition**: A test of three entry methods on double bottoms: buy immediately, wait for confirmation, or place a buy stop `"a penny above the top of the twin bottom."`
- **measured_performance**: Study period `"bull market data from January 1990 to December 2018"` on **502 stocks**, `"I logged 5,467 trades in the test"`. Buy stop above the pattern top: median rise **23%** (average rise **44%**), 5% failure rate **17.7%**, **2,703** trades — `"the lowest failure rate of any row in the table"`. Waiting for confirmation: median rise **22%**, failure rate **17.9%**, **1,913** trades. Buying immediately: median rise **10%**, failure rate **37.4%**, **4,438** trades. Also: **41%** showed a buy-signal trigger before confirmation, and only **6%** of trades did not confirm.
- **detection_notes**: The buy-stop entry is trivially computable (`stop = pattern_high + 0.01`) and is the same level as confirmation for a double bottom, so the 23%-vs-22% median difference is a timing artifact within the same bar in most cases. **This is the only page in the file that publishes a median alongside a mean** — median **23%** vs average **44%** on the same 2,703 trades, i.e. the distribution is severely right-skewed. Every other "average rise" number in this file is a mean with no median published, and by this one data point the mean is roughly **double** the median.
- **conflicts**: **The single most important number in this file for calibrating expectations.** Average rise 44% / median rise 23% on the same sample means the headline "average rise" figures throughout Bulkowski's work are inflated by a long right tail. `missing: medians for every other pattern — they are not published.` Note also that this study spans **January 1990 – December 2018**, a different window from the 8/26/2020 pattern-page stats and the July 1991 – July 2025 window on udb.html.

## Frequency study (for base rates on how often patterns appear)

- **origin / source_name**: Bulkowski, https://thepatternsite.com/frequency.html.
- **measured_performance**: Sample **500 stocks**, period **July 1991 to July 1996**, covering **35 patterns with upward breakouts and 34 with downward breakouts**. Most frequent upward: pipe bottoms (`"4 out of 22"`); most frequent downward: bump-and-run reversal tops (`"5 out of 10"`). The page's own caution: `"The rankings had nothing to do with how often a pattern appears."`
- **conflicts**: The frequency study covers **1991–1996** while the performance statistics are stamped **2020** — a 24-year gap. Frequency figures from this page must not be combined with performance figures from the pattern pages as if they described the same population.
