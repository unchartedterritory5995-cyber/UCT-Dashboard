## 1. What it is

**In two sentences.** An episodic pivot is the day a neglected stock is re-priced by surprise news: it gaps up 10% or
more at the open, on volume many times its normal, and holds the gap. The trade buys that first day's strength (or the
first tight pause after it) with a stop under a nearby low, and rides the new trend that big buyers need weeks or months
to build.

**Why it works (the psychology).**

- **Big buyers cannot buy it all in one day.** Kullamägi: "Remember, when institutions buy, they don’t buy in one day.
  It can take many months for them to get to their desired allocation."
  (https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/). The gap is the first day of that buying, not the last.
- **The surprise is biggest where expectations were lowest.** Bonde: "This works best on stocks where the market has
  very low expectations." (https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2023/07/earnings-episodic-pivots-season-is.html). Kullamägi
  asks the same question from the other side: "If the stock has already made a big, multi month move into the gap-up, is
  it really a surprise to the market?" (https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/).
- **The news forces a new valuation.** Kullamägi: "The fundamental logic for an EP is news that catches the market off
  guard and forces a revaluation of the stock." Bonde calls the right news "game changing": "Game changing news is news
  that leads to market participants fundamentally re evaluating the prospect of the company for longer term holding."
  (https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2021/12/night-time-is-right-time-to-find-big.html).
- **Academic support for the drift after surprises.** A 1995 NBER paper by Chan, Jegadeesh and Lakonishok: "Past return
  and past earnings surprise each predict large drifts in future returns after controlling for the other." and "The
  results suggest a market that responds only gradually to new information." (https://www.nber.org/papers/w5375). Bonde
  grounds the EP in this effect (PEAD) himself (section 3.7).
- **Who is trapped.** My reading, not a sourced claim: the people who sold the neglected stock before the news, the
  shorts, and the funds that were underweight. Bonde's own reason for buying on day 1: "So if you don't enter on the earnings day you will be just a
  observer." (https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2007/03/how-to-trade-earnings.html).
- **Volume is the commitment.** Kullamägi: "Volume is the #1 thing to focus on." Bonde, on earnings breakouts that went
  on to big moves: "typically of 10 times or more compared to average volume"
  (https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2010/02/what-are-episodic-pivots-and-how-to.html).

**Why it fails.** The news is a one-day event; the gap is too big and becomes an exhaustion gap; the stock had already
run (no surprise left); it is a second EP late in a move. Bonde on late-cycle surprises: "It can lead to blowout runaway
move or it can lead to reversal after one day gap up." (https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2007/04/episodic-pivots_25.html).
On very large gaps, in his daily study of stocks up 50% or more in 40 days: "But if the gaps are more than 20%, the
stocks pull back most of the time. Smaller gaps are better." (https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2024/09/gaps-in-50-plus-moves.html).

## 2. Origin and naming

**Who named it.** Pradeep Bonde (the Stockbee blog). The earliest uses I found are his posts of February 2007, for
example the post titled *Episodic Pivots and "Idea Pickle"* (2007-02-26,
https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2007/02/episodic-pivots-and-idea-pickle.html). I did not find an earlier use by anyone,
but I could not search the web after my search budget ran out, so the earliest I found is all I can say.

*Checker's addition.* The blog's own post feed (every post back to its first, 2006-12-27) was searched for "episodic".
The earliest post that uses the term and was not edited later is "Up stocks and down stocks" (2007-02-13,
https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2007/02/up-stocks-and-down-stocks.html; the feed shows it last updated the same
morning). There he writes the term in quotation marks and gives the idea before the method: "Sometimes a major one day
move kicks off a multi month or multi year rally." and "The new information in some case is so significant that it
fundamentally alters market participants view of the stock or sector." A short post titled "Episodic Pivots" followed
on 2007-02-23 (https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2007/02/episodic-pivots.html, a group move after an Analog Devices
earnings report). Four earlier-dated posts (2007-01-11, 2007-02-01, 2007-02-06, 2007-02-08) also contain the words,
but the feed shows each was edited years later (2013 or 2021), so they are not counted. This
settles the date on his blog only; the wider web was not searched for an earlier use by someone else.

**How he built it (his own account in that post).** A study of stocks that doubled: "Majority of the stock in the list
had one day 10% or plus move within first 20 to 25% of their 100% or more growth in 260 days." He then adapted a
price-and-volume system attributed to George Fontanills in Jon D. Markman's book *Swing Trading: Power Strategies to Cut
Risk and Boost Profits*, and noticed the same thing in Mark Minervini's published trade list: "After close xamination I
found that most of them were triggered by news pivots and the entries were post such high volume spikes." (sic). The
one-line idea: "The basic idea is to study large one day move to find stock likely to go up." The same post says he
had "around 12 Episodic Triggers which have high probability of starting off a 100% plus move."

**Who popularised it.** Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie), who credits Bonde: "a setup I learned from Pradeep Bonde
(Stockbee), that has rewarded me several tens of millions in profits over the past couple of years alone"
(https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/, 2021-11-02). It is one of his three setups (with
breakouts and parabolic shorts) on his 2021-01-08 page and his FAQ.

## 3. Every definition, source by source

### 3.1 Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee), the originator, 2007 to 2026

Bonde never wrote one fixed rule set. His posts span 19 years and his numbers change. I list them by topic with dates.
All links are stockbee.blogspot.com.

**What it is.**
- "The central idea behind the Episodic Pivot is a big price or volume move plus some episode." and "The news element is
  as critical as the price/volume action." (his comment of 2007-03-23 on /2007/02/episodic-pivots-and-idea-pickle.html)
- "EP is a very specific pattern post earnings that shows up on stocks with years of neglect." (2014-09-02,
  /2014/09/everyday-hunt-for-stocks-with-big.html)
- "Episodic Pivot is a news-based setup that looks for a stock with news that is capable of making an explosive move."
  (2024-10-06, /2024/10/news-moves-stocks.html)
- It is not mechanical: "The Episodic Pivots approach gives you really really top quality ideas to focus on. But it is
  not purely mechanical." (2007-02-26). On his 2010 scan: "I buy less than 1% stocks from that scan." (comment,
  2010-02-22, /2010/02/what-are-episodic-pivots-and-how-to.html)

**The size of the move (his scans).** Every daily scan he published measures the **price against the prior close**
(`C - C1`, `c/c1`), not the open, so a stock that opens flat and runs up qualifies. One scan (the 2007 fifth scan) asks
only for a close above the prior close, of any size, plus a volume spike. His after-hours and pre-market screens (below) are, in effect, early readings of the gap.
- 2007 (five separate bullish TC2000 scans): up 20% on volume over 10,000; up 30% on volume over 3,000; up $5 on volume
  over 10,000; "4 ( 100 * (C - C1) / C1) >= 10 AND V > 1000 AND C >= 5"; and "5 C > C1 AND V > 5 * AVGV50.1 AND V > 3000
  AND C > 5". In old TC2000, volume was counted in hundreds of shares: the post's own translation of Fontanills' "1 up or
  down 30% in a single day on at least 300000 shares traded or" became the scan "2 ( 100 * (C - C1) / C1) >= 30 AND V >
  3000 AND C >= 5". So V > 3000 means 300,000 shares (my reading of his own translation).
- Why 10%: "Based on that analysis and ease of use I decided on 10% move." and "In the overall scheme of things the 1 and
  2 are more important signal than 4." (comment, 2007-03-23). Read together, the 10% scan (number 4) is his loosest
  bullish scan, not his main signal: the same morning he wrote "The fourth scan basically gives more candidates to work
  with." and, a few hours later, "The reason I kept them seperate is because the first 3 scans I monitor for a year
  for subsequent entries." (both comments of 2007-03-23; scans 1 and 2 are the 20% and 30% moves).
- 2010: "(( 100 * (C - C1) / C1) >= 8 AND V > 3000 AND (100 * V / AVGV100) >= 300) AND C > 1)", or-ed with a dollar
  branch for high-priced stocks, "((C - C1) >= 5 AND V > 10000 AND C >= 62.50 AND V > V1)". So: up 8% or more, 300,000+
  shares, volume at least 3 times the 100-day average.
- 2014 process flow: "During the day Run EP scan c/c1>1.04 and v>3*avgv50.1 and v>=300000 multiple times , see if there
  is stock with neglect+ game changing earnings" (2014-07-30, /2014/07/my-process-flow-for-episodic-pivots-ep.html).
  He explains `avgv50.1`: "volume is 3 times average 50 day volume calculated as of a day before today" (comment,
  2023-06-27). After hours: "Analyse all after hours movers up 4% plus on 50k volume to see if there is stock with
  neglect+ game changing earnings". These are first-pass screens, followed by the catalyst and neglect check.
- His 2021 after-hours and 2026 pre-market screens are looser still: "Primarily focus on stocks that are up 2% plus on 50000 plus volume
  in after hours." (/2021/12/night-time-is-right-time-to-find-big.html); the 2026 scan looks for stocks up or down 2%
  (or 90 cents) pre-market on 100k volume (/2026/07/how-to-find-and-research-pre-market.html). The 2014 process
  flow's own pre-market step is the same idea: up at least 2% on 50k shares.
- 2024, on gap size: "But if the gaps are more than 20%, the stocks pull back most of the time. Smaller gaps are
  better." and "Big gaps can usually be ignored as they tend to be one-day moves." He adds: "Do your own study to verify
  this." (2024-09-27, /2024/09/gaps-in-50-plus-moves.html). Context: the post is about his daily study of stocks up 50%
  or more in 40 days, not an EP-only study, and he says of the big gaps "Most are in biotech or in buyout speculation
  stocks."
- 2024, on the setup itself: he says he developed "more powerful setups as an alternative to the Episodic Pivots setup,
  which has deteriorated over the years due to large first-day gaps." (2024-09-28,
  /2024/09/what-are-your-trading-problems-and-what.html)

**Volume.** 3 times the 100-day average (2010 scan), 3 times the 50-day (2014 scan), 5 times the 50-day for the
volume-spike scan (2007, which also needs an up close). For the big winners: "typically of 10 times or more compared to average volume" (2010).
"A genuinely surprising earnings in most cases leads to big gap up and huge pre market volume." (2014-09-02)
The same 2010 post ties the EP to the highest volume ever, the owner's HVE: "In many cases the volume on earnings day
might be the highest volume in the history of the stock or multi year high volume." (checker's addition)

**The catalyst.** Earnings first: "Earnings EP are easiest to understand and act on." (2010). He tracked about twenty
kinds ("On a daily basis I monitor around 20 types of Episodic Catalyst.", 2010), and ranked them: "I have got a list of
high probability events and low probability events." (2007-02-26). In that post the low-probability ones include
publication mentions and analyst upgrades. Big-name coverage weakens it: "Earnings breakout on companies with significant analyst coverage do
not do as well as the first kinds." (2010)

**Fundamentals (earnings EPs).** "I only track companies whose earnings are up 100% or more quarter over quarter and the
earnings should be at least 5 cents. Sales/revenue should be up 5% or more." (2007-03-30,
/2007/03/how-to-trade-earnings.html). In 2025 his MarketSurge scan looks for stocks incorporated or listed in the last
10 years, under about $11 billion in market cap, with two quarters of revenue growth of 39% or more
(/2025/09/find-young-episodic-pivots.html), and: "The most explosive Episodic Pivots occur in stocks that have Gone
Public in the last 10 years and have a capitalization of less than $ 10 billion once they enter their growth phase."

**Neglect and prior trend.**
- "I am looking for stocks which have not rallied in anticipation of earnings." with a 65-day look-back (2007-03-30).
- "A weakness or consolidation prior to earnings of 20 to 65 days is ideal." (2007-04-05,
  /2007/04/improving-odds-in-earnings-breakout.html)
- "Hence I use 65 day weakness in price to pick stocks from the Episodic Pivot list" (comment, 2007-03-20,
  /2007/03/episodic-pivots.html)
- "Also out of those stocks I only focus on those which had Episodic Pivots for the first time in last six months."
  (2007-03-20). "Similarly Episodic Pivots at begining of move are more valuable than later." (comment, same post)
- "You want to see stock with neglect, that has significant first or second earnings surprise." (2014-09-02).
  "First time is real charm." (2014-07-28, /2014/07/how-to-find-stock-with-big-catalyst.html)
- "Best Episodic Pivos are on low priced stocks with months or years of neglect and that have very low volume leading in
  to EP." (sic, 2014-09-02)
- What neglect looks like on a chart (2021-12-09, /2021/12/night-time-is-right-time-to-find-big.html; checker's
  addition): "Neglect will reflect in no big rally pre earnings. A extended sideways trading period or a down move coming
  in to earnings." On later surprises: "Third and fourth earnings surprise most of the time are priced in or are tricky
  as they often lead to big gap and then reversal."

**Float.** "Float below 25 million is ideal for this. The best moves happen on float below 10 million." (2010)

**Price floor.** His scans use $5 (2007) or $1 (2010). On stocks under $5: "For 0-5 the problem is noise to signal
ratio is high." (comment, 2007-03-28)

**Entry.**
- Early: "most of the time we enter these kind of trades in pre market or at open." (comment, 2010-09-30); "If I find a
  good EP I buy immediately in pre or post market." and "Most EP where I made big money I bought in pre market." (2014
  process flow). Order type: asked "Do you buy ATM or limit orders?", he answered "ATM" (comment, 2021-11-08).
  Earlier (checker's additions): "Most of the time I will enter in the morning and add to position later if the volume
  climbs above average volume." (2007-03-30, /2007/03/how-to-trade-earnings.html) and, contrasting his day-1 buy with
  Bulkowski's flag, the best surprises "are best bought on the earnings day" (2007-04-04,
  /2007/04/earnings-and-bulkowski.html).
- Later: "Large float seldom start rallying immediately, tend to have a pullback and then go after few weeks. Entry on 4%
  plus breakout on high volume." (comment, 2007-04-23). "Even if you miss the first-day move, many of these make big moves
  later after a brief pullback or consolidation." (2023-07-10). The delayed entry has a name: "It also helps to enter them
  as delayed reactions EP." (2025-09-01)

**Stop.** Three versions: "Stops: I put stops 1 dollar below the gap low if it is gap up or at 2 days low and trail with
stop." (2007-03-30); "In such cases the stop is the low of last 2 days before entry." (comment, 2010-09-30); "Yes. It is
normally low of the EP day." (comment, 2017-01-31, on the 2014 process-flow post). Asked whether he waits before placing
it: "no . I put stop immediately." (comment, 2021-11-16)

**Time stop.** "Yes 5 days from entry." (comment, 2007-03-22). "The stock should move immediately (within 5 days) after
Episodic Pivot, otherwise you go on to next opportunity." (comment, 2007-03-20)

**Exits.** "The exits are in 4 parts with profit target of 20% plus." (comment, 2010-09-30). "In these trades I move my
stops quickly once it makes 20% move." (2007-03-30). "My holding periods range from few weeks to months. There are no
price targets for exit. Exits are based on trailing stops." (comment, 2007-03-28). In 2018 he says the opposite about
targets: "I always use stops and move them as stock goes up. I sell when stock hits my target or hits stop" (comment,
2018-03-27, /2014/09/everyday-hunt-for-stocks-with-big.html). In 2024: "Solution: 80/20 to lock in sure profits and
moving stop on 20%" (/2024/10/your-success-in-trading-is-determined.html); note that this answers "Problem: stocks
break out and later fade." It is a general rule in a list of trading problems, not an EP-specific exit, and the post
does not define 80/20.

**Market conditions.** Asked how much the overall market matters: "no role" (comment, 2021-01-10,
/2014/07/few-good-earnings-trade-can-change-your.html). In a range-bound market in July 2026 he wrote "Breakouts are
unlikely to have follow-through here till a breadth thrust happens." and "Focus : EP EP9(very selectively) DEP"
(/2026/07/situational-awareness-for-july-20-2026.html, read on the 2026 archive page). In 2007 (checker's
addition, /2007/03/how-to-trade-earnings.html): "Even in bear markets you will find opportunities using this
strategy." and "When market expectations are low, or market has had several months of correction, you will find 50 to
100 opportunities in an earning season."

**How much it matters to him.** "the method that has made me the most money in absolute dollar terms in shortest period
of time is Earnings based Episodic Pivots." (2014-07-08). "It infrequently produces good candidates" (same post).
"Problem: Not many Episodic Pivots happen in a year" (2024-10-05).

### 3.2 Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie), the populariser, 2021

Two pages on his own site. They differ in places, and I note where.

**Definition.** "What is an EP? My definition (this may differ from Pradeeps) is a gap up of 10% or more." (2021-11-02,
https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/). "An EP needs to be a gap of 10%+ and big volume should
be there, if not in pre-market then the first 5-10 minutes after the open." (2021-01-08,
https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/)

**What counts as a gap that fails.** On an NVDA chart: "Now the middle red arrow, the stock gapped up slightly but sold
off. This is not an EP." (2021-01-08)

**Volume.** "There has to be massive volume near the open, ideally the stock should trade the average daily volume the
first 15-20 minutes or even quicker." (2021-11-02). "Many times the best ones have traded their average daily volume in
the first 15-30 minutes after the open." (2021-01-08). Three different windows (5-10, 15-20, 15-30 minutes) across the
two pages.

**Prior trend.** "The best EPs are on stocks ones that have gone sideways for 3-6 months or more." (2021-11-02). "It’s
best if the stock has not rallied over the past 3-6 months." (2021-01-08). On second EPs: "these can work but the
failure rate is higher and the move probably won’t be as big." and "Also note that some of them already had a second EP
recently and these ones I generally tend to avoid" (2021-11-02).

**Catalyst and fundamentals.** Types: political and regulatory, FDA and biotech, contracts and partnerships, earnings and
guidance, and "-sector EP (when you have a sector that is red hot, the stocks in that sector can move in gaps and make big
moves without any specific news)". "I have personally found most success in earnings EP as they are easy to find and easy
to understand." For earnings EPs: "Ideally triple digit year over year (YoY) earnings and sales growth but mid/high double
digits works really well too." and "Ideally you want to see a big analyst beat (altough many smaller stocks don’t have any
analysts on them) and a big guidance higher." (2021-11-02). The 2021-01-08 page: "preferably mid/high or even triple digit
EPS and revenue growth and a significant beat to analyst expectations." He does not require earnings at all (2021-11-02,
checker's addition): "Many stocks don’t have any earnings at all but many times just high sales growth and in this
cycle those have worked really well too".

**Entry.** "Enter opening range highs. ORH can be the highs of the first 1-, 5-, or 60-minute candle. The stop is at the
lows of the day." (2021-01-08). His 60-minute candle is the first half hour: "on the 60-minute timeframe the first candle
is always only 30 minutes, 9.30-10 AM" (same page). "I rarely trade in extended hours but prefer to wait for the opening
range highs" and "Often I miss the 1-minute opening range and I will buy the 5-minute highs or 60-minute highs. No need to
be first in." (2021-11-02)

**Stop.** "Stop is always at the lows of the day." and "Make sure your stop is no more than 1x, or maximum 1.5x the
average daily range or the average true range." (2021-11-02)

**Trail.** "Trail your stop with the 10- or 20-day moving average once they surpass your initial stop." (2021-01-08).
The same page, a few lines earlier: "You can trail these with the 20- or 50-day moving average, or whatever you find to
work the best." (Two different trails on one page.)

**Frequency and learning time.** "EP is the most infrequent (meaning the least amount of signals and noise), the one you
can get the best risk/reward" and "It will take you 3-4 earnings seasons to get good at trading EP" (2021-11-02). The
earlier page says "Learn this setup, it takes 4-8 earnings seasons (1-2 years) to get good at this setup and understand
it." On failure, from the 2021-11-02 page, about the four setups in his study database (EP among them): "None of
them work all the time, the failure rate is high, but they all provide excellent risk rewards."

**Risk.** "My risk on most trades is usually 0.25-1%." (2021-01-08)

### 3.3 Gil Morales and Chris Kacher: the buyable gap-up (a close relative)

From the glossary on Morales' site, The OWL Trader (https://theowltrader.com/glossary-trading-methods/):
- Gap size: "should be at least 0.75 times the stock’s 40-day Average True Range."
- Volume: "A buyable gap-up move should occur on volume that is at least 1.5 times the average daily trading volume."
- Context: "Buyable gap-ups should occur within an uptrend or constructive consolidation, not while a stock is in a
  downtrend." and "Buyable gap-ups should occur in fundamentally sound and leading stocks, or there should be a compelling
  thematic basis for consideration."
- Stop: "A buyable gap-up should hold above the intraday low of the gap-up day, and one can therefore use the intraday low
  of the gap-up day as a selling guide."
- Short mirror: "A shortable gap-down (SGD) can be thought of as the reverse of a buyable gap-up (BGU)." and "The intraday
  high then becomes a covering guide."

The BGU is not an EP: it wants an uptrend or a constructive base and a leading stock, where the EP wants neglect. I
include it because our Chart IQ engine uses it and because Bulkowski measured it (section 4).

Checker's addition: the same glossary has a variant that is closer to the EP's neglect. Under the BGU entry: "In the
case of a “bottom-fishing” buyable gap-up (BFBGU) the gap can occur coming out of a low base consolidation as a stock
is trying to round out the lows of and recover from a prior intermediate- to longer-term decline." The glossary credits
the BGU rules to Morales and Kacher's book *Trade Like an O’Neil Disciple* (Wiley, 2010); the site's About page is
signed by Gil Morales. Bulkowski (section 4) took his version of the rules from their December 2010 *Active Trader*

### 3.4 Thomas Bulkowski (thepatternsite.com): event patterns

**Notice on the site.** The live Bulkowski pages carry this line (an older February 2026 copy of the Kacher-Morales
page did not have it yet): "This website, in whole or in part, shall not be used to train artificial intelligence (AI) systems, technologies, large language models, or similar systems, including
use to create works of a similar style." I read and cite the pages with links and short quotes only. The site blocks
plain command-line downloads; I read the live pages through the WebFetch tool and checked wording against Wayback Machine
copies dated 2026-02-12 (Kacher-Morales) and 2026-10-16 (the others).

**Good earnings surprise** (https://www.thepatternsite.com/earnsgood.html). Guidelines: "Look for announcements in which
price makes a large intraday price swing, 2 or 3 times the average daily intraday price range over the last month."
"Select patterns with heavy announcement day volume, above the 30-day average." "For best performance, select
announcements that occur within a third of the yearly low, just make sure price is trending upward." Entry: "A breakout
occurs when price closes above the high posted on the announcement day." Market: "Buy an upward breakout only if the
market and industry are trending higher." The page also says the pattern "works best in a bull market", and it points
to his *Encyclopedia of Chart Patterns*, second edition, pages 868 to 879 (the earnings flag page points to pages 893 to
907 of the same edition; the page numbers are as the web pages state them, the book was not opened).

**Earnings flag** (https://www.thepatternsite.com/earnflag.html): a big earnings move, then a flag, then a breakout.
"An earnings flag is the best performing event pattern." "The best performers are tight congestion patterns, not loose
price structures with the stock meandering up and down." Exit: "If price shows weakness in the first week, consider
selling." Bonde saw this pattern as a different, later way to play the same event: "It basically involves buying a bull
flag post earnings." (2007-04-04, https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2007/04/earnings-and-bulkowski.html)

**Kacher-Morales setup** (https://www.thepatternsite.com/KacherMorales.html), Bulkowski's reading of their gap rules:
"Find up gaps in stocks that are at least 75% of the 40-day average true range (ATR)" and "Sell if price closes below the
low posted on the day price gaps up." His results are in section 4.

### 3.10 Where the sources disagree

1. **Gap or close?** Bonde's published scans measure the price against the prior close, and one of them asks only for
   an up close of any size on a volume spike. Kullamägi defines the EP by the opening gap. A stock that opens flat and closes up 12% is a Bonde candidate and
   not a Kullamägi EP. Our app follows Kullamägi's gap but cites Bonde's numbers for it (section 5.8).
2. **How big.** 4% (Bonde's 2014 intraday screen), 8% (his 2010 scan), 10% (his 2007 choice; Kullamägi's floor). And the
   top end: Bonde in 2024 prefers gaps under 20% and says the EP "has deteriorated over the years due to large first-day
   gaps"; the Model Book's worked example is a 50% gap.
3. **How much volume, measured how.** A multiple of an average (1.5x, 2x, 3x, 5x, 10x, over 20, 50 or 100 days) or a pace
   (the whole ADV in the first 5 to 30 minutes). A multiple and a pace cannot be converted into each other.
4. **Neglect versus leadership.** Bonde and Kullamägi want a stock that has not rallied for 3 to 6 months and was
   forgotten. Morales wants a leader in an uptrend (his "bottom-fishing" BGU is the exception: a gap out of a low base
   after a decline). Our rulebook's C31 wants RS 80+ leaders.
5. **When to buy.** Bonde buys pre-market or at the open; Kullamägi waits for the ORH and rarely trades extended hours;
   Bulkowski and our production detector wait for a close above the gap-day high, a day or more later.
6. **Where the stop goes.** EP-day low (Bonde 2017, Kullamägi, Morales), $1 under the gap low or the 2-day low (Bonde
   2007), the low of the 2 days before entry (Bonde 2010), the 30-minute low (Model Book), the EP-day low minus 1.5% (app).
   Kullamägi adds a width cap (1x, at most 1.5x ADR) that nobody else states.
7. **Exits.** Bonde: fixed targets (20%+, 4 parts) in 2010 and 2018, "no price targets" in 2007, 80/20 in 2024 (a
   general rule for breakouts that fade, not stated for EPs).
   Kullamägi: a trail on the 10/20-day or the 20/50-day. Model Book: the 9-EMA.
8. **Market.** Bonde says it plays "no role"; Bulkowski advises buying only when the market and industry
   trend higher; the Model Book prefers a confirmed uptrend.
9. **Close location.** From "sold off" (Kullamägi's failure case) to "top 25%" (Model Book). No originator gives a number.
10. **How long to learn it.** Kullamägi gives 3-4 seasons (2021-11-02) and 4-8 seasons (2021-01-08).

## 4. Measured evidence

No originator has published a success rate for the EP as they trade it. What exists:

**Bonde's own counts (selected on winners, so no failure rate).**
- "If you look at the 289 stocks in the 100% plus in 260 days list, you will notice 268 out of them had one or more
  episodic pivots at the beginning of their move." and "Similarly if you analyse the IBD 200 list, 173 out of the list had
  an episodic pivots in first one third of their move." (2007-03-20, https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2007/03/episodic-pivots.html).
  Limits: his definition of an EP for this count is not given; it starts from the winners, so it says nothing about how
  many EPs failed.
- "14 big trades like these have contributed to 80% of my profit in last 14 years." (2014-09-02). Self-reported, not
  audited. It shows the shape of the edge: a few huge winners.
- "Going by past experience and research typically a stock which makes 85 plus kind of move on earnings tend to do well."
  (comment, 2010-09-30; "85" reads like a typo for 8%, given the question he answered.)

**A reader's backtest, in the comments of Bonde's 2010 post (low reliability: a reader signing as "ppmoore", Paul,
2010-02-21; Amibroker, method not shown).** Run mechanically, the 2010 scan "didn't find the result ecouraging (about 6%
CAR)." With a flag after the gap (he cites Bulkowski's earnings flag), "the results for the period 2000 to the present
were about 50% CAR." Bonde's reply (2010-02-22): "I buy less than 1% stocks from that scan." He went on to name the
extra filters: eps growth, sales growth, extent of neglect and nature of catalyst.

**Bulkowski's test of the Kacher-Morales gap setup (a BGU, not an EP; his reading of their December 2010 *Active Trader*
article)** (https://www.thepatternsite.com/KacherMorales.html):
"I used 557 stocks in the test with daily price data from March 12, 2001 to October 1, 2010." Across his 16 test variants:
"Average gain ranges between 1.2% and 3.1%" and "Win/loss ratio ranges between 28% and 32%". Two findings that matter
for EPs: "Having volume or not didn't change the results much." and "I found that when price trended downward leading to
the start of the gap, it produced better results." (the second fits the EP's neglect rule). His verdict: "I don't
recommend this setup, but test it for yourself and make up your own mind." Limits: his own coding; he says he skipped the
"leading stock" rule and did not test the base breakout; no catalyst filter.

**Bulkowski's good earnings surprise, bull market** (https://www.thepatternsite.com/earnsgood.html): overall rank 5 of 6
(his ranking, 1 is best); break-even failure rate 29%; average rise 24%; throwback rate 41%; 76% meet his price target. And:
"Almost half (48%) rise just 10% before tumbling and that occurs in less than 2 weeks." Limits: "The above numbers are
based on hundreds of perfect trades." The page does not give the sample size or years; "perfect trades" is his own term,
defined in his glossary, which I did not read.

**Bulkowski's earnings flag, bull market** (https://www.thepatternsite.com/earnflag.html): rank 1 of 6; break-even
failure rate 10%; average rise 34%; throwback rate 63%; 86% meet his target. Same "perfect trades" limit. This is the
later entry (a flag after the earnings move), not the day-1 buy.

**Bulkowski on gaps as support** (https://www.thepatternsite.com/GaugingGaps.html): "Gaps in an uptrend (rising windows)
show underlying support 20% of the time in a bull market and 16% of the time in a bear market." So buying the first test of
the gap is not a measured edge on its own.

**Academic PEAD.** The drift after earnings surprises is one of the most studied effects in finance (Bonde's summary).
The only paper I read directly is the NBER abstract quoted in section 1; it gives no EP-style number.

**Our app's own records (in the public repo).**
- `api/services/ep_base_rate.py` (docstring, measured 2026-09-28 on the owner's engine database): of the engine's
  resolved EP flags, "by id the same record reads 76/447 = 17.0%" followed through (final tracked price above the flag-day
  entry, with a -8% stop), versus 189/448 = 42.2% by a tie-broken method the docstring calls wrong. The backlog entry
  (`docs/terminal-research/10-roadmap/backlog.md`, TERM-090) gives the window: 506 candidates over 119 flag dates,
  2026-02-20 to 2026-09-25. Limits: the engine's EP flag definition lives in the private engine repo and is not
  reproduced here; seven months, one market.
- `api/services/pattern_engine/tag_textbook.py`: the member EP tag passed a blind recognition review with a pooled score
  of 2.65 (pass bar 2.4). That measures whether judges agreed the chart is an EP, not whether it made money.
- `api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/uct/uct_ep_v2.py`: on the lab's test half, recall rose from 21.1% to 47.4% and
  the fire rate from 0.87% to 1.20% of random ticker-days; a blind review of random-day fires judged 13 yes, 4 no, 8
  ambiguous, and 0 of 10 decoys yes. Again recognition, not outcome.

### 4.1 My own check for this dossier (reproducible, not evidence of an edge)

**What I did.** I read every gap-up day of 4% or more from 2005-01-03 to 2026-08-31 in the app's sandbox daily-bar
database (195,311 gap days on 3,497 tickers) and recorded, for each, the facts our rulebook cares about. I ran the app's own

early entry with a tight stop, and holding the few big winners. A course should teach it that way and not promise a win
rate.

## 5. Our app: what the code, cards and tags actually say

### 5.1 `api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/uct/episodic_pivot.py` (pattern id `episodic_pivot`, member-facing)

What it tests, for each of the last 5 bars as the EP bar:
- Gap: open at least 4% above the prior close (`_MIN_GAP_PCT = 0.04`, line 79; floor moved from 8% by owner decision on
  2026-09-04).
- Range: the bar's high-low range at least 2x the 20-bar average range.
- Volume: at least 2x the 20-bar average volume.
- Close strength: close in the top 30% of the bar (`>= 0.70`).
- Base: the longest window of 15 to 60 bars before the EP bar whose high-low depth is 25% or less, with the EP close
  above that window's high.
- Rejects only a fully hostile context (Stage 4 and bearish MA stack and RS falling, all three).
- Confidence = 0.40 geometry + 0.25 volume + 0.20 context + 0.15 a fixed 50 "historical" score; must be 50+.
- No liquidity or price floor. No earnings or news check.
- Levels (lines 452-455): entry = EP-bar high x 1.001, to be confirmed by a daily close above it on at least average
  volume; stop = EP-bar low x 0.985 (1.5% under the low); targets = the EP close plus 1.5x and 2.5x the base height.

### 5.2 `uct_ep_v2.py` (pattern id `uct_episodic_pivot_v2`, admin-only, experimental)

Same gap (4%+), volume (2x 20-day) and range (2x) tests; close strength lowered to 0.50; the base becomes a label, not a
gate; the gap day can be up to 4 sessions back. Liquidity floor: the shared `liquidity_floor` ($2 price, $10,000 average
daily dollar volume). Levels: entry = the latest close; pivot = the prior close (the gap edge); stop = the gap-day low;
targets 2R and 3R (`uct_setups_common.make_levels`). The docstring cites the traders' scored calls t13505, t10541 and
t14339 for its two changes.

### 5.3 The member tag (`api/services/setup_tags.py` plus `pattern_engine/tag_textbook.py`)

Members see "Episodic Pivot" beside a chart only when the production detector fires **and** the textbook check passes on
that session: open at least 5% above the prior close, volume at least 3x the 50-day average, close at 60% of the range
or higher, the 40 days before within a 35% range, and the prior close no more than 15% above its level 40 days earlier.
The tag's description says: "A gap up of 4% or more on at least twice the average volume and range that closes near the
high and above a sideways base; the news behind it is not checked."

### 5.4 Chart IQ (`api/services/chart_iq/engine/v2_setups.py`, `v3_levels.py`, `v3.py`)

EP = gap of 8%+ on 3x the 50-day volume and a close in the upper half; the stop line is the EP-day low. In v3, a smaller
big gap is called "Power gap" (checker's precision, `v3_levels.py`: a gap that opened up less than 8% but closed up 10%

50-day) draws its stop at the gap midpoint.

### 5.8 What disagrees with the sources or looks wrong

1. **The gap floor rests on a misreading.** The detector and `docs/uct-scanner-intelligence/decision-log.md` call 8%
   "Bonde's 2010" number and 4% his "2014 process-flow" number for an opening gap. Both measure the price against the prior close
   (`C - C1`, `c/c1`), not the open, and the 4% is a first-pass intraday screen that he follows with a catalyst and neglect check, not a
   definition. When Bonde chose a single number he chose 10% ("Based on that analysis and ease of use I decided on 10%
   move."). The Phase-5 audit file (`tier1_validation/data/power_earnings_gap+episodic_pivot_full.json`) also describes
   the 2010 scan as an "open-vs-prior-close move"; it is not.
2. **Unsupported claims in the production narrative** (`episodic_pivot.py`, the text returned with every detection of a
   member-visible detector):
   - "codified by Pradeep Bonde of Stockbee from decades of pattern study on liquid US equities" (line 497): Bonde's best
     EPs are in low-priced, low-float, thinly traded stocks; "decades" is not his claim.
   - "both Bonde and Kullamägi's published versions of this setup require a genuine opening gap" (line 506): Bonde's
     published scans test the price against the prior close, and one asks only for an up close on a volume spike.
   - "Bonde teaches that this is the day institutional buyers stop accumulating quietly" (line 513): not found in his posts.
   - "anything below 2x is suspect, above 3x is gold" (line 533): no source.
   - "Avoid chasing more than 5% above the EP bar high - Bonde explicitly teaches that EPs which extend too quickly often
     fade" (line 552): not found; our own chase rule is 4% (C28).
   - "both targets typically hit within 4-12 weeks on clean EPs" (line 561): an invented statistic.
   - "'filling the EP bar' in Bonde's terminology" (line 568): not found.
   - "exactly the profile Bonde teaches as institutional capitulation to the upside" (line 418) and "where EPs convert at
     the highest rate" (line 422): no source for either.
3. **Volume too loose.** 2x the 20-day average is below every originator number (3x the 50 or 100-day; 10x for big
   winners). My data: gaps on 2-3x volume filled within 20 days half the time.
4. **Base window too short and the prior run unchecked.** A 15-bar (3-week) base passes, where Kullamägi wants 3-6 months
   sideways. Nothing stops a stock that has doubled in 6 months (my worst group). v2 removes the base entirely. The member
   tag's 40-day check is closer but still short.
5. **Entry, stop and targets do not match any originator.** Entry on a later close above the EP-day high is Bulkowski's
   confirmation, not Bonde's or Kullamägi's day-1 buy. The stop sits 1.5% below the EP-day low, wider still than the
   originators' low-of-day stop, and the base-height targets have no source. v2's 2R/3R targets are not our trims (3R and 5R, B19 and E51).
6. **No liquidity floor in production; v2's floor is $2 and $10,000 a day**, far below B23 ($10 and $20M).
7. **No catalyst test anywhere in the chart detectors.** The tag text says so honestly. Every originator says the news is
   half the setup.
8. **EP versus PEG.** The catalyst engine forbids the EP label on earnings gaps. Bonde ("Earnings EP are easiest to
   understand and act on."), Kullamägi ("most success in earnings EP") and UCT's own posts ("EP on earnings", the FSLY and
   MXL notes) all call earnings gaps EPs. Owner question 1. Checker's addition: the owner's own verdict log points the
   other way for established names. Reviewing High Volume Edge examples he cut earnings-day spikes on ASML, ORCL, RL,
   DUOL and GM as PEGs ("Again, just a PEG", ASML 2024-01-24; AXON was filed as a PEG with no objection), and the log
   records, in its note on ASML, the rule "an earnings-day
   volume spike on an established name is a Power Earnings Gap example, not an HVE example" (`owner_verdicts.jsonl`
   lines 128-133). He kept FSLY 2026-02-12 as "100% A+" (line 152; the log's own note, not his words, says "after a
   long slide"). My reading, for him to confirm: his usage fits a split by the kind of stock (forgotten versus
   established), not by the catalyst.
9. **The Model Book attributes rules to Kullamägi that I could not find in his writing**: waiting 30 minutes, a stop at the
   30-minute low, and holding overnight only on a top-25% close. His pages say ORH of the 1, 5 or 30-minute candle and a
   stop at the low of the day. These may come from his streams; I could not check. Its "trail the daily 9-EMA" differs from
   E52 and from the curriculum's 10/20-day. Its 50% worked example sits against Bonde's 2024 caution on gaps over 20%.
10. **The voice knowledge base says "Stops are tight: low of the gap day or breakout bar."** In my data the gap-day low was
    a median 6.7% from the close for the production rule; it is tight only for an early entry near the open.
11. **The member tag text understates its own gate**: it says 4% and 2x; the textbook check requires 5% and 3x the
    50-day.
12. **Chart IQ's BGU stop is the gap midpoint**; Morales' own glossary uses the gap day's intraday low.
13. **The Study bank teaches "First third off at 2-3R"**, which predates B19's hard rule of a third at 3R.

## 8. Proposed rules for our course

Each line is measurable where it can be. Where sources disagree I give the options and a recommendation.

### 8.1 What it is (all must be true at the buy)

1. **Catalyst**: a named surprise released after the close or before the open. Company news first: earnings and
   guidance, then a contract or partnership, a regulatory or FDA decision. A named sector-wide event can also count
   (Kullamägi's "sector EP", which he says can move "without any specific news"; whether we teach it is owner question
   12). Write it in the plan. No named catalyst, no EP. [originator] [UCT]
2. **Gap**: the open is at least **10%** above the prior close. [originator: Kullamägi's gap floor; Bonde's 2007 10% is
   a close-to-close move, not an opening gap]
   Options: 4% (the app today; Bonde's 2014 intraday screen), 8% (his 2010 scan, Chart IQ), 10%. Recommend 10%: it is
   Kullamägi's definition and the number Bonde said he "decided on" in 2007 (though he also called that 10% scan a
   weaker signal than his 20% and 30% scans); 4% is only a screen. Gaps of 4-10% on news are taught as
   "earnings gappers" or PEGs (setup 21), not as EPs. [my proposal]
3. **Gap size caution**: gaps over 25% are graded down (B- at best), not banned. [my proposal: Bonde's 2024 number is
   20% ("more than 20%, the stocks pull back most of the time"), from a study of all 50% movers; in table B the 20-30%
   band was mixed against 10-20% (weaker over 20 days, better over 60) and only the 30%+ band was clearly worse]
4. **Volume**: the gap day trades at least **3x** its 50-day average volume, and by the buy it is on pace to do so: the
   stock has traded its full ADV within the first 30 minutes (Kullamägi's 15-30 minutes). [originator; the 30-minute
   cut-off is my proposal] Ideal: 5x or more,
   or the highest volume in years (an HVE). [UCT; originator: Bonde 2010, "highest volume in the history of the stock or
   multi year high volume"]
5. **Neglect**: the close before the gap is no more than **25% above** its close 126 sessions (6 months) earlier, and no
   more than 15% above its close 63 sessions earlier. [originator: Kullamägi 3-6 months, Bonde 65 days; the numbers are
   my proposal; table C tests only the 6-month line, the 63-session line is untested]
6. **First EP**: no qualifying EP in the prior 126 sessions. [originator: Bonde "first time in last six months";
   Kullamägi avoids second EPs]
7. **Close**: the gap day closes in the **upper half** of its range and never trades back to the prior close (no fill).
   If buying intraday, price is above the open and above the ORH at the buy. [source consensus that the gap must hold and
   not sell off: Kullamägi's sold-off gap "is not an EP"; Morales' gap-day low as the selling guide; also our journal
   template and vision rubric. No source gives a number: the upper-half line is my proposal, the same line the app's v2
   and Chart IQ rules use] The Model Book's "top 25%" is a grading cue, not a gate. [my proposal]
8. **Location**: the price at the buy is above the 200-day SMA. [house rule B26] The gap may carry it there. [my proposal,
   owner question 7]
9. **Liquidity**: price $10+ and $20M a day [house rule B23], measured on the gap day's own dollar volume. [my proposal,
   owner question 5]
10. **Fundamentals (earnings EPs)**: EPS or sales growth of at least 25% year over year with a beat; triple-digit growth
    and a raised guide are A+ cues. [originator: Kullamägi "mid/high double digits"; Bonde 100%+ EPS; house rule D40]
    The 25% floor is my proposal.

### 8.2 The buy

1. **Day 1 (the textbook EP).** After the first 5-minute candle (or the first 30 minutes for a slower start), buy at
   market (E57) when price trades above the ORH, the volume test (8.1.4) is on pace, and the stop under the low of the day
   so far is **within 3%** (A13) and within 1x the stock's ADR. If the stop is wider, do not buy day 1; wait for 8.2.2.
   [originator: Kullamägi ORH; house rules E57, A13] No pre-market or after-hours buys. [house rule E58] No buy more than
   4% above the ORH. [house rule C28]
2. **Delayed entry (day 2 to day 10).** While the gap holds (no trade below the gap-day low, no fill to the prior close),
   buy the first tight trigger with a stop within 3%: an inside day or tight day, then a break of its high; a break of the
   gap-day high; a red-to-green move through the prior close (the house "Red to Green" entry trigger, SET.06); the house Go Signal (a
   pullback into the 9 EMA, then a wide candle that closes above the catalyst high; SET.34 calls it "the confirmation
   layer for EP and PEG entries"); or an undercut and reclaim of the gap-day close (the HVC) or the rising 10/21-day.
   [UCT: the owner's FSLY entry, SET.34; originator: Bonde's delayed reaction EP and "Even if you miss the first-day
   move, many of these make big moves later after a brief pullback or consolidation."; Model Book day-2+ entry] The
   10-session window is my proposal; after that the chart is graded as its new base or flag setup. [my proposal]
3. **Not after 3 green days in a row.** [UCT, the 2026-04-19 starter list, offered as a guide]

### 8.3 The stop

1. Day 1: a few cents under the low of the day at the time of entry. [originator; house rule F62]
2. Delayed entry: a few cents under the trigger day's low. [house rule A3]
3. Never widened; moved only up. [house rule A4]
4. A gap through the stop: the first 30-minute low is the new stop, out past 2R. [house rule B20]
5. If the honest stop is wider than about 3%, skip the trade rather than tighten it. [house rules A3, A13]

### 8.4 Trims and trail (house defaults)

- Breakeven at 2R to 5R. First third at 3R (hard rule), ideally 3R to 6R; second third at 5R. [house rules B19, E51]
- If it opens above a trim level, trim at the open. [house rule F63]
- Trail the last third on a close below the **10-day** average while the stock is moving fast, switching to the 21-day only
  after it builds its first base. [house rule E52 for the 10-day and 21-day; originator: Kullamägi 10/20-day; the switch
  after the first base is my proposal] (Owner question 8 on 10/20 versus 10/21, and on weekly trails.)
- Sell a big part into a climax run. [house rule C34]
- Time stop: still under 1R after 10 sessions, sell. [house rule C33; Bonde used 5 days]
- Next earnings: hold through only if up 5R. [house rule B22]

### 8.5 What invalidates it

- The gap fills (a trade back to the prior close) on day 1, or the gap-day low breaks before entry. [UCT house material:
  our journal template "If it fills the gap, step aside." and the vision rubric; Morales uses the gap-day low]
- The stock gaps up and sells off below the open on day 1 (Kullamägi's NVDA "not an EP" case). [originator]
- The volume is not there by the first 30 minutes. [originator: Kullamägi's volume-at-the-open test; the 30-minute
  cut-off is my proposal]
- No named catalyst, or a low-probability one (a media mention, an analyst upgrade alone). [originator: Bonde]
- A second EP after a big run, or a stock up 50%+ in the prior 6 months: graded down hard. [originator; my data]

### 8.6 The short mirror (bearish EP)

A gap down of 10% or more on 3x+ volume on bad news (an earnings miss or guidance cut), in a stock already below a
falling 200-day (B26), with RS 20 or less (C31 mirror), not hard to borrow (D48). Short at market on a break of the
opening range low; stop a few cents above the high of the day; trims and trail mirrored. [house rule D47; originator:
Bonde's bearish EP scans (2007) and his 2026 bootcamp topic "EP Bearish with DEP"; Morales' shortable gap-down with "The
intraday high then becomes a covering guide."] I found no published statistics for the bearish side. Our app's "Bear
gap" lesson defines no trade (TRADE_PLANS.md). [my proposal for the details]

### 8.7 Best market conditions

- Earnings season: most EPs come in a 3-4 week window each quarter (Kullamägi).
- Allowed in Green and Yellow by F65 (Yellow smaller and fewer); in Orange only A+ EPs; in Red "mostly cash and
  shorts" (D46, F65; a guide, not a hard cap, A10). [house rules]
- EPs often work when the indexes do not, and after corrections (Bonde "no role" and, in 2007, "Even in bear markets you
  will find opportunities using this strategy."; owner 2025-11-23 and 2026-03-08).
  Bulkowski's advice leans the other way for earnings surprises ("Buy an upward breakout only if the market and
  industry are trending higher."). Recommend: keep the house filter, and teach that EPs are the first longs to try as a
  correction ends. [UCT; house rule F68]

### 8.8 Common mistakes

1. Buying a gap without the volume, or a gap that sold off below the open.
2. Taking a day-1 close entry with the gap-day low as the stop (7% or more away), so the size is tiny or A13 is broken.
   Buy early near the ORH or wait for the first tight pullback.
3. Buying pre-market (E58) or chasing more than 4% over the ORH (C28).
4. Buying a second EP late in a run, or a stock that already doubled into the news (table C).
5. Treating every gap as one that must fill, or fading strong gaps (Bonde: "People who have misconceptions like all gaps
   get filled and the stock is overextended or overbought will have very difficult time trading this.").
6. Selling everything in the first day or two (Model Book) and missing the multi-week trend.
7. A trail that is too loose for a fast mover (the owner's FSLY weekly 9-EMA lesson).
8. No named catalyst, or a weak one.
9. Ignoring the liquidity rule on small, thin names.

## 9. Grading: what makes an A+ and what makes a B-

E50: grading is the owner's judgment and no fixed checklist is taught as ours. These are observable cues from the
sources, for him to accept or change.

**A+ cues**
- An earnings EP with triple-digit EPS and sales growth, a big beat and a raised guide (Kullamägi; Bonde).
- The first EP in 6 months or more, after 3-6 months or more of sideways action or years of neglect (both originators).
- A gap of 10% to about 20% that holds above the open and closes near the top of its range (Bonde 2024; Kullamägi;
  the Model Book's "top 25%").
- Volume 5-10x the 50-day or the highest in years; the full ADV traded in the first 15-30 minutes (originators; UCT HVE).
- A young, small company: listed under 10 years, under $10B in value, low float (Bonde 2010, 2025).
- The gap clears the base and the 200-day; a strong or newly hot group (Bracco's "thematic"; owner's "first of it's group").
- A tight stop available: within 3% of the ORH, or a tight first pullback (the owner's FSLY entry).
- Green market, or a market just coming out of a correction.

**B- cues**
- Gap of 4-10%, or over 25%.
- Volume only 2-3x the average.
- Up 50%+ in the 6 months before the news; a second EP.
- Close in the lower half of the day's range; a filled or nearly filled gap.
- A weak catalyst (a mention, an upgrade alone) or a widely covered large company (Bonde: "do not do as well").
- A stop only possible at 5% or more.

## 12. Examples (from our data)

**The scan's rule (section 8 plus the house rules, on daily bars).** Daily bars from the local sandbox, run over the
2,630 names that ever met the house liquidity rule (the scan completed: 2,630 of 2,630, nothing cut for time). A gap
day is one whose open is at least 10% above the prior close (8.1.2) and at most 60% above it, whose volume is at least
3x the prior 50-day average (8.1.4), that closes green, in the upper half of its range, and never trades back to the
prior close (8.1.7, no fill); the gap-day close is at least $10 and the gap day's own dollar volume at least $20
million (B23, 8.1.9); the close before the gap is no more than 25% above the close 126 sessions earlier and no more
than 15% above the close 63 sessions earlier (8.1.5, neglect); and no earlier gap of 10%+ on 3x volume in the prior
126 sessions (8.1.6, first EP). Daily bars cannot see the opening range, so the day-1 buy (8.2.1) is proxied by a
market buy at the gap-day close with the stop 5 cents under the gap-day low, taken only when that stop is within 3%
(A13); otherwise the delayed entry (8.2.2) is the first of these in days 2-10 while the gap holds (no trade below the
gap-day low): a break of the post-gap high, or an inside day and then a break of its high, bought at the next tick
over the pivot (or at the open if it gapped over it, never more than 4% above the pivot, C28), skipped after three
green days in a row (8.2.3), only above the 200-day SMA (B26), with the stop 5 cents under the lower of the trigger
day's and the prior day's lows (or the trigger day's low alone) and only if that stop is within 3%. One entry per
gap. The outcome is the first of the stop, 3R or 5R touched in the next 60 sessions from the day after entry (a
fixed stop, no trims, no trail, no breakeven move, no gap-through-the-stop rule: a base rate for the entry, not the
house trade). Not coded: the named catalyst itself, the fundamentals (8.1.10), the RS rating (C31), the market
reading and the trims. The catalyst of each gap is classed from the earnings database (Yahoo plus SEC 8-K Item 2.02,
complete from about 2005): "earnings" when the gap day is a report's reaction day, "news/other" when the name has
report dates and the gap is not on one, and "unknown" when the name has no report dates or the gap is before 2005.
Scripts: `_study-resume/scratchpad/setups/20-episodic-pivot/ep_scan.py` (the scan) and `ep_chart.py` (the picks and
charts); the hits are in `final/hits.json`, the charts and three phone sheets in `final/` (not in the repo).

**Base rate.** 541 hits, 1996-10-25 to 2026-11-04 (30 of them before 2005, where the catalyst cannot be classed).
**37.9% reached 3R and 27.7% reached 5R before the stop**; 451 of the 541 (83%) were eventually stopped at the
original stop inside the 60-session window (that includes winners, since the stop never moves here), and 231 (43%)
never reached 1R. Among the winners the median time to 3R was 2 sessions. **Caveat:** a fixed stop with no trims, no
breakeven move, no trail and no time stop, so it measures how often the entry delivers 3R and 5R, not what the house
trade earns. Cuts worth teaching (same scan): earnings gaps (358 hits) 37.7% / 25.7% and news gaps (138) 35.5% /
28.3%, about the same; the day-1 close-entry proxy (49 hits) 30.6% / 22.4% against the delayed entries (492) 38.6% /
28.3%; a break of the post-gap high (393) 41.2% / 31.3% did better than the inside-day trigger (99) 28.3% / 16.2%;
gaps of 10-15% (366) 36.6% / 27.9%, 15-20% (93) 35.5% / 21.5%, 20-30% (57) 36.8% / 26.3%, and the 25 gaps over 30%
68.0% / 52.0% (too few to lean on, and against the 8.1.3 caution; the owner's call); volume 3-5x (202) 39.6% / 27.7%,
5-10x (248) 35.5% / 26.2%, 10x+ (91) 40.7% / 31.9% (no edge beyond 3x in this cut); neglect held up: a stock flat or
down over the prior 6 months (320) 40.3% / 30.9% against one that was up (221) 34.4% / 23.1%; a gap-day close in the
top quarter of the range (353) 36.5% / 26.9% was not better than a close in the 50-75% band (188) 40.4% / 29.3%, so
the "top 25%" stays a grading cue, not a gate (8.1.7). The 47 hits with a stop under 1% (a trigger day that opened
at its low) reached 3R 63.8% of the time, but that R is tiny and inflated (one hit shows 551R on a 5-cent stop), so
the examples below were chosen with stops of 1% or more. Delayed entries came mostly on day 2 (205) and day 3 (94);
49 were day-1 proxies.
