## 1. What it is

### Part A: Qullamaggie's three setups

**The Breakout.** A leading stock makes a big, fast move (30% to 100% or more in days or weeks), then rests in an orderly,
tightening sideways range for 2 weeks to 2 months while riding its rising 10 and 20-day averages. You buy the day it
breaks out of that range, above the opening range high, with a stop at the low of that day.

**The Episodic Pivot (EP).** A stock, ideally one that has gone nowhere for months, gaps up 10% or more on news that
surprises the market (most often earnings), on huge volume. You buy the opening range high with a stop at the low of the
day. **The Parabolic Short (or long).** A stock that has gone nearly vertical for several days is shorted as it cracks
intraday; the mirror long is bought after a parabolic crash.

**The market psychology.** These points are reasoning drawn from the sources, not measured facts.
- **Leaders move in steps.** Kullamägi: "Meaning they will make a 20-50%+ move, pull back and go sideways for a while,
  then make another move." ([qullamaggie.com, 2021](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/))
  The sideways rest lets early buyers take profit and lets the moving averages catch up; the breakout says the new
  buyers have absorbed that supply.
- **Who buys the breakout.** Traders who missed the first leg, and institutions still building a position. Kullamägi on
  the EP: "Remember, when institutions buy, they don’t buy in one day. It can take many months for them to get to their
  desired allocation." ([qullamaggie.com, 2021-11-02](https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/))
- **Who is trapped in an EP.** The market priced the stock for the old story. Kullamägi: "The fundamental logic for an
  EP is news that catches the market off guard and forces a revaluation of the stock." (same post) Short sellers and
  holders who sold during the months of neglect must buy back higher.
- **Who is trapped in a parabolic.** Late buyers who chased the vertical move. Kullamägi: "Think of stocks as rubber
  bands, if they get really stretched short term, they can have powerful snapbacks." ([2021](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/))
- **Why the stop is tight.** All three setups buy at a point where being wrong shows fast. Kullamägi: "All of my setups
  are about finding low risk entries on fast moving stocks." (same post) He accepts a low win rate for large winners
  (section 4).

### Part B: Kell's Cycle of Price Action

**What it is.** A map of where a stock is in its trend, read from price against the 10 and 20-day exponential moving
averages (EMAs). Each phase tells you what to do: wait (Reversal Extension), buy (Wedge Pop, EMA Crossback, Base n'
Break), sell into strength (Exhaustion Extension), get out or short (Wedge Drop and the downside steps).

**The market psychology** (as Kell's articles describe it; reasoning, not measured fact).
- **The bottom is fear.** "At the bottom of the cycle is a general capitulation called a Reversal Extension. Fear takes
  over, and people finally sell, no longer able to withstand the pain of loss." ([TraderLion, Kell, 2024-09-10](https://web.archive.org/web/20250623154156/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/cycle-of-price-action-by-oliver-kell/))
- **The turn is a change of character.** Sellers who stayed through the decline are exhausted; price tightens under the
  averages, and the first strong push back through them, on volume, shows new buyers in control.
- **The trend is a series of rests.** "The stock will now begin to move sideways allowing moving averages to catch up."
  (same article) Each rest is a place to buy or add; each new rest is later in the move.
- **The top is greed.** "Extreme greed takes over and pushes the price to unsustainable highs." ([TraderLion, Kell, 2024-08-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20250122070523/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/exhaustion-extension-sell-into-strength/))
  Then "there are now fewer new buyers" and price "falls through the moving averages with an influx of sellers"
  ([cycle article](https://web.archive.org/web/20250623154156/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/cycle-of-price-action-by-oliver-kell/)).
  Buyers of the last leg are trapped.

- **Not verified.** The app's scanner text credits him with a "Monster Moves" framework and "Stockbee.tv content"
  (section 5). I found neither name on qullamaggie.com or anywhere else I read, and a web search in checking found
  neither.
- **A book interview exists that neither reader read.** "Market Wizards: The Next Generation" by Jack D. Schwager and
  George F. Coyle (Harriman House, 2026-06-09). The publisher's page describes, without naming him, "A security guard
  turned trader who parlayed $5,000 into over $100 million in under 12 years." UCT's own Substack (unsigned intro,

## 3. Every definition I could find, source by source

### 3.1 Kullamägi: the Breakout (originator, primary)

Source: ["3 TIMELESS setups that have made me TENS OF MILLIONS!"](https://qullamaggie.com/my-3-timeless-setups-that-have-made-me-tens-of-millions/),
2021-01-08 (updated 2021-04-28), plus his replies in that post's comments and his [FAQ](https://qullamaggie.com/faq/).

- **Universe.** "Scan for the 1 or 2% of stocks that are up the most over these 3 timeframes" (1, 3 and 6 months). He
  runs those scans "Weekly" (comment reply, 2021-07-29). "Too thin" means "Yes dollar volume" (reply, 2021-03-28).
- **Prior trend.** "A big move higher sometime in the past 1-3 months. This move can be anywhere from 30-100%+ and
  usually lasts a few days to a few weeks."
- **Shape.** "An orderly pullback and consolidation with higher lows and tightening range in the consolidation phase."
- **Length.** "The consolidation phase is usually 2 weeks to 2 months."
- **Depth.** Not stated.
- **Moving averages.** "During the consolidation the stock price “surfs” the rising 10- and 20-day. and sometimes toe
  50-day moving average." (his spelling) He does not say EMA or SMA.
- **Volume.** Not stated for the Breakout in the post. Asked to rank factors (1 huge move and riding the MAs, 2 ADR%,
  3 volume, 4 liquidity, 5 earnings, 6 momentum), he answered "4", then "1 and 6", then "2", then "3 and 5" (reply,
  2021-04-13): liquidity first, volume and earnings tied for last. In another reply (2021-02-09): "momentum is #1
  important thing".
- **Trigger and entry.** "Enter on the opening range highs. The opening range highs can be the highs of the first
  1-minute candle, the 5-minute candle or the 60-minute candle" ... "You don’t even have to use any intraday chart, just
  look at the daily chart and enter when the stock is starting to break out."
- **Anticipation.** "This method can also be traded by anticipating the breakout but I don’t find it as effective and
  more skill and experience is needed." Missed it by a day? "Sometimes I buy the retrace" (reply, 2021-02-24).
- **Stop.** "Stop is always lows of the day and stop should not be wider than the ATR or ADR of the stock". His ADR is
  "the average daily range in % over the past 20 sessions" (FAQ, with a TC2000 formula). Stop orders: "I always use
  market stops, never limit stops." (FAQ)
- **Trims and trail.** "You should sell 1/3 to 1/2 of the position after 3-5 days, and then move the stop to break
  even." Then: "The rest of the position should be trailed with the 10- or the 20-day moving average. Depends on how fast
  the stock is. If a beginner stick to the 10-day. You wait for the first CLOSE below the 10- day." Asked whether the
  10-day is just a tighter beginner rule, he replied: "Yes, exactly right!" (2021-07-29)
- **Targets.** None fixed. "In a bullish market it is very common to get moves that are 10-20x+ your initial risk if you
  are good at setup selection."
- **Market.** Asked whether his setups are generally breakout setups: "a) in bullish markets yes" (reply, 2021-02-09).
- **Sizing (his account, for context).** "Most of my positions are 10-20% of account size." "My risk on most trades is
  usually 0.25-1%." "I don’t believe you should ever have more than 30% of your account over night in any stock or ETF."
  His FAQ gives slightly different figures: risk "Most of the time 0.3-0.5%. Rarely more than 1%."; positions
  "Generally 5%-25% with most being around 10-15%."
- **His examples.** TSLA 2020, MNKD 2013, AAXN (then TASR) 2004.

### 3.2 Kullamägi: the Episodic Pivot (originator of this version; Bonde is the source of the idea)

Sources: the same 2021 post, and ["How to master a setup: Episodic Pivots"](https://qullamaggie.com/how-to-master-a-setup-episodic-pivots/), 2021-11-02.

- **Gap and volume.** 2021-01: "An EP needs to be a gap of 10%+ and big volume should be there, if not in pre-market
  then the first 5-10 minutes after the open." 2021-11: "My definition (this may differ from Pradeeps) is a gap up of
  10% or more. There has to be massive volume near the open, ideally the stock should trade the average daily volume the
  first 15-20 minutes or even quicker." (The 2021-01 post's own step list says "Many times the best ones have traded
  their average daily volume in the first 15-30 minutes after the open.")
- **Catalyst.** Earnings and guidance first; also FDA, contracts, political, sector moves. For earnings: "preferably
  mid/high or even triple digit EPS and revenue growth and a significant beat to analyst expectations." (2021-01) "Volume
  is the #1 thing to focus on." (2021-11)
- **Prior trend (neglect).** "It’s best if the stock has not rallied over the past 3-6 months." (2021-01) "The best EPs
  are on stocks ones that have gone sideways for 3-6 months or more." (2021-11) A second EP after a big first one works
  "but the failure rate is higher".
- **Entry.** Opening range high of the 1, 5 or 60-minute bar; "No need to be first in." (2021-11)
- **Stop.** Low of the day; "Make sure your stop is no more than 1x, or maximum 1.5x the average daily range or the
  average true range." (2021-11)
- **Trail.** "Trail your stop with the 10- or 20-day moving average once they surpass your initial stop." (2021-01) The
  same post also says "You can trail these with the 20- or 50-day moving average, or whatever you find to work the best."
- **His examples.** NVDA 2016-2017, FSLR 2007, BB (BBRY) 2004 (2021-01); AFRM (2021-11, named in the text; the other
  charts in that post are images, and readers' comments name AMBA, UPST and ASAN among them).

### 3.3 Kullamägi: the Parabolic Short (or long) (originator)

Source: the 2021-01 post.
- **Prior move.** "A stock up 50-100%+ in a few days or weeks (if larger cap) or 300-1000%+ (if smaller cap)." "The
  stock should be up 3-5+ days in a row."
- **Entry.** Short the opening range low (1 or 5-minute), or the first red 5-minute bar, or a failed bounce into VWAP.
- **Stop.** "The stop is highs of the day or if VWAP fail, a reclaim of the VWAP."
- **Target.** "The target area is the 10- and 20-day moving averages, that’s where these stocks usually bounce."
- **Reward.** "This setup is more like 5-10x risk reward, but if you wait for the perfect setups your win rate is
  probably going to be higher."
- **The long.** "any time a successful parabolic short goes down 50-60%+ in a few days, that’s the premise for the long
  setup." Entry: opening range high, the first green 5-minute bar, or any range break up.
- **His examples.** Short: LAZR 2020, AIG 2009, AMTD 1999. Long: GBTC 2017.
- **The house version** (Bracco, used by rule B26) is in section 6.5.

### 3.4 Pradeep Bonde (Stockbee): the Episodic Pivot (originator of the term)

Sources: ["What are Episodic Pivots and how to find them"](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2010/02/what-are-episodic-pivots-and-how-to.html),
2010-02-12; ["My process flow for Episodic Pivots (EP)"](https://stockbee.blogspot.com/2014/07/my-process-flow-for-episodic-pivots-ep.html),
2014-07-30; his replies in those comment threads.
- **Idea.** Post-earnings drift: "If a company announces a big earnings surprize and if it is not currently priced in to
  the stock it will go up." He credits Ball and Brown (1968) with first documenting the drift (I did not read that paper).
- **Volume.** Big winners "will have huge volume surge, typically of 10 times or more compared to average volume." (2010)
- **Scan (2010).** The 2010 post gives a Telechart scan with two branches; the second is "((( 100 * (C - C1) / C1) >= 8
  AND V > 3000 AND (100 * V / AVGV100) >= 300) AND C > 1)": an 8% close-to-close gain on at least 300% of the 100-day
  average volume. (The first branch is a $5 gain for stocks at $62.50 or more, on volume above the prior day's.) Our
  `episodic_pivot.py` docstring cites this 8% and the 2014 4% below.
- **Scan (2014).** "During the day Run EP scan c/c1>1.04 and v>3*avgv50.1 and v>=300000 multiple times" (a 4% gain on
  three times the 50-day volume, at least 300,000 shares). Both scans measure the close against the prior close, not a
  gap at the open.
- **Selection.** "I buy less than 1% stocks from that scan." (reply, 2010-02-22); the filters are earnings and sales
  growth, "extent of neglect" and the catalyst.
- **Entry and stop.** "Because we track earnings daily most of the time we enter these kind of trades in pre market or
  at open. In such cases the stop is the low of last 2 days before entry." (reply, 2010-09-30) Later: "Yes. It is
  normally low of the EP day." (reply, 2017-01-31) Orders: "ATM" (reply, 2021-11-08).
- **Exits.** "The exits are in 4 parts with profit target of 20% plus." (2010-09-30)

### 3.5 Kell: the cycle as a whole (originator)

### 3.6 Kell: Reversal Extension (originator)

Source: ["Reversal Extension: How Stocks Bottom"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250820211726/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/reversal-extension-how-stocks-bottom/),
TraderLion, bylined Oliver Kell, published 2024-06-11.
- **What it is.** "A Reversal Extension is formed when the price pulls away from the moving averages, then snaps back
  up and is accompanied by heavy volume."
- **How far.** "When the price is extended well below the 10-day moving average, and the moving averages are extended
  away from each other, begin to look that snap back." No number is given.
- **Confirm on a higher timeframe.** Daily extensions should find support on the weekly chart.
- **A trade or not.** "The Reversal Extension is not necessarily a playable setup but more of a change in mindset." If
  taken: "Stops are placed just below the low of the Reversal Extension bar. Be quick to take profits and raise stops
  with the first profit target at the retest of the 20 EMA." And: "Often, trading the daily Reversal Extension is best
  avoided."
- **On the index.** "Get aggressive trading individual stocks when you see a Reversal Extension on the index."
- **You only know it later.** In his newsletter: "The hammer into the broadening line had the potential to be the
  Reversal Extension and that is confirmed with the Wedge Pop Friday." ([2023-10-07](https://weeklieswatch.substack.com/p/nasdaq-daily-buy-signal-can-the-spx))
- **Examples named.** NVDA 2020, UBER 2023, ELF 2023, CRWD 2023, SWAV, ANF 2023 (intraday version).

### 3.7 Kell: Wedge Pop (originator)

Sources: ["Wedge Pop: The Money Pattern"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250425064619/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/wedge-pop-the-money-pattern/),
TraderLion, bylined Oliver Kell, 2024-09-11; the cycle article; the AMD and MU trade reviews (2024-09-13); his newsletter.
- **What it is.** "The Wedge Pop is the first point where a stock rallies back through its moving averages after a
  Reversal Extension." (cycle article) "The Wedge Pop, also called “The Money Pattern” by Oliver Kell, signals that a
  stock has bottomed and is starting a new uptrend." (Wedge Pop article)
- **The tightening first.** "When price and moving averages tighten, a pivot forms. A break above short-term resistance
  and a reclaiming of the moving averages offers a clear entry point with well-defined risk." (cycle article) The Wedge
  Pop article says moving averages "like the 10-day and 20-day" widen in a selloff and "start to tighten" as the stock
  consolidates.
- **Not just a cross.** "Many traders mistakenly believe the Wedge Pop is simply about crossing the moving averages.
  Instead, focus on price bars and volume."
- **Trigger and volume.** "Enter on a strong price bar with increased volume when the stock breaks through the pivot."
  "A new uptrend begins when the stock trades through the price range, reclaims the moving averages and exhibits
  increased accumulation volume."
- **Gaps.** "Most Wedge Pops that lead to new, powerful trends start with gaps."
- **Failures.** On QQQ in 2023: "Notice how the first two potential Wedge Pops failed with no increase in accumulation
  and consistent distribution volume." And: "If the price fails and drops, the timing isn’t right yet."
- **Stop.** MU review: "Initiating a new position, Oliver staggered his stops with the 10 and 20 exponential moving
  averages under the day’s low." ([MU review](http://web.archive.org/web/20250211124917/https://traderlion.com/profile/oliver-kell/mu-wedge-pop/))
- **The kicker link.** "NFLX-Wedge Pop on volume via a Kicker Candle." ([Weeklies Watch, 2023-03-23](https://weeklieswatch.substack.com/p/push-pull-continues))
- **Examples named.** NVDA 2023, DKNG 2024, CLS 2023, MSTR 2024, MHO 2023, VNT 2023, RCL 2023, QQQ 2023, CRWD 2023; AMD
  2023-11-01 and MU 2024-05-03 (trade reviews); NFLX 2023-03-23 and TSLA 2023-03-21 (newsletter).

### 3.8 Kell: EMA Crossback (originator)

Source: ["How to Buy Stocks on Pullbacks with the EMA Crossback Strategy"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250122081846/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/ema-crossback/),
TraderLion, bylined Oliver Kell, 2024-08-20.
- **What it is.** "The EMA Crossback refers to the first time the stock price retests its moving averages after a strong
  upward move, called a “Wedge Pop.”"
- **Shape.** "The EMA Crossback works best when the price gently moves sideways, allowing the moving averages to rise to
  the price. Sometimes, there will be a slow drift downward into the rising moving averages."
- **Entry.** "Wait for the price to dip back into the 10 or 20-day EMA after a strong rally." Then: "But always wait for
  confirmation that buyers are stepping in before making your trade."
- **Stop.** "Place your stop-loss just below the pivot or price bars." In his interview: "If I am looking for a stock to
  support on the 20EMA I might take a 15% position with a 1-2% stop (risking 15-30bps)." ([Stockbsessed](https://stockbsessed.substack.com/p/oliver-kell-interview-the-mind-and))
- **Examples named.** TOL 2023, NOW 2020, APP 2023, LLY 2023, META 2022, CELH 2023, PTON 2020, DDOG 2021.

### 3.9 Kell: Base n' Break (originator)

Source: ["Base n' Break: How to Catch Breakouts"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250211122023/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/base-n-break-how-to-catch-breakouts/),
TraderLion, bylined Oliver Kell, 2024-05-11.
- **What it is.** "The Base n’ Break is another pullback into the moving averages after the EMA Crossback."
- **Length.** "You will notice these steps in the Cycle Of Price Action form in only 1-3 weeks." The cycle article: "these
  tight basing patterns are defined within just a couple of weeks."
- **Entry.** "The proper buy area is through the pivot into new highs." Aggressive traders can add on support at the
  averages. "Be wary of “air” between the price and the 10-day EMA."
- **Stop.** "If stops are placed below the low of the pivot or breakout bar, you can ride the moving averages higher."
- **Counting.** "In a typical cycle, once you get two Base n’ Breaks, you should realize you are late in the
  intermediate-term cycle and begin looking for an Exhaustion Extension." "Sometimes, up to four Base n’ Breaks may be on
  a strong advance."
- **Role.** "The Base n’ Break is best used as an addition to the leading stocks that make up your core portfolio."
- **Examples named.** WING 2023, LLY 2021, NIO 2020, AFRM 2023, CLS 2023, TMDX 2022, ELF 2022, STRL 2023.

### 3.10 Kell: Exhaustion Extension (originator)

Source: ["Understanding the Exhaustion Extension: How to Sell Into Strength"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250122070523/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/exhaustion-extension-sell-into-strength/),
TraderLion, bylined Oliver Kell, 2024-08-11.
- **What it is.** "The Exhaustion Extension is a euphoric blowoff price movement well above the 10-day moving average, a
  sign that the stock may need time to form a new base."
- **Measure.** "Start to get defensive when there is “air” between the low of the day and the 10EMA." "Exhaustion
  Extensions can be confirmed if the price is extended above the 10-week moving average and also extended off the 10-day
  moving average." No percentage is given in the article. In his newsletter, for the Nasdaq 100 ETF: "On the daily
  extension February 2nd, I thought we were a little bit ahead of ourselves at 4-5% above the Daily 10EMA. That is a
  level that has signaled cooling off/basing in the past." ([2023-03-04](https://weeklieswatch.substack.com/p/holding-weekly-moving-averages))
- **Action.** "Use the Exhaustion Extension to gradually sell off positions when volatility spikes." Newsletter: "Take
  profits into 10EMA extensions until we see healthier breadth." (2023-03-19)
- **Topping signs.** "Gaps Down", "Bearish Candlestick Patterns", "Increased Selling Volume", "Wedge Drop After
  Consolidation". "Expect a longer consolidation period after 3-4 Exhaustion Extensions."
- **On the index.** "If you notice a stock setting up and breaking out of a base while the market is extended, the
  trade should most likely be avoided."
- **Examples named.** AFRM 2023, TSLA 2020, NVDA 2024, DOCU 2020, CRWD 2024, NET 2020, QQQ 2023, SMCI 2024.

### 3.11 Kell: Wedge Drop (originator)

Source: ["Wedge Drop: How to Sell Short"](https://web.archive.org/web/20250122064336/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/wedge-drop-how-to-sell-short/),
TraderLion, bylined Oliver Kell, 2024-07-11.
- **What it is.** "The Wedge Drop happens when the market trades back below the moving averages after a strong rally,
  known as an Exhaustion Extension." The cycle article: after the extension "the price will trade tight into the moving
  averages as there are now fewer new buyers."
- **Without an extension.** "Sometimes, you won’t see a big Exhaustion Extension, but the price will trade sideways and
  eventually lose momentum."
- **Action.** "It confirms that it’s time to exit or consider a short position." Short entry: "As the price breaks below
  moving averages with increased selling pressure, you can enter a short position with a stop just above the recent
  high." But: "It’s also best to avoid shorting early in new bull markets or strong uptrending stocks."
- **In a trade.** AMD review, 2024-01-02: "A gap down through the moving averages is more than enough for Oliver to exit
  the trade." ([AMD review](http://web.archive.org/web/20250211110316/https://traderlion.com/profile/oliver-kell/amd-wedge-pop/))
  Newsletter on TSLA: "If lost 166.50 would trigger a wedge drop short sale." (2023-05-13)
- **Examples named.** KBH 2023, META 2021, TSLA 2023, UPST 2021, QQQ 2023, AAPL 2023, ANF 2024, NVDA 2023, AMD 2024-01-02.

### 3.12 Kell: the downside steps (originator)

### 3.17 Where the sources disagree

1. **What the Qullamaggie Breakout requires.** Kullamägi requires a 30%+ move in the last 1-3 months and is silent on
   volume, depth and 52-week highs. Our scanner tests none of his first step and adds three tests he never states:
   within 5% of the 52-week high, depth 15% or less, and breakout volume 1.5x.
2. **The stop.** Kullamägi: the day's low, capped by ADR. Our scanner: the 20-day EMA (his trailing tool for the last
   piece, not his stop). Kell: the day's low or pivot; 1-2% on support buys. The house: a few cents under the day's low,
   usually within about 3%.
3. **The first exit.** Kullamägi: 1/3 to 1/2 after 3-5 days. Kell: sell into extensions from the 10 EMA. Bonde: four
   parts, 20%+. The house: a third at 3R, a third at 5R (B19, E51).
4. **EP gap size.** Kullamägi 10%+ gap. Bonde's scans are close-to-close gains, not gaps: 8%+ on 3x the 100-day volume
   (2010), then 4%+ on 3x the 50-day volume (2014). Our `episodic_pivot.py` uses a 4% gap at the open (owner decision
   2026-09-04, per its docstring, which reads Bonde's 4% scan as a gap floor).
5. **Which averages.** Kell: 10 and 20-day EMA. Kullamägi: "10- and 20-day moving average", type unstated. The house's
   Model Book cards for Kell's setups use the 9 and 20 EMA; UCT's Substack gives the stack "9EMA>20EMA>50SMA>200SMA"
   (unsigned intro, 2026-04-12), and the owner trails the 9 EMA in his PL trade (2026-03-22).
6. **Is the Wedge Pop a cross?** The house research file `docs/superpowers/research/setups/setup_criteria.json` quotes
   a brain template: "First close above BOTH 10 EMA and 20 EMA on the same day is the core trigger." Kell warns the
   opposite: the Wedge Pop is not "simply about crossing the moving averages" (3.7).
7. **Is the Reversal Extension a trade?** Kell: not necessarily; often best avoided on the daily. Bracco (KB 15731):
   "Technically you don’t buy the reversal extension, it’s more of a indication than a setup". The owner, 2026-11-08,
   says he will "gladly try some Reversal Extensions" (section 6).
8. **Adding.** Kell builds in pieces and adds at crossbacks and bases; Kullamägi adds on EPs. The house buys the full
   size at one price with one stop (A5).
9. **Where the cycle stages sit.** Kell's order is RE, WP, ECB, BnB (repeated), EE, WD, then the downside steps. Our
   `kell_cycle.py` uses RE, WP, EE, WD, "Base & Breakout", calls Base & Breakout the highest-edge stage, and omits the
   EMA Crossback. Kell calls the Wedge Pop "The highest-probability long entry" (kelltrading.com) and the Base n' Break
   "best used as an addition".

### 4.3 Peer-reviewed evidence behind the ingredients (not the setups themselves)

- **Momentum.** Jegadeesh and Titman (1993), *Journal of Finance* 48(1): 65-91: "strategies which buy stocks that have
  performed well in the past and sell stocks that have performed poorly in the past generate significant positive
  returns over 3-to 12-month holding periods." (abstract, via Crossref) This supports, in spirit, Kullamägi's universe
  of the top 1-2% of stocks over 1, 3 and 6 months. Limits: portfolios held for months (the abstract's 3-to-12-month
  holding periods), not daily breakouts or tight stops.
- **The 52-week high.** George and Hwang (2004), *Journal of Finance* 59(5): 2145-2176: "Nearness to the 52-week high
  dominates and improves upon the forecasting power of past returns (both individual and industry returns) for future
  returns." (abstract, via Crossref) This is the best support for our scanner's gate (close within 5% of the 52-week
  high), but note it is our scanner's gate, not Kullamägi's.
- **Post-earnings drift (the EP's logic).** Bonde names Ball and Brown (1968), *Journal of Accounting Research* 6(2), as
  the first study of the drift. A later paper on the same drift is Bernard and Thomas (1989), "Post-Earnings-Announcement
  Drift: Delayed Price Response or Risk Premium?", *Journal of Accounting Research* 27. I confirmed both on Crossref but
  did not read the papers, so I quote nothing from them and give no numbers.
- **No published study tests Kell's phases or Kullamägi's breakout rules as written.** I found none before the search
  budget ran out. Bulkowski's site was not used: I found no page of his under these setups' names (his site refused
  automated reads during checking, HTTP 406). His high-and-tight-flag statistics, which bear on the Breakout, are in
  dossier 03.

### 4.4 Our own scanners on real daily bars (my measurement; internal, not published)

**What I ran.** The app's two detectors, unchanged, on 204 US stocks and ETFs with daily bars from 2010-01 to 2026-09,
fed the same 200-bar window the app's universe scan uses (`SCAN_BASE_DEPTH = 200`, `registry.py`). The bars are a saved
yfinance-style set (another dossier's download). Script and output are in the scratch folder
(`setups/18-qullamaggie-kell/scripts/measure.py`, `measure/summary.txt`).

**Read before using any number.** (1) The 204 names are today's well-known stocks, so the sample is survivorship-biased
upward. (2) Daily bars cannot show the opening-range high or the order of the day's high and low; on a day that touches
both the stop and a target I counted the stop first. (3) No slippage, no gap-through-stop modeling, no exits other than
the stop over 60 sessions. (4) 2010-2026 was mostly a bull market. (5) Added in checking, from reading the script: the
"day-low" stop below is the entry day's final low (known only at that day's close), and the check for a stop or a
target starts the next session. A real day-low stop is the low at the moment of the buy; a trade that later broke that
low on the entry day would have been stopped and is not counted as stopped here. So the day-low rows hide day-one
stop-outs; their net bias is not measured. These numbers describe what our scanner picks, not the edge of the setup.

**`qullamaggie_setup.py`.** 1,331 fire-days, 1,000 distinct setups (first fire per stock, 14-day gap), 917 that pass the
house liquidity floor (B23) on the fire day.
- Only **44%** had Kullamägi's first step, a 30%+ low-to-high rise in the prior 63 sessions (median rise 27%).
- **76%** fired on a day that had already closed above the scanner's "entry" (base high + 0.1%).

- The app's stop (the 20 EMA) sat a median **4.1%** below the entry; wider than 3% in **70%** and wider than the stock's
  ADR (Kullamägi's cap) in **85%**.
- Bought on the next 5 sessions at the higher of the open and the entry (826 triggered): the buy was more than 4% over
  the entry (house C28) in **45%**, and more than 10% above the 10-day average (C29) in **31%**.
- **Stop at the 20 EMA** (median risk 7.9% from the buy): reached 1R / 2R / 3R / 5R before the stop in 51% / 26% / 13%
  / 4%.
- **Stop a few cents under the entry day's low** (Kullamägi and the house; median risk 1.2%): reached 1R / 2R / 3R / 5R

  reports; the winners ran far in R because the risk was small.
- Splitting by Kullamägi's prior-move test (30%+ or not) did **not** change these rates in this sample (3R: 37% vs 39%

- Stage 4 "Wedge Drop": **9.3%**. In **20.5%** of those, price was still above both the 10 and 20 EMA, which is not a

## 5. Our app: what the scanners, the Model Book and the journal actually say

### 5.1 `api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/uct/qullamaggie_setup.py` ("Qullamaggie Setup")

**The rule, as coded.** All must hold on the last bar:
- Close within 5% of the highest high of the last 252 bars (in a scan the detector gets 200 bars, so this is really a
  200-session high).
- Close > 10 EMA > 20 EMA > 50 SMA.
- A box of 15 to 30 bars, ending 1 to 6 bars before the last bar, whose high-to-low depth is 15% or less (the longest
  such box at the most recent offset).
- "ATR thrust": the average high-low range of the last 5 bars is at least 1.5x the box's average range.
- Last bar closes in the top 30% of its range (DCR >= 0.7).
- Last bar's volume is at least 1.5x the prior 20-bar average.
- A weighted confidence of at least 50.

**Levels.** Entry = box high x 1.001. Stop = the 20 EMA. Target = entry + 5 x the 20-bar average range.

**Where it is used.** Registered in `api/routers/patterns.py` (catalog text: "4-week consolidation + ATR-relative thrust +
low-volume retracement on liquid leader near 52w high"); named in the terminal's Breakout panel
(`app/src/pages/terminal/panels/BreakoutPanel.jsx`). It is in `DISPLAY_HIDDEN` in `registry.py` ("v2 blind round
(2026-10-17): mean < 1.5"), so charts do not draw it.

**What disagrees with Kullamägi or looks wrong (most serious first).**
1. **The stop is not his.** The code calls the 20 EMA "Kullamägi's standard institutional stop". His stop is "always
   lows of the day", capped by ADR; the 10/20-day average trails only the last piece. On our data the 20-EMA stop was
   wider than ADR 85% of the time (4.4).
2. **His first step is missing.** No test of the 30%+ move in 1-3 months. Only 44% of fires had it.
3. **The narrative invents facts.** It says he compounded "100,000x+ between 2017 and 2023", "published these rules in
   detail through his 'Monster Moves' framework, his Stockbee.tv content", that "Kullamägi's published track record on
   this setup" shows "a 60-70% follow-through rate", and that he says "never chase more than 2-3% above the trigger
   level". I found none of this in his writing; his FAQ gives a 25% win rate for 2019. Nothing on his site gives a 2017
   or 2023 account figure: his About page says "In 2017 I became financially independent", and his FAQ's account sizes
   run from $17M (2020-08-04) to $82M (2021-03-01). The 2026 book's publisher blurb, which a review ties to him
   (section 2), describes "$5,000 into over $100 million", about 20,000x, not 100,000x.
4. **"5R target" is mislabeled.** Entry + 5 x average range is 5 ATR, not 5R; R is entry minus stop. He sets no target.
5. **Tests he never states.** The 52-week-high gate, the 15% depth cap and the 1.5x breakout-volume gate are not his
   (he ranked volume, tied with earnings, last of six factors). The catalog's "low-volume retracement" is not tested at
   all.
6. **The thrust test fights the setup.** He wants a tightening range, then range expansion. The 5-bar window overlaps
   the last bars of the box, so a quiet end of the base pulls against the gate.
7. **Entry is a daily close level, not the opening range high.** And 76% of fires had already closed above it.
8. **Liquidity.** No price or dollar-volume floor in the detector itself.

### 5.2 `api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/uct/kell_cycle.py` ("Kell Cycle of Price Action")

**The rule, as coded.** Always returns one detection naming a "stage" 1-5, tested in this order:
- Stage 3 "Exhaustion Extension": any 10-bar close-to-close gain of 25%+ anywhere in the bars it gets (200 in a scan),
  and the last 5 bars' average range more than 2x the last 20 bars'.
- Stage 1 "Reversal Extension": a 10%+ change from 25 bars back to 5 bars back, then a 7%+ move the other way in the
  last 5 bars, with range expansion of 1.4x.
- Stage 4 "Wedge Drop": that same 25%+ 10-bar gain anywhere in the window, and the last 5 bars down or flat.
- Stage 5 "Base & Breakout": the last 15 bars range under 8% of their midpoint.
- Stage 2 "Wedge Pop": a 7%+ 5-bar move 10-20 bars ago and the same 15-bar consolidation.
- Otherwise stage 5.

Levels: stage 5 entry = 15-bar high + 0.1%, stop = 15-bar low - 1.5%, target = entry + 2 x the range; stage 4 entry =
15-bar low - 0.1%, stop = 15-bar high + 1%; stage 2 entry and stop at the box edges; stages 1 and 3 none.

**What disagrees with Kell or looks wrong (most serious first).**
1. **Stage 2 can never fire.** When the last 15 bars are "consolidating", the base-length count is always at least 15
   (every one of those bars sits inside its own range), so the stage-5 test always wins. 0 of 125,756 samples (4.4).
   Re-run in checking on the repo's own fixtures: of the five named stage fixtures, only `stage1_reversal` and
   `stage4_wedge_drop` come back as their own stage; `stage2_wedge_pop`, `stage3_parabolic` and `stage3_dramatic` all come
   back as stage 5. The test (`tests/pattern_engine/detectors/test_kell_cycle.py`) only asserts a stage from 1 to 5.
2. **The cycle is wrong.** Kell has eight named steps in a fixed order (section 2 Part B, and 3.5). The code has five, in a different
   order, with no EMA Crossback and no downside steps, and puts "Base & Breakout" last as the "highest-edge stage".
3. **No phase uses the 10 or 20 EMA.** Every Kell phase is defined by price against the 10 and 20-day EMAs. The code
   tests none of them; in 20.5% of its "Wedge Drop" labels price was above both.
4. **Reversal Extension is defined backwards half the time.** Kell's is a capitulation low after a downtrend. The code
   also labels a 7% drop after a rise as one (130 of 242 cases).
5. **"Parabolic" looks at the whole window.** A single 25% 10-day run up to about 9 months earlier (200 bars) makes the stock
   "parabolic" today (20.6% of samples), which feeds stage 4.
6. **Stage 4 levels contradict its direction.** A "bullish" stage 4 (27% of them) still carries a short entry below a
   stop placed above.
7. **Invented quotes.** The narrative puts these in Kell's mouth: "do nothing until the wedge pop sets up", "size the
   Wedge Pop trade aggressively because the structure tells you exactly where you are wrong", "when you see vertical,
   parabolic action on huge volume, the easy money has already been made", "size the Wedge Drop trade larger than your
   Wedge Pop", and "parabolic moves can stay parabolic longer than you can stay solvent". None appears in anything of
   his I read, and a web search found none. They should be removed.
8. **Wrong book title** ("Victorious Stock Operator"; it is "Victory in Stock Trading"). The plan file
   `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-05-13-pattern-recognition-phase-8-elite-framework-detectors.md` carries the same error,
   and so do four narrative strings in `api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/structure/stage_analysis.py` (found in
   checking).
9. **Thresholds labelled as Kell's.** The code comments credit Kell with ">=7% counter-trend break in 3-5 bars (Kell
   §2)", ">=25% gain in <10 bars (Kell exhaustion def.)", "base range < 8% of mid (Kell tight base)" and "Kell's textbook
   base >= 15 bars". None of these numbers is in any Kell text I read.

## 8. Proposed rules for our course

Every number below is measurable on daily bars unless it says intraday. Where sources disagree I give the options and a
recommendation. Part A first, then Part B.

### Part A: the Qullamaggie Breakout (the core "Qullamaggie setup")

#### 8.1 What it is

- **Prior move.** A rise of 30% or more from a low to a later high, both inside the last 63 sessions, made in 3 to 20
  sessions. [originator: "30-100%+", "past 1-3 months", "a few days to a few weeks"; my proposal: 63 sessions and 3 to
  20 sessions as the numbers for his words]
- **Leadership.** RS rating 80 or more [house rule C31]. A+ when the stock is in the top 2% of liquid stocks by gain over
  1, 3 or 6 months [originator: "the 1 or 2% of stocks that are up the most"].
- **Structure.** A sideways consolidation after the move with at least two rising swing lows [originator: "higher lows"];
  the range tightens: the average daily range of the last 5 sessions is below the average of the whole consolidation [my
  proposal, to make "tightening range" measurable].
- **Length.** 10 to 40 sessions [originator: "usually 2 weeks to 2 months"; my proposal for the session count].
- **Depth.** Consolidation high to low 25% or less and no more than half of the prior move [my proposal; the originator
  gives no number; our scanner uses 15%]. A+ at 15% or less [my proposal]. Caution found in checking: the owner's own
  "textbook 5 star" MRAM (2026-05-24) had run from about $10 to $50 and, by the levels he gives (a gap near $29, a
  $30 to $33 range), had come back roughly 34-42% from about $50 to $29-33. A 25% cap would reject it; the owner should rule on the
  number before it is taught.
- **Moving averages.** Through the consolidation, closes hold above the rising 20-day EMA (EMA is my choice; he does not
  say which kind); a brief undercut of the 20-day or a touch of the 50-day SMA is allowed [originator: price "surfs" the
  rising 10 and 20-day, "and sometimes toe 50-day moving average"]. On the breakout day: 10-day EMA above 20-day EMA,
  both rising [my proposal from the same line]. Above the 50-day and the 200-day SMA [house rule B26].
- **Volume.** No rule [originator: not in the post; he ranked volume last of six factors, tied with earnings]. Lighter
  volume late in the base and heavier volume on the breakout raise the grade only [my proposal].
- **Liquidity.** $10 or more and $20 million a day [house rule B23]. Record ADR (20-day average of high/low, minus 1);
  no minimum is set [originator gives none].

#### 8.2 The buy

- **Trigger.** On the day price breaks above the consolidation high, it trades above the opening-range high of the
  first 5-minute bar (1-minute or 60-minute also allowed) [originator]. Daily-chart version: price trades above the
  consolidation high [originator: "just look at the daily chart"].
- **Order.** A market order when the level breaks, set up from an alert placed the night before [house rule E57, D39];
  regular hours only [E58].
- **Do not buy** more than 4% above the trigger [house rule C28] or more than 10% above the 10-day average [C29].
- **No anticipation** inside the base [my proposal, from the originator: it "can also be traded by anticipating the
  breakout but I don’t find it as effective and more skill and experience is needed"]. A missed breakout that comes back to the breakout level may be
  bought as a new trade with its own trigger and stop [originator: "Sometimes I buy the retrace"; my proposal for the
  trigger and stop; house rules A3 and C28 still apply].

#### 8.3 The stop

- A few cents under the low of the breakout day [originator + house rule F62]. At the moment of an opening-range buy
  that is the day's low so far, usually the opening-range low [my reading of his "Stop is always lows of the day"]. It
  must be no wider than the stock's ADR [originator]; if it is wider, skip [house rule A3].
- **Options where it is wider than 3% but within ADR:** (a) skip [strict A13]; (b) take it with the size the stop allows
  (A5 makes the position smaller) [originator]. **Recommend (b)**: A13 says "usually", and the ADR cap already bounds the
  risk. Question 3.

#### 8.4 Trims and trail

- House default: breakeven at 2R to 5R; a third at 3R, a third at 5R; trail the last third on a close under the 10-day
  (fast movers) or 21-day (steadier) [house rule B19, E51, E52]. F63 if it gaps past a trim level.
- Option from the originator: sell 1/3 to 1/2 after 3-5 days and move the stop to breakeven [originator]. **Recommend the
  house default** (the house rule wins); teach Kullamägi's version as his, by name.
- Time stop: under 1R after 10 sessions, sell [house rule C33].

### Part B: the Kell Cycle of Price Action

#### 8.10 Shared definitions

- **Averages.** The 10-day and 20-day EMA for the trend; the 50 and 200-day SMA for the longer trend [originator].
  **Options:** (a) Kell's 10/20 EMA; (b) the house 9/20 EMA used on our cards and in UCT's stated stack (3.17 item 5).
  **Recommend (a)
  for Kell lessons** so the definitions are his, and say once that the house watches the 9 EMA; or (b) everywhere if the
  owner prefers one stack. Question 6.
- **Up cycle** = the 10 EMA above the 20 EMA, both rising. **Down cycle** = the 10 below the 20, both falling [my
  proposal from Kell: "When the 10-day exponential moving average is below the 20-day, there is no reason to be long"].
- **"Above both"** = a close above the 10 and the 20 EMA. **"Air"** = the day's low above the 10 EMA [originator].
- **Liquidity and leadership**: house rules B23 and C31 apply to every long phase.

#### 8.11 Reversal Extension (context; no trade by default)

- **What it is.** After a down cycle of at least 15 sessions, a session whose low is at least 2 ATR(20) under the 10 EMA,
  with the 10 and 20 EMA spread apart, on volume at least 1.5x the 50-day average; then a close back toward or into the
  averages [originator for the shape and heavy volume; my proposal for the numbers]. Best on the index first [originator].
- **Action.** Watch only. Start the watchlist of stocks holding up best [originator]. You only know it was the low after
  a Wedge Pop [originator; UCT].
- **If the owner allows a trade:** stop under the RE bar's low, first target the 20 EMA retest [originator]; only as a
  parabolic long (Bracco mirror) because of B26 [house rule]. Question 8.

#### 8.12 Wedge Pop (buy)

- **Setting.** After a Reversal Extension or a correction of 15% or more [my proposal], at least 3 sessions where price
  tightens under or against the 10/20 EMA and the two averages converge (their gap narrower than 5 sessions earlier) [my
  proposal to measure "price and moving averages tighten"].
- **Trigger.** Price trades through the pivot (the high of the tight range) and the day closes above both the 10 and 20
  EMA [originator: "breaks through the pivot", "reclaims the moving averages"; my proposal: a close above both as the
  measure of "reclaims"]. Volume at least the 50-day average; a gap up raises the grade [originator for "increased accumulation
  volume" and gaps; my proposal for the number]. A cross alone is not a Wedge Pop [originator].
- **Location.** Above the 200-day SMA [house rule B26]; if not, skip (Kell's own TSLA 2023-03-21 would be skipped).
  Question 7.
- **Order.** Market order through the pivot [house rule E57]. Kell avoids the first 10-15 minutes unless there is a
  catalyst [originator]; the house has no time rule [C30], so this is a grade cue only.
- **Stop.** A few cents under the day's low [house rule F62; originator]. Within ADR; usually within about 3% [A13]; skip
  if it does not fit [A3]. Kell's staggered stops under the 10 and 20 EMA are not used (one stop, A5).

#### 8.13 EMA Crossback (buy)

- **Setting.** The first pullback after the Wedge Pop that reaches the rising 10 or 20 EMA (the day's low within 1% of
  either, or an undercut of the 20 EMA that closes back above it), moving sideways or drifting down gently, on volume
  below the 50-day average [originator for the shape; my proposal for the numbers]. A+ = the first one [originator].
- **Trigger.** Buyers show: price trades above the prior session's high [my proposal to measure "wait for confirmation
  that buyers are stepping in"].
- **Stop.** A few cents under the pullback's low [originator: "below the pivot or price bars"; house rule F62]. Kell's
  support buys run "a 1-2% stop" [originator].

#### 8.14 Base n' Break (buy, or a new trade under A5)

- **Setting.** In an up cycle, near the highs, a tight base of 5 to 15 sessions (1-3 weeks) whose lows hold the rising
  10/20 EMA [originator for 1-3 weeks and support; my proposal for the session count].
- **Trigger.** Price trades through the pivot into new highs [originator].
- **Stop.** A few cents under the low of the pivot or of the breakout day, whichever is closer and honest [originator;
  house rule F62].
- **Late cycle.** The first Base n' Break: normal. From the second on, treat the stock as late in its cycle: grade
  down, sell into strength sooner and watch for an Exhaustion Extension [originator: "once you get two Base n’ Breaks,
  you should realize you are late in the intermediate-term cycle"; "As you see multiple Base n’ Breaks, take a quicker
  swing trade and sell into strength."].
- **Under A5** every Base n' Break is its own trade with its own stop and size, not an add [house rule A5, B21].

#### 8.15 Exhaustion Extension (sell into strength)

- **What it is.** "Air" (the day's low above the 10 EMA) and a close far above the 10 EMA, confirmed by the weekly being
  extended from the 10-week line [originator]. **Numbers:** for an index, 4-5% above the daily 10 EMA [originator, his
  newsletter]; for a stock, a close 10% or more above the 10-day [my proposal, borrowed from house rule C29 so the course
  uses one number].
- **Action.** No new buys [house rule C29]. Sell part into the strength: a judgment call [my proposal: apply house rule
  C34, which is written for Bracco's parabolic burst, to Kell's extension too; originator: "gradually sell off positions
  when volatility spikes"]. **Options:** (a) a third; (b) half; (c) leave to judgment. Question 11.
- Count them: after 3-4 extensions expect a longer base [originator].

#### 8.16 Wedge Drop (exit; short only where allowed)

- **What it is.** After an Exhaustion Extension or a stall, price trades tight into the averages and then closes below
  both the 10 and 20 EMA, on volume above the 50-day average [originator for the shape and selling; my proposal for the
  number]. A stall, made measurable in checking: 10 or more sessions without a new closing high [my proposal for Kell's
  "trade sideways and eventually lose momentum"].
- **Longs.** The house trail already exits: a close under the 10-day or 21-day [house rule E52].
- **Short.** Only below the 200-day SMA [house rule B26]. A first Wedge Drop at a top is almost always above it, so in
  our course it is usually an exit, not a short. Short entry where allowed: through the day's low [my proposal]; stop a
  few cents over the recent high [originator; house rule D47].

#### 8.17 Downside EMA Crossback and Downside Base n' Break (shorts)

- **Downside crossback.** In a down cycle, the first rally into the declining 10/20 EMA that fails; short when price
  breaks the pivot low; stop a few cents above the high of the rally or above the averages [originator].
- **Downside Base n' Break.** A brief tight base under the declining averages [originator]; short the break of its low;
  stop a few cents above its high [my proposal: Kell's article gives no entry or stop; this mirrors his upside Base n'
  Break stop, under D47].
- **Both:** below the 200-day only [house rule B26], RS 20 or less [C31 mirror], not hard to borrow [D48]. Not too late
  in the decline [originator]. Only in weak markets: Kell shorts the downside crossback "In more severe market
  conditions" and says to "limit shorting to confirmed downtrends" [originator]; the house's Red market allows shorts
  below the 200-day [house rule D46, F65].

#### 8.18 Best market conditions and common mistakes (Part B)

- Longs when the index is in its own up cycle or has just made a Wedge Pop; leaders that pop before the index are the
  best [originator]. Fewer trades and lower exposure when the index is below its 20 EMA [originator; UCT: "You will
  always wish you traded less when the indexes are below the 20EMA." (2026-04-05, unsigned)].
- Mistakes [originator unless marked]: treating the cross itself as the signal; buying the Reversal Extension;
  buying with "air" above the 10 EMA; buying a second or later Base n' Break as if it were early; holding through a
  Wedge Drop; shorting a strong uptrend; shorting late in a downtrend; trading the period between the Reversal
  Extension and the Wedge Pop [UCT: "the hardest period to trade"].

## 13. Examples (from our data)

(Numbered 13 because section 12 above is the cold check. This section covers Part A, the Qullamaggie Breakout, which is
the tradeable core of the setup; the Kell phases of Part B are context and exits, and their buy phases are charted in
dossiers 14 (EMA Crossback) and 15 (Wedge Pop).)

**The scan's rule (section 8.1-8.3 made measurable, plus the house rules).** On daily bars, a breakout day is one where
the high trades above the highest high of a consolidation of 10 to 40 sessions (the longest length that qualifies is
kept). The consolidation is 25% deep or less from its high to its low and gives back no more than half of the prior
move; the prior move is a rise of 30% or more from the lowest low of the 20 sessions before the base into the base's
first three sessions; the base makes higher lows (its last third's low above its first third's low, the middle third
not below the first); it tightens (the average daily range of the last 5 sessions below the base's average); 80% or
more of its closes hold within 2% of the 20-day EMA and no low undercuts the 50-day SMA by more than 2%. On the
breakout day the 10-day EMA is above the 20-day EMA, both rising, and the close is above the 50-day and 200-day SMA with
the 50 above the 200 (B26). Liquidity: close of $10 or more and $20 million or more traded a day over 50 days (B23).
The entry is a market order one cent through the consolidation high, or the open if it gaps over it (E57); no entry
more than 4% past the high (C28) or more than 10% above the prior day's 10-day average (C29). The stop is five cents
under the breakout day's low; the trade is skipped if that is wider than the stock's 20-day ADR (originator) or 8%
(hard cap; the dossier's option (b) in 8.3, so stops of 3-8% inside the ADR are taken). A new hit is allowed 30
sessions after the last. No RS rating exists in the sandbox; the 30% prior move stands in for C31.

**Base rate.** 5,038 hits on 1,969 of the 2,630 liquid names (the 420-second budget ran out at 1,969; the base rate
covers the scanned part), breakout days from 2000-01-03 to 2026-11-05, outcome measured over the 60 sessions after the
entry day. 28.9% reached 3R and 20.0% reached 5R before the stop; 92% were eventually stopped within the 60 sessions.
Caveat: a fixed stop under the breakout day's low with no trims, no breakeven move and no trail, so these are raw rates,
not the account's results; the owner's 3R/5R trims and the 10/21-day trail (B19, E51, E52) change them. Many hits had a
stop tighter than 2%, which inflates both the R multiples and the stop-out count; the ten examples below were chosen
with stops of 1.6% to 4.9%.
