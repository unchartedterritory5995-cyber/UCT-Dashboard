## 1. What it is

The EMA Crossback is Oliver Kell's name for the first pullback into the rising 10-day and 20-day exponential moving
averages (EMAs) after a stock has turned up out of a decline (his "Wedge Pop") or broken out. You buy when the pullback
holds the averages and buyers show up again, with a stop just under that low, so the risk is small and the entry comes
early in the new trend.

**Where it sits.** Kell's "Cycle of Price Action" runs in a fixed order: Reversal Extension (the capitulation low),
Wedge Pop (the first push back through the averages), **EMA Crossback** (the first retest of those averages), Base n'
Break (later bases along the averages), Exhaustion Extension (the blow-off), Wedge Drop (the first break down through
the averages), then the same steps downward (a downside EMA Crossback and downside Base n' Breaks) until a new
Reversal Extension ([TraderLion, "Cycle Of Price Action", Kell byline, 2024-09-10](https://web.archive.org/web/20250623154156/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/cycle-of-price-action-by-oliver-kell/)).

**The market psychology.** These points are reasoning drawn from the sources, not measured facts.
- **A new trend has just been proven, but most people still doubt it.** Kell: "The Wedge Pop confirms a change in
  character after the stock has bottomed when the price finds strength and moves up through the moving averages."
  ([Wedge Pop, 2024-09-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20241006111805/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/wedge-pop-the-money-pattern/))
  The first pullback is where the doubters sell and the early bottom buyers take quick profits. If the averages hold
  anyway, the selling had no follow-through.
- **The buyers who missed the pop are waiting below.** TraderLion's staff article: "Let the stock come to you, don’t
  chase the first spike." ([2025-05-22](https://web.archive.org/web/20250702020000/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/trading-the-ema-crossback/))
  Kell: "A strong stock will respect its moving averages, using them as a springboard to head higher."
  ([EMA Crossback, 2024-08-20](https://web.archive.org/web/20250122081846/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/ema-crossback/))
- **Quiet selling means little supply.** Kell on MELI in his own newsletter: "Down on very light volume all day Friday
  with a heavy volume last hour close." ([The Weeklies Watch, 2023-03-04](https://weeklieswatch.substack.com/p/holding-weekly-moving-averages))
  A light-volume drift into the averages is read as holders not leaving. (That is the reasoning; my own test did not
  find that quiet pullbacks did better, section 4.4.)
- **Who gets trapped (my reasoning, no source states it).** Traders who read the pop as a dead-cat bounce short the
  first pullback. When the averages hold and price takes out the hold day's high, they have to buy back, which adds
  fuel.
- **Why it is low risk.** The averages are right under price, so the honest stop is close. Kell: "If buying off
  support, it is usually against the 10 or 20EMA and will use that low as my stop."
  ([Stockbsessed interview, 2023-04-01](https://stockbsessed.substack.com/p/oliver-kell-interview-the-mind-and))
- **Why early beats late.** Kell: "Stay patient at the start of a cycle, but be quicker to lock in profits later on."
  (Cycle Of Price Action) On a single stock, from his downside article: "Be careful not to short too late in the
  downtrend, just like buying late in an uptrend." And on the market's own cycle (the line comes right after his remarks
  on the QQQ and his breadth indicators): "When I think we are “later in the cycle” I have been learning to just sit out
  or pass on a setup as it often doesn’t follow through in a big way." (Stockbsessed interview)
- **The caution.** A held average is not proof. Kell: "If the price fails at the pivot and reverses lower, then the
  timing isn’t right yet" (EMA Crossback, 2024-08-20).

**What "crossback" means.** In every Kell text I read, it is price coming back to the averages: "the first time the
stock price retests its moving averages" (2024-08-20). It is **not** the two averages crossing each other. Kell does
use the 10-over-20 cross, but as the sign that a new up-cycle has started, which comes before the crossback: "SPY
Daily- Triggered a daily up cycle four days ago. The 10EMA pushed through the 20EMA with a rising 50SMA."
([The Weeklies Watch, 2023-09-03](https://weeklieswatch.substack.com/p/heading-into-september)) This matters because
two of our own definitions read "crossback" as an averages cross (section 5).

**Other names for it.**

### 3.1 Kell: "How to Buy Stocks on Pullbacks with the EMA Crossback Strategy" (TraderLion, Kell byline, 2024-08-20)

Read from the [Internet Archive copy of 2025-01-22](https://web.archive.org/web/20250122081846/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/ema-crossback/).
- **Lines and prior move:** "By focusing on the 10 and 20-day exponential moving averages (EMAs), you can identify key
  moments to buy stocks when the price dips into these levels after a strong rally."
- **Which test:** "The EMA Crossback refers to the first time the stock price retests its moving averages after a
  strong upward move, called a “Wedge Pop.”"
- **Shape:** "After a stock rises quickly, it typically consolidates", and "it either moves sideways or drifts
  slightly downward." And: "The EMA Crossback works best when the price gently moves sideways, allowing the moving
  averages to rise to the price."
- **The pivot:** "This consolidation forms a “pivot point,” where the stock can either continue upward or reverse
  downward."
- **Depth:** "Wait for the price to dip back into the 10 or 20-day EMA after a strong rally." Either line counts.
- **Confirmation:** "Make sure the price is showing signs of stability or support near the moving averages." And: "But
  always wait for confirmation that buyers are stepping in before making your trade."
- **The buy (from the article's FAQ):** "Once the price stabilizes and starts to push higher again, that’s the signal
  to buy."
- **Stop:** "Place your stop-loss just below the pivot or price bars. As the stock moves higher, raise your stop-loss
  to lock in gains." The FAQ: "Stops can be set below the recent low to manage risk if the trade goes against you."
- **Time frames:** "Confirm the stock’s strength by analyzing both shorter and longer timeframes to get a clearer
  picture of the price action."
- **Where in the cycle (FAQ):** "The EMA Crossback happens after an initial pop off the lows and marks the first
  pullback in the cycle."
- **The index:** "When an index pulls back into its moving averages, the strongest stocks within that index are
  usually further along in their own price cycles, creating potential buying opportunities."
- Chart examples named on the page (not checked by me on charts): NOW 2020, APP 2023, LLY 2023, META 2022, CELH 2023,
  PTON 2020, DDOG 2021.
- **Not stated:** any number for depth, length, volume, stop width or targets.

### 3.3 Kell: "Downside EMA Crossback: Sell Short into Resistance" (TraderLion, Kell byline, 2024-06-11)

Read from the [archive copy of 2024-10-04](https://web.archive.org/web/20241004135245/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/downside-ema-crossback-sell-short-into-resistance/).

- **The long-side rule inside it:** "When the 10-day exponential moving average is below the 20-day, there is no

- **Definition:** "The EMA Crossback, to the downside, is the first retest of the moving averages after the Wedge Drop
  confirms the downtrend."
- **When to short at all:** "In more severe market conditions the EMA Crossback to the downside can be used as an
  opportunity to short the stock."
- **Entry and stop:** "When the price rallies into the moving averages, short the stock with a stop placed above the
  high." And: "Enter the short position when weakness drags the price through the pivot and place the stop above the
  moving averages."
- **Late is bad both ways:** "Be careful not to short too late in the downtrend, just like buying late in an uptrend."
- **His default:** "Stay in cash and let the cycle unfold when you recognize the moving averages act as resistance at

- Examples named: CRWD 2021, AMZN 2018, PLTR 2021, Z 2021, NVDA 2021, LULU 2018, SHOP 2019.

### 3.4 Kell: the neighbouring stages (TraderLion, Kell bylines, 2024)

- **Wedge Pop** ([2024-09-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20241006111805/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/wedge-pop-the-money-pattern/)):

### 3.8 TraderLion staff: "Oliver Kell’s EMA Crossback Uncovers Low-Risk Entries" (2025-05-22; educator)

Author shown as "TraderLion", not Kell. Read from the [archive copy of 2025-07-02](https://web.archive.org/web/20250702020000/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/trading-the-ema-crossback/).
- **Definition:** "It offers a clean, low-risk entry point after a strong rally off of the lows, when a stock pulls back
  to the 10-day or 20-day exponential moving average (EMA) for the first time."
- **What a Wedge Pop is:** "A Wedge Pop occurs when the price reclaims both the 10-day and 20-day EMAs after a sharp
  drop, signaling a shift into a new uptrend."
- **Shape:** "The cleaner and shallower the pullback, the better" and "A slow drift into the EMAs is ideal."
- **Signs of strength:** "Tight price bars (low volatility)", "Volume increasing on up days (accumulation)" and "Bullish
  candlestick patterns (like hammers or engulfing patterns)".

### 3.14 Where the sources disagree

1. **What comes before it.** Kell's articles: the Wedge Pop. Kell's newsletter also uses it after a breakout (DKNG,
   2023-04-03, after "DKNG-Strong breakout today. Looking for follow through on $19." on 2023-03-29: a push over a
   price level, not a new high; the sandbox bars show a 52-week high of 21.62 above that day's 18.86 close). The staff FAQ widens it to "the first pullback after a higher low". Our Model Book and UCT Method course say "after an
   extension", which is a different place in the cycle (section 5.4). Recommendation in section 8.
2. **Which line.** 10 and 20 EMA (Kell, TraderLion, Deepvue, Chart IQ) versus 9 and 20 EMA (UCT's stack, the Model
   Book, the screener structure). In practice the difference is tiny (a 9-day EMA weights the last close 20%, a 10-day
   about 18.2%; my arithmetic from 2/(n+1)).
3. **10 or 20.** Kell lets either line be the support ("10 or 20-day EMA"). Chart IQ requires the low to reach within 2%
   of the 20 EMA, so a stock that only tags the 10 EMA is not counted.
4. **The trigger.** "Buyers stepping in" (Kell, no price level) versus follow-through over the hold day's high (Kell's
   TSLA note; Bracco; the owner's general entry ruling) versus a 65-minute breakout (Kell's preference) versus a break
   of the consolidation range (staff). The screener and Chart IQ define no trigger.
5. **The stop.** The hold day's low (Kell interview), the pivot or price bars (Kell article), the swing low or base
   (staff), staggered under the 10 and the 20 EMA (Kell's MU trade). These are close to each other on most charts; the
   staggered version breaks our one-stop rule (A5).
6. **Building the position.** Kell buys in two pieces and adds; the staff article says scale in; Deepvue says "add to or
   start"; our curriculum calls the crossback an add-only play; our rule A5 buys the full size at one price and never
   adds. Only A5 binds our course.
7. **Short side.** Kell defines the downside crossback but mostly stays in cash and shorts only "In more severe market
   conditions". Our playbook makes the short a full half of the setup.

## 4. Measured evidence

**Bottom line: I found no published statistic (win rate, average gain, failure rate) for the EMA Crossback from any
source.** Kell gives no numbers for the shape of any stage (depth, length, volume) beyond the moving-average periods;
his only other numbers describe his own trading (the 1-2% stop, the 15% pieces, and "3-4% above the moving averages"
as too extended). A
web search for crossback backtests returned only generic moving-average-crossover tests, which measure a different
thing. Below is what exists, who measured it, and its limits.

### 4.1 The app's own lab, on the screener's version (internal, not published)

These measure `api/services/screener/base_catalog.py`'s `ema-crossback` structure, which is not Kell's setup (section
5.2), so they say little about the crossback as Kell or the owner trade it.
- `docs/base_lift_ledger.json` (measured 2026-08-31): the chance of +10% before -8% within 20 sessions was 39.44% after
  the structure versus a 26.5% baseline, a lift of +12.94 points (95% interval +12.12 to +13.72, n = 18,705). The same
  detector run on randomised price data (the ledger's method shuffles 21-bar blocks) reached at most +9.65 points over
  30 trials. The ledger's own note says most of the lift is
  the mechanical effect of buying a pullback in a stock that already rose 60% (its words; the code tests a range, see 5.2); only the margin over the random-data
  result, about 2.5 points, is the demonstrated edge.
- `docs/expectancy.json` (2026-09-01, 417 tickers, 10% target, 8% stop, 20 sessions): n = 2,328, win rate 40.59%,
  expectancy +0.37% per trade (+0.046R).
- `docs/noise_firing_rate.json`: it fired on 5.29% of real anchors and 5.80% of random-data anchors (ratio 0.912), so
  it fires about as often on noise as on real charts.
- **Limits:** a fixed 10%/8% bracket is not our trade; a 60% prior advance is not Kell's context; daily bars only.

### 4.2 The brain's published triggers (internal, tiny)

`docs/superpowers/research/setups/SETUP_NOTES.md` section 2.2 tabulates the brain database's `setup_triggers` (levels
"published pre-market", outcome recorded). For "EMA Crossback": n = 15, stop distance from 0.07% to 5.00%, median
4.01%, entry kind `ema_reclaim_21`, stop kind `ema_21_buffer`, outcomes 1 win, 6 losses, 6 open, 2 never triggered.
Fifteen trades prove nothing either way. Who chose those levels (the owner or an automated job) is not stated in the
file; I did not open the brain database.

### 4.3 What the Study bank's examples do and do not show

`docs/study/bank/cycle.price_action.json` holds 300 EMA crossback items (all drafts). Their forward 20-session return
looks good (median +6.0%, 91% positive), but `scripts/study/bank_v2/scan_chartiq.py` fills each slot with "a held
example first", and all 300 carry `held_20: true`. They were chosen because they worked. They are teaching examples,
not evidence of an edge.

### 4.4 My own lab test on the sandbox bars (not a published statistic)

**What I did.** A script in my scratch folder (not in the repo) ran three definitions over the sandbox daily bars
that the other setup dossiers use: 3,512 US tickers with at least 400 daily bars, data to 2026-11-06, split-adjusted.
- **Chart IQ's crossback**, using the engine's own `cycle_events` code point in time (without `confirmed()`, which looks
  ahead).
- **The screener structure**, `ema_crossback_state` re-implemented bar by bar with its own constants.
- **A baseline**: one in ten of all days on which a stock closed above its 20-day EMA in the upper half of its range.
- **The house trade on every event**: buy a cent over the hold day's high within the next 3 sessions (skip if the fill
  is more than 4% past it, C28); stop 3 cents under the hold day's low (F62); breakeven once 2R is touched (B19); a
  third at 3R and a third at 5R (E51, F63); the last third out on a close under the 21-day EMA (E52); under 1R at the
  10th session's close, sell (C33); a gap through the stop exits at the open. (Checked in the script: the 21-day trail
  starts only after the 5R trim, so before 5R the stop, breakeven and the 10-session rule are the only exits; a
  120-session cap; E52's 10-day option for fast movers and B20's 30-minute gap rule are not simulated.) **A second exit for comparison:** the
  stop, or the first close under the 21-day EMA (close to Kell's "it broke the 20EMA").
- **Filters applied:** B23 ($10 and $20 million a day, 50-day median) and B26 (above a 200-day SMA that is higher than
  20 sessions earlier). **Not applied:** the RS rating (C31) and the app's market reading (B18); in their place I used
  two stand-ins in a second pass: the stock beating the QQQ over 63 sessions, and the QQQ above its 20-day EMA. No
  earnings filter (B22).

**Results** (liquid, above a rising 200-day; "R" is the mean result per trade; the range is a 95% bootstrap range that
resamples whole tickers):

| Set | Events | Triggered | House trade, mean R (range) | Stop or close under 21 EMA, mean R | Reached 3R | Reached 5R | Median stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chart IQ crossback, all | 62,332 | 73% | +0.002 (-0.025 to +0.024) | +0.26 | 19.7% | 12.4% | 1.87% |
| Baseline days | 117,049 | 74% | -0.005 (-0.020 to +0.013) | +0.22 | 19.4% | 12.1% | 2.12% |
| Screener structure | 31,509 | 72% | +0.083 (+0.056 to +0.110) | +0.25 | 20.3% | 13.0% | 4.19% |
| Chart IQ crossback: first after the pop, within 20 sessions, QQQ above its 20 EMA, stock beat the QQQ over 63 sessions | 15,551 | 74% | +0.057 (+0.024 to +0.094) | +0.38 | 20.3% | 13.3% | 2.01% |

What the numbers say, and do not say:
1. **The shape alone is not an edge.** Chart IQ's crossback earned about nothing under the house trade (+0.002R),
   the same as a random strong day above the 20-day EMA. About 29% of trades won; the median trade lost 1R.
2. **Kell's conditions are where the difference came from.** The first crossback in a leg did better (+0.023R, range -0.004 to
   +0.048) than the second (-0.036R) and the third or later (-0.070R, range -0.132 to -0.003). Crossbacks more than 40 sessions after
   the pop lost (-0.048R, range -0.095 to -0.001). A stock that closed lower than 63 sessions earlier yet sat above a
   rising 200-day on the hold day (a fresh turn inside a long uptrend) did best (+0.107R, +0.047 to +0.160); one
   already up 20% to 50% did worst (-0.062R, -0.114 to -0.024). (These splits by pop distance and 63-day return use
   the 59,711 events that also had the 10-day EMA over the 20-day.) That fits Kell's warning against "buying late in
   an uptrend". With the market and
   leadership stand-ins added, the first early crossback earned +0.057R, with its range above zero. These conditions
   come from Kell, but I looked at the splits before combining them, so the combined number is a hint, not proof.
3. **Stops are naturally tight.** Median 1.87% from the entry; 80% within 3% (A13) and 55% within 2% (Kell's "1-2%").
   The screener structure's stops are much wider (median 4.19%; only 25% within 3%), because its event day is a
   reclaim day, not a quiet hold.
4. **A quarter of hold days (25%) broke their low before triggering.** Waiting for the trigger (8.2) avoided those.
5. **Light volume did not help in this test.** Pullbacks on under 0.7 times the 50-day volume did worse (-0.056R,
   -0.093 to -0.017) than those on average volume (-0.002R) or above-average volume (+0.04R to +0.05R). This runs
   against the common teaching that a quiet pullback is better (Brian Shannon's, for one; Kell's MELI note describes a
   light-volume crossback day but states no rule). One possible reason: quiet pullbacks had the tightest stops (median 1.69%), which daily noise hits
   more often. I would not make light volume a requirement on this evidence.
6. **The exit rules matter more than the setup.** In each of the four sets in the table, the baseline included,
   holding until a close under the 21-day EMA earned about 0.17R to 0.32R more per trade than the house management
   (breakeven at 2R, the 10-session time stop, trims at 3R and 5R). Not in every split: below the 200-day the gap was
   only about 0.07R for Chart IQ's crossback. That is a question about the rulebook, not about this setup; section 10
   flags it.
7. **The 200-day.** Liquid crossbacks below the 200-day did not do worse under the house trade (+0.068R) but did worse
   under the simple exit (+0.14R versus +0.26R). B26 is a hard rule and stays; the data neither condemns nor clears
   these trades.
8. **Chart IQ misses many crossbacks the sources named.** In the date windows I checked, it fired on TSLA
   (2023-03-24), NFLX (2023-04-06), MU (2024-05-21), OKLO (2026-01-14), PL (2025-12-10) and DKNG (2023-04-04), and did
   not fire on HOOD (late 2025), CRWV (2025-09), ARM (2024-05/06), MELI and AI (2023-03), POWL (2026-03), CHYM (2026-01)
   or CRCL (2026-05). Of the four it found that triggered, all four were stopped out; PL gapped past the chase limit
   and DKNG broke first. Four trades are an anecdote. Kell's own account of his TSLA trades that week, on the 65-minute
   chart: "Four trades, two scratches, two losers".

**Limits.** Today's tickers only, so delisted stocks are missing and results look better than reality. Split-adjusted
prices make the $10 floor approximate in old years. No RS rating, no earnings filter, no intraday entry (Kell's
65-minute entry would change the stops), no slippage. Each signal is one trade, whatever else is open. Daily bars
cannot order events inside a day: when a day touched both the stop and a target, I counted the stop first.

## 5. Our app: what exists, what it tests, and what looks wrong

### 5.1 Chart IQ's cycle reading: `api/services/chart_iq/engine/v44_read.py` (`cycle_events`)

This is the closest thing the app has to Kell's crossback. It walks the daily bars and names cycle stages with the 10
and 20-day EMAs. It labels a bar "EMA crossback" when all of these hold (thresholds in `CYC`):
- the last named stage was a "Wedge pop", "Back over the EMAs", "Base n' break" or "Exhaustion extension", and the pop
  (or the 3-closes-back-over-the-EMAs event) was at least 3 sessions earlier;
- the 20-day EMA is higher than 5 sessions ago;
- the bar's low is no more than 2% above the 20-day EMA (it may be below it), and the bar closes at or above the 20 EMA;
- the 3 bars before it all had lows above the 10-day EMA (`xb_prior` 3), so price was riding above the 10 before the
  test;
- the bar closes in the upper half of its range.

Its Wedge pop rule: at least 8 of the prior 15 closes under the 20 EMA, a tightening range (or a recent undercut and
up-close), a close over both EMAs and over the last 5 highs, in the top 40% of the range, on at least 1.05 times the
50-day volume. Its stated next step after a crossback: "the buy: hold over the 10/20 EMA, stop under the crossback
low". For display, `confirmed()` drops a crossback once a later close goes under its low (this looks at later bars;
fine for a chart label, not for a point-in-time test).

Where it differs from Kell:
- **Only the 20 EMA counts.** A first pullback that holds the 10 EMA and never comes within 2% of the 20 EMA is not
  named, though Kell says "the 10 or 20-day EMA".
- **Later tests get the same name.** After a "Base n' break" or "Exhaustion extension", the next qualifying pullback is
  again called "EMA crossback". Kell calls a later test a Base n' Break.
- **No volume, no long-trend filter.** Nothing about the 50 or 200-day, relative strength or liquidity. The bank scan
  adds a liquidity cut but not a 200-day cut (5.7).
- `api/services/chart_iq/engine/v4_compose.py` relabels a crossback bar as "Breakout" when it closes over the 20-day
  high on at least twice the 50-day volume ("not a pullback buy").

### 5.2 The screener structure: `api/services/screener/base_catalog.py` (`EMA_CROSSBACK`, `ema_crossback_state`)

Labelled in the Focus screener as "Pullback through the 9- and 20-day avgs" (`app/src/pages/screener/focus/plainWords.js`
line 170). It fires when, on the last bar:
- price closes above both the 9 and 20-day EMAs;
- some close in the last 30 sessions was under the 9 EMA, and one in the last 5 sessions was too;
- the 9 EMA crossed **up** through the 20 EMA within those 30 sessions;
- the range of the 120 sessions before that window was at least 60% (high over low).

What looks wrong:
- **It tests a different event.** An upward 9-over-20 cross in the window means the 9 had been under the 20, which by
  Kell's rule is a state where "there is no reason to be long the stock". A dip that drops the 9 under the 20 and then
  recovers is, in Kell's cycle, a pullback deep enough to need a new Wedge Pop. The true first crossback after a pop
  usually keeps the 10 above the 20 the whole time.
- **Its provenance note is out of date.** It says the 15-source corpus "contains NO Kell, so there is no
  published sentence to quote" (the header comment above `CB_EXTENSION`). Kell's published definitions exist
  (sections 3.1 to 3.6). The structure's description "Ours, not a published pattern" is therefore not accurate; it is
  ours in its thresholds, Kell's in its name.
- **The 60% prior advance is not in any Kell text.** Kell's context is the turn off a low (the Wedge Pop).
- **The "prior advance" test cannot tell up from down (a code bug).** `ema_crossback_state` computes
  `(peak - trough) / trough` over the 120 sessions, with no check that the low came before the high. A stock that fell
  from 100 to 60 in that window scores 67% and passes as a "prior advance". The criterion text says "there must be a
  trend to re-enter"; the code does not test that.
- Its own lab numbers (4.1) show most of its lift is mechanical.
- `tests/test_ema_crossback.py` locks these behaviours in, including the "NO Kell" provenance.

### 5.3 The pattern engine's Kell detector: `api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/uct/kell_cycle.py`

It classifies five stages: Reversal Extension, Wedge Pop, Exhaustion Extension, Wedge Drop, Base & Breakout. **It has
no EMA Crossback stage.** Other problems:
- It credits the framework to a work it calls Victorious Stock Operator (also `api/routers/patterns.py` line 524 and
  `api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/structure/stage_analysis.py`). I found no book or course by that title. Kell's
  ebook is *Victory in Stock Trading*.
- Its order (Wedge Drop as "Reversal trigger with new trend", then Base & Breakout as stage 5) does not match Kell's
  published order (section 1).
- Its numbers are labelled as Kell's ("Kell §2", "Kell exhaustion def.", "Kell's textbook base >= 15 bars") but none of
  them appears in any Kell text I read. The repo's own earlier sweep reached the same finding: "Kell publishes no
  numbers." (`docs/superpowers/research/setups/SETUP_NOTES.md`) Kell's Cycle page says of the Base n' Break that
  "these tight basing patterns are defined within just a couple of weeks", not 15 bars or more.

### 5.4 The Model Book card and playbook

- **Card** (`app/src/pages/modelbook/setupCatalog.js` lines 105-112, direction "both"): "Kell’s re-entry: after an
  extension, price crosses back into the 9/20 EMAs and reclaims them".
- **Playbook** (`app/src/pages/modelbook/setupPlaybooks.js` lines 281-331): Context: "Long version: a stock resuming an
  uptrend after a pullback, or an early reversal starting a fresh trend." Criteria: "Long: price crosses back down to
  the 9/20-EMA, the 9-EMA crosses the 20-EMA, price tightens and then remounts both." Trigger: "Long: as price holds
  and remounts the averages, or breaks the high of the candle that held them." Stop: "Long: below the candle that held
  the averages." Exit: "Long: trail the 9/20-EMA into the new trend." Mistake: "Long: anticipating the reclaim before
  price actually crosses back above the averages."

What agrees with Kell: the trigger (a hold, then the holding candle's high), the stop (under the holding candle), the
trail (the averages), and the main mistake (buying before buyers show).

What disagrees:
- **"after an extension"** Kell's crossback comes right after the Wedge Pop, early in the cycle. After an extension,
  his cycle expects a Base n' Break or, after an Exhaustion Extension, a Wedge Drop.
- **"the 9-EMA crosses the 20-EMA"** Not part of Kell's crossback (section 2). If the 9 has to cross the 20 again, it
  was under it, which Kell treats as no-long territory.
- **The short side "one of the four climax-day entries listed for the Parabolic Short".** Bracco's public post "The
  Parabolic Short" (2025-06-13) describes three climax-day intraday setups (the Quick Opening Range Breakdown, the
  "U-Turn" and the "Long-Drawn-Out Range Breakdown") and a backside opening-range breakdown. It never uses the word
  crossback. The four-entry list may come from a private playbook document that is not in this repo; I could not check
  it. Kell's own downside crossback follows a Wedge Drop, not a parabolic climax.

## 8. Proposed rules for our course

Measurable lines, each with its basis. Where a line needs a number that no source gives, I say so and mark it [my
proposal]. Section 4.4 has the lab numbers behind the proposals.

### 8.1 What it is (the setup, before any trigger)

- **Universe.** Price $10 or more and at least $20 million traded a day on the 50-day average; never more than 1% of
  daily volume. [house rule: B23, D42]
- **A leader.** RS rating 80 or more, in a strong group; growing sales and earnings preferred. [house rule: C31, D40;
  educator: Deepvue's account of Kell's screen, "Earnings and sales growth in recent quarters over 30%."]
- **Long trend.** Close above the 200-day SMA. [house rule: B26, hard] Prefer the 200-day rising (higher than 20
  sessions ago). [my proposal]
- **The new up-cycle came first.** One of:
  (a) a **Wedge Pop** 3 to 20 sessions earlier: the stock had spent most of the prior 3 weeks under the 20-day EMA, then
  closed back above both the 9 and the 20-day EMA, out of a tightening range, on above-average volume [originator: Kell
  "after a strong upward move, called a “Wedge Pop.”"; the 3-session minimum and the 8-of-15 test are Chart IQ's; the
  20-session maximum is my proposal; in my test crossbacks more than 40 sessions after the pop lost money]; or
  (b) a **breakout** over a base's pivot or a key level, 3 to 20 sessions earlier [originator: Kell's DKNG note,
  "Pulling back to the Crossback after a nice breakout." (DKNG's breakout was over a price level, not to a new high,
  3.14); the 3 to 20 sessions are my proposal. TraderLion staff's "breakout" is the pop over the EMAs (3.8), so it
  supports (a), not (b)].
  Recommend (a) as the textbook case and (b) as allowed.
- **The averages.** At the hold day: 9-day EMA above the 20-day EMA, and the 20-day EMA higher than 5 sessions earlier.
  [originator: Kell "When the 10-day exponential moving average is below the 20-day, there is no reason to be long the
  stock."; Chart IQ uses the same 5-session slope] Teach the 9/20 pair and say Kell uses 10/20. [UCT: the Model Book
  and UCT's writers use the 9/20 pair; the unsigned 2026-04-12 intro's "9EMA>20EMA>50SMA>200SMA" is a checklist for the
  indexes, not a stock rule]
- **Which test.** The **first** pullback into the 9/20 zone since the pop or breakout. A later test after a new high is
  a Base n' Break: graded lower here, or taught as its own setup. [originator: Kell "the first time the stock price
  retests its moving averages"; "The Base n’ Break is another pullback into the moving averages after the EMA
  Crossback."; my test agrees: first +0.023R, third or later -0.070R, section 4.4]
- **Shape and length.** A sideways or slow drift of about 2 to 10 sessions from the post-pop high into the averages.
  [originator: Kell "gently moves sideways"; educator: "A slow drift into the EMAs is ideal."; the session counts are
  my proposal]
- **Depth.** The hold day's low trades into the zone between the 9-day EMA and about 1% under the 20-day EMA, and the
  close is at or above the 20-day EMA. [originator: "dip back into the 10 or 20-day EMA"; Kell's TSLA undercut "into the
  Daily 20EMA and found support"; the 1% is my proposal; Chart IQ allows a low up to 2% above the 20 EMA]
- **Volume.** No hard volume rule. Common teaching prefers a quiet pullback [educator: Brian Shannon looks for "volume
  expanding in the direction of the primary trend and contracting during pullbacks." ([AlphaTrends FAQ](https://alphatrends.net/new-visitors/));
  originator: Kell notes one crossback day "Down on very light volume" (MELI) but states no volume rule; educator:
  TraderLion staff want "Volume increasing on up days (accumulation)"], but in my test quiet pullbacks did worse, not
  better (section 4.4, item 5). Recommend: teach a quiet drift as a grading cue only; a heavy-volume close under the 20-day
  EMA still invalidates (8.5). [my proposal]
- **Market.** The app's reading is Green or Yellow, and the Nasdaq is not below its 20-day EMA, or a market Wedge Pop has
  just happened. [house rule: B18, F65 (F65 itself still allows A+ setups in Orange, so cutting Orange is my proposal);
  originator: Kell trades less "during index corrections (below 20EMA)"; UCT, unsigned intro: "You will always wish you
  traded less when the indexes are below the 20EMA."; the Wedge Pop exception is my proposal. In my test the QQQ filter
  made little difference on its own: +0.005R above its 20 EMA, -0.025R below, ranges overlapping]

### 8.2 The buy

- **The hold day.** The first session of the pullback whose low reaches the 9/20 zone (8.1, depth) and which closes at
  or above the 20-day EMA in the upper half of its range. [app: Chart IQ's rule; originator: "wait for confirmation that
  buyers are stepping in"]
- **The trigger.** Buy with a market order when price trades a few cents above the hold day's high, on any of the next
  3 sessions. If it has not triggered by then, the setup is stale. [UCT: the owner's ruling "The entry is supposed to
  trigger OVER previous day high not at mkt close!" and Bracco's one candle to hold then break its high (note 11153);
  originator: Kell's TSLA "Follow through on Friday’s high"; house rule: E57; the 3-session window is my proposal]
- **Option for the owner: an intraday trigger on the hold day itself** (a 30 or 65-minute breakout, or red to green
  over the prior close). [originator: "The best case scenario for me is buying support on the daily chart via a
  breakout on the 65 Min chart."] Recommend it as the advanced version, with the same stop (the day's low).
- **No chase.** Skip if the fill is more than 4% above the trigger. [house rule: C28]
- **Not into earnings.** No new buy if a report is due within 3 sessions. [house rule: B22]
- **Size.** The full position at one price with one stop. No pilot, no add on follow-through. [house rule: A5]

### 8.3 The stop

- A few cents under the hold day's low. [house rule: A3, F62; originator: "will use that low as my stop"]
- If the honest stop is more than about 3% from the entry, size down to keep the risk (A5), and treat more than 5% or
  more than the stock's average daily range as a skip. [house rule: A13 and §2.4 (skip, never tighten the stop);
  originator: Kell's "1-2%" on his own support buys; related setup: Kullamägi, "stop should not be wider than the ATR or
  ADR of the stock" (his breakouts, not the crossback); the 5% is my proposal]
- A gap through the stop: B20 (the first 30-minute low becomes the stop; out if the loss passes 2R). [house rule]

### 8.4 Trims and trail (house defaults; this setup needs nothing special)

- Breakeven once the trade reaches 2R (the rule allows 2R to 5R). [house rule: B19]
- A third at 3R, a third at 5R; take them at the open if it gaps past. [house rule: B19, E51, F63]
- The last third: out on the first close under the 10-day EMA for a fast mover, the 21-day for a steadier one.
  [house rule: E52; originator: "I use the 10/20EMA as a trailing stop."]
- Under 1R after 10 sessions: sell. [house rule: C33]
- A climax run (Bracco's parabolic burst) or an Exhaustion Extension far above the 10-day EMA: sell a big part into it.
  [house rule: C34; originator: Kell sells "into extensions from the Daily 10EMA"]

### 8.5 What invalidates it

- Before the trigger: the hold day's low breaks; or no trigger within 3 sessions; or a close under the 20-day EMA on
  above-average volume. [my proposal; originator: Kell's stop-out "it broke the 20EMA"]
- The 9-day EMA crosses under the 20-day EMA. Kell: "there is no reason to be long the stock". [originator]
- It is not the first test (a Base n' Break, graded lower) or it comes after an Exhaustion Extension (then the next
  stage in Kell's cycle is a Wedge Drop, not a crossback). [originator]
- The stock's own history: repeated failed crossbacks on its chart. [UCT: Bracco, note 12152]
- Earnings within 3 sessions; price under the 200-day; RS under 80. [house rules B22, B26, C31]

### 8.6 The short mirror: the Downside EMA Crossback

Kell defines it exactly (section 3.3), and D47 says our shorts are the longs reversed. Proposed:
- Close **below** the 200-day SMA [house rule: B26], preferably a falling one [my proposal]; RS 20 or less, weakest
  groups [house rule: C31 mirror]; borrow available and cheap [house rule: D48]; a Red market [house rule: D46, F65;
  the rulebook names shorts only for a Red market, so leaving Orange out is my reading]
- A **Wedge Drop** 3 to 20 sessions earlier: a close back under both EMAs after a run above them. [originator: the
  Wedge Drop comes first; the 3 to 20 sessions mirror 8.1 and are my proposal]
- 9-day EMA under the 20-day, both falling; the first rally into the 9/20 zone stalls with a close at or under the
  20-day EMA in the lower half of its range. [originator: "the first retest of the moving averages after the Wedge
  Drop"]
- Short with a market order when price trades a few cents under that rejection day's low; stop a few cents over its
  high. [originator: "Enter the short position when weakness drags the price through the pivot"; house rule: D47]
- Kell's own caution belongs in the lesson: "In more severe market conditions" only, and "Stay in cash" is his default.
  Recommend: teach it, but as a Red-market setup only. [originator]

### 8.7 Best market conditions

- The start of a new market up-cycle: the index has just made its own Wedge Pop or follow-through, and leaders are
  crossing back while the index is still pulling back. [originator: Kell's index note; educator: TraderLion staff step
  1; UCT 2026-04-12 "Now looking for continuation and crossback."]
- Not late in the market's advance. [originator: "later in the cycle", said of the market] Nor late in the stock's own
  advance. [originator: "just like buying late in an uptrend"] In my test the best case was a fresh turn: a stock that
  closed lower than a quarter earlier yet sat above a rising 200-day (+0.107R); a stock already up 20% to 50% over the
  quarter did worst (-0.062R). [my test, section 4.4]
- Not when many crossbacks are triggering and failing on low volume (weak risk appetite). [UCT: Bracco, note 12121]

### 8.8 Common mistakes (each from a source)

1. Buying the touch before buyers show (no hold day, no trigger). [originator; UCT playbook mistake]
2. Calling a late-trend pullback, or a pullback after an Exhaustion Extension, a crossback. [originator: cycle order;
   our own house text makes this mistake, section 5.4]
3. Buying while the 9 is under the 20. [originator]
4. Mixing it up with the Wedge Pop: one close back over the 20 EMA is a pop setting up, not a crossback. [UCT: Bracco,
   note 12254; originator: "Many traders mistakenly believe the Wedge Pop is simply about crossing the moving
   averages."]
5. Buying 3-4% above the averages. [originator]
6. Re-buying the same consolidating stock again and again. [originator: Kell's "2-3x" lesson; house rule: E56]
7. Adding or scaling in. [house rule: A5]
8. Buying under the 200-day or into earnings. [house rules: B26, B22]

## 9. Grading: what makes an A+ versus a B-

E50 says grading is the owner's judgment and no fixed checklist is ours. These are cues from the sources, not a score.

**A+ cues**
- The first crossback after a **powerful Wedge Pop**: a gap, heavy volume, a close at the top of the range. Kell: "Most
  Wedge Pops that lead to new, powerful trends start with gaps."
- A **sideways drift** that lets the averages rise to price, with tight bars. [Kell; staff] (Light volume is the
  sources' cue too, but my test did not support it; section 4.4.)
- A **fresh turn**: the stock corrected over the prior quarter, held above a rising 200-day, and is just starting a new
  up-cycle. [Kell; my test]
- The hold day closes strong at or above the 20 EMA; the stop is 2% or less. [Kell's "1-2%"]
- A **true leader**: it turned before the market, RS near the top, strong earnings and sales growth. [Kell; C31; D40]
- The market is itself starting a new up-cycle (index Wedge Pop or follow-through). [Kell; UCT]
- The weekly chart is in an uptrend and not stretched far above its 10-week line. [staff "Always zoom out to the weekly
  timeframe."; Kell's "extended from its 10 emas on both a daily and weekly timeframe" via Moglen]

**B- cues**
- A second or later test (a Base n' Break), or late in a long advance. [Kell]
- A deep or violent pullback: heavy-volume down days, a close under the 20 EMA, a stop over 3%. [Kell; A13]
- The 9 EMA flattening toward the 20; the 50-day flat. [Kell]
- The market under its 20-day EMA, or crossbacks failing all around. [Kell; UCT]
- A stock whose past crossbacks failed; earnings near. [UCT; B22]

## 13. Examples (from our data)

(Numbered 13 because section 12 is the cold check above; the workflow asked for an "Examples" section.)

**The scan's rule (long only).** Daily bars of the 2,630 names that ever met the house liquidity rule, 2000-01-01 to
2026-11-06, all split-adjusted. A *Wedge Pop* day: close above both the 9 and 20-day EMA, the prior close at or under
the 20 EMA, at least 8 of the 15 prior closes under the 20 EMA, volume above the 50-day average (8.1a); or a *breakout*
day: close over the prior 20-session high on at least 1.2x volume (8.1b). On that day the stock closes above the
200-day SMA, at $10 or more, with $20M or more traded a day (B23, B26). The first session after the pop whose low
reaches the 9 EMA, 3 to 20 sessions later, starts the pullback; price may not close more than 1% under the 20 EMA
before it. The *hold day* is the first session of that pullback (within 4 sessions of the touch) whose low stays within
1% under the 20 EMA, closes at or above the 20 EMA in the upper half of its range, with the 9 EMA above the 20 EMA, the
20 EMA higher than 5 sessions earlier, and a 200-day SMA no lower than 20 sessions earlier; the drift from the post-pop
high to the hold day is 2 to 10 sessions (8.1, 8.2). *Buy* with a market order the first time price trades 2 cents over
the hold-day high within the next 3 sessions (a gap open above it fills at the open; E57), skipped if the fill is more
than 4% over the trigger (C28) or if the hold-day low breaks first. *Stop* 3 cents under the hold-day low; any stop
wider than 5% is skipped (8.3). Outcome: the first of the stop, 3R or 5R in the next 60 sessions, a fixed stop, no
trims, no breakeven move. NOT in the scan (no data or not codable): RS 80+ (C31), the market-colour rule, the
earnings-within-3-sessions rule (B22; checked by hand on the ten examples below), the 1%-of-volume rule, and the
owner's grading of the chart. Script: `_study-resume/scratchpad/setups/14-ema-crossback/scan14.py` (not in the repo).

**Base rate.** 21,461 triggered trades across all 2,630 names (the scan completed in 277 seconds). 28.9% reached 3R
and 17.6% reached 5R before the stop; 82.6% hit the stop at some point in the 60 sessions; the median best excursion
was 1.2R. The rate is steady across decades (2000-09: 29.0% to 3R, 2010-19: 29.3%, 2020-26: 28.3%), so it is not an
artefact of one bull market. Caveat: this is a fixed stop with no trims and no trail, so it measures how often the
pattern travels, not what the house plan (a third at 3R, a third at 5R, trail the rest) would have earned; and it has
no RS or market filter, so it is the raw setup, not the setup as the rulebook trades it. Splits inside the scan:

Read-across to section 8: the Wedge Pop version edges the breakout version (supports recommending 8.1a as the textbook

of section 4.4 item 5's finding on 30 names, so volume stays a grading cue, not a rule; a shallow undercut of the 20 EMA
does not hurt (supports Kell's TSLA undercut in 8.1).
