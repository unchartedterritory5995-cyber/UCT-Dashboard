**The one-paragraph answer.** The Wedge Drop is Oliver Kell's name for the first time a stock that has run up (often
after a blow-off "Exhaustion Extension") trades tight for a few days while its 10- and 20-day EMAs catch up, then
closes back down through both of them. Kell uses it mainly as the signal that the up-cycle is over: sell the long, or,
for aggressive traders, short with a stop just above the recent high. Two facts shape how it fits our account: most
textbook Wedge Drops happen above the 200-day line, where our hard rule B26 forbids shorts, and in my own test on
2008-2026 data, the app's mechanical "first close under both EMAs" version had no edge on its own (section 4.3). The
setup earns its place as a short only in a narrow form: below the 200-day, in a weak market, after a bounce that fails.
(Only the first two of those three filters were measured, and even that edge was thin and depended on the stop; the
"bounce that fails" part is my proposal, drawn from Kell's bear-market diagram and the owner's own short rule.)

## 1. What it is

After a strong up-move ends, a stock stops making progress and trades in a tight range while its 10- and 20-day
exponential moving averages rise to meet it; the Wedge Drop is the day it closes back down through both averages. It
tells a long holder the up-cycle is over, and it gives an aggressive trader a short with a stop just above the recent
high.

**The market psychology.** These points are reasoning drawn from the sources, not measured facts.
- **The buyers run out.** Kell's cycle page: "After the Exhaustion Extension, the price will trade tight into the moving
  averages as there are now fewer new buyers." ([TraderLion, "Cycle Of Price Action", Kell byline, 2024-09-10](https://web.archive.org/web/20250623154156/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/cycle-of-price-action-by-oliver-kell/))
- **Early buyers cash in, late buyers bail.** "After a big rally, stocks often need time to consolidate as early buyers
  take profits and late buyers sell at a loss." ([TraderLion, "Wedge Drop: How to Sell Short", Kell byline, 2024-07-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20250122064336/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/wedge-drop-how-to-sell-short/))
  The people trapped are the ones who bought the blow-off or the tight range near the high: the drop puts them under
  water at once.
- **Then the sellers arrive.** "The Wedge Drop confirms the reversal when the price falls through the moving averages
  with an influx of sellers." (Cycle page) Traders who trail with the 10- and 20-day lines all get their exit signal on
  the same close, which adds supply.
- **The averages flip from floor to ceiling.** "Either way, the moving averages will act as resistance, pushing the
  stock lower." (Wedge Drop page) The next rally into them is Kell's downside EMA Crossback.
- **A failed push is the tell.** "If prices can’t push past the previous day’s highs, especially after several
  attempts, it’s a sign the stock might be rolling over." (Wedge Drop page) Kell's own index example was a lower high:
  "in the bear market we would have these faster rallies that would put in a lower high, drop" and "and then it would
  lead to a down trend." ([Kell, The Weeklies Watch, "Important Week", 2023-02-20](https://weeklieswatch.substack.com/p/important-week))
- **The caution.** A falling stock is a volatile one. "However, be cautious. As stocks trend down, volatility
  increases." (Wedge Drop page) And many Wedge Drops are only pauses: "Depending on market conditions, this drop could
  be
  a temporary pause before a new base forms." (same page) In my measurement, 38% of the app's Wedge Drops closed back
  above both averages within 3 sessions (section 4.3).

## 2. Origin and naming

**Oliver Kell named it, as one stage of his "Cycle of Price Action".** Kell won the 2020 U.S. Investing Championship;
TraderLion's author box says he "achieved an impressive 941% return by winning the 2020 U.S. Investing Championship."
The earliest dated use in his own words that I found is his tweet of 2021-03-06, which lists the whole cycle:
"Reversal Extension-->Wedge Pop-->EMA Crossback-->Base n Break-->(EMA Pullbacks...(more Base n Breaks in strong
trend))-->Exhaustive Extension-->Wedge Drop-->EMA Crossback-->Base N Break (down)-->Reversal Extension"
([X, @OliverKell_, 2021-03-06](https://x.com/OliverKell_/status/1368286886268174337)). That is the whole tweet.
His book (below) is dated earlier, 2021-01-24 in Open Library's record, but it was not read, so I cannot say whether it
uses the term.

**Where else he wrote about it.**
- His tweets (2021).
- His free Substack, "The Weeklies Watch" (2023), where he calls Wedge Drops on the QQQ and names one short trigger.
- A TraderLion webinar deck, "Sell Rules and Stop Losses", March 2024, hosted on his newsletter site, The Swing Report.
- A series of TraderLion articles under his byline (2024). They speak of him in the third person (for example "Oliver
  Kell sees the Wedge Drop"), so they were probably drafted by TraderLion staff and published under his name. I treat
  them as close to the originator, one step below his own posts.
- His book, *Victory in Stock Trading* (2021; Open Library lists the publisher as Shipyard Design Studio, the date
  "Jan 24, 2021" and ISBN 9781639016983). **I did not read it.** I found no legal copy to read. A copy on a document-sharing site appeared in
  search results; it looked like an unauthorized upload and I did not open it. So I cannot say what the book says about
  the Wedge Drop.

**Why "wedge".** Kell never defines the word in what I read. Two readings fit his material:
1. **Price and averages converging.** On the long mirror, TraderLion writes: "As price volatility contracts, the stock
   will find resistance near the tightening moving averages." ([TraderLion, "Wedge Pop: The Money Pattern", Kell byline,
   2024-09-11](https://web.archive.org/web/20250425064619/https://traderlion.com/technical-analysis/chart-patterns/wedge-pop-the-money-pattern/))
   For the drop: "After an Exhaustion Extension, prices often trade in a tight range as moving averages catch up."
   (Wedge Drop page) The "wedge" is the gap between price and the averages closing up.
2. **A rising channel under lower highs.** On his own QQQ chart of 2023-02-16 (in the 2023-02-20 post), Kell drew two
   rising lines under a second lower high and labelled the last bar "Potential for a Wedge Drop" (label read off the
   image; see section 3.2). That one looks like a small rising wedge or bear flag.

Both readings end the same way: the drop is the close back through the 10- and 20-day EMAs.

**Other names.**
- Kell's tweet spells the stage before it "Exhaustive Extension"; the TraderLion pages, and Kell's own 2023
  newsletter ("Exhaustion Extension on 2/2"), say "Exhaustion Extension".
- UCT writes "wedge drop" and "wedge dropped", and calls the weak drift before it "wedging up"
  (section 6).
- The app spells it "Wedge drop" in Chart IQ and "Wedge Drop" in the Model Book.

**Not the same thing (close relatives).**
- **The classical rising wedge.** StockCharts' rising wedge "usually forms over a three to six month period"
  ([ChartSchool, Rising Wedge](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/chart-analysis/chart-patterns/rising-wedge)).
  Bulkowski asks for at least five touches of each line and "3 weeks is the minimum duration, otherwise it's a pennant."
  (thepatternsite.com). Kell's Wedge Drop is a matter of days and is defined by the moving averages, not by trendline
  touches.
- **Weinstein's Stage 4 breakdown.** This is the long-term version, below a falling 30-week average; see
  `10-weinstein-4b.md`.
- **Bracco's Parabolic Short.** This is a climax-day trade that targets the 9-day EMA. The Wedge Drop usually comes
  later,
  when price is already back at the averages (section 6.3).

**A naming error in our app.** The `kell_cycle` detector and the patterns API call Kell's book "Victorious Stock
Operator" (`api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/uct/kell_cycle.py` docstring and narrative;
`api/routers/patterns.py`). I found no book by that title. Open Library lists Kell's book as *Victory in Stock Trading*.

## 3. Every definition I could find, source by source

### 3.1 Oliver Kell on X (originator, primary)

Read through X's public oEmbed service (the official embed text), 2026-11-09.
- **Place in the cycle:** the 2021-03-06 tweet above. After the Wedge Drop he lists an EMA Crossback, a "Base N Break
  (down)" and then a Reversal Extension, so the Wedge Drop starts a down-cycle.
- **A live call, 2021-07-15:** "The leader of move officially triggered a wedge drop today through that 790 pivot area.
  2nd extension from 10EMA/extension from 10 week got out of trade and now confirmation today that exhaustion extension
  valid. Down cycle started." ([X, @OliverKell_, 2021-07-15](https://x.com/OliverKell_/status/1415726370890477568))
  - The trigger is a **pivot level**, not only the averages. He had already sold, at the second extension. The Wedge
    Drop is the "confirmation" that the extension was the top.
  - It was an **intraday** call: the tweet's id decodes to 17:34 UTC (1:34 pm New York time), before the close.
  - The tweet does not name the stock and I could not see its picture. NVDA's split-adjusted lows of 2021-07-08 and
    2021-07-09 were 19.70 and 19.75, which are 788 and 790 before NVDA's 4-for-1 (2021) and 10-for-1 (2024) splits.
    On 2021-07-15 NVDA closed at 18.97, under both EMAs. So NVDA fits, **but the ticker is my inference.**

### 3.4 TraderLion, "Wedge Drop: How to Sell Short" (byline Oliver Kell, published 2024-07-11, modified 2024-10-10)

The live page returns HTTP 403 to our tools, so I read the Internet Archive copy of 2025-01-22. The text speaks of
Kell in the third person.
- **Definition:** "The Wedge Drop happens when the market trades back below the moving averages after a strong rally,
  known as an Exhaustion Extension." And: "Oliver Kell sees the Wedge Drop as a key signal that an uptrend is over."
- **The shape before it:** "After an Exhaustion Extension, prices often trade in a tight range as moving averages catch
  up." A variant with no blow-off: "Sometimes, you won’t see a big Exhaustion Extension, but the price will trade
  sideways and eventually lose momentum."
- **Warning signs listed:** "Bearish reversal candlesticks", "Increased selling volume (distribution)" and "Decreased
  buying volume (accumulation)". Plus the failed push past the prior day's high (section 1).
- **Trigger and meaning:** "When the price drops below those averages, it signals the stock has likely peaked or needs
  time to reset."
- **Long holders:** "If you’re sitting on gains from an earlier entry, use the Wedge Drop as a signal to protect profits
  and reduce exposure."
- **Cash or short:** "After a Wedge Drop, it can take weeks or even months for the market to reset." Then: "For more
  aggressive traders, the Wedge Drop can also be an opportunity to go short."
- **Short entry and stop:** "As the price breaks below moving averages with increased selling pressure, you can enter a
  short position with a stop just above the recent high."
- **Short exit:** "If the stock regains its moving averages and pushes higher, exit the short position."
- **When not to short it:** "It’s also best to avoid shorting early in new bull markets or strong uptrending stocks."
- **Indexes and leaders:** "When several leaders fall below their moving averages in a Wedge Drop pattern, it’s a sign
  to get defensive." And: "So when an index like the S&P 500 or Nasdaq forms a Wedge Drop, it’s a warning that the
  market as a whole is weakening."
- **FAQ on stops:** "If you’re more aggressive, shorting can be a good play, but you’ll need to set tight stop-losses
  and be aware of rising volatility as the stock trends lower."
- **How big a top:** "Yes, a Wedge Drop can confirm either an intermediate or a long-term top."
- **Worked examples** (captions quoted; dates read off the charts and checked against our bars in section 8.11):
  - AAPL 2023: "Distribution volume set in as the price gapped below the moving averages requiring time for a new base
    to develop."
  - ANF 2024: "This “frozen rope” advance was ended by a Wedge Drop when the price broke below both moving averages on
    increased volume."
  - NVDA 2023: "In the summer of 2023, NVDA eventually saw a Wedge Drop after three multiple extensions." The NVDA
    chart is also labelled "Wide And Loose Price Action" (read off the image). The label sits under the July-August
    2023 range, and the chart still marks a Wedge Drop out of that range (early August) and a second one in early
    September. So the label describes the stall; it does not disqualify the drop.
  - Uncaptioned charts: a schematic drawing, META 2021, TSLA 2023 (one chart, two Wedge Drops marked: early March and
    late July), UPST 2021 (late October), QQQ 2023 (one chart, two marked: early August and mid-September).

### 3.5 TraderLion, "Cycle Of Price Action" (byline Oliver Kell, 2024-09-10)

Archive copy of 2025-06-23.
- "After the Exhaustion Extension, the price will trade tight into the moving averages as there are now fewer new
  buyers."
- "The Wedge Drop confirms the reversal when the price falls through the moving averages with an influx of sellers."
- "The Price Cycle is confirmed and a new downtrend begins."
- The no-blow-off variant again: "In some cases, stocks will stop advancing without exhaustion, and we will see a

- **The averages:** "10 and 20-day exponential moving averages guide us through the intermediate-term trend". The 50-
  and 200-day simple averages are for the longer trend.
- **Time frames:** "Hourly and 15-minute charts are for timing entries."
- **What comes next:** "Like an EMA Crossback to the upside, an EMA Crossback to the downside will retest the moving
  averages but is now met with more resistance, pushing the stock lower."
- **For long-only traders:** "If a stock tops and starts to decline, step aside and let the cycle reset before jumping
  back in."
- **The schematic** (read off the image): the price line peaks at "Exhaustion Extension", falls back to about the 10-day
  EMA, makes a small lower high, then crosses down through both EMAs at "Wedge Drop". In this drawing, the stall before

- **Caution:** "When a stock is below the declining moving averages it is best avoided as finding the bottom can be

### 3.11 The classical rising wedge (a different pattern, for contrast)

- **StockCharts ChartSchool** (undated): "The Rising Wedge is a bearish pattern that begins wide at the bottom and
  contracts as prices move higher and the trading range narrows." It takes at least two reaction highs and two reaction
  lows. It "usually forms over a three to six month period". "Bearish confirmation of the pattern does not come until
  the
  support line is broken in a convincing fashion." "Ideally, volume will decline as prices rise and the wedge evolves."
  It gives no target: "There are no measuring techniques to estimate the decline".
- **Thomas Bulkowski**, thepatternsite.com, "Bulkowski on Rising Wedges": "Price bounces between two up-sloping and
  converging trendlines." "Price should touch (at a minor high or minor low) each trendline at least five times to
  outline a good pattern." "3 weeks is the minimum duration, otherwise it's a pennant." **The site's notice says it
  "shall not be used to train artificial intelligence (AI) systems".** I read it through a fetch tool (plain curl was
  refused, HTTP 406) and use only short quotes with a link.

### 3.15 Where the sources disagree

1. **Which averages, and one or both.** The TraderLion pages say "the moving averages" and name the 10- and 20-day EMAs.
   Kell's own QQQ call of 2/16-2/17 came while price was still above the 20-day (section 3.2), and so did the dated AMD
   exit of 2024-01-02 (section 3.7). The ANF caption says "broke below both moving averages". The Model Book uses the 9- and
   20-day. Our rulebook trails on the 10- or 21-day (E52). The app's Chart IQ code requires a close under both the 10
   and 20.
2. **Where the stall sits.** Kell's drawings and TraderLion's examples (META, AAPL, ANF) show price stalling on or just
   above rising averages. The Model Book says it wedges up "beneath the averages", which is the next stage in Kell's
   cycle (the downside EMA Crossback), not the Wedge Drop.
3. **What comes first.** Most Kell examples follow an Exhaustion Extension. Both TraderLion pages allow a version with
   no
   blow-off. The Model Book writes "after a topping move or Reversal Extension". In Kell's cycle a Reversal Extension is
   the capitulation low at the bottom, so that phrase looks like a mirroring error.
4. **Trigger: a level or the averages.** Kell's tweets and his TSLA note use a price level ("790 pivot", "166.50").
   TraderLion and our app use the moving averages. The Wedge Pop page says not to treat it as a mere average cross.
5. **Stop.** TraderLion: "just above the recent high" (which can be the top of the stall, well above the trigger day).
   Its FAQ: "tight stop-losses". The Model Book: above the wedge high. Our house default: a few cents above the day's
   high (mirrored F62).
6. **Role.** For Kell it is first a sell signal and a reason to sit in cash; shorting is for "more aggressive traders".
   Our Model Book files it as a short setup.
7. **Pattern size.** The classical rising wedge is weeks to months. Kell's Wedge Drop is a few days.

## 5. Our app: what the scanners and pages actually say

There are four places in the code that define a Wedge Drop. They do not agree.

### 5.1 `api/services/chart_iq/engine/v44_read.py`, `cycle_events` (Chart IQ, and the Study bank's example finder)

This is the main rule. It is used by the Chart IQ pipeline (`api/services/chart_iq/pipeline.py`), by
`v4_compose.py`, and by the Study bank generator (`scripts/study/bank_v2/scan_chartiq.py`, `gen_chartiq.py`).

**The rule.** A bar is labelled "Wedge drop" when all of these hold:
- at least 8 of the prior 10 closes were above the 20-day EMA;
- the close is under both the 10- and 20-day EMAs;
- the close is in the bottom 40% of the day's range;
- the close is under the lows of the prior 3 sessions;
- the previous cycle label was not already "Wedge drop" or "Reversal extension".

It is the last branch of an if-else chain, so a bar that matches an earlier label (for example Wedge pop or EMA
crossback) never becomes a Wedge drop. The EMA here is seeded with the first close, not a 10-day average; with a long
history the difference is negligible. Its note to the member reads "next: stay out; wait for a reversal extension and a
new wedge pop" (`NEXT['Wedge drop']`).

**What it does not test:**
- no high or extension before it (only 6.2% of its 99,695 events came straight after the app's own "Exhaustion
  extension" label; the most common earlier label was "EMA crossback", 44,717);
- no tight stall or pivot;
- no volume;
- no 200-day or market test.

The median event was 7.3% off its 60-day high, and 70% sat above the 200-day (my measurement).

**Against Kell's marked examples.** It fired on the marked day, or within a day of it (as I read the chart arrows), for:
- META 2021-09-17, AAPL 2023-08-03, QQQ 2023-08-02, QQQ 2023-09-15, NVDA 2023-08-02, TSLA 2023-07-20, ANF 2024-03-08,
  TMDX 2023-08-02 and DECK 2021-11-26.

It also fires on DECK 2021-09-24 (the first DECK label, found in checking) and on Kell's NVDA 2021-07-15.

It missed:
- TSLA 2023-03-02, NVDA 2023-09-07 and AMD (no Wedge drop label on 2024-01-02, the source's date, or on 01-03, the
  first close under both);
- Kell's own QQQ call of 2023-02-16/17 (it fired on 2023-02-21 instead).

(Re-run in checking with the app's code on full history: same results.)

### 5.2 `v44_read.wedge_drop()`, the quality gate Chart IQ uses before drawing a Wedge Drop (`v4_compose.py`)

**What it requires:**
- trigger-day volume at least 1.2x the 50-day average;
- a run of at least 12% from the 30-day low into the drop;
- the 20-day EMA up at least 2.5% over 10 days;
- price at least 1.5 ATR over the 20-day EMA at some point in the prior 15 days;
- **hindsight:** no close over the drop-day high or over the 20-day EMA in the next 2 sessions, nothing more than 2%
  over the 20-day EMA in the next 3, and no reversal off the drop-day low in the next 2.

The chart composer (`v4_compose.py`) also drops a Wedge Drop if a big gap up from the prior 30 sessions is still
holding under it, if price later closes back over the drop-day high, or if price is back over the 20-day EMA with no
close under the 50-day since the drop.

**Problems.**
- The hindsight checks are fine for picking teaching examples. They mean the gate cannot run live on the trigger day.
- **It rejects the textbook form.** The 12% run and 2.5% slope rules throw out Kell's tight-at-the-highs Wedge Drops,
  because the averages flatten while price stalls. That flattening is exactly the "moving averages catch up" Kell
  describes.
- Of the marked examples tested, it passed only TMDX 2023-08-02 and Kell's own NVDA 2021-07-15 (the writer's list left
  NVDA 2021 out; it passes when re-run in checking), plus UCT's MSTR 2025-05-23 and SMH 2026-07-02. It rejected META,
  AAPL, QQQ (2023-08-02 and 2023-09-15), NVDA 2023-08-02, ANF, TSLA 2023-07-20, DECK (both dates) and Kell's QQQ of
  February 2023. META, for example, had risen only 6.3% from its 30-day low, and its 20-day EMA had risen only 1.2% in
  10 days.

### 5.3 `api/services/chart_iq/engine/v3.py` (an older Chart IQ setup list, still called by `pipeline.py`)

**The rule:**
- close under the 50-day SMA;
- close under both the 9- and 21-day EMAs, after a prior close at or over the lower of the two;
- at least 5 of the prior 8 closes over the 21-day EMA;
- relative volume 1.3 or more.

Its text reads "Wedge drop: lost the 9/21 EMA on volume after riding them."

**Problem:** the 50-day condition excludes most of Kell's examples, which were still above the 50-day on the drop day.
META 2021-09-17 closed at 364.72 with the 50-day at 363.28; AAPL 2023-08-03 at 191.17 against 186.88; QQQ 2023-08-02
at 374.39 against 364.92. It also uses 9/21, not Kell's 10/20.

### 5.4 `api/services/pattern_engine/detectors/uct/kell_cycle.py` (the "Kell Cycle" stage classifier, registered in `api/routers/patterns.py`)

**How it decides Stage 4 ("Wedge Drop"):**
- it labels every chart with one of five stages, every time;
- Stage 4 fires when any 10-bar gain of 25% or more appears anywhere in the bars passed and the last 5 closes are flat
  or down (and Stages 3 and 1 did not match first);
- its levels: entry 0.1% under the 15-bar low, stop 1% over the 15-bar high (often far wider than 3%).

**Problems:**
1. **The cycle order is wrong.** Its docstring runs Reversal Extension, Wedge Pop, Exhaustion Extension, Wedge Drop,
   Base & Breakout. It leaves out the EMA Crossback and puts a long "Base & Breakout" after the Wedge Drop. Kell's own
   tweet puts an EMA Crossback and a "Base N Break (down)" after it.
2. **It carries no information.** The app's own screener notes (`api/services/screener/pattern_join.py`) measured it
   firing on 100% of covered symbols.
3. **It misreads Kell's own examples.** Run on the 250 bars up to each marked day (empty context), it said "Stage 5
   (Base & Breakout)" for META, AAPL, QQQ (twice), DECK, QQQ 2023-02-21 and Kell's NVDA 2021-07-15. It said "Stage 1"
   for ANF and "Stage 4" only for NVDA 2023-08-02, TSLA 2023-07-20, TMDX and AMD. (Re-run in checking: same.)
4. **The book title is wrong** ("Victorious Stock Operator", section 2).
5. **It puts quotes in Kell's mouth that I could not find in any Kell source I read.** Examples:
   - "size the Wedge Drop trade larger than your Wedge Pop because the prior Exhaustion gives you better confirmation
     that the old trend is dead."
   - "when you see vertical, parabolic action on huge volume, the easy money has already been made."
   - "parabolic moves can stay parabolic longer than you can stay solvent"
   - "do nothing until the wedge pop sets up."
   - "size the Wedge Pop trade aggressively because the structure tells you exactly where you are wrong."

   These should be treated as invented until someone shows the source.

### 5.6 The Model Book card, the playbook and the journal tag

**The card** (`app/src/pages/modelbook/setupCatalog.js`):
- Family "Momentum & Trend", direction "short", averages [9, 20].
- Its essence: "price bounces and wedges upward beneath the averages" and "then drops through them, starting the
  down-cycle".
- The glyph draws a rally, two sharp down days, three small up days, then two large down days. That is close to Kell's
  schematic.

**The playbook** (`app/src/pages/modelbook/setupPlaybooks.js`):
- It says price "bounces and coils into a rising wedge", "often back up to the underside of the moving averages",
  before "losing the averages and confirming a new down-cycle".
- Context: "after a topping move or Reversal Extension".
- Criteria: "Volume dries up through the wedge."
- Entry: "Break of the wedge’s lower trendline together with the loss of the 9/20-EMA".
- Stop: "Above the wedge / consolidation high."
- Exit: "Trail the 9/20-EMA as the new downtrend develops; cover into weakness on reward-to-risk."
- Mistakes: "Entering inside the wedge before the drop confirms." and "Ignoring whether volume actually dried up through
  the coil."

**Where it came from:** commit 3aa9b1eeaf (2026-06-17), whose message says it "Inverts every section of Wedge Pop". It
is a machine mirror of the Wedge Pop write-up, not the owner's words.

**The journal tag:**
- `app/src/constants/setupGroups.js` lists 'Wedge Drop' in the Swing group, next to 'Wedge Pop'.
- `app/src/pages/journal-2-0/lib/setupTagMap.js` maps it to no plan template (`'Wedge Drop': null`).

**What looks wrong in the card and playbook (most serious first):**
1. **"Beneath the averages" / "the underside of the moving averages".** You cannot lose averages you are already
   under. In Kell's drawings and TraderLion's examples, price stalls on or just above rising averages, then closes
   through them. A bounce into the underside of falling averages is Kell's **downside EMA Crossback**, the next stage
   (see `14-ema-crossback.md`).
2. **"after a topping move or Reversal Extension".** A Reversal Extension is Kell's bottom (capitulation). The top-side
   twin is the Exhaustion Extension. This looks like an error from mirroring the Wedge Pop text.
3. **9/20 EMA.** Kell uses 10/20. Our trail rule uses the 10- or 21-day (E52).
4. **"Volume dries up through the wedge."** TraderLion lists "Decreased buying volume (accumulation)" and "Increased
   selling volume (distribution)". That is close (quiet bounces, heavy down days), but "dries up" alone loses the
   heavy-selling half.
5. **No 200-day test and no market test**, although our hard rule B26 governs every short.

**The Study glossary drafts** (`docs/study/bank/recall.glossary.json`, items si_7b05a3f0b4d2 and si_8a53969674e8, both
draft) repeat point 1: "A Wedge Drop is the mirror after a top: a rising wedge under the averages, then a drop that
loses them." They were re-keyed to the playbook in the 2026-10-21 audit
(`docs/study/corrections/recall.glossary.jsonl`). The audit treated the playbook as the firm's text; the commit history
shows it is a machine mirror.

**The course plan** (`docs/study/UNITS-method.md`, SET.17) plans to source losing examples from "classical rising_wedge
detections that failed (nearest detector)". The classical rising wedge is a different, longer pattern (section 3.11),
so those examples would teach the wrong shape.

## 8. Proposed rules for our course

Two jobs, one name. **Job 1, the sell signal:** every long holder should know it. **Job 2, the short:** only in the
narrow form our rules allow. The rules below say which job each line serves.

### 8.1 What it is (the setup, before the trigger)

1. **Location for a short:** the close is under the 200-day SMA. **[house rule B26]** Prefer a falling 200-day.
   **[my proposal]** For Job 1 (selling a long) there is no 200-day test. **[originator]**
2. **A prior up-move:** at least 8 of the last 10 closes were above the 20-day EMA. **[my proposal, the app's own
   threshold]**
   - For a short, this is a bounce inside a downtrend: Kell's "faster rallies that would put in a lower high".
     **[originator]**
   - It also matches the owner's "decent pop back up and rejection coinciding with the 50SMA or 20EMA". **[UCT]**
3. **The top:** at least one of these:
   - an extension: a close at least 2.5 ATR over the 20-day EMA in the last 20 sessions, or about 4% or more over the
     10-day for an index **[originator for the idea; the numbers are my proposal, the 4% from Kell's QQQ note]**;
   - a lower high: the bounce peaks under the prior high **[originator]**;
   - a failed push past the prior day's high **[originator]**.
4. **The stall (the "wedge"):** 3 to 10 sessions after the top in which:
   - no close is more than 1 ATR from the 10-day EMA;
   - no new high is made in the last 3 sessions;
   - the up days come on lighter volume than the down days.

   **[originator for the shape: "tight range as moving averages catch up", "Decreased buying volume"; the numbers are
   my proposal]** Whether wide, loose action should count is **my proposal**, not a source rule: TraderLion's NVDA
   chart labels "Wide And Loose Price Action" and still marks a Wedge Drop out of it (section 3.4). Grade it down
   (section 9) rather than exclude it.
5. **Liquidity:** price $10 or more and $20 million a day or more **[house rule B23]**; easy to borrow **[house rule
   D48]**.

### 8.2 The trigger and the order

6. **Trigger:** the first daily close under **both** the 10- and 20-day EMAs. **[originator and Deepvue for "below the
   moving averages" (TraderLion's Wedge Drop and Cycle pages, ANF "broke below both moving averages"); "both" and "on a
   daily close" are my proposal]** Kell's own dated calls were looser: QQQ 2023-02-16/17 and AMD 2024-01-02 came on
   closes under the 10-day only, and the NVDA tweet of 2021-07-15 was posted mid-session. It must also:
   - close in the lower 40% of the day's range **[my proposal, the app's threshold]**;
   - close under the low of the stall (the pivot) **[originator for a price-level trigger: Kell's "790 pivot" and
     "166.50" levels; using the stall low is my proposal, mirrored from the Wedge Pop's "Consolidation sets the level
     to trade against"]**.
7. **Volume:** trigger-day volume at least the 50-day average; 1.5x or more, or a gap down, is the strong form **[originator:
   "increased selling pressure", "influx of sellers", ANF "on increased volume"; also TraderLion staff's Exhaustion page,
   "Increased Selling Volume"; the numbers are my proposal]**. (Changed in checking from [source consensus]: the
   first three quotes come from two pages under Kell's byline, which this dossier tags [originator], and Deepvue says
   nothing on volume.) My test found that heavy volume raised the win rate but not the average R, so volume is a
   grading cue, not a gate.
8. **Order:** a market order in the last 15 minutes once the close under both averages is clear, or at the next open
   **[house rule E57; my proposal for the timing]**. Advanced option: Kell times on the 65-minute chart ("Try to get In
   on the 65 minute", from his slide on long breakouts; the Cycle page: "Hourly and 15-minute charts are for timing
   entries."), so an intraday break of the stall low counts only once the daily close confirms it **[originator;
   my proposal]**.
9. **No chase:** do not short more than 4% under the trigger level **[house rule C28 mirrored]**. Skip a short more
   than 10% under its 10-day average **[house rule C29 mirrored]**.

### 8.3 The stop

10. A few cents above the trigger day's high, or above the higher of the trigger day and the day before **[house rules
    A3 and F62 mirrored; TraderLion: "a stop just above the recent high"]**.
    - If that is more than about 3% away, skip or take the smaller size the rules give **[house rules A13, A5]**.
    - Do not use the 15-bar high (the app's `kell_cycle` level) or the top of a wide stall unless it fits 3%
      **[my proposal]**.
11. Gap over the stop: the first 30-minute high becomes the stop; cover if the loss passes 2R **[house rule B20
    mirrored]**.

### 8.4 Trims and trail

12. **House defaults:**
    - stop to breakeven at 2R to 5R;
    - cover a third at 3R and a third at 5R; take a trim at the open if it gaps past the level (F63);
    - trail the last third: cover on a close back above the 10-day EMA (fast movers) or the 21-day (steadier)
      **[house rules B19, E51, E52, F63 mirrored]**.
13. **What Kell expects next:** a rally into the falling averages (the downside EMA Crossback), then lower. If you are
    still short, a rally that stalls under the falling 10- and 20-day is a place to hold, not to panic **[originator
    for the expected crossback rally; "hold, not panic" is my proposal, and the house trail in item 12 still covers on
    a close back above the 10- or 21-day]**.
    Do not add there; that is a new trade with its own stop **[house rules A5, B21]**.

### 8.5 What invalidates it

14. A close back above both the 10- and 20-day EMAs: "If the stock regains its moving averages and pushes higher, exit
    the short position." **[originator]** In my test, 38% of the app's events did this within 3 sessions **[my
    measurement]**.
15. A close above the trigger day's high (the stop) **[house rule]**.
16. Under 1R of profit after 10 sessions: cover **[house rule C33]**.
17. For the long holder, the mirror: a Wedge Drop that is reclaimed within a few sessions is a shakeout. The stock can
    be bought again only on a new setup (Kell re-bought MU at the next EMA Crossback) **[originator; house rule B21]**.

### 8.6 The short mirror

18. The Wedge Drop **is** the short mirror of the Wedge Pop **[originator; house rule D47]**. Its own long mirror,
    read as
    an exit, is to sell the long on the Wedge Drop: "allow the wedge drop to protect you and take you out of the
    trade." **[originator]**

### 8.7 Best market conditions

19. **For the short:** the index itself in a down cycle (QQQ under its 50-day and its 20-day EMA). The market reading is
    Orange or Red **[house rules D46, F65; my measurement for QQQ under its 50-day, the only regime with an edge; the
    20-day EMA part was not measured and is my proposal]**.
20. The stock's group is weak and the stock lags the market **[house rule C31 mirrored]**.
21. **Avoid:** "early in new bull markets or strong uptrending stocks" **[originator]**. Also avoid shorting late in a
    fall: "Be careful not to short too late in the downtrend, just like buying late in an uptrend." **[originator,
    Downside EMA Crossback page]**
22. **For the exit (Job 1):** any market. The index's own Wedge Drop is a reason to cut exposure: "So when an index like
    the S&P 500 or Nasdaq forms a Wedge Drop, it’s a warning that the market as a whole is weakening." **[originator]**

### 8.8 Common mistakes (each from a source)

- **Shorting a leader at its highs** because it lost the 10-day once. It breaks B26, and the sources warn against strong
  uptrends. **[originator; house rule]**
- **Shorting the blow-off itself.** That is the Parabolic Short, a different setup with its own checklist. **[UCT,
  Bracco]**
- **Shorting inside the stall before the close confirms.** **[Model Book; the Wedge Pop mirror]**
- **Treating a cross of the averages as the whole setup.** "Many traders mistakenly believe the Wedge Pop is simply
  about crossing the moving averages." The same holds for the drop. **[originator]**
- **A stop too tight in a volatile name.** META 2022-01-05 stopped out at -1R, then fell 27% within 20 sessions. The
  source warns that "As stocks trend down, volatility increases." **[my measurement; originator]**
- **Late to the party:** "Be careful not to short too late in the downtrend". **[originator]**
- **Holding a long through it:** "If you don’t sell the second extension or it doesn’t materialize allow the wedge drop
  to protect you". The drop is the last exit, not a reason to hope. **[originator]**
- **Adding at the crossback** (A5 says no). **[house rule]**

## 9. Grading: what makes an A+ versus a B-

The owner ruled that grading is discretionary (E50). These are cues from the sources, not a checklist.

**A+ (as a house short)**
- Under a falling 200-day, after a bounce into the 50-day or 20-day that makes a lower high. This matches the owner's
  "pop back up and rejection" and Kell's bear-market diagram.
- The index in a down cycle (QQQ under its 50-day); the stock in a weak group.
- A tight stall of 3 to 10 days hugging the 10-day EMA, with quiet up days ("Decreased buying volume").
- The trigger is a wide red bar or a gap down that closes near its low, under both averages and under the stall low, on
  at least 1.5x volume ("increased selling pressure").
- The stop over the day's high is within 3%.
- Not late in the fall: "Be careful not to short too late in the downtrend".

**A or B**
- Most of the above, but one of: average volume, the 20-day lost a day after the 10, a stop of 3-4%, or the index only
  flat.

**B- or lower (size down per D38 and E49, or pass)**
- Wide, loose action before the drop (my proposal as a grading cue; TraderLion's NVDA chart labels "Wide And Loose
  Price Action" and still marks a Wedge Drop out of it).
- A close under the 10-day only (UCT's "wedge drop ish"), as a short. As an exit for a long it is enough: Kell's own
  QQQ 2023-02-16 call and AMD 2024-01-02 exit were of this kind.
- A low-volume drop (RSP 2026-08-31, 0.76x).
- A stop over 3%.
- An index in an uptrend.
- A leader with strong RS.
- A stock already far below its averages.
- Above the 200-day: **not a short at all** under B26. It is graded only as a sell signal for a long.

**The rule the scan used (section 8 as a house short).** Daily bars, the 2,630 names that ever met the liquidity rule.
Trigger day t: the close is under both the 10- and 20-day EMAs and the day before was not; the close is in the lower 40%
of the day's range; the close is under the 200-day SMA (B26); the price is $10 or more and the 50-day dollar volume $20
million or more (B23); at least 8 of the 10 closes before t were above the 20-day EMA (the prior bounce); QQQ closed
under its own 50-day SMA that day (8.7, item 19; the scan therefore starts 1999-04, when QQQ's bars allow it). The top is
the highest high of the 20 sessions before t; the stall is the 3 to 10 sessions between the top and t, with every close
within 1 ATR(14) of the 10-day EMA, no high in the last 3 sessions at or above the top, and the stall's up days on lighter
average volume than its down days. The top must be an extension (a close at least 2.5 ATR over the 20-day EMA inside
those 20 sessions), a lower high (the top is under the highest high of the 60 sessions before it) or a failed push (the
top day cleared the prior day's high and closed back under it). The close must be under the stall low (the pivot), not
more than 4% under it (C28 mirrored) and not more than 10% under the 10-day EMA (C29 mirrored). Entry: a market order at
the trigger day's close (E57). Stop: $0.03 over the higher of the trigger day's high and the day before; if that is over
3% away, $0.03 over the trigger day's high; if still over 3%, no trade (A13). Volume on the trigger day is recorded, not
required (8.2, item 7). Outcome: a fixed stop, no trims, no breakeven move, walked forward up to 60 sessions; "best R" is
the deepest point reached before the stop, so it is not a realised result.

**Base rate.** 618 hits across all 2,630 names (the scan finished in 304 s, nothing skipped), 1999-04 to 2026-11.
**24.3% reached 3R and 14.7% reached 5R before the stop**; 542 of 618 (87.7%) were stopped within 60 sessions; the mean
best R before the stop was 2.3. 37.9% closed back above both EMAs within 3 sessions, the same shakeout rate section 4.3
found for the app's own events (38%). The busiest year was 2022 (92 hits). Splits, for grading, not as rules: lower-high
tops 483 hits, 25.3% to 3R; extension tops 120 hits, 15.0%; failed-push-only tops 58 hits, 25.9%; trigger volume 1.5x or
more 39 hits, 25.6% (no better than the rest, as section 4.3 also found); a falling 200-day 437 hits, 26.5%. Caveats:
fixed stop and no trims (the house trade trims at 3R and 5R and trails the rest, so it keeps less of a 14R move and
loses less on a stop moved to breakeven); best-R excursion, not P&L; some same-day clusters (2011-08-02, 2022-06-08 to
06-10) are one market event counted many times; the QQQ regime filter was applied to every hit, so the rate says nothing
about Wedge Drops in a strong market.

**What the four losses have in common.** Three of the four were stopped inside two sessions by a close back above
both EMAs, the 38% shakeout that section 4.3 and section 13 measured; the loss was 1R each time because the stop sat
over the 2-day high and the stop was honoured. The other one (DXCM) was stopped by a wide-stall name whose first bounce
exceeded a 2.4% stop and then fell 32%. Two of the four came where the setup is weakest: early in a new bull market
(GOOGL, three weeks after the December 2018 low) and in a stock that was not weak (DIS, back above both averages in two
sessions and 11.6% higher a month later). For the member: the Wedge Drop is a one-day event that the next day confirms
or cancels; the close back above both averages is the exit, and a second short needs a new stall and a new close under
both, as a new trade.
