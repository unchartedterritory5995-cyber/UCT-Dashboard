Study, the full plan for review

Every piece of the Study product in one place, in plain language, numbered so we can walk it section by section and confirm or change each one. This page mirrors the design document in the repo and the decisions you made on 17 October 2026.

R1The vision

Study is a Duolingo for stocks, built on our own charts, data and method. A member opens Courses, sees today's ten questions, and learns by doing: naming a pattern on a real chart with the ticker hidden, placing a stop, calling the market on a real past date, sizing a trade, taking or passing a real Sunday Scans call, reading a real earnings quarter. Every answer comes back with the right answer, where it came from, what happened next, and one line of lesson in our voice.

Two curricula share one map. Foundations takes someone with a brokerage account and a little experience all the way through the general education a trader needs: market basics, chart reading, candlesticks, patterns, fundamentals, options to the advanced level, market history and macro, psychology, risk, orders, styles and process. The UCT Method drills the firm's own six-station system on the firm's own records.

It is built for people with short attention spans without thinning the content: short questions, quick sessions, streaks and XP, but every item carries real teaching and every chart is real.

What it is not

  • Not a trading game. Nothing is scored on money.
  • Not a chatbot. No AI runs inside the product at launch; every word is written in advance and reviewed by you. AI may be added later only where it is clearly good and needed.
  • Not a replacement for the course videos. Study links to the lessons and the lessons link back.
Confirmed 17 Oct. Your note: the objective is elite-trader education across simple and complex technical, fundamental, structural and process skills; AI allowed where it earns its place.

R6How a unit works

Intro (30-60 s video + one line)→ Questions, one at a time→ Answer + where it came from + one-line lesson→ End card: accuracy, XP, what to review next
  • Questions only. No reading pages. The teaching happens in the intro and in every reveal.
  • Three levels, three rungs each (Learn 1-3, Practise 1-3, Prove 1-3). Rung 1 recognises the idea; rung 9 applies it on a real chart or real numbers. Gold at rung 9.
  • Test out on any unit: one pass at the top rung skips it.
  • Formats: multiple choice, true/false, fill in the blank, tap the chart, scenario stories. Idealized drawings for teaching, real charts for testing.
  • Charts in drills are the real interactive chart, locked down: ticker hidden, live data off, zoom and crosshair on.
  • The reveal always shows the real ticker and dates.
Confirmed 17 Oct. Your note: "what are they about" — the named unit list for every track is the first Phase 0 file you review.

R7Voice

Your instruction: it must sound like us, a person, a friend and a mentor teaching, not AI slop. The rules every authoring session follows:

  • Write as a trader talking to a friend across the desk. Short sentences. "The market", "the stock", "your stop".
  • No internal shorthand ("regime", "the dial", "tape", "exposure rating") in a question until that idea has been taught in a unit, and then in plain words alongside it: "the firm's market-health score, the 0-150 number on the dashboard".
  • Reveals speak in first person as the mentor: "Here is what I'd do." No cartoon character, no mascot.
  • No hype, no "great job!", no emoji. A right answer gets a short, real line on why it is right.
  • Every number in a reveal must be in the question's own facts. The import step rejects anything else.
Confirmed 17 Oct. Your note: not too AI, but structured and easy to understand. Rule added: one idea per question, the same sentence shapes through a unit, the question before the chart, choices short enough to scan on a phone.

R9The daily loop and rewards

Today's Study

Ten questions, about five minutes, mostly from the member's current track with two or three from elsewhere. Finishing it keeps the streak. Unlimited extra practice after.

Streaks

Every calendar day counts. One free miss per week so a single busy day does not wipe a long run.

XP and levels

XP per correct answer, scaled by difficulty, with bonuses for refreshing a stale skill and for streak days. Daily goal selectable: 20, 50 or 100.

Mastery and freshness

Each skill shows Learning, Practised, Mastered, or Gold. A badge is never taken away. A skill shows Fresh or Needs a refresh, and refreshers pay bonus XP.

Badges

Few and tiered: one per module checkpoint (14), setup rep milestones (20 and 40 reps on one setup), streak milestones (7, 30, 100 days), and Gold on a skill. One line of copy and a gold icon each.

Leaderboard

Weekly XP with accuracy shown beside it, on by default with an opt-out, resets Sunday night. No discussion threads.

Feel

Subtle sound and haptics on right and wrong (on by default on phones, off on desktop, one toggle). Combo counter at 5 and 10 in a row. End-of-session card. Animated trail map. Cool, not over the top.

Reminders

Push notification plus the in-app bell, opt-in, at a time the member picks, once a day.

Confirmed 17 Oct. Your note: simple and easy to navigate.

R10The house sizing rule

From our workshop. Sizing is taught as a rule derived from the stop, not as a fixed grid.

  • Risk per trade: 0.25% to 2% of the account, chosen by setup quality, your recent performance, market conditions and your edge. 2% is the hard ceiling. Flat-risk sizing (the same percent on every trade) is taught as a valid alternative, not a mistake.
  • Stop first. The low of the day, or the prior day's low, is the default. The structure (pivot area, pullback low) and ATR are the supporting methods. If the honest stop does not fit the risk budget, skip; never tighten the stop.
  • Size from the stop. Position % = risk % ÷ stop distance %. A 1% stop at half size is 0.5% risk. A 10% stop at 10% size is 1% risk.
  • Portfolio layer. Total open risk under 10% of the account; 5 to 10 positions for a momentum swing book; progressive exposure: pilot, half, full, add only after the last step paid.
  • The app's existing ceiling table becomes a cap the Method mentions once, not the lesson. The printed sizing-gate card is regenerated from this rule.
Confirmed 17 Oct.

R11How content gets made and reviewed

Generate drafts from our data→ Write questions and lessons in our voice→ Check for near-duplicates→ You review every item, one by one→ Fixes logged as training data→ Commit and import
  • No AI in the product. Everything is written in scheduled sessions here on the subscription, reviewed, imported. No API spend at runtime.
  • No model ever writes a market fact. Charts, labels, levels and outcomes come from stored bars, the pattern engine, your labels, or the firm's records. History numbers are computed from our own data. Fundamentals use real quarters frozen into the question.
  • Nightly chart items from the pattern engine get a rule-filled lesson ("the engine called this a VCP: contractions 24%, 11%, 5%, pivot $41.20; target hit in 14 bars") and wait in your review queue. Nothing goes live unreviewed.
  • Never repetitive. No two questions test the same fact the same way; each unit is diffed against the whole bank before review.
  • Targets: 5,000+ Method items and 2,500+ Foundations items before launch, both built in parallel, interleaved weekly. Then a weekly batch forever.
  • Your corrections are training data. Every fix and reject is stored with its reason, exported for the next authoring session, and available to Compass later.
Confirmed 17 Oct. Your note: all real content and great examples.

Staying current without new authoring

  • Nightly: pattern-engine detections with resolved outcomes become draft chart questions.
  • Weekly: each new Sunday Scans issue becomes draft take-the-call questions, scored once the forward window closes.
  • As published: new Desk and #tsdr videos become draft listen-and-answer questions per chapter.
  • Daily: the morning wire's tier call becomes a call-the-regime draft, eligible after 60 days.
  • Daily: catalysts with a matching detection become what-happens-next drafts, every one reviewed by you.

Final sweep: what three independent reviews found

Three reviews ran against the code, the dossier and the firm's own rules, and against the best learning apps. They found three blockers, corrected several claims in this plan, and sharpened the product. The full record is section 13 of the design document. The short version:

Three blockers, each with a fix

  • Sunday Scans images are not stored anywhere we can serve them. They are links to Substack's servers, and the ticker attached to each image was guessed from nearby text. Fix: a one-time job copies every image into our own storage, and you confirm the ticker for each one in review.
  • The firm's daily market call is not in the wire archive. The archive keeps only the morning brief's HTML; the call word lives in a file that is overwritten every morning, and the full history sits only on your PC. The call also uses five words (Aggressive to Defensive) while the course uses four colours, and the 0-150 scale changed in March 2026. Fix: start saving the call word every morning from now on, bring the history over from your PC as reviewed facts, teach one mapping from the five words to the four bands, and for years before the engine existed ask "which warning signs had fired" instead of "what was the call".
  • Importing thousands of chart questions could trigger live data fetches on the web server. Fix: the importer only reads bars we already hold, reports what is missing, and runs in the background; authoring sessions fetch bars through the production API so the server and the review see the same bars.

Corrections to this plan

  • The course has 16 modules, not 14; the map and badges now follow the shipped course. Two shipped modules were missing from the map: the Scan Engine (the nightly funnel, A and B watchlists, alert to fill) and the Intraday toolkit (ORB, 30-minute pivot, Go Signal).
  • The 163 labelled chart cases are detector tests on eight mega-cap tickers (AAPL alone is 66); they are seed, not the bank. Real supply comes from nightly detections, the team call archive and the Model Book.
  • Six of the 26 setups have no labelled data at all (IPO Base, News Gapper, 2B Reversal, Failed Head and Shoulders, 30-Minute Pivot, Parabolic Long) and three more are near zero. Those ship only after you label examples yourself in the review screen. VCP and High Tight Flag, the two best-supplied setups, are not in the 26.
  • Candlestick direction is not a fact: on our own tape, measured from the next open, the hammer and shooting star flip sign. Candles are taught as names and what the session's auction said; any "what does it predict" question keys on the measured number.
  • Prices in questions must be percentages and bar positions, not dollars, because stored bars are split-adjusted and Scans images print the price of the day.
  • Sunday Scans "take the call" needs fair scoring: every pick was one the firm liked, so answers are scored against that issue's own base rate, the window opens at the next Monday open, "take" is defined by a trigger and a stop, and "watch" is right when neither printed.
  • Moving-average numbers differ by whose system (ours is 9/20/50/200); every number in a question is tagged by whose system it is.
  • Volume becomes three skills; relative-strength drills get a two-line chart (stock and stock over SPY); gaps, sector rotation, earnings-season maths, shorting mechanics and position-management maths each get a unit; the Method gets a psychology skill.
  • The daily set is created when a member opens it, not by a 6:30 job; the chart is mounted once per session; bars travel with the set; the drill opens fullscreen; right and wrong are a gold check and a muted cross, never green and red.

The number nobody had written down

Reviewing 7,500 items one by one is about 175 hours of pure review, and 250 to 350 hours once fixes are counted: six to nine weeks full time, or six to nine months at ten hours a week. Phase 1 alone is 90 to 130 hours. The launch target is a schedule, not a count. The review screen is specified so you never re-derive an answer: chart revealed, key highlighted, the source quote or detector numbers, what happened next, two automatic checks already run, and three keys: approve, fix, reject with a one-tap reason.

Eight calls the sweep raised, and your answers

  1. Walk-throughs: yes. Every unit opens with two or three unscored shown-answer items.
  2. Due slots: yes. Three of the ten daily questions are items coming back from 3, 10 and 30 days ago or from a skill that needs a refresh.
  3. Review: graduated trust. You review every item of a type until you have seen 20 to 30 clean examples; the review screen then asks whether to switch that type to auto-approve with a sample. A new source or template resets the count.
  4. Intraday setups stay, with an Intraday skill and a hand-labelling queue. You may add a few more setups later.
  5. VCP and High Tight Flag become their own setups: the catalog is 28.
  6. Options: full depth in Foundations, including directional trades, the Greeks, strike and expiry selection, and everything someone wanting to know options would need; flow and dealer positioning sit at the end of the track.
  7. Scans: the fair-scoring rule is adopted.
  8. Voice: your own prose is the source. The voice guide is derived from the 320 Desk transcripts, the toolkit cards, the Substack issues, the morning-wire voice exemplars and the playbooks, quoting real sentences; house words are allowed once taught in the first unit.
