## Four defects found while building the fix — three of them in the fix

### 1. `_deploy_identity` read key names the payload does not carry

It read `created_at` where `latest_deployment()` returns **`createdAt`**, and an `id` the payload
did not carry at all. Every settled row therefore hashed to `-|SUCCESS|-`, so R67's comparison
could only ever notice a **status** change: two different successful deploys, minutes apart, read
as one unchanged world.

⛔ That is the **proxy failure this guard exists to catch, wearing the costume of the fix.** Rail:
`test_R67_a_DIFFERENT_deploy_of_the_same_status_also_refuses`. The payload now carries `id`, and
`decide()` deliberately still ignores it.

### 2. R67's second read was served from the memo — structurally vacuous

`latest_deployment()` reads through `_ROWS_MEMO` (one CLI read per process, so guard 2 and guard 3
cannot describe two different worlds in one refusal message). Left in place for the second read,
the memo replays the first call's bytes: the two identities are equal **by construction**, and the
check prints nothing while proving nothing on a real push.

⛔ **The unit tests could not have seen it** — they substitute `latest_deployment` itself, so the
memo is not in their path. Fixed with a narrow, deliberate `_forget_rows()` *after* both guards
have spoken.

### 3. The rail for #2 tested the function, not the call site — and the mutation caught it

The first version of that rail called `_forget_rows()` itself and asserted the next read reached
the CLI. **M5 came back GREEN:** deleting the call site in `main()` left the whole suite passing.
It proved the function worked and said nothing about whether anything calls it — the
guard-that-tests-the-adjacent-thing defect, committed in the same hour as the paragraph warning
about it. `test_R67_MAIN_forgets_the_memo_before_its_second_read` now drives `main()` and makes the
memo the discriminator; M5 is RED.

### 4. R66 nearly added a sixth lever while retiring the fifth

The first draft made the **retired** deploy-window override refuse by name rather than be a no-op,
reasoning that a lever aimed at a retired gate still points at the gates that remain.
`tests/test_no_market_hours_window.py` went red on the variable's mere name, **and the rail was
right**: the owner's permanent-removal ruling says *presence was the problem, not the predicate* —
*"a retired rule that prints its own name on every push is not retired, it is advertised."*

Nothing reads that variable, so an operator who still has it set already gets the retired
behaviour: silence. The refusal, its two tests, its mutation and its runbook rows were **deleted**,
and the literal spelling was removed from the guard's own comments too — a dead name is best
treated by not saying it. ⭐ The distinction that survives: `UCT_SKIP_PREPUSH_GUARD` is a **live**
lever and is scoped; the window override is a **dead name**.
