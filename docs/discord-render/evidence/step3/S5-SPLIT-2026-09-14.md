## B1 · The three-way split, and the denominator that was hiding

`served_in_slo | served_late | refused_by_admission | failed | unresolved`, computed **over
OFFERS**, not over job rows.

⛔ **THE DENOMINATOR IS THE FINDING.** The 30-concurrent run held **360 job rows against 406
offers**. Forty-six requests were refused *before a job row existed*: `runtime.offer` returns
`"user_busy"` when a member already has `per_user_max` in flight, and `commands.py:206` answers with
an ephemeral **without calling `record_refused`**. Those forty-six are invisible to `success_rate`
in **both** directions — neither delivered nor counted — so **a system that refused every single
member at the door would report a success rate of 100 % over zero jobs.**

⭐ That is why the split is over offers and why the arithmetic has to close. A receipt that loses
requests reads as a quieter system than the real one.

**How the two refusal paths differ, and it matters to the member:**

| path | job row? | how the member is told | visible to S5? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `user_busy` (per-member in-flight limit) | **no** | **immediate** interaction response, type 4 | **no — invisible in both directions** |
| `queue_full` (bounded queue full at `offer`) | yes | deferred, then the failure PATCH | yes, as a failure |

## B4 · Is the refusal contract honoured — does the member hear back in time?

**Half of this is proven from an artifact that already exists; half is not measured, and the reason
is named rather than glossed.**

### The `user_busy` half — ✅ PROVEN

An admission refusal of this kind is **not** a PATCH. `commands._ephemeral` returns
`{"type": 4, …}` — the **interaction response itself** — so the member is answered inside the ack
budget, and S1 (≤ 3 s, zero tolerance) already bounds it. Measured on `load-closedloop-30.json`:

- reply kinds: **360 `deferred` (type 5) + 46 `immediate` (type 4)**, and the 46 immediates are
  exactly the 46 `refused_at_admission` the closed loop counted independently — two counts of one
  thing, agreeing;
- ack latency across all **406** offers: **p99 543.8 ms, max 545.9 ms, `over_3s` = 0**.

⭐ So every refused member was told, in plain contract wording, **inside 546 ms**.

### The `queue_full` half — ⚠️ NOT MEASURED

A `queue_full` refusal gets a job row, is deferred, and its failure message goes out through the
PATCH path. `observe._summary` computes `final_ms` **over delivered rows only**, so the time from
defer to refusal message is not in any artifact this programme holds. **That is an instrument gap,
not a pass** — filed rather than estimated.

## ⛔ OI-40 · `queue_full` means two different things, and one of them is a false sentence

A 13-offer shake-out at the design burst recorded **one `queue_full`** while the interactive queue
held at most **1 of 48** slots with six workers idle. Thirteen offers cannot fill forty-eight, so
`q.put_nowait` cannot have raised `queue.Full` — **that row did not come from `offer()`**. The other
producer is `runtime.py:285`, inside `resume_pending`: a job a dead pod left behind that cannot be
re-queued at boot is closed as `queue_full` with `outcome="restart_recovery"`.

A member on that path reads **"we're at capacity right now"** about a **pod restart**. The class is
overloaded and one of its two meanings is false.

⚠️ **Not asserted** — the deciding field is `outcome` and that sandbox is gone. The 300-second and
600-second runs above produced **zero** refusals of either kind, so they cannot settle it either. It
needs a run that deliberately provokes one, with `outcome` recorded. Filed as **OI-40**.

The directive's B5 ruling line was left blank, and the instruction for that case was explicit: report
the numbers, mark B5 blocked, **do not pick for the owner**. So:

**The question standing:** at fourteen times the design burst, is refusing 3.6 % a breach of S5 or
the system working as designed?

**What this pass adds to it, without answering it:**

1. At every load twenty days of real traffic has produced — and at **three times** the busiest ten
   seconds — the system refuses **nobody**. The floor is not under pressure from real traffic.
2. The 3.6 % is measured at a load no member population here has generated.
3. The refusals are **immediate and honest**, not timeouts: a refused member waits ~546 ms and is
   told why, with a Retry button.
4. **S5 cannot currently see 46 of the 59 refusals at all**, because they never become job rows. Any
   ruling that turns on "how many refusals is acceptable" needs that number fixed first, whichever
   way the ruling goes.
