## What it is
Price action is the record of where buyers and sellers agreed to trade: each bar shows the open, high, low and close of one session, and the sequence of bars shows whether the stock is making higher highs and higher lows (an uptrend), lower highs and lower lows (a downtrend), or overlapping ranges (sideways). Reading price action means describing that structure before adding any indicator.

## What to look for
Start with the sequence of swing highs and swing lows and name the structure plainly. Note where the close sits inside the bar's range (near the high, near the low, or in the middle) and how wide the bar is compared with recent bars. Look for places where the stock moved in a tight, orderly way versus wide, erratic swings, and mark the levels where moves started or stopped.

## Common mistakes
Reading one bar in isolation instead of the sequence around it. Calling a structure a trend when the highs and lows overlap. Adding indicators before the basic structure is described, then letting the indicator override what price plainly shows. Treating every wide bar as meaningful without checking what happened around it.

## Uncertainty
Price structure describes what has already happened; it does not tell you what the next bar will do. Two experienced traders can read the same chart differently, and a clean structure can break on the next session. Treat any reading as one input, not a forecast.
