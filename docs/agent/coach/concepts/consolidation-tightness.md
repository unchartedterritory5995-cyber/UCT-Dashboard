## What it is
A consolidation is a stretch where price trades in a narrowing or sideways range after a move, so the daily ranges get smaller and the closes cluster together. Tightness describes how small those ranges have become relative to the stock's usual movement. Traders study consolidations because they show where supply and demand have come into balance.

## What to look for
Compare recent daily ranges with the ranges during the prior move: are they contracting? Look at whether the closes are clustering near each other and near the top of the range. Note whether volume has dried up during the quiet stretch, and how long the consolidation has lasted compared with the move that preceded it.

## Common mistakes
Calling any sideways chop a base without checking whether ranges are actually contracting. Expecting a tight range to resolve in a particular direction. Confusing a dead, illiquid stock with a tight one. Measuring tightness without reference to the stock's own normal volatility.

## Uncertainty
Tightness says that movement has contracted; it does not say which way, or when, the range will resolve. Many tight ranges may simply widen again without a directional move. A consolidation can also fail quietly by drifting lower.
