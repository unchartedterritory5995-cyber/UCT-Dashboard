## What it is
An earnings gap is a jump between one session's close and the next session's open caused by an earnings report released outside market hours. The size of the gap reflects how different the report was from what the market expected, and the sessions that follow show whether the new price is accepted. Holding a position through a report means accepting the possibility of a gap that no stop can protect against.

## What to look for
Note the gap's size relative to the stock's normal daily range and the volume on the gap session. Watch whether the gap day's range holds in the sessions that follow (price stays above a gap up, or below a gap down) or whether it is filled. Compare the reaction with what the stock did after previous reports, and check the report date before planning a trade.

## Common mistakes
Treating the first minutes of a gap session as the whole reaction. Assuming a gap must fill, or must not. Holding through a report without deciding in advance that the gap risk is acceptable. Reading the headline numbers without the market's reaction to them.

## Uncertainty
The direction and size of an earnings reaction cannot be known in advance, and a strong initial reaction can reverse within days. Historical reactions for the same stock describe tendencies, not rules. Anyone holding through a report is taking a risk that sizing and stops do not control.
