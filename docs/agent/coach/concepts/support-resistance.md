## What it is
Support is a price area where a decline has previously stopped and buyers stepped in; resistance is an area where an advance has previously stopped and sellers stepped in. They are zones rather than exact prices, and they come from earlier swing highs and lows, prior consolidation ranges, round numbers and widely watched moving averages.

## What to look for
Mark the areas where price turned more than once, and prefer areas that were tested several times over a single touch. Watch how price behaves as it approaches a level: a slow, orderly approach reads differently from a fast one. When a level breaks, note whether price later comes back to it and holds on the other side, which is called a retest.

## Common mistakes
Drawing lines through every minor wiggle until the chart is covered in levels. Treating a level as an exact price rather than a zone. Assuming a level must hold because it held before. Ignoring the time frame: a level that matters on a weekly chart may be invisible on a five-minute chart, and the reverse.

## Uncertainty
Levels are where turns have happened, not where they must happen. A level can break on any session, and a break can reverse the next day. Use levels to organise what you watch and where you would be wrong, not as predictions.
