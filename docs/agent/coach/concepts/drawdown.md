## What it is
A drawdown is the decline in an account or strategy from its most recent peak to a later low, usually expressed as a percentage of the peak. Maximum drawdown is the largest such decline over a period. Drawdowns are a normal feature of every trading approach, including profitable ones, because wins and losses do not arrive evenly.

## What to look for
Track the equity curve and note the depth and length of each drawdown: how far it fell and how long it took to make a new high. Compare current drawdown with past ones for the same approach. Separate drawdowns caused by a run of ordinary losses from those caused by one oversized loss or a rule break, since they call for different responses.

## Common mistakes
Increasing size to recover a drawdown faster. Changing the approach after every losing streak. Measuring drawdown only in dollars so it looks different as the account changes size. Ignoring the time spent underwater, which is often harder to live with than the depth.

## Uncertainty
Past drawdowns set expectations but do not cap future ones: the largest drawdown of an approach may still be ahead. Streaks of losses longer than any seen before are possible with any win rate. Recovery takes a larger gain than the loss, and there is no fixed timeline for it.
