## What it is
A moving average is the average close over a fixed number of sessions, recalculated each day, so it smooths price into a line. Commonly watched lengths are the 20-day, 50-day and 200-day. Traders use them as context: where price sits relative to the average, whether the averages are rising or falling, and how they are stacked relative to each other.

## What to look for
Is price above or below the average you are looking at, and is that average sloping up, down or flat? Are the shorter averages above the longer ones (often read as an uptrend in progress) or below them? Note how price has behaved on previous visits to the average, and how far price is stretched away from it.

## Common mistakes
Treating an average as an exact support or resistance price rather than an area. Reading a crossover as a trigger without the surrounding structure. Switching between lengths until one fits the story. Forgetting that an average lags: it describes the past window, not the current session.

## Uncertainty
A moving average summarises what price already did. Price may cross an average and cross back the next day, and an average that held several times can give way on the next test. Stacked averages describe the trend so far; they do not extend it.
