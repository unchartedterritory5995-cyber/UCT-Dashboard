## What it is
A breakout is a move through a level or range boundary that had been containing price, often the top of a consolidation. A failed breakout is a move through such a level that does not hold: price comes back inside the range, usually within a few sessions. Both are common, and the failure case is part of how breakouts work, not an exception.

## What to look for
Where exactly was the boundary, and did the close finish beyond it or only the intraday high? Compare the breakout session's volume with the stock's recent average. Watch the sessions that follow: does price hold above the boundary, or close back inside it? Note the broader market context on the day, since breakouts can cluster, succeeding or failing together.

## Common mistakes
Treating an intraday poke through a level as a confirmed breakout. Ignoring volume and follow-through. Assuming a breakout must continue because it looked clean. Not deciding in advance what would show the breakout has failed, so the failure is noticed too late.

## Uncertainty
No pattern or volume signature makes a breakout certain to hold. Failure rates vary with the market environment, the stock and the time frame, and the same setup can work in one month and fail in the next. Any read of a breakout is a hypothesis with a defined point at which it is wrong.
