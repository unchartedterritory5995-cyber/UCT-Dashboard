## What it is
A stop is the price at which a trader has decided in advance that the reason for the trade is no longer valid, and the position will be exited. Placing a stop is a question of where the trade idea is wrong, not of how much loss feels comfortable. The distance from entry to stop is also what sets the position size.

## What to look for
Identify the structure the trade depends on, such as a consolidation low, a swing low or a level that was reclaimed, and place the stop where that structure is clearly broken rather than exactly at it. Consider the stock's normal daily range so the stop is not inside ordinary noise. Decide in advance whether and how the stop would be moved as the trade progresses.

## Common mistakes
Placing the stop at a round number or at a fixed percentage with no reference to the chart. Setting it so tight that normal movement triggers it. Moving the stop further away once price approaches it. Treating a stop order as a promise of the exit price.

## Uncertainty
A stop limits the planned loss, but execution can differ from the plan: gaps and fast markets can fill well past the level. Any structure can be undercut and then recover, so a stop that was logical can still be hit on what turns out to be a shakeout. Stop placement is about defining where you are wrong, not about avoiding losses.
