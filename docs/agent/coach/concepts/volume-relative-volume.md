## What it is
Volume is the number of shares traded in a session. Relative volume compares a session's volume with the stock's own recent average, so a figure above one means more trading than usual and below one means less. Traders read volume because it shows how much participation accompanied a price move.

## What to look for
Compare a session's volume with the recent average rather than reading the raw number. Note whether up sessions carry more volume than down sessions during a move, and whether volume dries up during a consolidation. Pay attention to unusually high volume on a session that reverses, and to large moves on light volume.

## Common mistakes
Reading raw volume without the stock's own baseline. Treating high volume as bullish or bearish on its own, without the price action it came with. Forgetting that index rebalances, options expiration and news can inflate volume for reasons unrelated to the chart.

## Uncertainty
Volume tells you how much trading happened, not who traded or why. A high-volume breakout can still fail, and a quiet move can continue. Volume patterns are context for a chart read, not a signal by themselves.
