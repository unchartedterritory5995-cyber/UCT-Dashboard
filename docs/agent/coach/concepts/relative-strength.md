## What it is
Relative strength compares a stock's performance with a benchmark, most often an index, over a period. A stock whose price has risen more than the benchmark, or fallen less, has shown relative strength; the relative strength line is simply the stock's price divided by the benchmark's, plotted over time. This is different from RSI, which measures a stock against its own recent movement.

## What to look for
Compare the stock's path with the benchmark's over the same window: did it hold up when the index pulled back, and did it move ahead when the index recovered? Watch whether the relative strength line is making new highs before or after price does. Look across a group of similar stocks to see which have held up best.

## Common mistakes
Confusing relative strength with RSI. Reading relative strength over a single window without checking others. Chasing a stock only because it has already outperformed, without looking at the chart structure. Ignoring that a stock can show relative strength simply because its sector is in favour.

## Uncertainty
Past outperformance is a description, not a promise. Leaders rotate, and a stock that has outperformed for months can lag sharply when the market's leadership changes. Relative strength narrows what to study; it does not say what will happen next.
