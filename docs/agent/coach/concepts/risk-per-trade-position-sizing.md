## What it is
Risk per trade is the amount a trader has decided they are willing to lose if a trade goes to its stop; it is often called one R. Position sizing is the arithmetic that turns that amount into a share count: risk per share equals the entry price minus the stop price, and shares equals the dollar risk divided by the risk per share. Outcomes are then measured in R, so a result is compared with what was risked rather than with the account.

## What to look for
Before an entry, three things must be known: where the stop is, how much is being risked in dollars, and the share count those two imply. After the trade, record the result as an R multiple so wins and losses are comparable across different stocks and sizes. Check that the planned risk does not change because the stock looks exciting.

## Common mistakes
Choosing a share count first and finding a stop that makes the maths work. Risking more on trades that feel certain. Ignoring the gap risk that a stop cannot cover. Comparing results in dollars when positions were sized differently.

## Uncertainty
The formula fixes the planned loss, not the actual one: gaps, slippage and halts can make a loss larger than one R. What dollar amount or percentage someone should risk is a personal decision that depends on their account, experience and circumstances; this concept describes the arithmetic, not the right number for anyone.
