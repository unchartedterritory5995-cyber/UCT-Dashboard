## 1. THE COMPOSITE MODULE MAP

**The reconciliation problem.** The research contains one real tension for UCT: the classic lineage teaches micro-before-macro (Weinstein teaches single-stock stages 6 chapters before applying them to indexes; O'Neil teaches M = market direction LAST in CAN SLIM even though it is the first gate checked live). But UCT's system is regime-FIRST — the regime/exposure model is not a flashy add-on, it is the load-bearing dependency for everything downstream (setup eligibility, sizing, exposure). Gagne's rule resolves it: sequence by prerequisite dependency, not by tradition. So the arc below teaches the stage lens on a single stock as a one-lesson perceptual gestalt (Weinstein's move), then immediately lifts it to the market level and installs the UCT regime engine in Module 3 — early, because it gates everything — while reserving UCT's flashiest proprietary asset (options/flow data) for the capstone tier, per Shannon's AVWAP rule (his signature technique is module 17 of 17).

**The second reconciliation: risk placement.** The lineage's early works are setup-first/risk-last (O'Neil, Minervini 2013, MTP workbook); Minervini's mature 2019 book — written after watching students misuse the system — inverts to risk-first. UCT's audience is retail members, not vetted prop trainees: use the mature ordering. Risk math (mechanical pass) lands in Module 5, BEFORE any setup family, and sizing is taught a second time (regime-adjusted, psychological, portfolio-level) in Module 11 — the "sizing taught twice" design.

**The third reconciliation: 24 setups.** Every source warns against equal-depth breadth (Qullamaggie: traders fail by jumping between setups; SMB rotates through ~20 setups in sim, then narrows hard to one at a time live). The 24-setup playbook is therefore taught as 4 family modules — ONE flagship setup per family at full depth, with the remaining family members taught as named, numerically-bounded VARIANT lessons ("same skeleton, different clothes" — the IFTA escalate-by-format move), followed by a mandatory specialization phase in the capstone paced in market cycles, not weeks.

## 2. SEQUENCING RULES

The ordering principles the research converged on, with supporting sources:

1. **Perception before tactics.** Learners see whole patterns and the full stage cycle rule-free before any buy/sell rule exists. (O'Neil's 100 winner charts in Ch.1 + chart reading in Ch.2 before any CAN SLIM letter; Weinstein teaches all 4 stages as one gestalt in Ch.2 before a single rule; NYIF and CMT both wall off chart construction from pattern analysis.)

2. **Construction before interpretation — always two separate units.** How to read the display is never merged with what to look for on it, even in a 2-day course. (CMT L1's separate Chart Construction and Chart & Pattern Analysis domains; NYIF Module 3 vs. Module 4.)

3. **Binary gates before discretionary judgment.** Teach the purely mechanical, all-or-nothing screen (Stage-2 trend template, 8 yes/no criteria) before anything requiring judgment. Separate "is this stock eligible" from "is this the right entry" and sequence the binary gate first. (Minervini Trend Template; SMB's Pyramid of Success — highest-statistical-edge, lowest-judgment skills first.)

4. **Regime early because dependency, not because tradition.** The lineage taught market direction last (O'Neil's M; Weinstein's Ch.8) — but instructional-design research says sequence by prerequisite dependency (Gagne/InstructionalDesign.org), and in UCT's system every downstream decision consumes the regime state. Deviation from the lineage is deliberate and defensible: keep Weinstein's single-stock-first perceptual on-ramp (one lesson), then lift immediately to the index level.

5. **Risk-first for this audience; sizing taught twice.** Minervini's own sequencing evolution is the clearest data point in the entire research set: his 2013 book and workshop are setup-first/risk-last; his 2019 book — after watching students misuse the system — puts five risk/process chapters before any setup. UCT's retail audience gets the mature ordering: mechanical risk math (R, expectancy, 2% rule, stop math) BEFORE setup families; regime-adjusted progressive exposure and portfolio heat AFTER them. (Think & Trade Like a Champion Ch.1-5 then Ch.8; Tharp's independent 5-rung ladder: R → expectancy → algorithm → volatility adaptation → instrument cases.)

6. **Buying fully closed out before selling begins — and selling split by intent.** No source interleaves entries and exits. Within selling, cutting losses (you were wrong) and taking profits (you were right) are separate lessons because the psychological trigger differs. (O'Neil Ch.10 vs. Ch.11; Weinstein Ch.3-5 before Ch.6; Minervini MTP Secs 18/22/23.)

7. **One lesson per NAMED setup; the lesson unit is a fused pattern+context+direction triple.** "Cup Cheat," "Power Play," "Three Weeks Tight" each get standalone sections; Investors Underground names chapters "ABCD and Long Trades," never "the ABCD pattern" in the abstract. Each of UCT's 24 plays gets its own named, numerically-bounded lesson inside its family module.

8. **Depth before breadth; specialization paced in market cycles, not weeks.** Time-boxed rotation through the families for personality fit (SMB: ~one week per setup in sim), then a hard narrowing to 1-2 setups. Mastery clock = repetitions of the pattern in live markets (Qullamaggie: EP mastery = 3-4+ earnings seasons), not calendar completion.

9. **Short side as a mirror module, after long mastery.** Weinstein Ch.7 reuses the long-side chapter beats point-for-point, deliberately last among directional tactics. UCT Family IV follows the same placement.

10. **The signature proprietary tool is the capstone, not the hook.** Shannon's Anchored VWAP — his brand, his book title — is module 17 of 17. UCT's options/flow data is the marketing headline but the SECOND-TO-LAST module, after the reflexes it overlays are built. (Weinstein also pushes options/futures to Ch.9 of 10.)

11. **Mistake catalogs and audits are late-stage review capstones.** O'Neil's 21 mistakes (Ch.13), Weinstein's Don't Commandments, IBD Level 3's post-analysis, SMB's "Why Traders Fail" checklist — always placed after the positive rules, functioning as an error-audit, not folded into the main teaching.

12. **Psychology macro-order: environment → self → application; diagnose before correct.** Douglas's Disciplined Trader completes "how markets behave psychologically" before "how you behave" before "what to do"; Tendler's system tools (map pattern, find root) precede all five emotion chapters, which precede correction. Every psych lesson opens with a self-assessment, not a technique.

13. **Spiral, not linear-by-topic; escalate by format, not new content.** IBD's 3 levels and CMT's 3 levels re-teach the same loop at higher resolution; IFTA escalates by assigning the same author's harder book. UCT reuses this: variants within a family reuse the flagship setup's skeleton; the capstone re-grades old material at application depth rather than adding topics.

14. **Design backward from the capstone.** Name the terminal skills and build the graduation assessment (playbook + trade-plan defense) before scripting lessons; any lesson that doesn't feed it gets cut. (Wiggins & McTighe UbD; Cathy Moore's action mapping; Google Data Analytics certificate mirroring the actual job workflow.)

15. **Register shifts across the arc.** Descriptive and pattern-based early (what a chart looks like) → prescriptive and numeric mid (2% ceiling, 2R minimum, cut-half rule) → judgment-based late (checklist deliberately removed, ambiguous scenarios, graded on the decision). (Minervini corpus register shift; Dreyfus model: rules for novices, stripped checklists for competents.)

## 3. LESSON-DESIGN STANDARDS

**Atomic unit and length**
- One concept per lesson, video capped at ~6-9 minutes. Engagement falls off a cliff past 6 minutes and again at 9 (Guo/Kim/Rubin, 6.9M edX sessions); if a topic needs 20 minutes, ship 3 sub-lessons with a micro-check between them. MasterClass's real ceiling: no lesson over 20 minutes, most far shorter.
- Every lesson resolves to NAMED, NUMERICALLY-BOUNDED, falsifiable criteria — "a double-bottom needs ≥7 weeks and the second low undercuts the first," "three-weeks-tight = closes within 1% for 3+ weeks on light volume, buy $0.10 above the high" — never a descriptive passage (IBD University, Chart School, VCP's quantified contraction stages 25-30% → 15% → 8% → 3-4%).
- Informal talking-head + drawing-on-chart out-engages polished studio production at the same length (Guo et al.) — record like Khan, not like a commercial.

**Internal lesson shape (Gagne's Nine Events, adapted)**
- Open with a real decision, scenario, or worked failure case the learner must act on — teach the concept while they solve it, not before (Cathy Moore action mapping; the Bloom's-aligned optometry RCT where case-first beat lecture-first).
- Then: one-sentence objective → one recall question tying to a prior module → core teaching → worked example with guidance → practice rep → immediate feedback → graded check → one transfer prompt. Skipping guided-practice/feedback and jumping content-to-quiz is the most common structural failure.

**Teach + apply as one inseparable pair**
- Every lesson or module closes with a named exercise (Moglen's per-chapter "Challenges"; Weinstein's quiz at the end of nearly every chapter — retrieval practice as a structural feature, not an add-on).
- Every module ships a matching discrete, reusable worksheet, 1:1 mapped (Tendler's library: one profile worksheet per emotion chapter, a separate A-to-C-game tool). The worksheet list IS the exercise curriculum.

**Practice conventions**
- Guess-before-you-check is the default drill format: cover the answer, predict, verify (IBD 50 Mini Charts; IBD University's four-stage funnel: definition → guided practice → daily-cadence habit → gamified drilling).
- Drilling is specified as a literal rep count or time box, never "practice this": 20-30 replay reps of ONE setup (Bear Bull Traders), one week per setup in rotation (SMB), 40 logged reps in the specialization contract.
- Late-course practice sets deliberately interleave previously-taught skills in one scenario (Peak/MIT interleaving research; the Module 9 four-family placement drill).

**Checkpoints and mastery gates**
- Gate each module behind a graded checkpoint at ~80% mastery with real remediation targeted at the missed sub-skill — this specific mechanic, not better teaching, produced Bloom's 2-sigma effect and is the backbone of Khan/Duolingo/Codecademy.
- Mastery is perishable: later checkpoints recycle earlier modules' material (Make It Stick spacing/retrieval; Khan's decaying mastery scores; Duolingo checkpoints reusing old skills). Module 2's chart-reading questions reappear inside Module 8's and Module 12's checkpoints.
- Assessment format escalates by tier, CMT-style: recall quizzes early (L1 mode), scenario-graded application mid (L2 mode), trade-plan defense and case judgment at the capstone (L3 mode, ~95% application-weighted, essay/case format).
- Phase-scoped capstones: each setup-family module ends with a mini-capstone testing ONLY what has been taught so far (Tech Elevator's weeks-1-4 capstone deliberately excludes later content).

**Review-trade pedagogy (the engine that outlives the course)**
- Fixed-field journal schema, never free-form: physical state, timing, strategy anticipated, sourcing, entry quality, management, exit-vs-plan, plus an annotated screenshot (Bear Bull's 7-field journal; David Ryan's screenshot-and-file-by-date ritual). Structure is what turns "I traded poorly" into a named, fixable failure category.
- Grade every reviewed trade on three independent axes — idea quality / execution / preparation — never P&L alone (Steenbarger's daily SMB reviews), rolled up into a monthly 4-pillar review (results vs. target, setup-level stats, goal reflection, forward plan — Ganik).
- Three nested review cadences feeding one artifact: daily tag → weekly/monthly setup-level statistics → the living playbook document (SMB).
- Best-trade write-ups on the SMB PlayBook template (Play Name, fundamentals, technicals, tape/flow read, management) are mentor-annotated and the best get promoted into a shared community playbook — review IS content production.

**Completion design**
- Module 1 must deliver a real, usable skill in the first sitting (read the regime dial, write an exposure plan) — 50% of dropout happens in weeks 1-2 (MOOC research).
- Decompose into separately-completable milestones with their own certificates/badges rather than one long syllabus: sub-2-hour units complete at 80-90% vs. 5-10% for 40+ hour monoliths.
- Impose artificial pacing even if self-paced: drip-released modules, suggested weekly deadlines, a hard capstone date — cohort/deadline formats complete at 60-95% vs. 3-48% self-paced (Ruzuku, 32,000 courses), and the mechanism is the deadline structure itself.
- Publish hour estimates per module/tier so pacing expectations are explicit (Van Tharp publishes 410 hours for his first tier).

UCT can build the strongest version of this in the industry because the practice environment is the live product: a "call the regime" daily drill against the dashboard, cover-the-answer chart reps on UCT's own charts, breadth-scorecard checks against real morning data. No book-based competitor has this.

2. **Shannon's daily-cadence application layer as the retention engine.** AlphaTrends treats premarket/midday/EOD videos as the perpetual spaced-repetition layer on top of a taught-once framework. UCT already ships Morning Wire, the Options Desk, and Sunday Scans — explicitly cross-link course lessons to these live artifacts ("today's Wire is your Module 4 scorecard, filled in") so the daily products become the course's application layer and the course becomes the products' onboarding.

One mental model at three levels: chart, position, portfolio.

5. **Chart School's two-tier taxonomy applied to the 24-play book.** Classify every play by FUNCTION: primary (opens a position) vs. secondary (adds to a winner) vs. exit-side. This gives the 24 plays an organizing skeleton beyond the 4 families and answers "when would I ever use this one."

6. **Weinstein's per-chapter quiz + Moglen's per-chapter Challenge.** Retrieval practice embedded in every unit as a structural feature. Cheap to build, largest evidence base in the whole research set (Bloom, Make It Stick).

7. **TraderLion's free-tier architecture.** One shallow full-breadth pass (their 115-page guide) plus a drip micro-lesson under 10 minutes each (Trade Lab Edge) as the funnel into the paid deep system — maps directly onto UCT's coming-soon/waitlist funnel and Substack presence.

8. **Tendler's 1:1 worksheet library.** Every module ships its own named, reusable worksheet (regime scorecard, family rubric, mental hand history, playbook template), designed to be refilled on recurrence — the course's downloads ARE its exercise system.

9. **CMT's assessment-format escalation.** Same content, three grading modes: recall quiz → scenario application → defended trade plan. The graduation gate is a decision defended under constraints, not a final exam.

10. **Google Data Analytics' terminal "go live" module.** Their capstone comes second-to-last; the final course is purely "translate the skill into the real outcome." UCT's Module 14 ends the same way: specialization contract, live-trade audit, graduation into the community/advanced tier — treating "run it live inside UCT" as its own explicit final unit.

11. **SMB's career-stage gating.** Advancement gated on a demonstrated, reviewed personal playbook and logged reps — not calendar time or video-completion percentage. Maps to gating an advanced UCT membership tier or alumni room behind capstone completion.

12. **Qullamaggie's market-cycle pacing, stated out loud.** Tell learners explicitly that EP-family mastery takes 3-4 earnings seasons. Honest pacing expectations differentiate UCT from get-rich-quick course sellers and pre-frame the subscription relationship (the course teaches the framework once; the membership is where the repetitions happen).

## 5. ANTI-PATTERNS

What the research says NOT to do:

1. **Do not open with the flashiest proprietary tool.** Shannon buries his own brand technique at module 17 of 17. Leading with options/flow before chart literacy and regime reflexes exist would produce signal-chasers, not traders.

2. **Do not teach all 24 setups at equal depth, and do not let learners hop.** Qullamaggie's explicit diagnosis of why traders fail: jumping between setups instead of mastering one. Flagship-deep + variants-shallow + forced specialization is the researched structure. Equally: do not leave the exploration/rotation phase open-ended (SMB time-boxes it at ~a week per setup).

3. **Do not interleave buy and sell content, and do not merge loss-cutting with profit-taking.** Every lineage source keeps these as separate sequential blocks; the psychological trigger differs even when the mechanical action is identical (O'Neil Ch.10/11).

4. **Do not ship one long undifferentiated course.** 40+ hour open-ended syllabi complete at 5-15%; Duke's MOOC got 2.8% to the final. Decompose into short, separately-completable, badge-bearing modules with an early win in module 1.

5. **Do not exceed the ~6-9 minute video ceiling or bundle multiple concepts per lesson.** Engagement and downstream quiz-attempt rates fall regardless of content quality (Guo et al.). Split, don't compress.

6. **Do not test a skill once and move on.** "Cover it, quiz it, forget it" is the anti-pattern Make It Stick demolishes; mastery decays (Khan) and must be recycled into later checkpoints.

7. **Do not write vague practice instructions.** "Practice identifying bases" is the weakest instruction found in every source; specify 20-30 reps, one week, 40 logged trades (Bear Bull, SMB).

8. **Do not make risk management a closing appendix for a retail audience.** Minervini himself inverted his ordering after watching students misuse the setup-first system. Equally, do not make risk an afterthought in depth — even the setup-first curricula give it multi-chapter weight (two closing chapters in the 2013 book, three closing sections of the MTP workbook).

9. **Do not fold volume/breadth confirmation into pattern lessons.** CMT isolates confirmation as its own graded domain at every level; "does this signal have a witness" is a distinct skill that dies when it becomes a bullet point inside a breakout lesson.

10. **Do not use free-form journaling.** Unstructured notes cannot be aggregated into diagnosis; the fixed-field schema is what converts "I did poorly" into a named failure category (Bear Bull's 7 fields).

11. **Do not grade progression on calendar time or completion percentage.** Gate on demonstrated mastery (80% checkpoints with remediation — Bloom) and on market-cycle repetitions for setup mastery (Qullamaggie) and on a reviewed playbook artifact (SMB), never on "watched all videos."

12. **Do not build content-first.** The information-dump course — background material before the learner is ever asked to decide anything — is Cathy Moore's named enemy and the root of bloat; every lesson must trace back to the capstone assessment or be cut (UbD).

13. **Do not teach shorting in parallel with the long side.** It is psychologically harder and is taught as a late mirror module everywhere it is taught well (Weinstein Ch.7).
